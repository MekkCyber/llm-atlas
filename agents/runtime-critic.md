# Runtime critic
*Depth — one specific technique, grounded in its source paper(s).*

**TL;DR:** A **runtime critic** is a small executable snippet (usually generated code) that watches an agent's actions *as they happen* and can veto or trigger a recovery skill without waiting for the episode to end. Introduced at scale by Zetta (Ding et al., 2026) as the mechanism that turns an "open-loop" embodied harness into a closed-loop one: the frozen base policy plus a growing library of critics/recovery snippets, kept in sync by validation-gated updates.

---

## What it is

An **agent runtime critic** is a function of the form `critic(state, planned_action) → {ok, veto, recover_with(skill)}` that runs at action frequency inside an agent loop. It's not a general reward model: it's a small, cheap, often *symbolic* or *code-based* check attached to a specific sub-skill (e.g. "before closing the gripper, verify the object is within tolerance").

Zetta contributes two things that make runtime critics practical at scale:

1. **Generated by an LLM designer**, not hand-coded — a strong model proposes candidate critics and recovery snippets between episodes.
2. **Promoted by a validation gate**, not merged blindly — a candidate becomes part of the live library only after passing offline replay against past rollouts.

---

## How it works

Three timescale-separated loops in Zetta:

- **Action-frequency loop.** The critic library is queried per action tick. Any matched critic vetoes or substitutes a recovery skill without a base-policy call. Runs at 10s–100s of Hz — orders of magnitude faster than re-prompting a base LLM.
- **Rollout-level loop.** After each episode, an LLM "designer" reads the trace + failure summary and proposes new critics or recovery snippets as code.
- **Skill-update loop.** Candidates go through a validation gate (replay against past rollouts + safety checks). Only survivors are promoted into the live library.

The base policy is **frozen** throughout — no gradient updates. All learning happens in the growing library.

## Why it matters

- **Governs execution as it unfolds.** Post-hoc reflection ("what did I do wrong?") can't recover from an in-flight failure; a runtime critic can.
- **Cheaper than online policy fine-tuning.** The base model is frozen; only the library grows. Zetta reports an **11.1× inference speedup** vs open-loop harnesses that re-prompt on every uncertain step.
- **Skills transfer.** Because critics are executable code (not model weights), they move zero-shot between tasks that share a skill vocabulary.

## Gotchas & tricks

- **Validation gate is load-bearing.** Without offline replay, LLM-generated critics regress the policy on tasks they weren't tuned on. A candidate must reduce failures on a held-out replay set before promotion.
- **Requires a decomposable skill vocabulary.** Critics attach to *sub-skills*; a fully end-to-end action policy has nothing granular to guard.
- **Cost of the LLM designer amortises poorly on short horizons.** Zetta targets long-horizon embodied tasks where the designer's per-episode call cost is a rounding error.
- **Not a substitute for reward.** A runtime critic prevents/recovers from failure; it doesn't tell the base policy what to do differently next time. Combining with online RL is future work.
