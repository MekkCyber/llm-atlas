# WEFT — Scaling Tool-Use Post-Training
*Depth — one specific technique, grounded in its source paper(s).*

**TL;DR:** Prior tool-use post-training scales one piece — the environment pool — and lets the task bank, agent harness, and evaluator drift. WEFT instruments all four, scales each, and uses execution-driven task evolution plus a stabilized training recipe. WEFT-14B beats comparable 14B baselines by +6.41 / +2.23 / +12.27 pp across three agent benchmarks. Introduced in *WEFT* (2026, arXiv:2609.36887).

## What it is

A systems-level recipe for tool-use post-training. The paper argues that an agent-training run has four scalable pillars; neglecting any one of them bottlenecks the others:

1. **Environment** — the executable sandbox and tool catalog.
2. **Task bank** — the prompts/goals the agent is trained on.
3. **Agent harness** — how tools are invoked, how turns are formatted, how errors surface.
4. **Evaluator** — scoring of rollouts (reward signal and training-time triage).

## How it works

Each pillar is scaled independently and coupled through execution-driven feedback:

- **Scalable system construction.** Containerized environments with a common tool-call interface; harness and evaluator share the same execution contract so training and eval are directly comparable.
- **Execution-driven evolution.** New tasks are generated by taking the current agent's execution failures and mutating them — "the task bank follows the agent." Tasks that reliably succeed are retired; tasks that reliably fail are investigated. Avoids static benchmarks that saturate within one training run.
- **Stable post-training recipe.** On-policy RL with a mix of trajectory-level reward (from the evaluator) and format-level shaping. Stability tricks (KL-to-reference, clipped-ratio, group-relative baseline) are specialized for the high-variance tool-rollout regime.

## Why it matters

A credible "scaling laws" view of agent post-training: compute per pillar matters, not just total compute. If the four-pillar framing holds, teams that only scale environments (the common bottleneck) have been leaving large gains unclaimed. Reported gains: WEFT-14B +6.41 / +2.23 / +12.27 pp over comparable 14B baselines across three benchmarks the paper evaluates on.

## Gotchas & tricks

- **Execution-driven task evolution needs a kill switch for degenerate failures.** A task that is unsolvable from the start will keep producing failures; a filter for "failures that look recoverable" is needed to avoid training on noise.
- **The evaluator can itself be gamed.** If the reward signal rewards format over function, the agent learns format tricks. Rotate evaluator variants during training as a cheap check.
- **Harness choice shapes what the agent learns.** The same environment and task bank with a different harness produces materially different models — the harness is a training-time choice, not just a deployment-time wrapper.
- **The system-level framing is the contribution.** Any individual pillar's recipe is replaceable; the paper's claim is that treating them together is what moves the number.
