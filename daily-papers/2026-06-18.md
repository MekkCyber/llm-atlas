## 1. Kairos

**Authors:** Kairos Team — *(affiliations not disclosed on HF page)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.16533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16533) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.16533) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.16533)
**Topics:** `world-model`, `multimodal`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

A native, end-to-end world model stack for Physical AI: a unified architecture that learns from open-world video + human + robot data, runs long-horizon rollouts with bounded error, and deploys on consumer-grade hardware. Kairos pitches itself as the world-model analogue of frontier LLM stacks — pretraining curriculum, architecture, and deployment co-design under one roof.

### Key idea

Three coupled contributions: (1) a **Native Pre-training Paradigm** with a Cross-Embodiment Data Curriculum that progresses from open-world video → human behavioral data → robot interaction; (2) a **Native Unified Architecture** with Hybrid Linear Temporal Attention — sliding-window for local dynamics, dilated sliding-window for mid-range, and gated linear attention for persistent global memory — with a formal proof that this factorization strictly bounds error accumulation across long horizons; (3) **Deployment-Aware System Co-Design** that targets low-latency observation–action–feedback loops on server and consumer GPUs.

### Main finding

Top-level performance on embodied world-model, long-horizon prediction, and action-policy benchmarks with a "strong efficiency–capability trade-off" (numbers not in the abstract). The headline claim is the theoretical bound on temporal error accumulation — a property prior video-generation world models lack.

### Why it matters

World models are pivoting from "pretty video generators" to operational infrastructure for embodied agents. Kairos is the first stack to publicly bundle the three things this transition needs — curriculum, hybrid-attention long-horizon architecture, and consumer-grade deployment — into one named system. If the long-horizon bound holds up in practice, this is the world-model template the field has been waiting for.

## 2. STARE

**Authors:** Haipeng Luo, Qingfeng Sun, Songli Wu, Can Xu, Wenfeng Deng, Han Hu, Yansong Tang — *Tsinghua SIGS / Tencent Hunyuan*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.19236](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19236) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.19236) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.19236)
**Topics:** `RL`, `RLVR`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

A token-level patch to GRPO that fixes its chronic policy-entropy collapse. STARE reweights advantages on **entropy-critical tokens** — those whose surprisal places them in the four-quadrant "near-criticality" region of the entropy gradient — and runs a closed-loop controller that keeps policy entropy in a target band over thousands of RL steps.

### Key idea

First-order gradient analysis of token-level entropy under GRPO shows per-token entropy change factors as `trajectory advantage × entropy-sensitivity function` — defining an advantage×surprisal quadrant structure. STARE: (1) picks entropy-critical tokens via batch-internal surprisal quantiles, (2) reweights their effective advantages so they move entropy in the desired direction, and (3) closes the loop with a target-entropy gate that adjusts the reweighting strength step-by-step.

### Main finding

Across 1.5B → 32B models on Short-CoT, Long-CoT, and Multi-Turn Tool Use, STARE keeps entropy in band for thousands of steps and beats DAPO + competitive baselines by **4–8% average accuracy** on AIME24 / AIME25, with reflection-token count and response length growing in tandem (the "good kind" of length growth).

### Why it matters

Entropy collapse is the single most common failure mode of long-horizon RLVR — runs die after a few hundred steps, exploration vanishes, length explodes uselessly. STARE turns that into a controlled variable rather than a hyperparameter people pray about, and ties it to a mechanistic story (the surprisal × advantage quadrant) rather than a bag of tricks.

## 3. EfficientRollout

**Authors:** Minseo Kim, Minjae Lee, Seunghyuk Oh, Kevin Galim, Donghoon Kim (FuriosaAI); Coleman Hooper, Harman Singh, Amir Gholami (UC Berkeley); Hyung Il Koo, Wonjun Kang (FuriosaAI) — *FuriosaAI / UC Berkeley*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.18967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18967) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.18967) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.18967)
**Topics:** `RL`, `efficient-inference`, `quantization`

### TL;DR

Speculative decoding for RL rollouts done right. EfficientRollout uses a **quantized self-drafter** (no separate model, no separate pretraining) that automatically tracks the evolving policy, plus a **system-aware toggle** that only speculates when the active batch is small enough to be memory-bound. Up to **19.6% rollout latency reduction** at preserved final quality.

### Key idea

Two RL-specific obstacles to vanilla speculative decoding: the target policy keeps changing (so a fixed drafter drifts), and active batch sizes shrink as rollouts finish (shifting the regime between compute- and memory-bound). EfficientRollout induces the drafter by **quantizing the target itself**, so the drafter follows the policy for free, and runs a system-aware controller that turns SD on only in memory-bound regimes and adapts draft length to acceptance rate.

### Main finding

Up to **19.6% rollout latency** and **12.7% end-to-end RL step latency** improvement vs an already-accelerated autoregressive baseline, with final model quality unchanged. The wins come specifically from the system-aware toggle: naively running SD throughout hurts in compute-bound regimes.

### Why it matters

Rollout latency is the dominant cost in production RL post-training — long-tailed generations gate every step. Speculative decoding has been a solved problem for static-model serving for two years, but RL invalidates the static-drafter assumption. This is the first paper to articulate why and provide a fix that doesn't require maintaining a separate, ever-stale drafter.

## 4. From Trainee to Trainer

**Authors:** Chao Chen, Chengzu Li, Zhiwei Li, Yinhong Liu, Zhijiang Guo — *LARK Lab @ HKUST (GZ)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.17682](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17682) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.17682) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.17682)
**Topics:** `RL`, `agents`, `data`

### TL;DR

A self-improving RL loop where the **policy itself redesigns its training environment**. The model analyzes its failure trajectories, proposes configuration changes to the env (difficulty, agent count, layout), and trains against the updated curriculum. On a multi-agent path-finding × frozen-lake benchmark, a Qwen3-4B trained this way outperforms much larger proprietary baselines.

### Key idea

LLM-as-Environment-Engineer: after each rollout phase, the policy is prompted to inspect its own failures and emit structured edits to the env config. Edits go through a verifier, the env is rebuilt, and RL continues. This frames curriculum learning as a *reasoning task* the policy itself handles, rather than an outer loop the trainer runs.

### Main finding

A Qwen3-4B trained against the MAPF-FrozenLake benchmark with self-redesigned environments beats larger proprietary models and a fixed-environment baseline at multi-agent reasoning. Exact percentages aren't in the HF summary.

### Why it matters

Curriculum design is currently a human bottleneck for agentic RL — picking which environments to train on, in what order, with what difficulty schedule. If the policy can do this for itself, the data-engineering side of RL becomes self-driving. Same pattern is being explored elsewhere (R-Zero, self-play envs), but this is the cleanest "policy-as-env-designer" formulation to date.

## 5. SAE Interventions are Unreliable

**Authors:** Mingyue Cui, Linghui Shen, Xingyi Yang — *The Hong Kong Polytechnic University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.18322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18322) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.18322) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.18322)
**Topics:** `interp`, `safety`, `eval`

### TL;DR

A critique of SAE-based safety interventions: clamping an "unsafe" SAE feature can *appear* to suppress a behavior while leaving the behavior **fully recoverable** from the post-clamp residual stream. The paper introduces post-intervention recovery as a diagnostic and identifies the SAE reconstruction residual as the main leak.

### Key idea

Standard SAE intervention: identify a feature that activates on harmful content, clamp it to a low value at inference, and observe the behavior disappearing. The authors train a small recovery probe on the **defended** residual states and find it can reliably re-elicit the suppressed behavior, even while the clamped feature stays near its clamped value. The reconstruction residual (the part of the activation the SAE *doesn't* explain) carries the suppressed information.

### Main finding

Across multiple settings, suppressed behaviors are recoverable from post-intervention activations. The clamp blocks one visible route, not the underlying capability. The paper proposes post-intervention recovery success rate as a standard diagnostic that any future SAE-defense paper should report.

### Why it matters

SAE-based latent defenses have become a load-bearing assumption in the mech-interp safety literature. If the headline interventions don't actually remove the behavior — they just close one path — every SAE-based safety claim needs to be re-evaluated. This is exactly the kind of unfalsifiability bug the field needs to fix before SAEs ship as real production guardrails.

## 6. Sumi

**Authors:** Keito Kudo, Wataru Ikeda, Ryosuke Matsuda, Keisuke Sakaguchi, Jun Suzuki — *Tohoku University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.19005](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19005) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.19005) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.19005)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `LLM`, `pretraining`

### TL;DR

The first **fully open, scratch-pretrained, large-scale uniform diffusion language model**: 7B parameters, 1.5T tokens, open weights + checkpoints + data recipe. Sumi closes the gap with autoregressive LMs at comparable token budgets on knowledge/reasoning/code, lags on commonsense (likely a data-mix artifact).

### Key idea

Uniform diffusion LMs let *any* token be updated at *any* step (vs masked-diffusion's monotonic unmask schedule), in principle giving more generation flexibility. But until now no UDLM existed at scale that the community could study and build on. Sumi is the reference point: same scale class as Llama-2 7B and equivalent token budget, trained from scratch as a UDLM.

### Main finding

Competitive with AR baselines at comparable token budgets on knowledge, reasoning, and coding. Underperforms on commonsense — the authors flag their education-heavy data mixture as the likely contributor rather than a UDLM intrinsic. Full training recipe (data mixture spec, hyperparameters, checkpoints) is released.

### Why it matters

Up to now the diffusion-LM literature had no public model at the scale where scaling behavior, generation dynamics, controllability, and trade-offs become measurable. Sumi is that artifact. Whether or not UDLMs win, the community now has a clean reference to compare against AR and masked diffusion in head-to-head studies.

## 7. CEO-Bench

**Authors:** Haozhe Chen, Zhuang Liu, et al. — *Princeton University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.18543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18543) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.18543) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.18543)
**Topics:** `agents`, `eval`

### TL;DR

A long-horizon agent benchmark: operate a simulated startup for **500 days**. The agent must navigate noisy interconnected databases, translate signals into strategy, and coordinate hiring / pricing / programming decisions toward profit. Only Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.5 finish above the $1M starting balance, and neither consistently turns a profit.

### Key idea

Most agent benchmarks test isolated short-horizon tasks (SWE-Bench, customer service). CEO-Bench bundles the four skills that *real* long-horizon agentic work demands — navigating long horizons under uncertainty, acquiring info in noisy environments, adapting to a changing world, and orchestrating many moving parts — into a single 500-day startup-simulator scenario with a hard, programmatic success metric (cash above $1M).

### Main finding

State-of-the-art models survive 500 days but don't reliably profit. Only Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.5 stay solvent; profitability is sporadic. The gap to a competent human operator remains large and quantifiable.

### Why it matters

Long-horizon agentic capability is becoming the next eval frontier (after reasoning), and existing benchmarks (Long-AgentBench, OS-World) saturate quickly. CEO-Bench's combination of long horizon × noisy info × coordination × programmatic scoring gives the field a benchmark that's both hard and machine-graded. Expect this to become standard for long-horizon agent papers.

## 8. Xcientist

**Authors:** Hanqi Li, Ziyue Yang, Zijian Hu, et al.; Lu Chen (corresponding) — *X-LANCE Lab, Shanghai Jiao Tong University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.18874](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18874) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.18874) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.18874)
**Topics:** `agents`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

A research harness for AI scientists that **externalizes** literature synthesis, idea generation, experimental plans, ablation records, and repair traces into inspectable, contract-governed artifacts. The reasoning that links prior evidence → hypothesis → experiment → claim stops living inside opaque model inference and starts living in versioned objects the agent can revise.

### Key idea

Rather than building a better AI scientist *model*, build a better AI scientist *substrate*. Xcientist organizes the scientific loop into persistent typed artifacts (literature evidence, idea state, implementation plan, ablation record, repair trace) with contract-style constraints between them. Generated mechanisms get grounded, executed, tested, and revised without losing their evidential basis — and humans can audit each step.

### Main finding

The paper positions Xcientist as a substrate / harness rather than a benchmark winner. Specific results aren't in the abstract; the contribution is the structural design and the contract grammar between artifacts.

### Why it matters

Most "AI scientist" papers either ship a thin agent loop on top of an LLM or chase end-to-end benchmark wins. Xcientist names what's missing: the artifacts and contracts that make the loop inspectable. This is the systems-design counterpart to the model-side work everyone else is doing, and probably the right shape for the substrate AI-science agents will run on.

## 9. OmniAgent

**Authors:** Ruiyang Xu, Yuxuan Wang, Jinzheng He, Ziyang Ma, Qize Yang, Yunfei Chu, Jin Xu, Junyang Lin, Chi-Wing Fu, Pheng-Ann Heng — *CUHK / SJTU / Qwen Team @ Alibaba / NTU*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.19341](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19341) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.19341) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.19341)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `RL`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

The first native omni-modal agent that formulates **video understanding as a POMDP** with an Observation–Thought–Action loop, selectively distilling audio + visual info into persistent textual memory rather than chewing every frame uniformly. Trained with Agentic SFT + Agentic RL using **TAURA** (Turn-aware Adaptive Uncertainty Rescaled Advantage) for credit assignment.

### Key idea

Most video MLLMs encode the full frame stream and pay quadratic compute. OmniAgent recasts the task: at each turn the model observes a slice, decides which part to attend to next, writes a textual memory, and acts. Agentic SFT bootstraps with synthesized OTA trajectories; Agentic RL with TAURA rescales each turn's advantage by entropy-based uncertainty so credit lands on decision points that mattered.

### Main finding

A 7B OmniAgent outperforms a 10× larger baseline on omni-modal benchmarks while using significantly fewer frames per query. Active perception beats passive bulk encoding at the same compute budget.

### Why it matters

Video / omni-modal scales catastrophically with frames. Active perception — the model decides what to look at next — is the only path that scales to long video without exploding context. OmniAgent ties that idea to a concrete POMDP formulation and a working RL recipe (TAURA), pushing the field past "stuff more frames in" into "decide which frames matter."

## 10. SR-ReaL

**Authors:** Yatai Ji, An-Chieh Cheng, Yang Fu, et al. — *NVIDIA / The University of Hong Kong / UC San Diego*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.17539](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17539) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.17539) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.17539)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `RL`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

A spatial-VLM RL recipe with **two complementary reasoning paths**: Language-Only Reasoning (linguistic deduction over scene descriptions) and Detect-Then-Reason (detect 3D region tokens first, then run explicit geometric inference). Trained jointly so the model picks the right path for each question.

### Key idea

Pure language reasoning over scene captions is great for relational queries but bad at metric / geometric ones; explicit detection-first reasoning is the opposite. SR-ReaL gives the VLM both as native skills and lets the policy choose per-question. Region tokens act as the bridge between visual detection and symbolic geometric reasoning.

### Main finding

State-of-the-art on complex spatial reasoning benchmarks requiring multi-step inference that chains depth cues, distance comparisons, and scene relations. Specific scores not in the HF summary.

### Why it matters

Spatial VLMs have hit a wall at depth + distance + scene-relation chaining — the kinds of queries that matter for robotics and AR. Dual-path reasoning is a clean way to escape the wall: don't pick one reasoning style for the whole task, let the policy switch per question. Likely a template other multi-modal reasoning systems adopt.

## 11. DRL

**Authors:** Nicolas Beltran-Velez, Felix Friedrich, Zhang Xiaofeng, Reyhane Askari-Hemmat, Xiaochuang Han, Adriana Romero-Soriano, Michal Drozdzal et al. — *AI at Meta*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.19162](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19162) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.19162) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.19162)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `RL`, `reward-modeling`

### TL;DR

Discriminator-Guided RL: train a discriminator to separate real data from base-model samples in a *pretrained representation space* (e.g. DINOv3), then use its **logit as the reward** in KL-regularized RL fine-tuning. No human preferences needed; the discriminator's logit is the optimal reward for matching the data distribution.

### Key idea

Score- and flow-matching losses regress on velocity fields under training-time marginals — they don't actually measure visual or semantic quality. RL fixes this by evaluating the model on its own samples, but requires a reward signal. Prior work uses human preferences (expensive, conflated with annotator taste). DRL replaces the reward with a discriminator trained in a pretrained representation space, restricting it to perceptually meaningful directions; the logit is provably the log-likelihood ratio between data and model.

### Main finding

On SiT, JiT, REPA, and RAE, **guidance-free FID drops** (9.38 → 2.62 on SiT) and semantic-space FD on DINOv3 drops (88.2 → 19.3 on SiT). Human-preference rewards also improve without training on them. Better Pareto frontier when stacked with subsequent preference-based RL.

### Why it matters

Flow / score matching has been the dominant generative paradigm but always needed extra hacks (guidance, preference RLHF) to look good at inference. DRL says: the data itself is the reward signal, you just needed a discriminator in the right space. This is the cleanest principled fix for the "matching loss ≠ sample quality" mismatch we have, and it composes with preference RL rather than replacing it.

## 12. RNG-Bench

**Authors:** Shengyuan Ding, Xilin Wei, Xinyu Fang, Haodong Duan, Dahua Lin, Jiaqi Wang, Yuhang Zang — *Fudan / Zhejiang U / CUHK / Shanghai AI Lab / Shanghai Innovation Institute*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.19338](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19338) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.19338) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.19338)
**Topics:** `eval`, `multimodal`, `agents`

### TL;DR

A benchmark that isolates one thing: can an MLLM **reconstruct past observations** and act on them during a multi-step interaction, when those observations are no longer visible? Two games: **Matching Pairs** (memorize briefly-revealed card identities) and **3D Maze** (integrate egocentric views into a spatial map).

### Key idea

Existing benchmarks for closed-loop MLLM policies either keep the full state visible (no hidden-state reasoning needed), conflate memory with other skills, or test recall only after the episode ends. RNG-Bench keeps the agent acting under partial observability and scores it on whether it correctly reconstructs and uses the hidden state during play.

### Main finding

A clean diagnostic for the hidden-state reconstruction skill. The two games stress the skill in different shapes (object-identity memory vs spatial-map integration). Specific model scores aren't in the abstract.

### Why it matters

MLLMs are being deployed as closed-loop policies (computer use, embodied agents) where memory of past frames is non-negotiable. Without a clean benchmark, the field can't tell whether a model's failure is perception, planning, or memory. RNG-Bench cuts memory out as its own metric — the same role IFEval plays for instruction following.

## 13. Bag of Dims

**Authors:** *(not disclosed on HF page)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.12629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12629) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.12629) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.12629)
**Topics:** `interp`

### TL;DR

The standard basis of transformer hidden states is **already** a training-free, architecture-general feature basis. Sign per dimension (±1) carries semantic content; magnitude carries confidence. Dimensions function as independent binary registers. Validated across Qwen 3.5-4B, Gemma 3-4B, Mistral 7B with four progressive experiments.

### Key idea

You don't need SAEs or any other learned dictionary to get interpretable features. Just look at the *sign pattern* of the residual-stream dimensions: replace all magnitudes with 1 and you still get 72–93% top-5 next-token accuracy through the LM head. Pure Hamming-distance scoring without any decoder reaches 80–90% top-4096. Sign patterns also organize into 175 semantic categories (mean AUC 0.80) discovered from 50 anchor tokens with zero training.

### Main finding

Across three model families, sign-only representations preserve most of the head's predictive power, and sign patterns alone organize into semantic categories. The implication: features are already there, you've been overcomplicating the search.

### Why it matters

The mech-interp field has been investing huge compute into SAEs to find a "better basis." This paper argues the standard basis is good enough for many uses, no training required. Whether the field accepts this or not, it's a serious challenge to the SAE program and a useful baseline every SAE paper should now beat.

## 14. REVES

**Authors:** Yuanxin Liu, Ruida Zhou, Xinyan Zhao, Amr Sharaf, Hongzhou Lin, Arijit Biswas, Mohammad Ghavamzadeh, Zhaoran Wang, Mingyi Hong — *Northwestern / Amazon AGI / Qualcomm AI Research / Univ. of Minnesota*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.18910](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18910) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.18910) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.18910)
**Topics:** `RL`, `reasoning`, `test-time`

### TL;DR

Test-time-scaling-aware training: standard post-training optimizes single-shot objectives, but inference uses sequential revision. REVES converts intermediate steps in successful recovery trajectories into **decoupled revision** and **verification** prompts, then alternates online data augmentation with policy optimization so the policy is explicitly trained for both error identification and answer correction.

### Key idea

Multi-turn RL over revision trajectories optimizes the full trajectory directly and wastes signal: high-quality *intermediate* mistakes that the model later recovered from are the exact training data for verification + revision skills. REVES splits the recovery trace into a revision prompt and a verification prompt, and trains both heads separately in an iterative augmentation loop.

### Main finding

The decoupled framing concentrates training compute on the two distinct sub-skills that test-time revision actually exercises. Specific benchmark numbers aren't in the HF summary, but the paper positions itself against multi-turn-RL baselines.

### Why it matters

Test-time scaling via revision is now standard (DeepSeek-R1-style verifier-driven loops, OpenAI's self-correction). Training the underlying skills explicitly — rather than implicitly via multi-turn RL — is the obvious next step, and REVES gives the field a clean recipe for it.
