## 1. DanceOPD: On-Policy Generative Field Distillation

**Authors:** Wei Zhou, Xiongwei Zhu, Zelin Xu, Bo Dong, Lixue Gong, Yongyuan Liang, Meng Chu, Leigang Qu, Lingdong Kong, Wei Liu, Tat-Seng Chua — *ByteDance Seed / NUS / UMD / HKUST*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.27377](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27377) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.27377) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.27377)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `distillation`, `multimodal`

### TL;DR

A distillation framework for flow-matching image models that lets one student model **compose multiple capabilities** — text-to-image, local editing, global editing — that normally fight each other when stacked into a single network. Each capability lives as its own velocity field over the shared flow state space, and the student trains against fields queried on its own on-policy rollout states.

### Key idea

Treat every capability (T2I, edit, CFG, realism) as a **velocity field** $v_k(x_t, t)$ over the same noisy-image manifold. For each training sample, route to one capability's field, sample one student rollout state $x_t^{\text{stu}}$, query the chosen teacher field at that state, and minimize plain velocity MSE. The "on-policy" twist is that distillation targets are evaluated at the *student's* induced states — not the teacher's — so the student learns where it actually goes. The same formulation also "absorbs" classifier-free guidance as another velocity field, removing CFG's 2× inference cost.

### Main finding

Improves T2I quality, local edit fidelity, and global edit fidelity *simultaneously* over independent specialists, while letting CFG be absorbed into the student weights. Wins on capability composition without the usual editing-vs-T2I regression.

### Why it matters

Unified image models keep losing T2I quality when bolt-on editing is added. DanceOPD gives a clean recipe — one student, multiple velocity fields, on-policy state queries — for *additive* capability composition in flow-matching models, plus a principled way to fold CFG and reward-style "realism" rewards into one network. Likely template for future unified image/video generators.

## 2. ViQ: Text-Aligned Visual Quantized Representations at Any Resolution

**Authors:** Xumin Yu, Zuyan Liu, Zhenyu Yang, Yuhao Dong, Shengsheng Qian, Jiwen Lu, Han Hu, Yongming Rao — *Tencent HY Vision / Tsinghua / NTU / CAS*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.27313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27313) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.27313) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.27313)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `tokenization`

### TL;DR

A visual tokenizer that produces **discrete tokens for any-resolution images** while keeping both low-level reconstruction detail and high-level text-aligned semantics. The goal is one drop-in code-vocabulary for multimodal LLMs that treats images symmetrically with text.

### Key idea

ViQ trains a quantized visual encoder whose codebook is *text-aligned* (so the discrete tokens land near CLIP/text directions in semantic space) but whose decoder still reconstructs fine details. Crucially it supports **native resolution input** without center-crop or fixed-grid retokenization — a long-standing pain point for VQ encoders feeding multimodal LLMs.

### Main finding

Balances reconstruction PSNR and downstream multimodal-LLM accuracy better than reconstruction-only VQ-VAEs and better than semantic-only discrete tokenizers, at variable resolutions. Positioned as a unified discrete representation for arbitrary visual inputs.

### Why it matters

The "tokenize images as text" agenda has been blocked by the detail-vs-semantics tradeoff in discrete encoders. A tokenizer that gets both right at any resolution is a building block for unified text+image autoregressive models — including the wave of "any-to-any" frontier models trying to skip continuous projections entirely.

## 3. OPID: On-Policy Skill Distillation for Agentic RL

**Authors:** Shuo Yang, Jinyang Wu, Zhengxi Lu, Yuhao Shen, Fan Zhang, Lang Feng, Shuai Zhang, Haoran Luo, Zheng Lian, Zhengqi Wen, Jianhua Tao — *Tsinghua / ZJU / CUHK / NTU / Tongji*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.26790](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26790) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.26790) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.26790)
**Topics:** `RL`, `agents`, `distillation`

### TL;DR

Adds **dense token-level skill supervision** to outcome-based agentic RL without external skill memories: skills are mined from the agent's own completed trajectories, then re-injected to score the same response with and without skill context. The log-prob shift becomes a self-distillation advantage that combines with the outcome advantage.

### Key idea

Each completed trajectory yields two kinds of "hindsight skills":
- **Episode-level** — global workflow / failure-avoidance summaries.
- **Step-level** — local critical-decision knowledge at high-leverage timesteps.

A critical-first router uses the step-level skill at flagged decision points and the episode-level skill as default. The old policy re-scores the *same* sampled response under both the original and the skill-augmented history; the log-prob delta is the token-level self-distillation advantage, added to outcome RL.

### Main finding

On ALFWorld, WebShop, and Search-QA, OPID generally improves task success, sample efficiency, and robustness over outcome-only RL and over existing skill-distillation baselines that rely on retrieved external skill memory.

### Why it matters

Outcome rewards are sparse and trajectory-level; existing skill-conditioned variants drag in costly external memory and produce off-distribution context. OPID shows hindsight-from-own-rollouts can produce dense, distribution-matched supervision *for free*, keeping RL as the primary loss. Plausible template for stabilizing agentic RL on long-horizon environment tasks.

## 4. Qwen-Image-Agent: Bridging the Context Gap in Real-World Image Generation

**Authors:** Zekai Zhang, Jiahao Li, Jie Zhang, Kaiyuan Gao, Kun Yan, Lihan Jiang, Ningyuan Tang, Shengming Yin, Tianhe Wu, Xiaoyue Chen, Xiao Xu, Yan Shu, Yanran Zhang, Yixian Xu, Yuxiang Chen, Zhendong Wang, Zihao Liu, Zikai Zhou, Huishuai Zhang, Dongyan Zhao, Chenfei Wu — *Qwen / Alibaba*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.26907](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26907) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.26907) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.26907)
**Topics:** `agents`, `multimodal`, `diffusion`

### TL;DR

An agentic wrapper around a T2I model that closes the **Context Gap**: real user prompts are underspecified, implicit, or rely on out-of-date world knowledge, and a frozen T2I model has no way to fill in the missing constraints. Qwen-Image-Agent plans, reasons, searches, remembers, and self-feedbacks until it has enough context to render.

### Key idea

Two specialized capabilities on top of an image generator: (1) **Context-Aware Planning** — explicit identification of what context is missing and how to acquire it; (2) **Context Grounding** — sub-agents that gather context by reasoning, web search, episodic memory, and rendered-output feedback. The image-generation call is just one tool the agent invokes after the context is sufficient.

### Main finding

State-of-the-art on the paper's IA-Bench (covering Plan / Reason / Search / Memory image-agent capabilities), Mindbench, and WISE-Verified, beating strong agentic and non-agentic baselines.

### Why it matters

Closes a gap that "better diffusion model" can't fix — many failures of frontier T2I come from missing world context, not weak rendering. Treating image generation as a tool call inside an agentic context-construction loop is the natural extension of the now-standard tool-using LLM pattern to multimodal output.

## 5. The Verification Horizon: No Silver Bullet for Coding Agent Rewards

**Authors:** Dayiheng Liu, Xuwu Wang, Binghai Wang, Chenlong Zhang, Jiajun Zhang, Jiawei Chen, Mouxiang Chen, Rongyao Fang, Siyuan Zhang, Yuheng Jing, Zeyao Ma, Zeyu Cui, et al. — *Qwen Team / Alibaba*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.26300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26300) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.26300) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.26300)
**Topics:** `RL`, `agents`, `eval`

### TL;DR

A position-plus-experiments paper that argues **verifying solutions is now harder than generating them** for coding agents, and characterizes any reward signal along three axes — *scalability*, *faithfulness*, *robustness* — that can't be maxed simultaneously. Compares four concrete reward constructions (unit-test verifier, rubric verifier, user-as-verifier, automated agent verifier) and the failure modes of each as policy capability grows.

### Key idea

Reframes coding-agent RL around the "verification horizon" — the point past which any fixed verifier becomes a proxy that diverges from real intent under optimization pressure (reward hacking, signal saturation). The three-axis taxonomy is then used to design targeted verifiers per task type: test verifiers for general coding, rubrics for frontend, the user themselves for real-world tasks, agent-verifiers for long-horizon.

### Main finding

Targeted verifier design suppresses reward hacking, lifts task completion, and yields gains across multiple internal and public benchmarks. The headline argument is qualitative though: **no fixed reward function stays effective as policy capability grows — verification must co-evolve with the generator.**

### Why it matters

A practical statement of what frontier labs have been quietly finding — the bottleneck in agentic RL has shifted from "can we generate?" to "can we score?" Anchors a research direction on co-evolving generator+verifier loops, and offers a concrete taxonomy for reasoning about reward design rather than picking one verifier and hoping.

## 6. JetSpec: Breaking the Scaling Ceiling of Speculative Decoding with Parallel Tree Drafting

**Authors:** Lanxiang Hu, Zhaoxiang Feng, Yulun Wu, Haoran Yuan, Yujie Zhao, Yu-Yang Qian, Bojun Wang, Peng Zhao, Daxin Jiang, Yibo Zhu, Tajana Rosing, Hao Zhang — *UCSD / Zhejiang U. / UIUC / Nanjing U. / StepFun*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.18394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18394) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.18394) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.18394)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

A speculative-decoding (SD) draft head that produces a **causal tree of draft tokens in one forward pass**. Targets the long-standing causality-efficiency dilemma — autoregressive drafters give high acceptance but expensive trees, bidirectional block-diffusion drafters give cheap drafting but mutually inconsistent trees that waste budget.

### Key idea

JetSpec trains a single causal parallel draft head on top of fused hidden states from the frozen target model. The head emits an entire candidate tree in one pass, but each branch is *causally* conditioned on its parent path, so the tree's joint score matches the target model's autoregressive factorization. Larger draft budgets convert into longer accepted prefixes without exploding drafter cost.

### Main finding

Up to **9.64× speedup on MATH-500** and **4.58× on open-ended chat** on H100 with Qwen3 dense and MoE models, beating bidirectional-head and tree-based SD baselines. Further latency gains under realistic serving load via vLLM integration. Code and models released.

### Why it matters

SD throughput has stalled because no head architecture simultaneously had cheap drafting, branch-wise causal conditioning, and tree-shaped candidates. JetSpec is the first design that hits all three, and it shows the headroom (~10×) that's still available on long-reasoning workloads where draft acceptance can be very high. Useful template for the next-gen of vLLM/SGLang SD heads.

## 7. Fast LeWorldModel

**Authors:** Yuntian Gao, Xiangyu Xu — *(affiliations not listed on arXiv abstract page)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.26217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26217) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.26217) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.26217)
**Topics:** `world-models`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

A JEPA-based latent world model for visual planning that swaps **repeated one-step rollout for action-prefix prediction**. Given a current latent and an action sequence, Fast-LeWM encodes each action prefix and predicts the future latent reached at that prefix length, *all in parallel*.

### Key idea

In LeWM, planning evaluates candidate action sequences by repeatedly applying a local one-step latent transition. Both cost and prediction error grow with horizon. Fast-LeWM redefines the prediction unit as the **action prefix**, so the predictor learns latents-at-the-end-of-prefix directly, supervised at many horizons jointly. Planning becomes a single parallel pass; the last prefix token's predicted latent stands in for the full rollout.

### Main finding

Improves average success on multiple control tasks while substantially reducing planning latency, with open-loop latent loss whose growth slows as the rollout horizon increases — exactly opposite the autoregressive failure mode.

### Why it matters

JEPA-style latent world models are a leading non-generative substrate for visual planning, but their per-step rollout cost has capped horizons. Action-prefix prediction is a simple, general fix that decouples planning compute from horizon length, and the same trick should transfer to other latent-dynamics models for VLAs and robotics-foundation models.

### Knowledge graph integration

*Borderline: included because the technique generalizes to any latent-dynamics model used as a world model for foundation policies, not just classical control.*

## 8. Why Multi-Step Tool-Use RL Collapses and How Supervisory Signals Fix It

**Authors:** Yupu Hao, Zhuoran Jin, Huanxuan Liao, Kang Liu, Jun Zhao — *CASIA, Institute of Automation / UCAS*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.26027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26027) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.26027) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.26027)
**Topics:** `RL`, `agents`, `SFT`

### TL;DR

A careful diagnosis of a recurring failure mode in agentic RL: tool-use ability **collapses abruptly** after some steps. Authors find the underlying tool-use skill is intact — what breaks is the probability of a few control tokens that govern structured execution. Interleaving SFT with RL is the cleanest fix.

### Key idea

Track per-token probabilities during agentic RL runs that show collapse. Finding: rather than the policy "forgetting how to use tools," **a small set of structural control tokens** (tool tags, separators, schema markers) develop unexpected probability spikes, breaking parse-correct emission of tool calls. The latent capability survives, masked by format failure.

They then sweep a battery of supervisory signals — off-policy SFT, hint-based guidance, erroneous-example supervision — under synchronous and interleaved RL schedules. **Interleaved SFT + RL** wins on stability but pays an OOD-format generalization tax.

### Main finding

Interleaved SFT+RL substantially reduces collapse rate vs RL-only baselines, at the cost of degraded performance under format / content OOD evaluation. Learning rate has a strong moderating effect.

### Why it matters

Mechanistic explanation for one of the most common production headaches in agentic RL today. The "structural tokens spike, capability hides" story is much sharper than the usual "RL is unstable" hand-wave, and the SFT-interleaving recipe is a portable tactic. Also a cautionary note: stability-vs-OOD-generalization is a real tradeoff in the supervisory-signal design space.

## 9. Running the Gauntlet: Re-evaluating the Capabilities of Agents Beyond Familiar Environments

**Authors:** Mykola Vysotskyi, Runqi Lin, Grzegorz Biziel, Michal Zakrzewski, Sebastian Montagna, Damian Rynczak, Shreyansh Padarha, Kumail Alhamoud, Zihao Fu, William Lugoloobi, Kai Rawal, Hanna Yershova, Xander Davies, Taras Rumezhak, Guohao Li, Fazl Barez, Baoyuan Wu, Arkadiusz Drohomirecki, Yarin Gal, Chris Russell, Christopher Summerfield, Adam Mahdi, Volodymyr Karpiv, Philip Torr, Adel Bibi — *University of Oxford et al.*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.14397](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14397) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.14397) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.14397)
**Topics:** `eval`, `agents`, `multimodal`

### TL;DR

**GauntletBench** — a web-based agent benchmark targeting three under-evaluated capability axes (temporal perception, graphical understanding, 3D reasoning) across five unusual professional apps (Video Editor, Workflow Builder, 3D Modeller, Flight Analyser, Circuit Designer), 100 vision-intensive tasks total. Frontier agents reach 19.1%; non-expert humans reach >80%.

### Key idea

Most current agent benchmarks recycle popular apps (browsers, terminals) where models have memorized affordances. GauntletBench deliberately picks **less-covered professional applications**, each requiring spatial/temporal reasoning that text-trace benchmarks underrepresent. A modular pipeline (env + task suite + automated evaluator) makes it framework-agnostic.

### Main finding

State-of-the-art agents achieve **19.1% success** vs **>80% for non-expert humans** — a much larger gap than typical web-agent benchmarks reveal. Headroom is concentrated in the three flagged capability axes.

### Why it matters

Useful pressure-test on the common claim that frontier agents are approaching human-level on real-world computer use. The benchmark is constructed to defeat memorization-driven success and exposes that agentic shortfall is concentrated in visual / spatial / temporal capacities, not in tool-orchestration syntax. Sets a sharper north star for VLA / computer-use training.

## 10. LISA: Likelihood Score Alignment for Visual-condition Controllable Generation

**Authors:** Yanghao Wang, Hongxu Chen, Jiazhen Liu, Zhenqi He, Rui Liu, Zhen Wang, Long Chen — *HKUST / Huawei Research*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.27192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27192) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.27192) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.27192)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `multimodal`

### TL;DR

A small extra loss that **explicitly aligns the side network's intermediate features to an approximated likelihood score** in dual-branch controllable diffusion / flow models (ControlNet-style). Re-interprets the classic dual-branch architecture through the score-based generative lens — the main net contributes a prior unconditional score; the side net implicitly contributes a likelihood.

### Key idea

Hook a feature from a designated layer of the side branch, project it via a lightweight decoder into the score latent space, and regularize the decoder output toward an approximated likelihood-score target. Trained jointly with the standard diffusion loss. Zero added inference cost; small training overhead.

### Main finding

Across multiple image/video tasks, architectures, and diffusion/flow backbones, LISA consistently **speeds up training convergence** and improves final controllable-generation quality. Also nudges side-network features to be more disentangled along the conditional axis.

### Why it matters

A simple principled grounding for an architecture (dual-branch with frozen main + trainable side) that the field has used heuristically for years. The score-decomposition interpretation is a useful conceptual upgrade and the regularizer is plug-and-play across flow models — likely default ingredient for next-gen ControlNet-style trainings.

## 11. ICWM: In-Context World Modeling for Robotic Control

**Authors:** Siyin Wang, Junhao Shi, Senyu Fei, Zhaoyang Fu, Li Ji, Jingjing Gong, Xipeng Qiu — *Fudan / SII*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.26025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26025) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.26025) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.26025)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `agents`

### TL;DR

Treats system identification for a VLA as an **in-context adaptation problem**. Before executing a task, the policy ingests a short history of *self-generated, task-agnostic* interactions in its context; this implicitly captures camera pose / morphology / dynamics, letting the same weights generalize to novel setups without fine-tuning.

### Key idea

Standard VLA conditioning uses only the current observation + instruction, which assumes the training-time execution context. ICWM adds a preamble of self-generated probing interactions to the context window. Unlike traditional ICL — which uses demos to *specify what* — ICWM uses the context to *infer how* the world operates. The model learns to read these probes to adjust its action policy at run time.

### Main finding

ICWM significantly beats standard VLA baselines on novel camera viewpoints in both simulation and real robots — without any parameter updates per new configuration.

### Why it matters

VLA generalization to small viewpoint / morphology / calibration changes is the operational blocker for deploying foundation-model robots. Moving system-ID into the prompt — recoverable from short on-robot probes — is a far cheaper alternative to per-deployment fine-tuning, and naturally extends to other in-context adaptations for foundation policies.

## 12. CoffeeBench: Benchmarking Long-Horizon LLM Agents in Heterogeneous Multi-Agent Economies

**Authors:** Daichi Hattori, Kazuo Araragi, Keita Ogawa, Shota Onose, Taro Makino, Teppei Usuki, Takashi Ishida — *KPMG AZSA / Sakana AI*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.16613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16613) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.16613) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.16613)
**Topics:** `eval`, `agents`

### TL;DR

A 90-day simulated **coffee supply-chain economy** of six heterogeneous LLM agents — two farmers, two roasters, two retailers — each autonomously negotiating, transacting, and managing cash / inventory / pricing to maximize cumulative net income.

### Key idea

Existing agent benchmarks evaluate a single agent against a passive environment. CoffeeBench inverts this: the environment is *other LLM agents* with their own private objectives, and the long horizon (90 simulated days) forces multi-step memory, planning, and price discovery. Roles are heterogeneous (different cost structures), removing the symmetry crutch that simplifies symmetric multi-agent benchmarks.

### Main finding

Provides a richer multi-agent economic testbed than prior work. (Specific frontier-model leaderboard numbers not visible from the abstract.)

### Why it matters

Economic simulation is one of the few domains where long-horizon multi-agent coordination and incentive-aware behavior are forced rather than optional. CoffeeBench gives the field a standard testbed for that capability, complementing existing single-agent web/computer-use suites.

## 13. Discretizing Reward Models

**Authors:** Vijay Viswanathan, Shiqi Wang, Devamanyu Hazarika, Chirag Nagpal, Tongshuang Wu, Graham Neubig, Yuning Mao — *CMU / Meta Superintelligence Labs*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.21795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21795) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.21795) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.21795)
**Topics:** `RL`, `reward-modeling`

### TL;DR

Argues continuous reward models are systematically **oversensitive** — they assign different scores to equally-good responses — and that oversensitivity, not low accuracy, is the dominant cause of bad RL policies. Proposes a training-free **MC-dropout discretization** that collapses continuous scores into a small set of reward clusters.

### Key idea

Reframes RM quality with two distinct measures: **discriminative ability** (separates better from worse responses) and **specificity** (the complement of oversensitivity — equally-good responses get the *same* score). Theoretically shows that perfectly-accurate reward models can still be highly oversensitive and break RL.

The fix: run the RM with Monte Carlo dropout, cluster the resulting score distribution per prompt into discrete buckets, and use the bucket label as the reward. Training-free, applies to any neural RM, provably reduces oversensitivity at small expense to discrimination.

### Main finding

In controlled and natural RL settings, discretized rewards yield **less reward hacking and better policies** than the original continuous rewards.

### Why it matters

A clean, deployable fix for a widely-felt but rarely-formalized RM pathology. Reframes the long debate between learned RMs and verifiable rewards: the gap may be partly explained by continuous-RM oversensitivity, and partially closed by simple discretization. Likely default ingredient in future reward-model serving stacks.

## 14. InfoKV: Information-Aware KV Cache Compression for Long Reasoning

**Authors:** Zhuiri Xiao, Alexandra Birch, Zhouhan Lin — *SJTU LUMIA Lab / University of Edinburgh*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.26875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26875) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.26875) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.26875)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

Attention-weight-based KV compression undercounts the tokens that matter most for *future* long-context reasoning. InfoKV adds an **information-theoretic** signal — token-level predictive uncertainty plus layer-wise representation evolution — to keep the tokens whose influence reaches farther into the rollout.

### Key idea

The authors define **Forward Influence** — how much compressing a token affects future representations. Empirically, attention-selected tokens dominate *local* future tokens but high-entropy tokens dominate *distant* future tokens. InfoKV combines per-token entropy and layer-wise representation drift with the standard attention-score signal to pick which KV entries to keep during long reasoning prefill and decode.

### Main finding

Outperforms attention-only KV compression baselines on long-context reasoning benchmarks with Llama-3.1, Llama-3.2, and DeepSeek-R1, in both prefill and decoding scenarios.

### Why it matters

Long-CoT reasoning has made KV-cache size the live bottleneck on inference. Most cache-compression work has equated "important" with "high attention weight," but high-uncertainty tokens are the carriers of *future* useful information — a different optimum. Plausible default in future inference engines.
