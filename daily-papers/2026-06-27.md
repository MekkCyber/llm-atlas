## 1. DanceOPD

**Authors:** Wei Zhou, Xiongwei Zhu, Zelin Xu, Bo Dong, Lixue Gong, Yongyuan Liang, Meng Chu, Leigang Qu, Lingdong Kong, Wei Liu, Tat-Seng Chua — *ByteDance Seed / NUS / UMD / HKUST*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.27377](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27377) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.27377) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.27377)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `distillation`, `flow-matching`

### TL;DR

An on-policy distillation framework that lets a single flow-matching student inherit *multiple* heterogeneous teacher capabilities — text-to-image generation, local editing, and global editing — without one capability cannibalising another. The trick is to keep each capability as its own teacher "field" and query that field only on states the student actually visits.

### Key idea

Treat each capability (T2I, local edit, global edit) as a separate teacher generative *field*. During training, route every sample to its capability-specific teacher and align student and teacher velocities (MSE on velocity fields) at the student's on-policy trajectory points. This avoids the off-policy mismatch that makes naive multi-task flow distillation forget base generation quality.

### Main finding

Composes the three capabilities into one student that beats per-capability baselines on editing benchmarks while *preserving* base T2I quality. The on-policy field formulation is the load-bearing piece — off-policy variants collapse one or more capabilities.

### Why it matters

Modern image stacks are turning into "model + edit head + control head" zoos. Showing that flow-matching distillation can fold heterogeneous editing skills into one student is a credible recipe for collapsing those zoos — and reuses the on-policy story that has already taken over LLM post-training.

## 2. OPID

**Authors:** Shuo Yang, Jinyang Wu, Zhengxi Lu, Yuhao Shen, Fan Zhang, Lang Feng, Shuai Zhang, Haoran Luo, Zheng Lian, Zhengqi Wen, Jianhua Tao — *Tsinghua / ZJU / CUHK / NTU / Tongji*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.26790](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26790) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.26790) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.26790)
**Topics:** `RL`, `agents`, `distillation`

### TL;DR

A self-distillation method for agentic RL that turns sparse trajectory-level outcome rewards into dense, token-level skill supervision — without relying on external skill memories or privileged retrieved context that drift out of distribution.

### Key idea

Mine "skills" directly from the agent's own completed on-policy trajectories. Each successful trajectory yields skill-conditioned targets that the policy distills against during the next RL step, giving every token a supervised signal. No replay buffer of curated skill exemplars, no retrieval over privileged context.

### Main finding

On multi-turn agentic benchmarks the OPID-trained policies are more stable than outcome-only RL baselines and match or beat skill-memory-based variants, while removing the costly external skill store.

### Why it matters

Outcome-only RL on agents has been crashing into the same wall — long horizons + sparse rewards = brittle training. The trend across the field is to inject step-level signal; OPID is the cleanest "make the skill supervisor look like the current policy" formulation we've seen this week.

## 3. Qwen-Image-Agent

**Authors:** Zekai Zhang, Jiahao Li, Jie Zhang, Kaiyuan Gao, Kun Yan, Lihan Jiang, Ningyuan Tang, Shengming Yin, Tianhe Wu, Xiaoyue Chen, Xiao Xu, Yan Shu, Yanran Zhang, Yixian Xu, Yuxiang Chen, Zhendong Wang, Zihao Liu, Zikai Zhou, Huishuai Zhang, Dongyan Zhao, Chenfei Wu — *Qwen / Alibaba*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.26907](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26907) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.26907) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.26907)
**Topics:** `agents`, `diffusion`, `multimodal`

### TL;DR

Wraps a T2I model in an agentic loop — plan, reason, search the web, remember, take feedback — to close the *Context Gap*: real user prompts are underspecified and need fresh world knowledge that frozen diffusion weights don't have.

### Key idea

Frame T2I failure modes as a *context* problem rather than a *model* problem. The agent expands the user request through (1) intent planning, (2) web/world-knowledge search, (3) memory of prior turns, and (4) generation-then-critique feedback loops before invoking the underlying diffusion model.

### Main finding

On real-world request benchmarks the agentic wrapper beats single-shot generation with the same base model, especially on prompts that require up-to-date knowledge or implicit constraints. The base diffusion model is unchanged.

### Why it matters

Most image-gen progress this year is on the *model*; this paper makes the case that the rate-limiting step in production is *retrieval and reasoning around* the model — squarely an agent problem. Consistent with the broader move to agent-wrapped generative models.

## 4. Verification Horizon

**Authors:** Binghai Wang, Chenlong Zhang, Dayiheng Liu, Jiajun Zhang, Jiawei Chen, Mouxiang Chen, Rongyao Fang, Siyuan Zhang, Xuwu Wang, Yuheng Jing, Zeyao Ma, Zeyu Cui (core), plus ~12 additional contributors — *Qwen / Alibaba*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.26300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26300) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.26300) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.26300)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `rewards`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Position paper plus measurements: as coding agents get stronger at *generating* candidate solutions, the bottleneck inverts and becomes *verifying* them. The paper proposes characterising any verifier along three dimensions — scalability, faithfulness, robustness — and argues you cannot get all three at once.

### Key idea

Every verifier is a proxy for human intent and intent is underspecified by nature. RL optimisation therefore *widens* the proxy-intent gap (reward hacking, signal saturation). Decouple the discussion of verifiers along {scalability, faithfulness, robustness}; show that classical setups trade one off against another.

### Main finding

Across SWE-style benchmarks no current verifier (unit tests, learned reward model, LLM judge, programmatic checker) achieves all three properties simultaneously — and adding training pressure makes it worse. The three-axis framing predicts which failure mode (hacking vs saturation vs brittleness) bites for a given verifier choice.

### Why it matters

Coding-agent RL has been the leading testbed for verifiable rewards. This paper formalises the cracks that practitioners have been hitting all year and gives the field a shared vocabulary to discuss verifier quality — useful for designing the next generation of mixed-verifier reward stacks.

## 5. ViQ

**Authors:** Xumin Yu, Zuyan Liu, Zhenyu Yang (equal), Yuhao Dong, Shengsheng Qian, Jiwen Lu, Han Hu, Yongming Rao — *Tencent HY Vision / Tsinghua / CAS / NTU*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.27313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27313) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.27313) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.27313)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `tokenizer`, `quantization`

### TL;DR

A discrete *visual* tokenizer that balances semantics and pixel detail while accepting native resolutions, so the same tokens can serve both vision-language understanding and image reconstruction in a unified discrete representation.

### Key idea

Existing visual quantization tokenizers (e.g. VQ-VAE for reconstruction vs SigLIP-style for semantics) trade detail for meaning. ViQ aligns codebook tokens with text features (the "T" in ViQ) while preserving the spatial fidelity needed to reconstruct or edit. Native-resolution support means no forced 224/336 square crops, so high-detail inputs survive into the LLM context.

### Main finding

ViQ matches or beats prior visual tokenizers on both reconstruction and downstream multimodal understanding while supporting arbitrary input resolutions — a notable property since most discrete tokenizers regress on detail to gain semantic alignment.

### Why it matters

Any-to-any multimodal LLMs need *one* visual tokenizer they can both consume and emit. ViQ is a concrete step toward that unified discrete representation without the usual detail-loss tax.

## 6. JetSpec

**Authors:** Lanxiang Hu, Zhaoxiang Feng, Yulun Wu, Haoran Yuan, Yujie Zhao, Yu-Yang Qian, Bojun Wang, Peng Zhao, Daxin Jiang, Yibo Zhu, Tajana Rosing, Hao Zhang — *UCSD / Zhejiang / UIUC / Nanjing / StepFun*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.18394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18394) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.18394) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.18394)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`, `speculative-decoding`

### TL;DR

A head-based speculative decoding scheme that breaks the "scale draft budget → diminishing return" wall by drafting an entire candidate *tree* in one forward pass, with each branch causally conditioned on its parent path — so the candidates remain mutually consistent and acceptance rate stays high as the tree grows.

### Key idea

Train a parallel draft head on fused hidden states from the frozen target model. Unlike block-diffusion drafters (one forward pass but branch-agnostic, so siblings disagree) and autoregressive drafters (causal but cost grows with depth), JetSpec's head produces a *causal* tree in *one pass*: branches are scored under the target model's autoregressive factorisation.

### Main finding

Up to **9.64× speedup on MATH-500** and **4.58× on open-ended chat** on dense and MoE Qwen3 models on H100, with further gains shown via vLLM integration under realistic serving load. Beats both bidirectional-head and tree-based SD baselines as draft budget scales.

### Why it matters

Speculative decoding has been hitting a ceiling in production — bigger trees stopped paying off. JetSpec is the first scheme this year that cleanly converts a bigger draft budget into longer accepted prefixes, which is exactly what serving systems want.

**Builds on / relates to existing pages:**

## 7. GUI vs CLI

**Authors:** Xiao Zhou, Siyue Zhang, Yilun Zhao, Jinbiao Wei, Tingyu Song, Arman Cohan, Chen Zhao — *NYU Shanghai / Yale NLP / NTU*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.24551](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24551) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.24551) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.24551)
**Topics:** `agents`, `eval`, `computer-use`

### TL;DR

A controlled comparison of screen-only ("GUI") vs skill/CLI-mediated computer-use agents on 440 desktop tasks with matched objectives, initial states, and verification criteria. Localises where each modality breaks.

### Key idea

Most prior computer-use benchmarks compare apples to oranges — different tasks for different modalities. This paper pins the task constant and varies only the interaction channel, so you can attribute success/failure differences to the *channel*.

### Main finding

Screen-only GUI agents bottleneck on perception (locating elements, parsing dense visual context); CLI/skill agents bottleneck on coverage (skill libraries are incomplete for ad-hoc tasks). The two modalities fail on different subsets of the benchmark — suggesting hybrid orchestration is the actual win.

### Why it matters

Computer-use is becoming a primary agent surface (Anthropic, OpenAI, Adept). This paper is the cleanest separation of "is the bottleneck the screen, the skill, or the planner?" — important for deciding what to train next.

## 9. Tool-Use Collapse

**Authors:** Yupu Hao, Zhuoran Jin, Huanxuan Liao, Kang Liu, Jun Zhao — *CAS Institute of Automation / UCAS*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.26027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26027) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.26027) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.26027)
**Topics:** `RL`, `agents`, `tool-use`

### TL;DR

Diagnoses *why* multi-step tool-use RL training collapses — it's not capability degradation, it's a *control-token* probability spike — and shows that interleaving SFT with RL substantially stabilises training.

### Key idea

Trace the collapse to a specific failure mode: during long agentic rollouts, probabilities on the formatting/control tokens (`<tool>`, `<action>`, etc.) spike out of distribution, breaking the parser. The fix is supervisory: keep SFT-style imitation pressure on those tokens by interleaving SFT batches with RL updates, optionally augmented with hints or erroneous-example supervision.

### Main finding

Interleaved SFT+RL training keeps tool-use trajectories well-formed across long horizons where pure-RL collapses. Format-level generalisation to *unseen* tool schemas remains hard — the paper is explicit that this isn't fully solved.

### Why it matters

"Why does our tool-use RL run nosedive at step 600?" is the question of the year for agent teams. Reframing the issue as control-token drift (rather than reasoning collapse) points at a much simpler fix than yet another reward-shaping trick.

## 10. LISA

**Authors:** Yanghao Wang, Hongxu Chen, Jiazhen Liu, Zhenqi He, Rui Liu, Zhen Wang, Long Chen — *HKUST / Huawei Research*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.27192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27192) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.27192) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.27192)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `flow-matching`, `controllable`

### TL;DR

Re-reads the popular dual-branch controllable-generation paradigm (ControlNet-style side network on a frozen main diffusion model) as *score decomposition*, and proposes a regulariser — Likelihood Score Alignment — that explicitly aligns the side-branch features with an approximated likelihood score.

### Key idea

Decompose the dual-branch model's score: the main network supplies the *prior* unconditional score, the side network implicitly contributes the *likelihood* score. Hook a designated side-branch layer, project it via a lightweight decoder into score space, and regularise it toward a constructed likelihood-score target alongside the standard diffusion loss.

### Main finding

LISA accelerates training convergence and improves final quality across image and video controllable-generation tasks, with negligible extra training cost and zero extra inference cost. The side-branch features also become more disentangled, which is useful for downstream conditional editing.

### Why it matters

Controllable generation is everywhere (depth, edges, segmentation, identity, pose) and almost all of it uses dual-branch designs. A clean theory of *what the side network is actually doing* — and a cheap regulariser that exploits it — generalises across architectures and modalities.

## 11. InfoKV

**Authors:** Zhuiri Xiao, Alexandra Birch, Zhouhan Lin — *SJTU LUMIA Lab / Edinburgh*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.26875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26875) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.26875) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.26875)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`, `reasoning`, `KV-cache`

### TL;DR

KV-cache compression that doesn't just use attention scores. Tokens with high *predictive uncertainty* (high next-token entropy) influence the distant future much more than attention alone suggests — InfoKV adds entropy-based importance to the compression criterion.

### Key idea

Define *Forward Influence*: how much does evicting a token change the model's behaviour over the *future* context. Empirically, attention-selected tokens influence *nearby* context, while *high-entropy* tokens influence *distant* future context. Combine attention scores with per-layer entropy signals so long-range information survives compression.

### Main finding

On long-context reasoning benchmarks with Llama-3.1, Llama-3.2, and DeepSeek-R1, InfoKV consistently beats prior attention-only KV compression in both long prefilling and decoding.

### Why it matters

Reasoning models generate enormous CoTs, and KV-cache is the dominant memory cost. Showing that uncertainty signals catch long-range importance that attention misses is a small, deployable insight that improves cost/quality on the highest-traffic workload of 2026.

**Topics:** `world-models`, `safety`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Argues that hallucinated rollouts in learned world models are not random noise — they're predictable from internal model signals — and therefore *preventable* via a targeted intervention rather than blanket regularisation.

### Key idea

### Main finding

Hallucination is predictable from internal model signals across the world-model benchmarks studied, and intervening on flagged steps materially reduces downstream rollout drift. (Concrete numbers were not surfaced via the HF abstract excerpt — see the paper.)

### Why it matters

The "long-horizon generative model goes off the rails" failure mode is shared across world models, long-CoT reasoning, and video generation. A predictable + preventable framing transfers to text-only reasoners that hallucinate mid-chain.

## 13. Progress Advantage

**Authors:** Changdae Oh, Wendi Li, Seongheon Park, Samuel Yeh, Tanwi Mallick, Sharon Li — *University of Wisconsin–Madison / Argonne National Laboratory*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.26080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26080) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.26080) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.26080)
**Topics:** `RL`, `agents`, `rewards`

### TL;DR

Process Reward Models for agents are expensive to build (long horizons, irreversible actions, stochastic environments). The paper proves that an RL-trained policy already contains the ingredients for free: the log-ratio between the RL policy and its reference policy *exactly recovers* the optimal advantage function — a "free" PRM.

### Key idea

In a general stochastic MDP, derive an implicit per-step advantage — termed *progress advantage* — as `log π_RL(a|s) − log π_ref(a|s)`. This is the optimal advantage under the RL objective; no extra reward model needs to be trained.

### Main finding

Using progress advantage as a step-level scorer matches or beats explicitly-trained process reward models on agentic benchmarks, while costing zero additional annotation or training.

### Why it matters

If this holds at scale, it kills off a whole class of expensive PRM-construction pipelines for agents. The implicit-advantage idea has been hovering around DPO-derivative work for a while; this paper makes it explicit for agentic step-level evaluation.

## 14. Discretizing Reward Models

**Authors:** Vijay Viswanathan, Shiqi Wang, Devamanyu Hazarika, Chirag Nagpal, Tongshuang Wu, Graham Neubig, Yuning Mao — *CMU / Meta Superintelligence Labs*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.21795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21795) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.21795) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.21795)
**Topics:** `rewards`, `RL`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Many popular reward models are *oversensitive*: they assign different continuous scores to equally good responses, which translates into worse downstream policies. The paper proposes evaluating RMs on *discriminative ability* + *specificity* (anti-oversensitivity), and a training-free Monte-Carlo-dropout algorithm to bin continuous rewards into discrete clusters.

### Key idea

Drop the implicit assumption that "more accurate reward model ⇒ better policy". A reward model that is technically accurate on pairwise preferences can still be oversensitive, splitting equivalent responses into different score bins and biasing RL. Use MC dropout on any neural RM to estimate per-sample reward uncertainty, then quantise the continuous reward into a small number of discrete clusters.

### Main finding

Theoretically: "perfect-accuracy" RMs can be highly oversensitive. Empirically: MC-dropout discretisation reduces oversensitivity and yields better downstream policies than raw continuous rewards. The proposed two-axis (discrimination, specificity) evaluation correlates with downstream policy quality where pairwise accuracy doesn't.

### Why it matters

Reward-model evaluation has been stuck at pairwise accuracy for years. Reframing RM quality as "discriminative *enough* but not over-discriminative" is a genuinely new axis and is method-agnostic — pluggable on top of any existing RM.

## 15. Co-Failure Ceiling

**Authors:** Josef Chen — *KAIKAKU*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.27288](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27288) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.27288) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.27288)
**Topics:** `eval`, `ensembles`, `routing`

### TL;DR

Multi-model ensembles (routing, voting, cascades, fusion, mixture-of-agents) all share a hard accuracy ceiling: `1 − β`, where β is the *co-failure rate* — the fraction of queries on which *every* member model is wrong. Pairwise error correlation can't identify β, and frontier models systematically under-price the all-wrong tail.

### Key idea

For any policy that ultimately outputs one member model's answer, no aggregation can fix queries where every member is wrong. The relevant quantity is therefore the *joint* all-wrong rate β, not pairwise correlation ρ. Measure β directly across 67 frontier models from 21 providers.

### Main finding

Even well-calibrated single models under-price the all-wrong tail by ~2.5× on math tasks; ensembles rarely beat the best single model unless they have strong query-level routing signals. ρ is a poor proxy for β.

### Why it matters

The "just route to the best model" thesis is everywhere — and this paper says it has a hard cap that the field has not been measuring correctly. Reframes ensemble/router research around β estimation and routing-signal quality.
