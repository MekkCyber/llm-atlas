## 1. Formalizing Latent Thoughts

**Authors:** Fahd Seddik, Fatemeh Fard — *University of British Columbia*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.27378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27378) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.27378) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.27378)
**Topics:** `interp`, `eval`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

An axiomatic, benchmark-free framework for evaluating *latent thought representations* in LLMs. The authors define four functional axioms — Causality, Minimality, Separability, Stability — each with a quantitative metric computed directly on hidden states, and audit open-weight dense, reasoning-distilled, and RL-trained LLMs across 23 reasoning tasks.

### Key idea

Decouple representation quality from downstream accuracy. For each axiom, define a probe that operates only on the representation (e.g. Separability: can the representation distinguish two distinct questions in the same task?). This makes representational failures visible even when benchmark accuracy looks fine.

### Main finding

**No audited model satisfies all four axioms.** Representations reliably distinguish *task type* but not *individual questions within a task*, and encode almost no information beyond the input embedding. The deficit is consistent across model size, distillation, and RL training — suggesting a *structural* limit, not a scale or recipe artifact.

### Why it matters

Reframes "latent thoughts" / chain-of-thought-in-activations claims with falsifiable criteria. If R1-style RL distillation does not improve any of the four axioms, the apparent reasoning gains may live mostly in token-space planning rather than richer hidden representations — a sobering signal for interp-driven post-training research.

## 2. PhysisForcing

**Authors:** Yufan Deng, Shangkun Sun, Juncheng Ma, Duomin Wang, Jonas Du, Zilin Pan, Ye Huang, Hao Liang, Songyan Huang, Ruihua Zhang, Enze Xie, Ming-Yu Liu, Daquan Zhou — *Peking University, NVIDIA*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.28128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28128) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.28128) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.28128)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `video`, `multimodal`, `world-model`

### TL;DR

Trains video DiTs to be physically plausible world simulators for robotic manipulation by focusing supervision on contact regions and inter-entity relations. Adds a pixel-level trajectory alignment loss (point-tracker supervision on DiT features) and a semantic relational alignment loss (anchored on a frozen video understanding encoder).

### Key idea

Most physical implausibility comes from two failure modes: object deformation under motion, and bad spatio-temporal correlations during contact. Inject this prior directly into DiT training via two auxiliary losses on *informative* regions, instead of asking the model to learn physics from raw pixels alone.

### Main finding

On R-Bench, the framework lifts Wan2.2-I2V-A14B by **+22.3%** and Cosmos3-Nano by **+9.2%** (vs +7.1% / +3.7% for vanilla fine-tuning). As a world model under the WorldArena action-planner protocol, closed-loop manipulation success rises from **16.0% → 24.0%**, and downstream policy success also improves.

### Why it matters

Embodied "video as world model" is bottlenecked by physical inconsistency. Showing that targeted auxiliary supervision is enough — no architectural surgery — to unlock measurable closed-loop gains is a strong signal that physically-aligned video pre-training will become a standard ingredient for VLA pipelines.

## 3. Translation as Bridging Action

**Authors:** HKU-MMLab and ByteDance Seed (full byline not exposed on HF page) — *HKU-MMLab, ByteDance Seed*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.28133](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28133) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.28133) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.28133)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `VLA`, `data`

### TL;DR

Reframes human → robot skill transfer by stripping rotation from the human action signal and only sharing **relative wrist translation in the initial head-camera frame** — an action space natively shared by humans and parallel-gripper robots. Trains a π₀-like VLA with interleaved action tokens + attention masking to tolerate missing action components per embodiment.

### Key idea

Don't ask the model to learn the noisy human 6-DoF pose and somehow map it to a parallel gripper. Instead, define a minimal *bridging* action — relative translation — that both embodiments can produce cleanly, and let the policy use whichever components an embodiment actually exposes.

### Main finding

Bridging-action transfer beats 6-DoF human-action baselines on novel bi-manual tasks and **scales with the volume of human data**, which 6-DoF transfer does not. Establishes a much more favorable data-cost curve for using uncurated human video.

### Why it matters

The "human video for robots" thesis has been bottlenecked by embodiment mismatch. A principled minimal-action interface — rather than yet another retargeting trick — turns a noisy modality into a cleanly scalable one and is directly compatible with π₀-style VLA training.

## 4. Qwen-Image-2.0-RL Technical Report

**Authors:** Yixian Xu, Kaiyuan Gao, Yuxiang Chen, Yilei Chen, Zecheng Tang, Zihao Liu, Zikai Zhou, Deqing Li, Hao Meng, Kuan Cao, Jiahao Li, Jie Zhang, Liang Peng, Lihan Jiang, Ningyuan Tang, Shengming Yin, Tianhe Wu, Xiaoyue Chen, Yan Shu, Yanran Zhang, Yi Wang, Yu Wu, et al. — *Qwen / Alibaba*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.27608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27608) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.27608) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.27608)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `RL`, `tech-report`, `image-generation`

### TL;DR

Tech report for the RL post-training stack behind Qwen-Image-2.0. Combines composite reward modeling, a GRPO-based RL optimizer adapted to flow-matching diffusion, and an on-policy distillation phase to compress the RL-improved policy into a faster student. Targets both text-to-image generation and image editing.

### Key idea

Treat image-diffusion alignment as an end-to-end post-training pipeline analogous to LLM RLHF: (1) build composite reward models for quality + instruction following + editing fidelity, (2) optimize the flow-matching policy with GRPO instead of PPO, and (3) on-policy distill the improved teacher into a deployable student that keeps the gains under aggressive step counts.

### Main finding

The combined RLHF + on-policy distillation pipeline improves visual quality and instruction-following on text-to-image and editing benchmarks over the supervised Qwen-Image-2.0 baseline, with the distilled student preserving most of the teacher's wins at substantially lower inference cost (exact numbers in the report).

### Why it matters

Diffusion post-training has been a patchwork of DPO/NFT/AWM variants. A frontier image-model team committing to GRPO + on-policy distillation as the recipe — and shipping a tech report with both — turns this into a reference stack for the field, mirroring how DeepSeek-R1 standardized RL for reasoning.

## 5. MultiHashFormer

**Authors:** Huiyin Xue, Atsuki Yamaguchi, Nikolaos Aletras — *(affiliation not disclosed on HF page; lead author historically University of Sheffield)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.28057](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28057) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.28057) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.28057)
**Topics:** `LLM`, `architecture`, `tokenization`

### TL;DR

Replaces the standard vocabulary embedding matrix with a **hash-signature** representation: each token becomes a short sequence of discrete hash IDs from multiple independent hash functions, encoded into a single latent by a Hash Encoder and decoded back token-by-token by a Hash Decoder around an otherwise-standard Transformer.

### Key idea

Prior hash-based LMs collapsed many tokens onto one vector, breaking causal next-token prediction. Multi-hash signatures sidestep collisions: each token has a **unique** signature, so autoregression still works, and parameter count of the embedding+output head is *decoupled* from vocabulary size.

### Main finding

At **100M / 1B / 3B** scales, MultiHashFormer beats standard Transformer LMs on multiple benchmarks. It also absorbs **multilingual vocabulary expansion at constant parameter footprint** — no growth in the embedding matrix when adding new languages.

### Why it matters

Embedding+output projections dominate the parameter budget at the small-to-mid scale and quietly tax multilingual tokenizer expansions. Decoupling them from `|V|` opens a clean path to scaling vocabulary (multilingual, code, scientific) without paying a parameter tax.

## 6. SingGuard

**Authors:** Author byline not exposed on HF page — *Affiliation not disclosed*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.22873](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22873) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.22873) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.22873)
**Topics:** `safety`, `multimodal`, `RL`, `guardrail`

### TL;DR

A multimodal LLM guardrail that treats the **active safety policy as a runtime input**: given natural-language rules, the model checks content rule-by-rule and emits both a safety label and the triggered rule. Supports fast/hybrid/slow inference along a deliberation spectrum, optimized via fast–slow decoupled RL.

### Key idea

Most guardrails bake the policy into the weights with a fixed taxonomy. SingGuard makes the policy a prompt-level argument and trains the model to **deliberate proportionally** to risk — direct judgment for the easy stuff, policy-grounded reasoning for the hard stuff — with a decoupled RL objective that rewards both fast and slow tracks separately.

### Main finding

On **SingGuard-Bench** (56,340 examples, 80+ fine-grained risk types) and **35 datasets across 6 benchmark families**, SingGuard reaches state-of-the-art average F1 in every family.

### Why it matters

Production guardrails need to ship under shifting region-/product-specific policies without retraining for each policy change. Making the policy a runtime input — and letting the model decide when to deliberate — is exactly the right abstraction for moderation in deployment, paralleling adaptive-compute reasoning in agentic LLMs.

## 7. GBC

**Authors:** Xiaocheng Yang, Abdulrahman Alrabah, Dilek Hakkani-Tür, Gokhan Tur — *UIUC*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.28187](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28187) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.28187) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.28187)
**Topics:** `agents`, `LLM`, `optimization`

### TL;DR

Brings **gradient-based credit assignment** to LLM multi-agent systems. Treats the MAS as a computational graph, attaches per-edge gradient-based connection weights at token level, and back-propagates a task loss through the graph to identify which agents/steps caused errors — then uses the attribution for targeted prompt optimization.

### Key idea

Existing MAS optimization uses coarse end-to-end signal. GBC adds **token-level differentiable connection weights** between agent outputs and downstream agents, builds an attribution graph, and runs prefix-based gradient computation (implemented in *AgentChord*) to localize blame and steer prompt updates accordingly.

### Main finding

On **MultiWOZ** and **τ-bench**, GBC beats strong single-agent and multi-agent baselines, and the authors empirically show that **higher attribution quality correlates with greater optimization gains** — evidence the gradient signal is genuinely informative, not just regularization.

### Why it matters

Prompt-graph optimization (e.g. DSPy, TextGrad family) has been searching for a credible per-agent credit signal. Pushing token-level gradients through the MAS provides an actually-differentiable handle, paralleling the move from REINFORCE to PPO inside a single agent.

## 8. ProMSA

**Authors:** Hangrui Xu, Kai Shi, Zhuohong Chen, Yunyao Yu, Chuanrui Zhang, Zirui Liao, Jun Yang, Zhenyu Yang, Haonan Lu, Haoqian Wang — *OPPO AI Center, Tsinghua SIGS, NTU*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.27974](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27974) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.27974) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.27974)
**Topics:** `agents`, `multimodal`, `RL`, `RAG`

### TL;DR

A KB-VQA agent that iteratively picks between image search, text search, or stop — under explicit tool-call budgets with retrieval deduplication. Trained with rejection-sampling SFT for tool-use format, then RL with **TN-GSPO**, a sequence-level objective that normalizes updates by both generation length and tool-interaction depth.

### Key idea

Drop the static retrieve-then-generate pipeline. A multimodal agent dynamically routes between image and text retrieval with a budget, while TN-GSPO addresses the well-known length/depth bias problem in tool-using RL by normalizing the policy gradient over *both* axes.

### Main finding

On **E-VQA** and **InfoSeek**, ProMSA consistently improves over strong RAG and agent baselines on both retrieval quality and end-to-end answer accuracy.

### Why it matters

KB-VQA is a stress test for tool-using multimodal agents because retrieval modality matters. Dual-axis normalization (length + tool depth) is a generally useful trick for any agentic RL where rollouts can blow up in either dimension.

## 9. ConvFill

**Authors:** Vidya Srinivas, Zachary Englhardt, Maximus Powers, Shwetak Patel, Vikram Iyer — *Paul G. Allen School of Computer Science & Engineering, University of Washington*
**Links:** [arXiv:2511.07397](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07397) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2511.07397) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2511.07397)
**Topics:** `agents`, `efficient-inference`, `multimodal`, `voice`

### TL;DR

Splits a conversational voice agent across a small on-device "infiller" model and a large cloud backend. The local 360M model generates contextually appropriate dialogue immediately and **streams in** knowledge from the backend as it arrives — decoupling perceived latency from backend capability.

### Key idea

Frame voice-agent serving as **conversational infill**: the local model commits to the conversational frame and fills in placeholders that the cloud model resolves, rather than waiting for the cloud round-trip. Trained on synthetic multi-domain conversations to learn the infill pattern.

### Main finding

Across multiple backend models, ConvFill (360M params) improves accuracy by **36–42%** over standalone same-size local models while keeping **sub-200 ms response latency**, the threshold for natural-sounding turn-taking.

### Why it matters

Voice agents are constrained by perceptual latency budgets that contradict cloud-LLM round-trips. Reframing the small/large split as infill instead of cascade is a much better fit for streaming conversational settings, and is broadly applicable beyond voice (e.g. real-time captioning agents).

## 10. Vesta

**Authors:** Zhiqi Li, Yunze Man, Jing Wang, An-Chieh Cheng, Sifei Liu, Shihao Wang, Zhiding Yu, Abhishek Badki, Stan Birchfield, Valts Blukis, Yevgen Chebotar, et al. — *NVIDIA (and academic collaborators)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.20905](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20905) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.20905) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.20905)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `agents`, `VLA`, `embodied`

### TL;DR

A single embodied generalist foundation model that consolidates localization, spatial reasoning, navigation, and long-horizon planning into one stack. Pairs a large curated spatial-grounding corpus with a simple multimodal memory harness for reasoning over extended time horizons.

### Key idea

Replace the multi-model "specialist stack" with one model trained on a heterogeneous spatial-grounding mixture, plus a lightweight external **multimodal memory** module so that long-horizon tasks don't require unbounded context windows.

### Main finding

Across diverse embodied benchmarks, Vesta beats individual SOTA specialists by **>20%** on average and beats an ensemble of per-category-best specialists by **>10%**. On real-world robotic tasks needing memory + reasoning it improves task success by **>35%**.

### Why it matters

The specialist-stack approach has compounding errors and serving complexity. Vesta's win against a per-task-best ensemble is strong evidence that a single generalist is now Pareto-better for embodied — paralleling what foundation-model generalization did to per-task NLP pipelines.

## 11. AgentOdyssey

**Authors:** Zehao Wen, Alvin Zhang, Andrew Wang, Jianwen Xie, Daniel Khashabi, Tianmin Shu — *Johns Hopkins University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.24893](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24893) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.24893) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.24893)
**Topics:** `agents`, `eval`, `RL`, `continual-learning`

### TL;DR

A procedurally-generated open-ended text-game benchmark for **test-time continual learning agents**. Long-horizon worlds, rich entities, deliberate inter-leaving of learning and inference, plus diagnostic probes for world-knowledge acquisition, episodic memory, exploration, action diversity, and cost.

### Key idea

Drop the standard ML assumption that learning stops at deployment. AgentOdyssey *requires* agents to keep learning during evaluation, and its multifaceted diagnostics let you say *why* an agent stalls — not just "score plateaued" but "episodic memory didn't update."

### Main finding

Performance scales with base-model strength but **all** evaluated agents — including the strongest — remain far below human. Among mechanisms, **short-term memory** is broadly beneficial across paradigms and emerges as a key component of test-time training.

### Why it matters

The agent-eval landscape is dominated by static-task benchmarks that under-measure continual learning. AgentOdyssey provides falsifiable headroom and a diagnostic toolkit, and it elevates short-term memory from a tool-design choice to a measurable capability axis.

## 12. Ko-WideSearch

**Authors:** Authorship not disclosed on HF page — *Affiliation not disclosed*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.27595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27595) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.27595) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.27595)
**Topics:** `agents`, `eval`, `web-agents`, `multilingual`

### TL;DR

A Korean **breadth-search** benchmark for web agents: instead of pinning one obscure answer behind a chain of constraints, tasks require *exhaustive enumeration of a closed set* plus a per-item attribute table. 228 tables over 190 entities, three difficulty tiers controlled by two structural knobs (table width and a 2-D composite key).

### Key idea

Treat *breadth* as a first-class agent capability: complete-set enumeration grades agents on Item-F1, Column-F1, and Row-F1, and an automated synthesize-and-verify pipeline keeps the gold sets actually complete (otherwise breadth benchmarks rot quickly).

### Main finding

The two structural knobs (width + composite key) dial cross-product membership from **0% → 100%** across tiers, letting the benchmark sweep difficulty continuously. (Frontier-agent results on the benchmark are in the paper.)

### Why it matters

Most agent benchmarks reward deep retrieval at the expense of completeness, biasing model improvements toward needle-in-a-haystack. A breadth benchmark forces the field to address closure, deduplication, and per-item verification — and doing it in Korean broadens the multilingual web-agent eval surface.

## 13. NormGuard

**Authors:** Lianyu Pang, Cheng Da, Huan Yang, Changqian Yu, Kun Gai, Wenhan Luo — *HKUST, Kuaishou Technology, UCAS*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.27771](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27771) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.27771) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.27771)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `RL`, `post-training`, `flow-matching`

### TL;DR

Identifies a clean signature of RL drift in flow-matching post-training: **per-step velocity norm `‖v_θ‖` inflates by 5–15%** relative to the reference across NFT, AWM, and DPO. NormGuard is a training-time hinge penalty that activates only when `‖v_θ‖` exceeds `‖v_ref‖`, additively composing with any velocity-local base loss.

### Key idea

The same norm-inflation seen in CFG re-emerges under RL, but inference-time renormalization no longer works because the inflation is **co-adapted into the weights**. An adjoint sensitivity analysis confirms velocity magnitude rescaling carries no coherent first-order reward signal at the batch level — so the fix has to be at training time, and a hinge penalty is enough.

### Main finding

Across **2 base models × 3 post-training methods × 2 reward proxies**, NormGuard consistently improves MLLM-judged image quality and forensic realism **while preserving reward**, with gains amplifying under few-step inference and not explained by early stopping.

### Why it matters

Diffusion RLHF suffers from reward-hacking artifacts that perceptual metrics catch but reward models don't. A drop-in, additive regularizer with a clean theoretical justification (adjoint sensitivity) is the kind of building block that should ship in every flow-matching RL stack.

## 14. Simplified Sparse Attention

**Authors:** Author byline not exposed on HF page — *Affiliation not disclosed*
**Links:** [arXiv:2604.20920](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20920) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2604.20920) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.20920)
**Topics:** `architecture`, `efficient-inference`, `long-context`, `attention`

### TL;DR

Sparse attention with **no architectural changes**. Continued pre-training on sequences interleaved with "gist" tokens trains the model to pack each chunk's information into the gists; at inference time, queries are scored against the small gist set and only the top-k chunks are *selectively unfolded* back into full attention.

### Key idea

Avoid the bespoke components of prior sparse attention by piggy-backing on the standard attention mask: an attention mask during continued pre-training forces gist tokens to summarize their chunk, and the same mask drives sparse selection at inference. Adds **H-SSA** (gist-of-gists) for log-linear decoding.

### Main finding

On **LongBench**, SSA outperforms compression and inference-time sparse-attention baselines at the same compression ratio. In RAG it actually **beats the continued-pretrained full-attention model by >5.7 points**, attributed to selective unfolding filtering query-irrelevant noise. Decoding gets a **3.37× speedup over Flash-Decoding**, with decoding latency staying ≈flat as context length grows.

### Why it matters

Long-context inference economics are dominated by KV memory bandwidth. SSA gets a sparse-attention win without paying the engineering tax of bespoke modules — and the RAG result is a striking case of *sparse beating dense*, suggesting noise filtering, not just compute savings, drives part of the gain.

## 15. Cluster, Route, Escalate

**Authors:** Yasmin Moslem, Magdalena Kacmajor, Vasudevan Nedumpozhimana, Ammar Abbas, Solmaz Panahi, David Lynch, Zhuangzhuang Nie, Alexandros Agapitos, Aleksandar Milenovic, Hongmeng Song, Yucheng Shi, Yue Pan, Patricia Buffini, John D. Kelleher — *ADAPT Centre, Trinity College Dublin; Huawei Research*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.27457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27457) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.27457) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.27457)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`, `LLM`, `systems`, `routing`

### TL;DR

A two-stage cost-aware LLM serving stack. **Stage 1**: cluster incoming queries and assign each cluster to its most cost-effective model, with the cost budget controlled by a single interpretable hyperparameter tuned offline. **Stage 2**: a quality-estimation cascade re-routes low-quality outputs to a stronger model.

### Key idea

Move beyond per-query model selection by clustering queries first (so the router operates on a much smaller decision space), then add a QE-driven escalation only for the cases that need it. The cost knob is exposed as a single, interpretable hyperparameter rather than as a learned reward.

### Main finding

On the paper's test datasets, the cascaded system retains **97–99% of the strongest model's accuracy** while **reducing TPOT** (time-per-output-token). It uses only task-correctness labels and adapts to changes in the model pool without manual reconfiguration.

### Why it matters

Multi-model serving is now table stakes for cost-conscious deployments, but most routers are opaque learned models that drift as the pool changes. A clustering + QE-cascade decomposition with a single interpretable knob is operationally appealing and easy to monitor.
