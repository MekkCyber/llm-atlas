## 1. Jet-Long

**Authors:** Han Cai — *NVIDIA*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.07740](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07740) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.07740) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.07740)
**Topics:** `LLM`, `long-context`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

A tuning-free zero-shot long-context extension for RoPE-based LLMs that avoids the "one-scaling-factor-fits-all" trap. Jet-Long runs two attention windows in parallel — a local RoPE-faithful window and a long-range window whose RoPE rescaling factor adapts to the current sequence length — then merges them with inclusion–exclusion. The result behaves like the base model on short inputs and extrapolates cleanly on long ones, and it fuses into a single CuTe kernel that outpaces FA2 at long-prefill.

### Key idea

A **dynamic bifocal RoPE**: keep a short local window at the original RoPE frequency, and pair it with a long-range window whose rescaling factor is chosen at runtime as a function of the actual sequence length. An on-the-fly RoPE "correction rotation" reconciles the two views inside attention, and an inclusion–exclusion merge avoids double-counting tokens that fall inside both windows. Because the merge is analytic, the whole construction collapses to a single fused CuTe kernel — effectively free at inference.

### Main finding

Long-context prefill throughput reaches **up to 1.39× FlashAttention-2 on H100** (approaching Hopper-only FA4), and single-batch generation adds ≤ 4% overhead at every length. Short-context accuracy is recovered exactly (dynamic factor collapses to identity at short inputs), and long-context quality is preserved without any fine-tuning.

### Why it matters

Frontier deployments extend context zero-shot because retraining is expensive; existing zero-shot methods (PI/NTK/YaRN/ABF) pick a rescaling factor at load time and pay short-context fidelity for long-context reach. Jet-Long removes that dial — one artifact serves both regimes — and does it at speeds that beat FA2 rather than costing throughput. That reshapes the "which RoPE-extension do I ship?" decision for anyone running long-context LLMs on H100.

## 2. Linear Attention Architectures

**Authors:** Tommaso Cerruti, Tim Rieder, George Rowlands, Lingfeng Jin, Imanol Schlag — *ETH Zurich / ETH AI Center*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.07953](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07953) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.07953) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.07953)
**Topics:** `architectures`, `linear-attention`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

A head-to-head study of softmax attention against four recent recurrent linear-attention variants — DeltaNet, Gated DeltaNet, Kimi Delta Attention, and Gated DeltaNet-2 — under matched training budgets. The paper unifies them under a single "delta rule" recurrence, compares long-context behavior and in-context recall, and proposes a **cross-layer routing** scheme that mixes softmax and linear layers in the same stack.

### Key idea

Reframe modern linear-attention variants as parameterizations of a **generalized delta rule** with per-layer gates. From that view, differences between DeltaNet, Gated DeltaNet, Kimi Delta Attention, and Gated DeltaNet-2 collapse to (i) how the recurrent state is updated, (ii) whether the update is gated, and (iii) whether values are pre- or post-gated. A cross-layer router then decides which layers stay softmax versus linear, trading recall for throughput.

### Main finding

Under matched compute the hybrid stack roughly matches full-softmax perplexity while inheriting linear attention's O(n) inference cost. Purely-linear stacks trail on recall-heavy tasks; hybrids close most of the gap with only a small fraction of softmax layers. The routing decision matters more than the specific linear variant.

### Why it matters

The linear-attention ecosystem has exploded (DeltaNet, GDN, KDA, GDN-2, RWKV-7, Mamba variants) with unclear practical tradeoffs. This paper provides the first apples-to-apples comparison and a concrete recipe — "keep a handful of softmax layers, route the rest through gated delta-rule layers" — that captures most of the benefit. Aligns with what Kimi-K1.5 and DeepSeek-V3 already do implicitly.

## 3. UP

**Authors:** Chongyu Fan, Pengfei Liu, Jingjia Huang, Sijia Liu, Yi Lin — *ByteDance Seed*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.06987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06987) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.06987) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.06987)
**Topics:** `RL`, `post-training`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

An asymmetric fix to PPO/GRPO-style clipping for LLM RL: keep the pessimistic clip on **negative-advantage** updates (stability) but remove it on **positive-advantage** updates (exploration). The resulting objective — Unbounded Positive Asymmetric Optimization — is a drop-in replacement for the clipped surrogate and applies uniformly across RL algorithms and model architectures.

### Key idea

Standard PPO/GRPO clip the importance ratio symmetrically to bound off-policy drift, but the same clip also suppresses large positive updates on newly-discovered high-reward trajectories — killing exploration exactly when the model finds a novel solution. UP breaks the symmetry: **unbounded** positive updates (let the model chase the reward hard) with **clipped** negative updates (don't let bad rollouts blow up the policy). Applies as a one-line change to the loss.

### Main finding

Consistent gains across RL algorithm families (PPO, GRPO) and multiple model scales — better exploration on hard reasoning problems without the instability that fully-unclipped objectives usually cause. Positioned as a plug-and-play "universal objective."

### Why it matters

The exploration-vs-stability tradeoff is the central open problem in LLM RL post-training. GRPO and its descendants (DAPO, VAPO, etc.) all argue about *where* to clip. UP argues about *which side* — a structurally different move that composes with whatever ratio-shaping tricks a lab already uses. If the result holds, it becomes a default rather than a paper.

## 4. Proactive Memory Agent

**Authors:** Lizhu Zhang, Yuhang Zhou, Mingyi Wang, Bo Peng, Serena Li, Xiangjun Fan, Zhuokai Zhao — *Meta AI*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.08716](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08716) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.08716) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.08716)
**Topics:** `agents`, `memory`, `RL`

### TL;DR

Names a real failure mode — **behavioral state decay**, where decision-relevant facts get pushed past the context window in long-horizon agent runs — and fixes it with a *separate memory agent* running alongside an unmodified action agent. The memory agent maintains a structured bank from the trajectory and decides when to inject a short reminder into the action agent's context, plug-and-play with any frontier agent harness.

### Key idea

Split memory from action. A second LLM watches the action agent's trajectory, updates a structured memory bank, and — critically — **decides whether to intervene** on each step (inject a memory-grounded reminder or stay silent). This "selective intervention" policy is trained via SFT + GRPO on top of Qwen3.5-27B (SETA dataset) and is what distinguishes the paper from prior "just keep a scratchpad" memory schemes.

### Main finding

**+8.3 pp pass@1 on Terminal-Bench 2.0** and **+6.8 pp on τ²-Bench** across both weak and strong action agents. Ablations show selective intervention beats passive bank exposure, always-on injection, advisor-only guidance, and general retrieval. Trained memory policy transfers partially to unseen benchmarks.

### Why it matters

Every frontier agent harness (Claude Code, Codex, Terminal-Bench baselines) hits behavioral state decay at some horizon. The prevailing fix is either bigger context or hand-crafted "summarize now" heuristics. This paper argues the fix is a learned memory *policy* — a second agent trained to decide when to speak. That reframes memory as an intervention rather than passive retrieval, and slots directly into existing agent stacks.

## 5. Flash-BoN

**Authors:** Reza Shirkavand, Sayak Paul, Yuxin Wen, Heng Huang, Yizheng Chen, Tom Goldstein, Gowthami Somepalli — *University of Maryland / Hugging Face*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.04461](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04461) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.04461) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.04461)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `inference-time-scaling`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

Under wall-clock budgets, plain Best-of-N with **cheap draft candidates** beats guided-search methods that invest compute in intermediate verification. Flash-BoN generates many low-cost drafts via **timestep truncation, layer skipping, and activation proxies**, verifies them in stages, and refines the best draft at full quality. Also accelerates diffusion RL post-training by ~10×.

### Key idea

Reframe inference-time scaling for diffusion around the true bottleneck: wall-clock. Cheap drafts + late-stage verification outperform expensive per-step guided search once compute is measured, not steps. The trick is a three-way draft-generation recipe (truncate timesteps, skip layers, use activation proxies as pseudo-rewards) that produces many candidates for the cost of one full sample.

### Main finding

Consistent quality gains across benchmarks and model scales; larger models benefit more (**+8% AUC** on the biggest scale). Composes with orthogonal techniques like reflection-based prompt optimization, and drops the wall-clock of RL post-training convergence by roughly **10×**.

### Why it matters

Inference-time scaling was the story of 2025 in LLMs (o1/R1) and is now moving to diffusion. Most diffusion "scaling" proposals add per-step verification cost that eats the compute they were supposed to save. Flash-BoN's message is the same one BoN made in LLM-land: when your unit of compute is a full sample, cheap drafts + a good picker win. Reframes what "test-time compute" means for diffusion pipelines.

## 6. OpenCoF

**Authors:** ByteDance Seed / CUHK MMLab / CUHK IMIXR (equal-contribution team) — *ByteDance / CUHK*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.08763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08763) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.08763) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.08763)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `reasoning`, `diffusion`

### TL;DR

Introduces **Chain-of-Frame (CoF) reasoning** — using a video generator's temporally-connected frames as a reasoning trace — and provides both a dataset (OpenCoF-17K, 11 task families) and a fine-tuned model (Wan-CoF, on top of Wan2.2-I2V-A14B) that exploit it. Also equips the generator with explicit **visual and textual reasoning tokens** that capture low-level cues and high-level priors separately across denoising steps.

### Key idea

Treat frame trajectories as the video analogue of Chain-of-Thought. Because vanilla video generators are trained on general corpora, they lack the temporal supervision needed for CoF; the paper fixes that with a curated multi-task reasoning dataset and adds structural affordances (dedicated reasoning tokens) that give the model a place to store intermediate state across space and time during denoising.

### Main finding

Wan-CoF shows considerable gains over the Wan2.2-I2V-A14B baseline across four video reasoning benchmarks. Attention analysis confirms visual and textual reasoning tokens play distinct roles across depth/denoising step/space/time. Dataset, model, and code are open-sourced.

### Why it matters

Reasoning-through-generation has become a research direction of its own (Sora-style world modeling, image CoT). OpenCoF is one of the first to codify it for video: a dataset + evaluation + open model that gives the community a shared baseline. Positions "temporal supervision" as the missing ingredient rather than model scale.

## 7. Arabic Dialect Steering

**Authors:** Kareem Elozeiri, Mervat Abassy, Omar Kallas, Fahim Dalvi, Preslav Nakov, Kentaro Inui, Nadir Durrani — *MBZUAI / QCRI / Tohoku U. / RIKEN*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.03936](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03936) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.03936) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.03936)
**Topics:** `interp`, `steering`, `LLM`

### TL;DR

Uses interpretability as a control lever for a real deployment problem — Arabic LLMs overproducing Modern Standard Arabic instead of dialects. Two complementary inference-time approaches: (1) identifying **sparse dialect-specific neurons** and amplifying/suppressing them, and (2) extracting **dialect activation directions** and injecting them at inference. Both work without fine-tuning.

### Key idea

Dialect information isn't cleanly localized — some of it lives in a small population of dedicated neurons, but much of it is entangled across many. So use **both** interventions: neuron amplification/suppression for the localized parts, and directional activation steering for the entangled parts. Neither requires touching weights or preparing dialect-specific SFT data.

### Main finding

Both methods successfully steer generation toward target Arabic dialects at inference; combining them exposes the geometric structure of dialectal knowledge in the model. Provides a training-free path to dialect control that generalizes beyond Arabic.

### Why it matters

Two things worth noting. First, activation steering is finally being aimed at *deployment* problems, not just alignment demonstrations. Second, the neuron + direction combination formalizes the "localized vs. distributed" question mech-interp has argued about for years — and shows the answer is often "both." Practical result for LLM producers serving multi-dialect regions, and a template for other under-represented linguistic variation.

## 8. UniClawBench

**Authors:** Zhekai Chen, Chengqi Duan, Kaiyue Sun, Bohao Li, Yuqing Wang, Manyuan Zhang, Xihui Liu — *HKU MMLab / Meituan*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.08768](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08768) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.08768) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.08768)
**Topics:** `agents`, `eval`, `multimodal`

### TL;DR

A **capability-driven** benchmark for proactive agents on real-world tasks (400 bilingual tasks) that runs inside live Docker containers with step-by-step checkpoints. Explicitly separates five foundational capabilities — Skill Usage, Exploration, Long-Context Reasoning, Multimodal Understanding, Cross-Platform Coordination — so failures can be attributed rather than just aggregated. A closed-loop harness (executor + hidden supervisor + user agent) simulates multi-turn feedback without leaking grading criteria.

### Key idea

Existing agent benchmarks conflate capabilities inside scenario-based tasks (one "book a flight" task simultaneously tests planning, tool use, and multimodal parsing) and rely on static single-turn traces. UniClawBench decomposes tasks by *underlying capability* and grades via **live containers with fine-grained checkpoints**, then wraps it in a three-role closed-loop harness so the multi-turn signal is realistic without contaminating evaluation.

### Main finding

Comparing SOTA models across multiple agent frameworks reveals that **base-model capabilities and framework design jointly shape real-world performance** — neither dominates. Concrete numbers for individual models are held for the paper; the eval methodology is the contribution.

### Why it matters

Agent evaluation is broken in a specific way: benchmarks pass without predicting production behavior because they test in sandboxes or with static answers. UniClawBench's live-container, checkpointed, capability-decomposed design is a plausible template for the next generation of agent evals — and the code is open.

## 9. CausalDS

**Authors:** Andrej Leban, Yuekai Sun — *University of Michigan (Statistics)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.08093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08093) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.08093) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.08093)
**Topics:** `agents`, `eval`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

Benchmarks LLMs acting as **data-science agents** at all three rungs of **Pearl's causal hierarchy** (association / intervention / counterfactual). Generates synthetic scenes containing structural causal models, observational data, and domain-grounded narratives, with deterministic scoring, optional observation noise, and **non-identifiable cases treated as first-class outcomes** (agents must decline, not fabricate).

### Key idea

Existing benchmarks either test symbolic causal reasoning without data or test data analysis without principled causal structure. CausalDS combines both: a synthetic-SCM generator produces linked (model, data, narrative) triples, and the eval scores across five interlocking axes — symbolic causal reasoning, data science, uncertainty quantification, abstention, and tool use — jointly.

### Main finding

Frontier and open-weight models "dissociate primarily on **epistemic axes** rather than symbolic reasoning" — i.e. the gap is in knowing when to abstain and how to quantify uncertainty, not in the reasoning itself. Ties into a broader story that LLM agents fabricate answers under ambiguity.

### Why it matters

Puts a concrete number on the "should the agent decline?" question, which is currently vibes-based. Also links to reward-model design for RL post-training on data-science agents: if the epistemic axis is where models fail, training rewards need to price abstention correctly.

## 10. Video-Oasis

**Authors:** Sungjune Park, Jaeyun Lee, Inwoong Lee, Taeoh Kim, Dongyoon Wee, Minho Shim, Yukyung Choi — *Sejong University / NAVER Cloud*
**Links:** [arXiv:2603.29616](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29616) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2603.29616) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2603.29616)
**Topics:** `eval`, `multimodal`, `video`

### TL;DR

Diagnostic suite that audits existing Video-LLM benchmarks and finds **55% of samples are solvable without visual input or temporal context** — i.e. they measure text/knowledge priors, not video understanding. After filtering, state-of-the-art Video-LLMs perform "only marginally above random guessing" on the remaining video-native challenges.

### Key idea

Instead of proposing yet another benchmark, treat existing benchmarks as data and audit each sample for whether visual or temporal information is required to answer. The **distilled challenges** (video-native subset) become a testbed for studying which algorithmic choices actually contribute to video understanding.

### Main finding

**55% shortcut rate** across audited Video-LLM benchmarks. On the video-native remainder, SOTA models sit near chance. Uses the filtered testbed to run ablations on Video-LLM design choices.

### Why it matters

Video-LLM leaderboard progress has been running ahead of actual video-understanding capability. Video-Oasis explains why (benchmark contamination by non-visual shortcuts) and provides a reusable filter. Puts a large asterisk on prior Video-LLM claims and gives labs a diagnostic to run before publishing.

## 11. Ideas Have Genomes

**Authors:** Yifan Zhou, Qihao Yang, Yan Li, Donggang Li, Xiru Hu, Hokin Deng, Ziyang Gong, Xuanyi Zhou, Huacan Wang, Xiangchao Yan, Wanghan Xu, Wenlong Zhang, Shaofeng Zhang, Yue Zhou, Yifan Yang, Zhihang Zhong, Xue Yang — *Shanghai Jiao Tong University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.08758](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08758) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.08758) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.08758)
**Topics:** `eval`, `reasoning`, `LLM`

### TL;DR

A benchmark for **scientific lineage reasoning** — can an LLM trace how ideas descend from prior work — and for **lineage-grounded idea generation** — can it propose new ideas that plausibly extend a known lineage. Papers are represented as "Idea Genome" objects, a genetics-inspired schema that lets the benchmark score both reasoning and generation deterministically.

### Key idea

Encode a scientific work as an "Idea Genome": a structured object with parent works, borrowed mechanisms, and novel contributions. Given this graph, an LLM can be graded on (a) recovering lineage from citation-free evidence, and (b) generating new "child" ideas that plausibly extend a parent set. The structural representation gives the eval determinism.

### Main finding

Provides paired reasoning and generation splits over the genome objects; individual model numbers are the paper's own contribution. The framing itself is the durable output: scientific novelty framed as a scored lineage problem rather than a subjective judgement.

### Why it matters

## 12. aria Runtime

**Authors:** Anonymous (PNRR-funded, likely University of Padova/UniPD ecosystem based on the aria GitHub org) — *funded by EU NextGenerationEU / PNRR*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.08526](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08526) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.08526) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.08526)
**Topics:** `quantization`, `inference`, `steering`

### TL;DR

A dependency-free native runtime that runs the full **Stable Audio 3** text-to-music pipeline on ordinary GPUs, CPU-only machines, and even a **Raspberry Pi 5** — no Python or deep-learning framework under it. Systematic quantization study: **INT8 has no measurable quality loss** on prompt adherence, quality, or "taste preservation," while **INT4 shrinks the 1.2B model enough to run on an 8 GB Pi** at a small bounded cost. Also exposes activation steering as a control knob.

### Key idea

Own every tensor. Because aria is a native, framework-free runtime, it can (a) quantize in place rather than on top of a graph, saving memory instead of adding to it, and (b) expose activation steering cheaply — an interface the ML frameworks don't hand you. The paper measures quality against seed-to-seed variance (a much fairer bar than eyeballing) across three independent metrics.

### Main finding

- **INT8:** no measurable quality loss on any metric; fastest mode on GPU.
- **INT4:** small bounded cost; fits SA3-1.2B on an 8 GB Raspberry Pi 5.
- **Startup:** ~7× faster than the official implementation.
- **Activation steering** provides genuine, bounded control ("sonic seasoning") for a subset of audio attributes.

### Why it matters

The audio-generation stack has been Python + PyTorch + CUDA. This is the first paper to demonstrate a **quantized, dependency-free runtime with in-place activation steering** for a frontier open audio model. Establishes that "on-device semantic audio for IoT" is not vaporware. Also a template for how to compare quantization quality honestly — against seed variance rather than a golden sample.

## 13. CineMobile

**Authors:** Zelai Deng, Xu Wang, Xizhong Xiao, Zhijie Deng — *Nankai U. / SJTU / Transsion*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.03803](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03803) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.03803) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.03803)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `distillation`, `quantization`

### TL;DR

Ships a **1 GB image-to-video diffusion model** for cinematic camera effects (bullet time, dolly zoom, slow motion) on mobile phones. Three-stage pipeline: distillation-guided **pruning** for a compact base, **4-step generation** via diffusion distillation + RL, and **hybrid post-training quantization** to shrink footprint. Achieves a **40× speedup** over the Wan 2.1 teacher.

### Key idea

Chain three compression techniques rather than pick one. Prune to a compact backbone (distillation guides which channels to keep), distill the multi-step DiT down to 4 steps and refine with RL to preserve motion fidelity, then apply hybrid PTQ for on-device deployment. Each stage compounds the others' savings.

### Main finding

49-frame 480p video in **0.6s per step on H200**, **20s per step on a MediaTek Dimensity 8400 5G**, peak memory **1.8 GB**, model file **<1 GB**. **40× speedup** vs. Wan 2.1 with comparable visual quality on cinematic-motion prompts.

### Why it matters

Practical proof that image-to-video diffusion is deployable on 2026 flagship phones. The compression stack (prune → distill+RL → PTQ) is exportable to other video-DiT models, and the RL step for step-count reduction — often skipped in distillation papers — is what preserves the motion quality here.

## 15. Canvas360

**Authors:** Haoran Feng, Ruiyang Zhang, Longyi Zhang, Dizhe Zhang, Lu Qi — *Insta360 Research / Tsinghua / Beihang / Wuhan U.*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.08765](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08765) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.08765) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.08765)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `multimodal`, `pretraining`

### TL;DR

Two-stage in-context panoramic image generation: **geometry-aware pretraining** plus task-specific fine-tuning. Pretraining uses **parallel depth generation**, **velocity circular padding** (respects the panoramic wrap-around), and **similarity-loss regularization** to make the model aware of panoramic geometry. A **1M-sample Canvas360Dataset** underwrites the recipe. Downstream tasks (style transfer, inpainting, outpainting, editing) all share the same backbone.

### Key idea

Panoramas break every geometric assumption image generators have (no wrap-around, no equirectangular distortion, no depth prior). Bake all three into pretraining rather than fixing them at inference: parallel-depth for 3D awareness, velocity circular padding for the seam, similarity loss for consistency. Then use in-context concatenation of task tokens to unify downstream applications.

### Main finding

Improves the panorama-specific FAED metric and lands competitive-or-better numbers across the reported evaluations, with a single backbone spanning multiple in-context tasks.

### Why it matters

Panoramic content is a growing surface (VR, 360° video, Insta360 hardware) that's been underserved by mainstream diffusion. Canvas360 is one of the first to treat panoramic geometry as a *pretraining* problem rather than a post-hoc fix. The velocity circular padding trick in particular is transferable to any generator dealing with wrap-around domains.
