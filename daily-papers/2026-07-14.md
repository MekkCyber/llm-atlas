## 1. Weak-to-Strong Generalization via Direct On-Policy Distillation {#1-direct-opd}

**Authors:** Shiyuan Feng, Huan-ang Gao, Haohan Chi, Hanlin Wu, Zhilong Zhang, Zheng Jiang, Bingxiang He, Wei-Ying Ma, Ya-Qin Zhang, Hao Zhou — *SIA-Lab, Tsinghua AIR + ByteDance Seed*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.05394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05394) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.05394) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.05394)
**Topics:** `RL`, `distillation`, `RLVR`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

Reasoning RL is expensive because rollouts scale with model size. Direct-OPD instead **runs RL on a small model, then transfers the RL-induced policy shift** — the log-ratio between the post-RL and pre-RL small checkpoints — to a stronger student via on-policy distillation. The student never sees the sparse verifier reward directly; it learns from the "delta" the small run discovered.

### Key idea

Given a small teacher pair $(\pi_\text{ref}^s, \pi_\text{RL}^s)$, treat $\log(\pi_\text{RL}^s / \pi_\text{ref}^s)$ as a **dense implicit reward** signal on the student's *own* on-policy rollouts. This decouples exploration cost (paid once, on the small model) from student updates. Multiple such deltas can be composed sequentially.

### Main finding

On AIME 2024, Direct-OPD lifts **Qwen3-1.7B from 48.3% → 58.3%** using an even smaller teacher pair, in **~4h on 8×A100**. It also boosts a 7B student from 56.7 → 63.1 using a 1.5B teacher pair — even though the student already outperforms the post-RL 1.5B (51.3). Vanilla distillation of the post-RL teacher *hurts* the student; distilling the shift helps.

### Why it matters

Reframes an RL run's output as more than a checkpoint: the *policy delta* is a reusable, transferrable object. Cuts the cost of scaling RLVR to frontier-size models and enables cross-scale RL supervision without re-running rollouts.

## 2. Proxy Exploration and Reusable Guidance: PUST {#2-pust}

**Authors:** Daocheng Fu, Rong Wu, Yu Yang, Xuemeng Yang, Jianbiao Mei, Licheng Wen, Pinlong Cai, Yong Liu, Botian Shi, Yu Qiao — *KnowledgeXLab, Shanghai AI Lab*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.11505](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11505) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.11505) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.11505)
**Topics:** `post-training`, `RL`, `distillation`

### TL;DR

Proxy-guided Update Signal Transfer (PUST) also decouples RL exploration from the primary model. A lightweight proxy is optimized on some domain, the pre/post *relative improvement* is extracted, and that directional signal is transferred to a larger primary. Signals become cacheable and reusable across model families.

### Key idea

Three stages: (1) **proxy exploration** — run cheap post-training on a small proxy; (2) **signal extraction** — compute the relative-improvement direction between the proxy's initial and optimized states; (3) **signal transfer** — apply that directional update to the primary model's policy alignment step. Because signals are *relative*, not absolute distributions, weak proxies can guide stronger primaries.

### Main finding

On Qwen3-family primaries (math + code), update signals extracted from substantially weaker proxies robustly improve stronger primary models. The pipeline is asynchronous — proxy exploration happens once, its signal is cached, and multiple primaries reuse it.

### Why it matters

Alongside Direct-OPD (same day, same idea in a different flavor), PUST marks a shift: **treat post-training as producing reusable optimization signals**, not just final checkpoints. Turns monolithic online RL into a modular, cost-efficient factory.

## 3. Multi-Agent LLMs Fail to Explore Each Other (MACE) {#3-mace}

**Authors:** Hyeong Kyu Choi, Jiatong Li, Wendi Li, Xin Eric Wang, Sharon Li — *University of Wisconsin–Madison*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.11250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11250) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.11250) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.11250)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Formalizes multi-agent exploration as a partially observable stochastic game (POSG): agents must probe peers to infer their capabilities, then pick effective interaction strategies. Current LLM agents fail at this — they exhibit myopic, polarized selection patterns. Multi-Agent Contextual Exploration (MACE) is a lightweight framework that explicitly structures peer-selection to encourage exploration.

### Key idea

Peer-selection is treated as a contextual bandit with structured exploration incentives rather than left implicit in the LLM's policy. Theory: **value of exploration grows with agent diversity** — the more heterogeneous the agent pool, the higher the payoff of probing.

### Main finding

Across contextual and parametric diversity settings, MACE improves both exploration behavior and downstream task performance over multiple LLM-agent baselines. Ablations show the failure mode is not just poor policies but poor *epistemic* behavior — agents pick partners based on surface signals rather than uncertainty reduction.

### Why it matters

A concrete diagnosis of *why* multi-agent LLM systems underperform: they don't explore. Names the failure and gives a plug-in fix. Relevant to any agent-society / debate / multi-agent RL pipeline.

## 4. NeuroCogMap: Cognitive Organization of LLMs {#4-neurocogmap}

**Authors:** Zhongxiang Sun, Haolang Lu, Qiang Ma, Qi Li, Qipeng Wang, Liang Pang, Chenyu Liu, Qiankun Li, Hao Sun, Kun Wang, Yi Zeng, Jun Xu, Guoqi Li, Ji-Rong Wen — *RUC / CAS / partners*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.00397](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00397) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.00397) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.00397)
**Topics:** `interp`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Borrows cognitive-neuroscience parcellation methods to organize LLM internal features into **functional parcels** linked to interpretable cognitive functions (memory, control, decision, refusal, etc.). The parcels are partly conserved across model families and predict output behavior.

### Key idea

Cluster internal representations into "parcels" using a neuroscience-inspired functional grouping, then map each parcel to (a) an interpretable function, (b) a cognitive capability, and (c) a position in a cognitive hierarchy. Failures like hallucination, bias, refusal failure, and sycophancy each correspond to distinct disruptions in specific parcel systems.

### Main finding

The parcellation reproduces across model families with stable, semantically coherent structure. Parcel-level signatures **predict human cortical responses during naturalistic language comprehension**, with the strongest match in higher-order association cortex — suggesting shared functional organization. Targeted interventions on parcel disruptions yield mechanism-guided detection of specific failure modes.

### Why it matters

A framework for mapping model-wide functional organization, not just single circuits. Bridges mech-interp and cog-neuroscience: gives a common vocabulary for "what's going on inside" that's grounded in an existing scientific tradition.

## 5. AdvancedMathBench: Advanced Proof Generation & Verification {#5-advancedmathbench}

**Authors:** Lingkai Kong, Zijian Wu, Yuzhe Gu, Haiteng Zhao, Wenyong Huang, Shuang Sun, Zhicheng Xiong, Xiaotian Zhang, Shuya Zhao, Yan Wang, Disheng Xu, Wenwei Zhang, Kai Chen — *Shanghai AI Lab (Intern Large Models)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.11849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11849) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.11849) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.11849)
**Topics:** `eval`, `reasoning`, `math`

### TL;DR

A benchmark suite for **advanced** (undergrad + doctoral qualifying-exam) mathematical proof capabilities in LLMs. Two components: **ProverBench** (296 proof-generation problems) and **VerifierBench** (888 model-generated proofs paired with expert ground truth for judging proof validity). Comes with an automatic proof-verification pipeline trained on expert annotations.

### Key idea

Existing math benchmarks (AIME, MATH500) measure final-answer correctness on high-school and olympiad problems. AdvancedMathBench extends both **scope** (advanced math) and **evaluation granularity** (proof-level validity + error typing) via a trained verifier that produces fine-grained per-step assessments.

### Main finding

Frontier models struggle: best model (GPT-5.5-xhigh) reaches only **75.8 / 66.1** on UGD / QE proof-generation splits. On verification, best Balanced-F1 is **65.1**, with low true-negative rates — models can generate plausible proofs but *can't reliably detect wrong ones*.

### Why it matters

Fills a hole in the reasoning-eval landscape at the top end of math. The proof-verification split is especially important — it's a direct test of the "LLM-as-judge for reasoning" capability that RLVR and self-critique pipelines increasingly depend on.

## 6. MET: Theory-Grounded, Culture-Aware Multilingual Moral Reasoning {#6-met}

**Authors:** Ayoung Lee, Ryan Kwon, Yunxiang Zhang, Yuxuan Liu, Peter Railton, Lu Wang — *(University of Michigan-adjacent)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.11736](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11736) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.11736) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.11736)
**Topics:** `safety`, `alignment`, `multilingual`

### TL;DR

Three contributions in the moral-reasoning stack: **MCLASH** (a multilingual, culturally-adapted moral benchmark, not machine-translated), **MET** (a two-step prompting method that first selects theory-grounded moral "grounds" curated by philosophers/psychologists, then reasons over them in the user's native language), and **MET-D** (self-distillation of MET at training time, no external teacher).

### Key idea

Existing multilingual moral evals translate English items and lose cultural specificity; existing moral-reasoning scaffolds are English-centric. MET uses **expert-curated, theory-grounded moral premises** as an intermediate step, so the model reasons over principled objects rather than free-form CoT. MET-D bakes MET into weights via self-distillation without external supervision.

### Main finding

MET-D improves macro-F1 across Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B, Gemma3-4B by an average of **+3.71** on MCLASH and **+4.23** on MMoralExceptQA, peaking at **+12.94** on Malay for Qwen3-8B. Native-language reasoning increases by **+62.13** on average — models actually start reasoning in the user's language, not English.

### Why it matters

Two lessons: (1) theory-grounded scaffolds beat generic CoT for value-loaded tasks; (2) self-distillation is enough to internalize prompt scaffolds without a stronger teacher — relevant to any "distill your prompt engineering into weights" pipeline.

**Topics:** `survey`, `reasoning`, `eval`

### Why it matters

Anchors a lot of scattered work (LLM-as-judge, self-consistency, uncertainty estimation, self-critique) under one umbrella. Useful for the KG because it maps a class of techniques that currently live in separate corners.

## 8. LightMem-Ego: Lightweight Streaming Multimodal Memory {#8-lightmem-ego}

**Authors:** Yijun Chen, Boyi Xiao, Yixian Zhao, Haoting Xia, Buqiang Xu, Jizhan Fang, Yanya Li, Yaqi Zheng, Xuehai Wang, Zirui Xue, Liuxin Zhang, Hui Li, Ningyu Zhang — *ZJUNLP*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.11487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11487) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.11487) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.11487)
**Topics:** `agents`, `multimodal`, `memory`

### TL;DR

An on-device streaming multimodal memory for wearable/mobile personal assistants. Continuously captures egocentric video + audio, aligns them on a shared timeline, and organizes them into a **hierarchical current/short-term/long-term memory**. Retrieval routes queries to the right level and generates answers grounded in multimodal evidence.

### Key idea

Hierarchical memory (rolling current → summarized short-term → indexed long-term) with modality-aligned timestamps, small enough to run on a phone or AR glasses. Dynamic retrieval decides *which memory level* to consult based on the query, avoiding expensive full retrieval every turn.

### Main finding

System-paper contribution — demonstrations on smartphones and AI glasses support object finding, conversation recall, life summarization, routine discovery, and personalized assistance. No headline benchmark, but a working reference implementation.

### Why it matters

Concrete instantiation of the "personal-assistant memory layer" that many agent stacks (see ABot-AgentOS below) treat as a slot. Design pattern is broadly applicable to any agent needing bounded, lifelong context.

## 9. ABot-AgentOS: General Robotic Agent OS with Lifelong Memory {#9-abot-agentos}

**Authors:** Jiayi Tian, Shiao Liu, Yuting Xu, Jia Lu, Zihao Guan, Honglin Han, and 20+ others — *Alibaba AMAP CV Lab*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.10350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10350) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.10350) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.10350)
**Topics:** `agents`, `multimodal`, `memory`

*Borderline: robotics-flavored, but the "Agent OS" abstraction is a general LLM-agent architecture and the memory + self-evolution loop apply beyond robots.*

### TL;DR

A general "agent OS" that sits **above** low-level VLA controllers: adds deliberative reasoning, tool use, verification, edge-cloud collaboration, and **Universal Multi-modal Graph Memory** — a typed-node/typed-edge substrate for dialogue, observations, spatial and temporal context, and task traces. Also introduces EmbodiedWorldBench (16 scenes, 200+ tasks) to evaluate long-horizon embodied execution.

### Key idea

Two novel pieces: (1) the graph memory is *source-grounded* — every node/edge links back to its evidence, enabling audit; (2) a **failure-driven self-evolution loop** converts diagnosed memory failures into gated evo-assets that are promoted only to later evaluation splits, preventing ground-truth leakage while still enabling continual improvement.

### Main finding

On memory benchmarks: **LoCoMo 87.5→88.7, OpenEQA-EM 59.9→60.4, Mem-Gallery 88.6→89.0** with self-evolution enabled. On EmbodiedWorldBench improves over single-controller baselines on both task success and goal completion.

### Why it matters

The "OS layer above the policy" pattern is the natural next abstraction for agent systems — worth documenting as its own architectural stance, distinct from monolithic VLA or LLM-agent-with-tools designs.

## 10. ABot-N1: General Visual Language Navigation Foundation Model {#10-abot-n1}

**Authors:** Ruiyan Gong, Yingnan Guo, Junjun Hu, Jintao Kong, Xiaoxu Leng, Tianlun Li, Weize Li, Fei Liu, Zhicheng Liu, Jia Lu, Minghua Luo, Chenlin Ming, Yanfen Shen, Jiyue Tao, Zhengbo Wang, Mingyang Yin, et al. — *Alibaba AMAP CV Lab*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.10383](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10383) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.10383) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.10383)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `VLA`, `reasoning`

*Borderline: navigation-specific, but the slow-fast architecture with an explicit pixel-goal interface is a general VLM-plus-action-expert pattern applicable to other embodied tasks.*

### TL;DR

A visual-language navigation foundation model that **decouples cognition from control** via a slow-fast architecture: a slow VLM reasoner does explicit CoT and emits a **pixel-goal** (image-space anchor point); a fast action expert consumes the pixel goal + textual cues and outputs waypoints at control rate. Same interface serves point-goal, object-goal, POI-goal, instruction-following, and person-following.

### Key idea

Use **pixel coordinates as a universal task interface** between a heavy reasoner and a lightweight controller. That interface is simultaneously interpretable (visible anchor in the image) and grounded (avoids the coordinate-drift failures of monolithic policies).

### Main finding

New SOTA on urban navigation: **POI arrival +35.0% (to 77.3%)**; **95.4% / 92.9% SR** on complex indoor / outdoor scenes. Robust across five navigation task types. Ships new Point-Goal / POI-Goal benchmarks.

### Why it matters

Pixel-goal-as-interface is a clean pattern for splitting slow reasoning from fast control — likely to generalize to manipulation and other VLA settings.

## 12. Latent-Identity Tuning in Text-to-Image Personalization {#12-latent-identity-tuning}

**Topics:** `diffusion`, `personalization`

### TL;DR

For T2I personalization models, edits an identity by moving in the **latent space of a frozen encoder** rather than editing pixels. Discovers semantically meaningful directions (age, expression, hair, spatial regions) within subsets of the encoder's identity tokens, with no additional training. Produces diverse images of the *same edited identity* rather than a single edited image.

### Key idea

Identity in a personalization encoder is carried by a set of latent tokens; different tokens correspond to different facial regions/semantics. Meaningful directions exist inside sub-token subspaces, and can be found without training — a training-free steering method for identity.

### Main finding

Enables fine-grained, localized, semantically-coherent facial edits that maintain identity consistency across generations. Both qualitative and quantitative gains over pixel-editing baselines.

### Why it matters

Extends the "steering vectors" idea (interp: activation steering) to diffusion-model latent spaces for identity. Bridge point between mech-interp of transformers and analogous manipulation in image-generation models.
