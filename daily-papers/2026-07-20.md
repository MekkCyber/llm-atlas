## 1. Xiaomi-Robotics-1

**Authors:** Jun Guo, Piaopiao Jin, Jason Li, Peiyan Li, Yingyan Li, Futeng Liu, Wanli Peng, Yifei Su, Nan Sun, Qiao Sun, Runze Suo, Heyun Wang, Yunhong Wang, Rujie Wu, Caoyu Xia, Lina Zhang, Jack Zhao, et al. — *Xiaomi Robotics*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.15330](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15330) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.15330) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.15330)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `VLA`, `agents`, `pre-training`

### TL;DR

A foundational vision-language-action model trained on 100K+ hours of real-world manipulation trajectories collected via UMI (Universal Manipulation Interface) devices, plus a two-stage pretrain/post-train recipe. The pretraining pass consumes auto-labeled trajectory clips describing scene *state transitions*; post-training then re-aligns those state-transition capabilities with human-issued imperative task prompts and specific robot embodiments.

### Key idea

Split VLA training the way LLMs split pretrain/post-train. During pretraining, an auto-labeling pipeline annotates trajectory clips with natural-language descriptions of what changed in the scene, giving the model dense, embodiment-agnostic conditioning. Post-training then translates those descriptive-state-transition heads into policies conditioned on imperative task prompts and specific robot embodiments — the analog of instruction tuning for VLA models.

### Main finding

Xiaomi-Robotics-1 sets a new SOTA of 57.6% on RoboCasa365 (vs. previous best 46.6%) and 20.07 on RoboDojo (vs. 13.07). Scaling curves on both data and model size hold across pretraining and post-training, and the resulting model fine-tunes to novel dexterous tasks with strong data efficiency.

### Why it matters

This is the first robotics VLA report to argue that the *pretraining* pass can be scaled the same way LLMs scale — 100K+ hours of trajectories, an auto-labeled state-transition supervision signal that decouples data collection from downstream task specification, and clean scaling curves on both axes. If it holds up in replication, it repositions VLA as a scaling problem rather than a data-collection-per-task problem.

## 2. RAGU

**Authors:** Mikhail Komarov, Ivan Bondarenko, Stanislav Shtuka, Oleg Sedukhin, Roman Shuvalov, Yana Dementyeva, Matvey Solovyov, Nikolay O. Nikitin — *Novosibirsk State University · ITMO University (Laboratory of Automated Multi-Agent AI)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.11683](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11683) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.11683) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.11683)
**Topics:** `retrieval`, `LLM`, `agents`, `distillation`

### TL;DR

An open-source modular GraphRAG engine that replaces the usual single-pass entity/relation extractor with a five-stage pipeline (two-stage typed extraction → DBSCAN dedup → LLM summarization → Leiden community detection). A 7B distilled extractor ("Meno-Lite-0.1") trained on only *language skills* (comprehension, extraction, in-context reasoning) matches or beats Qwen2.5-32B on KG construction while running at a fraction of the cost.

### Key idea

Split "language skills" (comprehension, extraction, reasoning over given context) from "world knowledge" (facts you must recall). The former scales weakly with parameter count, the latter scales steeply. A GraphRAG pipeline needs the former, not the latter — so train a compact model specialized on those language skills and let the graph carry the world knowledge.

### Main finding

Meno-Lite-0.1 (7B) beats Qwen2.5-32B by +12.5% relative harmonic mean on KG construction and matches it on English GraphRAG tasks — at roughly 1/5 the parameter count. The full RAGU pipeline separates extraction from consolidation, yielding cleaner entities and more stable retrieval than single-pass GraphRAG.

### Why it matters

RAG-heavy systems are increasingly a place where tiny specialist models win over general frontier models. If "language-skill-only" extractors reliably match giant generalists on graph construction, GraphRAG pipelines can drop to consumer hardware — and the pattern (skill-decomposition-based distillation) is portable to other inference-time-heavy agent stages.

## 3. Resource2Skills

**Authors:** Yijia Fan, Zonglin Di, Zimo Wen, Yifan Yang, Mingxi Cheng, Qi Dai, Bei Liu, Kai Qiu, Yue Dong, Ji Li, Chong Luo — *Microsoft Research / UC Santa Cruz / Shanghai Jiao Tong University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.29538](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29538) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.29538) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.29538)
**Topics:** `agents`, `multimodal`, `distillation`

### TL;DR

A framework that mines executable *skills* (parameterized action recipes) for software agents from ordinary human-authored resources — tutorials, docs, wikis, videos — and organizes them into a maintainable multimodal skill library. Covers seven authoring domains (slides, web pages, spreadsheets, Blender, CAD, UE5 scenes, music production); the resulting skills give agents consistent gains over both no-skill baselines and strong harness baselines.

### Key idea

Reframe skill acquisition for software agents as *distillation from resources*, not from expert demonstrations or trial-and-error rollouts. Human-created resources already encode the reusable operational knowledge; the framework extracts skills from those resources, stores them in a wiki-organized multimodal library, and equips the agent with selection + online acquisition of skills at execution time.

### Main finding

Across the seven authoring domains, skill access yields consistent gains over no-skill agents and strong harness baselines. Ablations isolate each design choice: source mix, multimodal format, wiki organization, selection strategy, and online acquisition all contribute independently.

### Why it matters

Most agent skill libraries are hand-authored or bootstrapped from expensive expert traces. If arbitrary human documentation is enough of a training signal for reusable executable skills, the ceiling on agent capability moves from "how much expert data can we collect" to "how well can we extract from what humans already wrote."

## 4. Loop the Loopies (Loopie)

**Authors:** Zitian Gao, Yilong Chen, Yihao Xiao, Xinyu Yang, Ran Tao, Joey Zhou, Bryan Dai — *IQuestLab*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.16051](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16051) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.16051) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.16051)
**Topics:** `architecture`, `MoE`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

A looped-Transformer MoE line: 20B (2B active) and 6B (0.6B active) models where the same block-stack is executed multiple times per forward pass. Prior looped Transformers always lost to a same-compute unrolled model of N× the width; Loopie claims the first recipe where looping matches or beats the equivalent unrolled baseline. A dedicated post-training pipeline lifts it to IMO / IPhO 2025 gold-medal reasoning without tool use.

### Key idea

Marry a looped Transformer with fine-grained MoE and a training curriculum that keeps looped depth genuinely useful (rather than a re-scoring pass). The paper's central engineering argument is against the long-standing "N× params always beats N loops" pattern; the recipe details how they close the gap.

### Main finding

Loopie's ablation vs. a vanilla 30B-A3B baseline (same compute) shows looped MoE now wins outright. The 20B-A2B variant reaches gold-medal performance at IMO 2025 and IPhO 2025 without tools.

### Why it matters

Looped Transformers are a natural fit for reasoning — the same block re-applied to a working memory is a compact form of "adaptive compute per token." If a training recipe genuinely closes the loop-vs-unroll gap, this opens a scaling axis that has been effectively dormant since Universal Transformers.

## 5. xHC: Expanded Hyper-Connections

**Authors:** Xiangdong Zhang, Xiaohan Qin, Sunan Zou, Tuo Dai, Xiaoming Shi, Huaijin Wu, Yebin Yang, Zhuo Xia, Shaofeng Zhang, Lin Yao, Yuliang Liu, Yu Cheng, Junchi Yan — *SJTU / Xiaohongshu / USTC / Peking / CUHK*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.14530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14530) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.14530) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.14530)
**Topics:** `architecture`, `scaling`, `residual`

### TL;DR

Hyper-Connections (HC) split a Transformer's residual stream into N parallel streams and let each block read/write across them, giving a memory-scaling axis beyond width and depth. All HC-family methods so far cap at N=4 — beyond that, gains flatten and training cost balloons cubically. xHC identifies two bottlenecks (insufficient write-back information and cubic residual-mixing cost) and proposes fixes that enable meaningful expansion beyond N=4.

### Key idea

Two coupled fixes: (1) a richer write-back path so that each of the N streams gets enough signal to remain useful as N grows, and (2) a residual-mixing kernel whose cost no longer scales cubically in N, so N=8/16/… stays practical.

### Main finding

Where prior HC/mHC saturated at N=4, xHC keeps improving with N under matched training compute — the paper reports it as the first HC-family method to expand meaningfully beyond N=4. Full numbers in the paper.

### Why it matters

Residual-stream width has quietly become a scaling axis distinct from model width and depth. If HC-style expansion holds beyond N=4, it gives architects a third knob to trade compute for capacity — potentially cheaper than adding layers.

## 6. On-Policy Delta Distillation (OPD²)

**Authors:** *(NAVER AI Lab; full author list not shown on HF page)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.15161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15161) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.15161) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.15161)
**Topics:** `RL`, `distillation`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

An on-policy distillation method for reasoning that supervises the student not with the teacher's full output distribution, but with the *delta signal* — the difference between the reasoning-tuned teacher and its pre-tuning base. That delta isolates what reasoning tuning added, giving a cleaner transfer signal than raw distribution matching.

### Key idea

Replace `KL(student ‖ teacher)` with a signal derived from `teacher − teacher_base`. The delta captures the reasoning capabilities *added* by post-training, filtering out the base capabilities the student already shares, so the student focuses its update budget on the residual reasoning skill.

### Main finding

OPD² consistently beats conventional on-policy distillation across math, science, and code reasoning benchmarks, reaching strong reasoning performance with a short post-training run. Code will be at `github.com/naver-ai/opd2`.

### Why it matters

On-policy distillation is emerging as a cheap alternative to RL for reasoning transfer, but existing recipes waste signal on skills the student already has. A delta-signal fix is a clean, general change that should apply to any teacher/base pair — a small algorithmic upgrade with a broad blast radius.

## 7. From Human-Centric to Agentic Code Review

**Authors:** Suzhen Zhong, Shayan Noei, Bram Adams, Ying Zou — *Queen's University (ECE + MCIS Lab)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.13196](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13196) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.13196) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.13196)
**Topics:** `agents`, `eval`, `SE`

### TL;DR

An empirical study of 1.02M reviewed pull requests across 207 GitHub projects, split into three "generations": human-centric review, LLM-assisted review, and agentic code review. Measures how the transition affects review efficiency and review quality — one of the first large-scale before/after studies of AI-assisted code review at real project scale.

### Key idea

Treat "who does code review" as a categorical variable with three levels (human, LLM-assisted, agentic), then measure downstream review-quality and efficiency signals across the same GitHub projects as they transition. Not a new technique but a large real-world eval of an increasingly common deployment pattern.

### Main finding

Full quantitative findings are in the paper; the framing establishes a three-era taxonomy and a corpus large enough to make claims stable. Interesting as a baseline for anyone deploying agentic reviewers in real repos.

### Why it matters

Agentic code review is being deployed everywhere without much evidence about its downstream effect on merged code quality. A 1M-PR-scale study anchored to real-world projects (rather than benchmarks) is the kind of ground truth that safety / eval discussions have been missing.

## 8. Understanding Reasoning from Pretraining to Post-Training

**Authors:** Jingyan Shen, Ang Li, Salman Rahman, Yifan Sun, Micah Goldblum, Matus Telgarsky, Pavel Izmailov — *NYU / Modal Labs / UCLA / UIUC / Columbia*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.16097](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16097) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.16097) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.16097)
**Topics:** `RL`, `reasoning`, `scaling-laws`, `interp`

### TL;DR

A controlled study — using a chess-based testbed where ground-truth quality is cheap — of how pretraining choices (model size, data mixture) shape the returns to RL post-training compute, and of what RL post-training actually does to a model beyond the aggregate benchmark bump.

### Key idea

Pick a domain (chess) where you can measure *actual* move quality independently of self-play tricks, then sweep pretraining × RL compute jointly. This isolates two questions almost never studied together: how does pretraining set the ceiling for RL returns, and what internal changes does RL actually make?

### Main finding

The paper reports scaling laws that couple pretraining and RL compute — you can't optimize one in isolation — and characterizes what RL post-training modifies inside the network (rather than treating it as a black-box benchmark lift). Numeric details in the paper.

### Why it matters

The last two years of RLVR / long-CoT / reasoning-RL literature treats pretraining as fixed context. This paper is the first serious attempt to close the loop and give practitioners guidance on *which* base models are worth spending RL compute on — the missing piece behind every "we did RL post-training and it worked" report.

## 9. Qwen-Music Technical Report

**Authors:** *(Qwen team; full author list not shown on HF page)* — *Alibaba / Qwen*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.11699](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11699) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.11699) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.11699)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `audio`, `LLM`

### TL;DR

Qwen's music generation stack: **Qwen-Music-Tokenizer** compresses audio to a 25 Hz single-codebook stream of "Music Semantic Tokens"; **Qwen-Music-LLM** does autoregressive semantic modeling with a *melody-token chain-of-thought* that plans melody before generating the full song; **Qwen-Music-Render** does generative stereo rendering to recover the acoustic detail that discrete semantic tokens lose. Trained on 5M+ hours of multilingual music.

### Key idea

Two structural moves. First, factor music generation into "semantic tokens → high-fidelity rendering," letting the LLM plan structure at low bitrate. Second, add an explicit *melody CoT*: the model predicts a melody-token skeleton first, then conditions the rest of the song on it — a music-specific analog of reasoning CoT.

### Main finding

Qwen-Music produces highly musical, high-fidelity songs with complete vocal singing across both text-to-music and cover-song generation, on 5M+ hours of multilingual training data (details in the report).

### Why it matters

Audio-LLM stacks are converging on the same three-stage pattern (tokenizer → LLM → rendering), and Melody-CoT is a clean example of transplanting the reasoning-CoT trick into a non-text domain. Anyone building modality-specific LLM stacks should be paying attention to how the semantic/acoustic split is drawn here.

## 10. Recursive Harness Self-Improvement (RHI)

**Authors:** Hyunin Lee, Jinglue Xu, Jeffrey Seely, Donghyun Lee, Matei Zaharia, Yujin Tang — *UC Berkeley (Zaharia lab) / Sakana AI (assumed from author list)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.15524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15524) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.15524) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.15524)
**Topics:** `agents`, `self-improve`, `harness`

### TL;DR

Represent an agent harness (the scaffold: loop, memory, tool wiring) as a *prompt-level specification*, then iteratively refine it with pairwise comparisons over its own revision history. A few RHI iterations lift low-reasoning-effort agents *above* the max-reasoning-effort setting on the same task while cutting inference cost by up to 60%.

### Key idea

Move the "self-improvement" locus from the model to the *harness*. Represent the harness textually (prompt-level agent-loop spec), evolve it via pairwise-comparison feedback across revisions, and gain from better context management rather than longer chains of thought. Motivated by an information-theoretic hypothesis: RHI is implicitly optimizing inter-agent information flow.

### Main finding

Across 30 synthetic ML-research tasks (quant finance, robotics, pharmacy), a few RHI iterations let low-reasoning-effort agents exceed the max-reasoning-effort baseline on the same tasks *and* run up to 60% cheaper. Gains come from better context management, not longer reasoning traces.

### Why it matters

Most agent-improvement stories today are model self-improvement (better weights) or harness engineering (better human-designed scaffolds). RHI is a middle path: keep the model fixed, evolve the scaffold automatically. Cheap, safe, and directly usable in production agent stacks where you can't retrain the underlying model.

## 11. When Does Muon Help Agentic Reinforcement Learning?

**Authors:** *(not disclosed on HF page)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.16169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16169) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.16169) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.16169)
**Topics:** `RL`, `optimizer`, `agents`

### TL;DR

A first exploratory study of Muon (a matrix-oriented optimizer that has been competitive with AdamW at pretraining scale) applied to sparse-reward agentic RL post-training on ALFWorld with Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct. Under Group-in-Group Policy Optimization (GiGPO), Muon applied only to hidden weight matrices lifts final-window validation success from 0.290 to 0.546 (+88%); the effect strength depends on the advantage estimator and the LR.

### Key idea

Muon operates on parameter *matrices* rather than element-wise like AdamW, giving it structurally different update geometry. That geometry can pay off in the sparse-reward RL regime — but only when combined with the right advantage estimator (GiGPO, GRPO, GraphGPO tested) and learning rate. The paper's contribution is mapping when Muon actually helps agentic RL vs. when AdamW controls dominate.

### Main finding

- GiGPO + Muon (hidden weights only): success 0.290 → 0.546 (+88%); high-rate AdamW controls retain no post-update success.
- GRPO + Muon at LR 3×10⁻⁵: 0.161 → 0.268.
- GraphGPO + Muon at LR 10⁻⁵: reaches 0.901; normalized validation AUC 0.399 → 0.556; reaches 0.5 / 0.75 success 30 / 60 updates earlier.

### Why it matters

Muon's pretraining wins are established; whether it transfers to post-training RL was open. This paper argues *yes, conditionally* — a mapping between optimizer, advantage estimator, and learning rate that practitioners haven't had before. Exploratory (single-seed), but the direction is worth pursuing.

### Knowledge graph integration

## 13. Beyond Entropy: Correctness-Aware Advantage Shaping via CPO

**Authors:** Weiwen Xu, Jia Liu, Hou Pong Chan, Long Li, Deng Cai, Min Chen, Hao Zhang — *CUHK / SCUT / NTU*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.14614](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14614) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.14614) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.14614)
**Topics:** `RL`, `RLVR`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

RLVR pipelines routinely use entropy for advantage shaping — but entropy conflates *useful* uncertainty (exploration) with *detrimental* confusion (broken reasoning). Contrastive Policy Optimization (CPO) replaces entropy with a token-level contrastive disagreement between reference-guided and vanilla generation distributions, giving a correctness-aware shaping signal.

### Key idea

For each token, sample distributions with and without reference guidance; the KL/contrast between them signals whether the token is in a "helpful uncertainty" or "broken" regime, and that signal replaces entropy in the advantage-shaping term. Correctness-aware, not variance-based.

### Main finding

CPO improves RLVR reasoning performance relative to entropy-based advantage shaping (specific numbers in the paper, not on HF preview).

### Why it matters

Almost every RLVR variant published in the last year uses entropy in the advantage estimator. If a drop-in replacement with a genuine correctness signal is cheap, it's a broadly applicable upgrade — worth trying against every RLVR/GRPO/DPO baseline.

## 14. VideoRAE

**Authors:** Zhihao Xie, Junfeng Wu, Xinting Hu, Junchao Huang, Li Jiang — *CUHK-Shenzhen / HUST / Shenzhen Loop Area Institute / USTC*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.14088](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14088) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.14088) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.14088)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `video`, `multimodal`, `representation`

### TL;DR

Conventional 3D-VAEs used as latent-space substrates for video diffusion/AR models optimize for pixel reconstruction — which produces latents with weak semantic and spatio-temporal structure. VideoRAE takes a *frozen* Video Foundation Model (V-JEPA 2, VideoMAEv2) and converts its representations into compact, generation-friendly latents suitable for downstream video generative modeling.

### Key idea

Rather than train a fresh 3D-VAE for reconstruction, wrap a frozen video foundation model (VFM) with a Representation Autoencoder that produces latents which are both (a) compact enough for generation and (b) semantically rich because the VFM was pretrained on semantic-heavy objectives. The generative model then operates in *semantic* latent space rather than pixel-fidelity latent space.

### Main finding

VideoRAE demonstrates that frozen VFM representations can be turned into compact, reconstruction-capable, generation-friendly latents — improving downstream synthesis quality relative to pixel-optimized 3D-VAE baselines. (Concrete benchmark numbers in the paper.)

### Why it matters

For text-to-video and world-model work, latent quality is the biggest single lever. Coupling generative modeling to *semantic* representations (V-JEPA-family) rather than reconstruction-optimized VAEs pulls video diffusion closer to the "generate ideas, not pixels" regime that image diffusion moved toward with DALL-E 3.

## 15. DSWorld

**Authors:** Zherui Yang, Fan Liu, Hao Liu — *HKUST (Guangzhou)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.15901](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15901) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.15901) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.15901)
**Topics:** `agents`, `world-model`, `RL`

### TL;DR

A *Data Science World Model* — a learned predictor of "if I run this operation on the current data-science workflow state, what state do I get?" — combined with cost-aware routing between lightweight real execution and an LLM-based simulator. DSWorld accelerates RL-based data-science-agent training ~14× and search-based inference ~3–6× while remaining competitive on outcome quality.

### Key idea

Data-science agents burn most of their budget on *actually running* candidate operations. DSWorld replaces the expensive ones with a learned world-model simulator, keeps the cheap ones as real execution, and routes between them cost-awarely. Trained on an 8K-scale transition-trajectory dataset with a Reflective World Model Optimization RL loop that penalizes prediction errors.

### Main finding

DSWorld accelerates RL agent training ~14× and search-based inference ~3–6×, and outperforms the strongest LLM baseline by +35.6% on transition prediction. Code at `anonymous.4open.science/r/DSWorld`.

### Why it matters

Learned environment simulators are becoming the default way to make agentic RL affordable — the same move that made game-playing RL possible. DSWorld is one of the first solid demonstrations of the pattern in a real productivity domain (data science) rather than games / robotics.
