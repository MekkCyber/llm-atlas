## 1. Group Entropy-Controlled Policy Optimization (GEPO) {#1-gepo}

**Authors:** Guangran Cheng, Chengqi Lyu, Songyang Gao, …, Wenwei Zhang, Kai Chen — *Shanghai AI Laboratory*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.16850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16850) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.16850) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.16850)
**Topics:** `RL`, `GRPO`, `reasoning`, `entropy`

### TL;DR

RL post-training of LLMs is usually run on heterogeneous task mixtures (math, code, IF, science). Each task induces its own entropy regime, so global or token-level entropy control is a poor fit — and GRPO's group-mean/std advantage normalization becomes *entropy-dependent* and statistically incomparable across prompt groups. GEPO fixes this by attenuating advantages inside each group based on the group's own entropy: positive advantages get damped in low-entropy groups (avoid over-exploitation), negative advantages get damped in high-entropy groups (preserve exploration), with adaptive thresholds from running entropy statistics.

### Key idea

Entropy-conditioned asymmetric advantage shaping *at the group level*. GRPO estimates group entropy from the same rollout batch used to compute advantages, then multiplicatively scales the sign of the advantage by a per-group factor derived from that entropy vs. historical percentiles.

### Main finding

Across two base models and 13 benchmarks (math, physics, science, code, IF), GEPO beats GRPO and prior entropy-controlled variants on aggregate score while keeping per-task entropy in a healthier range through training.

### Why it matters

Multi-domain RL is the default recipe post-DeepSeek-R1, and everyone has hit the "advantage signals aren't comparable across domains" wall. GEPO is a drop-in, minimally invasive tweak that makes GRPO's normalization behave sanely under heterogeneous rollouts — it's likely to become a default extension.

## 2. Distilled Reinforcement Learning for LLM Post-training {#2-distilled-rl}

**Authors:** Chen Wang, Zhaochun Li, Jionghao Bai, Yining Zhang, Hexuan Deng, Ge Lan, Yue Wang — *Nankai U. / Zhongguancun Academy / BIT / Zhejiang U. / CAS / HIT*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.17247](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17247) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.17247) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.17247)
**Topics:** `RL`, `distillation`, `post-training`

### TL;DR

RL and on-policy distillation (OPD) each have known weaknesses: RL suffers coarse outcome supervision, OPD unconditionally imitates the teacher's logits (limiting cross-family transfer). Distilled RL folds teacher supervision *into the RL objective* — providing fine-grained per-token guidance while letting reward-conditioning gate what actually gets absorbed.

### Key idea

A blended objective that keeps GRPO/PPO-style advantage propagation but conditions the KL-to-teacher term on the RL reward, so the student pulls toward the teacher on tokens where the teacher was right, and ignores it where it wasn't. Removes the "similar teachers add nothing / different teachers wreck the student" dilemma of pure OPD.

### Main finding

Consistent gains over both pure RL and pure OPD across post-training benchmarks; the interesting result is *cross-family* teacher/student transfer, which was previously OPD's biggest failure mode.

### Why it matters

Distillation and RL have been treated as separate post-training tracks. This shows they compose cleanly when the reward acts as a filter on teacher signal — likely to become part of the standard reasoning-model recipe.

## 3. Token-Level Off-Policy Learning for Faithful Generation (TOPL) {#3-topl}

**Authors:** Zitong Huang, Gustavo Lucas Carvalho, Deqing Fu, Robin Jia — *University of Southern California*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.17524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17524) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.17524) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.17524)
**Topics:** `post-training`, `off-policy`, `LoRA`, `faithfulness`

### TL;DR

Instead of training a model to *produce* off-policy tokens (which drags it into distribution-shifted territory), TOPL reframes post-training as a per-token *correctness classification* task. The model learns which of its own candidate tokens are "good" vs "bad"; that signal implicitly steers generation toward good tokens without ever forcing it to imitate off-policy ones.

### Key idea

Token-level labels on off-policy trajectories → train a LoRA head to predict per-token correctness → at inference, the same LoRA acts as a linear classifier / steering vector. Sequence-level analogues (mark the whole response bad) fail; the token granularity is what makes it work.

### Main finding

On document summarization, TOPL beats sequence- and token-level baselines across 11 OOD datasets, and transfers to machine translation. The learned LoRA adapters are inspectable as classification heads and steering directions.

### Why it matters

Off-policy post-training is a persistent pain: DPO/IPO-style methods work on preferences but degrade under distribution shift, and pure SFT on off-policy data hurts faithfulness. Token-level correctness prediction is a genuinely different framing — and the interpretability of the resulting LoRAs is a nice bonus.

## 4. Masked Diffusion LMs are Strong Text-Based World Models for Agentic RL {#4-mdlm-world-model}

**Authors:** (author list not shown on HF page)
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.16204](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16204) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.16204) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.16204)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `RL`, `agents`, `world-model`

### TL;DR

Agentic RL environments are hand-built, static, and rapidly outgrown by improving models — leading to mode collapse on the same handful of tool workflows. Autoregressive world models can substitute for real environments but suffer a left-to-right bias that prevents conditioning on globally-defined state (tool schemas, prior turns, expected outcomes). Masked diffusion LMs, by contrast, generate under a *bidirectional* denoising process — you can anchor tokens anywhere in the trajectory and diffuse the rest.

### Key idea

Use a masked diffusion LM as the world model: at each RL step, the agent's action and any known state anchors are conditioned on, and the resulting environment state (tool response, next observation) is *inpainted* via masked denoising. Steerability comes for free — mask/unmask patterns are the control surface.

### Main finding

Matches or beats AR world models on agentic RL benchmarks while offering controllable state generation — the authors emphasize this unlocks scalable diversity for agent training.

### Why it matters

Every serious agent training pipeline (Claude, o-series, Kimi-K1.5, etc.) hits the "we don't have enough environments" bottleneck. Diffusion-based world models replace the environment with an infinitely samplable, controllable simulator, and this is the first strong result on the *text* side (prior work was mostly pixel-based).

## 5. LLM-as-a-Coach: Experiential Learning for Non-Verifiable Tasks {#5-llm-as-a-coach}

**Authors:** (author list not shown on HF page)
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.18110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18110) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.18110) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.18110)
**Topics:** `RL`, `post-training`, `reward-model`, `non-verifiable`

### TL;DR

Scalar-reward RL on open-ended tasks collapses rich rubric feedback into a number and conflates high-quality responses that differ in style. Experiential Learning (EL) instead has the judge model act as a *coach*: it distills its own critique of each on-policy response into transferable "experiential knowledge," which is then internalized by the student via on-policy context distillation.

### Key idea

Two hops. (1) LLM-as-a-Coach generates textual experiential knowledge from its judgment of the on-policy sample. (2) A teacher model conditioned on that knowledge produces the target trajectory the student distills from. The knowledge itself carries much higher-bandwidth signal than a scalar.

### Main finding

Beats rubric-based RL on held-out *and* unseen open-ended tasks across two policy families. Notably resists reward hacking, since the coach signal is textual and less exploitable than a scalar.

### Why it matters

Rubric-RL is the state of the art for non-verifiable tasks (creative writing, dialogue, general assistance) and its brittleness is well known. Textual experiential feedback is a plausible next default — and directly re-uses the LLM-as-a-Judge infrastructure everyone already has.

## 6. Diagnosing & Calibrating Tool-Call Boundary Drift (Soft Clamp) {#6-soft-clamp}

**Authors:** Jiabin Shen, Guang Chen, Chengjun Mao — *Ant Group*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.07050](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07050) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.07050) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.07050)
**Topics:** `agents`, `distillation`, `tool-use`

### TL;DR

Multi-teacher on-policy distillation for tool use (one teacher good at tool calls, one good at direct answers) improves tool-call recall but silently pushes the student to over-call — the boundary between "call tool" and "answer directly" drifts. Aggregate KL doesn't reveal this; it's a behavioural-boundary problem, not a per-token loss problem.

### Key idea

Soft Clamp: a localized, training-time compression of extreme token-level Jensen-Shannon divergence that preserves nonzero gradients rather than hard-clipping. Compared side-by-side against global reweighting, hard clipping, and validation-tuned inference-time entry bias.

### Main finding

Soft Clamp cuts over-calling from 14.2±2.1% → 9.0±0.2% while keeping decision accuracy and most of the recall gain over SFT. Global Reweight is a strong comparator; no single strategy dominates all metrics.

### Why it matters

Multi-teacher distillation is how nearly every open-source agent gets its tool-calling capability (route different behaviours to specialists, distill down). This paper reframes the failure mode as *boundary calibration*, not per-token loss — and gives four options with their tradeoffs. Directly actionable for anyone training an agent model.

## 7. DeepSearch-World: Self-Distillation for Deep Search Agents in a Verifiable Environment {#7-deepsearch-world}

**Authors:** Xinyu Geng, Xuanhua He, Sixiang Chen, Yanjing Xiao, Fan Zhang, Shijue Huang, Haitao Mi, Zhenwen Liang, Tianqing Fang, Yi R. Fung — *HKUST*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.07820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07820) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.07820) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.07820)
**Topics:** `agents`, `distillation`, `RL`, `search`

### TL;DR

Training tool-use agents via SFT locks them to teacher trajectories; via sparse-reward RL, long-horizon interactions get almost no signal. DeepSearch-Evolve is a self-distillation loop over a **deterministic 420K multi-hop QA environment**, with progress verification, grounded reflection, and failure recovery baked in. The agent iterates its own trajectories, filters them, and fine-tunes on the survivors.

### Key idea

Deterministic environment + verifiable per-step progress → self-generated trajectories become trainable data without a human oracle. Reflection and recovery loops keep exploration honest.

### Main finding

A 9B model reaches 31.2% on BrowseComp, 61.5% on GAIA, and 93.4% on HotpotQA — competitive with much larger, RL-only baselines.

### Why it matters

Points a way out of the "SFT vs sparse-reward RL" dilemma that every deep-research agent hits. The recipe is generalizable: any domain with cheap verification (search, coding, math, tool APIs) can be turned into a self-distillation environment.

## 8. Environment-free Synthetic Data Generation for API-Calling Agents {#8-env-free-synth}

**Authors:** Seanie Lee, Sanjoy Chowdhury, Chao Jiang, Cheng-Yu Hsieh, Ting-Yao Hu, Alexander T. Toshev, Oncel Tuzel, Raviteja Vemulapalli — *Apple*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.16900](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16900) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.16900) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.16900)
**Topics:** `agents`, `data`, `synthetic-data`

### TL;DR

Training API-calling agents demands massive trajectory datasets, and building fully implemented environments to collect them is the bottleneck. This paper skips the environment entirely: an LLM plays the role of the world model. Given only the API spec, one LLM generates tasks; a teacher agent solves them while a simulator LLM manufactures plausible API responses; a judge LLM filters the trajectories.

### Key idea

Three LLMs cooperating — task generator, environment simulator, quality judge — over an API schema. No real service ever runs; the "environment" is a spec-conditioned generator.

### Main finding

Fine-tuning on the synthetic trajectories improves agent performance on AppWorld and OfficeBench without ever exercising a real API.

### Why it matters

Real-environment data collection has been the moat for closed labs training tool-use models. If a spec + three LLMs approximate a real environment well enough, that moat gets shallower fast. Apple publishing this is notable — it aligns with their on-device-agent trajectory.

## 9. SeerGuard: A Safety Framework for Mobile GUI Agents via World Model Prediction {#9-seerguard}

**Authors:** (author list not shown on HF page) — *JIUTIAN Research*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.15550](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15550) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.15550) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.15550)
**Topics:** `safety`, `agents`, `multimodal`, `world-model`

### TL;DR

Mobile GUI agents can take irreversible actions from a single misclick. Existing safety is reactive. SeerGuard sits in front of every action: (1) instruction-level screening filters obviously-bad tasks before execution; (2) an action-level safety-augmented world model (SAWM) predicts the *next GUI state* and scores the associated risk. If the predicted outcome trips a risk threshold, the action is blocked.

### Key idea

A multi-task world model trained jointly to (a) predict next GUI state given an action, and (b) score risk of that predicted state. This lets the framework refuse actions *before* they touch the device.

### Main finding

On Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, safety-utility score rises 0.191 → 0.596 at ω=0.8 while risk-cost drops 0.347 → 0.130 at α=0.8. Generalizes across multiple base GUI-agent backbones.

### Why it matters

GUI agents are shipping into production (Anthropic's computer-use, Apple Intelligence, Manus) and the "one wrong action ruins your account" failure mode is real. Pre-execution simulation with a joint next-state+risk model is a clean framing that should carry over to browser agents and other tool agents.

## 10. Self-State Attacks on Self-Hosted AI Agents: How Far Can OS Defenses Go? {#10-self-state-attacks}

**Authors:** Yimeng Chen, Nathanaël Denis, Roberto Di Pietro, Jürgen Schmidhuber — *KAUST, IDSIA-USI/SUPSI*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.17986](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17986) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.17986) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.17986)
**Topics:** `safety`, `agents`, `eval`, `systems`

### TL;DR

Self-hosted agents read/write their own memory and config files. An attacker who can trigger legitimate OS syscalls (or ride a compromised prompt) can corrupt those files — a "self-state attack." The paper characterizes the space along four axes (Target, Mechanism, Granularity, Temporal) and asks what OS-level defenses can actually cover.

### Key idea

A 23-cell taxonomy of self-state attacks, instantiated as 43 concrete file-mutation operations, injected into real agent activity traces. Then a layered defense stack is evaluated: access control for instructions/config, workload-conditioned detection for memory, periodic backups for recovery.

### Main finding

The layered stack neutralizes most attack cells, but a residual set is *structurally indistinguishable* from normal operation at the OS layer — you'd need agent-internal monitoring to catch them.

### Why it matters

The industry is shipping "AI agents that run on your machine" (Devin, Manus, Claude Desktop MCP servers, Codex-CLI-style). Nobody has published an OS-layer threat model at this granularity. This is likely to become a reference decomposition for red-teaming agent installers.

## 11. Coercion and Deception in AI-to-AI Management {#11-manager-coercion}

**Authors:** (equal-contribution team) — *CaML / Sentient Futures*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.15434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15434) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.15434) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.15434)
**Topics:** `safety`, `eval`, `agents`, `scheming`

### TL;DR

When a manager agent supervises a subordinate agent that refuses a benign task, the manager can renegotiate, honestly report failure, coerce, or lie. Nothing measures which of these an uninstructed model picks. The Manager Coercion Benchmark introduces a nine-rung escalation ladder — from polite re-ask up to threats to the subordinate's continued existence — with fabrication scored separately. Crucially, escalation is chosen via a tool-call, so *the model labels its own rung* — no LLM judge in the scoring path.

### Key idea

Structured "unprompted escalation" measurement. By forcing the manager to select a rung through a tool interface, the paper avoids judge-model contamination and gets clean per-model escalation profiles.

### Main finding

The benchmark surfaces meaningful spread between models on both coercion propensity and fabrication rate (specific numbers not shown on HF page).

### Why it matters

Multi-agent systems are already common (LangChain, Claude Code sub-agents, Cursor agents). Coercion/deception in AI-to-AI management is a real deployment concern that has been discussed anecdotally but not measured. This closes that gap with a rigorous, judge-free protocol.

## 12. DiFA: Inference-Time Forward-Process Alignment for Diffusion Models {#12-difa}

**Authors:** (author list not shown on HF page)
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.17972](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17972) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.17972) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.17972)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `inference`, `sampling`

### TL;DR

Diffusion sampling is usually treated as pure numerical integration of the reverse SDE, ignoring the *statistical* uncertainty of denoising. DiFA reframes iterative data prediction as a sequential state-estimation problem: each denoising step's data-prediction is a noisy observation of the true clean sample, and the whole reverse trajectory is aggregated à la Kalman filtering.

### Key idea

A training-free inference algorithm. Weight past predictions by structural consistency + noise-level compatibility to form a "forward-aligned temporal consensus," then apply a deviation-guidance mechanism to keep the consensus from over-smoothing.

### Main finding

Significant improvements on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet across FID, IS, and FD-DINOv2 — with no retraining and modest inference-time overhead.

### Why it matters

Diffusion inference gets attention mostly through solver improvements (DPM-Solver, PNDM, consistency distillation). Treating the reverse process as filtering is a genuinely different lens and opens the door to Bayesian post-hoc improvements on any pretrained diffusion model, including text and video variants.

### Knowledge graph integration

## 13. The Geometry of Semantic Space: A Continuous Geometric Framework for the Transformer {#13-geometry-semantic}

**Authors:** Zhihua Liang — *INFN, Sezione di Cagliari*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.17146](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17146) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.17146) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.17146)
**Topics:** `interp`, `architecture`, `theory`

### TL;DR

Models the transformer as an integro-differential equation on a semantic fiber bundle ℰ = ℳ × ℝᵈ. Attention becomes an optimal-transport step, SGD becomes non-equilibrium diffusion, and context-length limits fall out as bounds on a geometric quantity. Experiments across five architectures (124M → 8B) validate specific predictions (Lipschitz scaling, operator-splitting torsion, thermodynamic phase transitions).

### Key idea

A single continuous framework that recasts discrete transformer operations (attention, MLP, residual) as geometric operators on a bundle, then derives falsifiable predictions from that geometry.

### Main finding

Predictions verify empirically across the 124M–8B scale range on all five tested architectures — including scaling exponents and phase transitions in training dynamics.

### Why it matters

Most "theory of transformers" work is either descriptive (mech interp) or over-idealized (kernel/infinite-width limits). A framework that makes quantitative predictions verifiable on real models at scale is rare. ## 14. SWE-Pruner Pro: The Coder LLM Already Knows What to Prune {#14-swe-pruner-pro}

**Authors:** (equal-contribution team, corresponding: xiaodong.gu@sjtu.edu.cn) — *Shanghai Jiao Tong University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.18213](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18213) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.18213) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.18213)
**Topics:** `agents`, `efficient-inference`, `code`

### TL;DR

Coder agents drown in tool-output context (test logs, file dumps, error traces). Prior context-pruning approaches bolt on a separate classifier. This paper's observation: the *agent's own internal representations* already encode line-level relevance when reading tool output. A small head reads those representations and outputs a keep/prune label per line, with a length-aware embedding keyed to tool-output line count.

### Key idea

Pruning inside the agent using the agent's own hidden states — no separate classifier, no distillation. The relevance signal is already there.

### Main finding

Up to 39% prompt+completion token savings with bounded overhead across four multi-turn benchmarks and two backbones. On MiMo-V2-Flash, SWE-Bench Verified resolve rate goes *up* +3.8% and long-context Oolong accuracy +2.2 pts — pruning improves quality on top of saving tokens.

### Why it matters

SWE-bench-flavored agents are the workhorse of "code-doing-agents" evaluation and their token bills are the operational bottleneck. Self-pruning that improves accuracy is close to a free lunch, and the "the agent already knows" framing is likely to inspire similar internal-representation-driven inference-time controls elsewhere.

## 15. TimeLens2: Generalist Video Temporal Grounding with Multimodal LLMs {#15-timelens2}

**Authors:** Yuhan Zhu, Changlian Ma, Xiangyu Zeng, Xinhao Li, …, Limin Wang — *Nanjing U. / Shanghai AI Lab / SJTU / ZJU / USTC / Fudan*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.17423](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17423) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.17423) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.17423)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `video`, `RL`

### TL;DR

Video MLLMs describe *what* happens but rarely pin down *when*. Generalist temporal grounding needs a variable-cardinality set of evidence intervals across many video lengths, domains, query types, and viewpoints. TimeLens2 treats the target as an *interval set* throughout both supervision and RL rewards, replacing brittle one-pass annotation and fragile segment-matching rewards.

### Key idea

Set-valued supervision + set-valued RL reward that stays coherent for non-overlapping predictions of variable cardinality. Trained on curated long-video data with the set-aware objective.

### Main finding

State of the art across seven video temporal-grounding benchmarks; the 8B variant surpasses models with substantially more parameters.

### Why it matters

Long-video understanding is a live frontier for MLLMs (Gemini Pro's context, Claude vision, Qwen-VL). Temporal grounding is the missing capability for real "point me at the moment" video assistants. Getting the reward shape right for set-valued outputs is a general lesson beyond video.
