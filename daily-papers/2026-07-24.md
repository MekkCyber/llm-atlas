## 1. AREX

**Authors:** Shuqi Lu, Chaofan Li, Kun Luo, Zhang Zhang, Hui Wang, Hongwang Xiao, Zheng Liu, Lei Xiong, et al. — *Affiliation not disclosed on HF page*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.21461](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21461) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.21461) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.21461)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `reasoning`, `MoE`

### TL;DR

A "deep research" agent that exploits the discovery-vs-verification asymmetry: finding a full multi-constraint answer is expensive, but checking constraints one at a time is cheap. AREX runs two nested loops — an inner research loop that gathers evidence and drafts an answer, and an outer self-improvement loop that constraint-wise audits the draft and re-launches targeted follow-up research. To survive long horizons it learns an autonomous *context-update tool* that compresses interaction history into a compact improvement state, and is trained via agentic mid-training + long-horizon RL with key-step credit assignment. Ships as a 4B dense model and a 122B-A10B MoE.

### Key idea

Cast recursive self-improvement (RSI) as **verify-then-refine**: the outer loop treats the agent's partial answer as evidence to prune, not as prompt bloat. A learned context-update tool replaces external summarizers so the compression policy itself is trained end-to-end with the researcher.

### Main finding

Beats comparable-scale baselines on BrowseComp, WideSearch, DeepSearchQA, and Humanity's Last Exam; the 122B-A10B MoE remains competitive with models using substantially more activated parameters. Long-horizon RL with dense credit on "decisive-evidence" steps is the main lever for stability.

### Why it matters

Most deep-research agents scale by searching longer. AREX shows that scaling by *auditing more* — turning verification into a routine outer loop and training the memory compressor as part of the agent — is a cleaner axis, and one that composes with existing tool-use RL stacks.

## 2. SANA-Video 2.0

**Authors:** Junsong Chen, Jincheng Yu, Yitong Li, Shuchen Xue, Haozhe Liu, Jingyu Xin, Yuyang Zhao, Tian Ye, Zhangjie Wu, Zian Wang, Daquan Zhou, Ping Luo, Song Han, Enze Xie — *NVIDIA*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.21553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21553) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.21553) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.21553)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `architectures`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

A hybrid video-DiT at 5B / 14B that dodges quadratic attention over long token sequences by mixing gated linear attention with periodic softmax "anchor" layers at a 3:1 ratio, and threads earlier block outputs into later linear layers via *Block Attention Residuals*. Trained from scratch (no linearization of a pretrained DiT), reaches VBench 84.30 at 480p in 13.2s on a single H100 with 40-step sampling.

### Key idea

Two levers on top of a pure linear-attention DiT: (1) **Hybrid Linear–Softmax Attention** — most layers use O(N) gated linear attention for cheap mixing, but every 4th layer is a full-softmax "anchor" that restores full-rank token interactions; (2) **AttnRes** — completed anchor block summaries are routed into downstream linear layers, letting subsequent layers reuse the softmax-refreshed representations and raising deep-layer effective rank by ~12%.

### Main finding

25% softmax layers is the sweet spot for the quality/efficiency trade-off — established by reduced-resolution proxy studies. The hybrid matches full-softmax video DiTs on VBench while retaining the long-sequence scaling advantage of linear attention, all trained end-to-end without any DiT distillation.

### Why it matters

Long-video generation is dominated by attention cost. Linearizing pretrained diffusion transformers usually loses quality; SANA-Video 2.0 shows that **training the hybrid from scratch** at the right softmax fraction is the path to keeping quality *and* linear-time scaling.

## 3. WorldWeaver

**Authors:** Sicheng Mo, Yuheng Li, Ziyang Leng, Krishna Kumar Singh, Bolei Zhou — *UCLA & Adobe Research*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.21594](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21594) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.21594) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.21594)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `multimodal`, `agents`

### TL;DR

Streaming multi-agent video diffusion (W²) that maintains a persistent *world state* across agents and views via learnable **world state registers** — tokens dynamically updated after every generated chunk. A Mixture-of-Transformers backbone uses separate weights for world-state modeling and visual-frame modeling; auxiliary supervision includes per-agent status, bird's-eye views, and scene text. Evaluated on two-agent Minecraft video generation.

### Key idea

Rather than carrying growing observation history in the conditioning context, allocate a **fixed-size register bank** that (i) stores shared world information, (ii) tracks each agent's status, and (iii) is refreshed after each generated chunk from grounded supervision signals — decoupling world state from context length.

### Main finding

Explicit world-state modeling improves logical consistency and visual quality in multi-agent generation; the MoT split (world tokens ≠ visual tokens) is the architectural piece that makes this trainable at scale.

### Why it matters

The dominant paradigm for autoregressive video diffusion (KV-carry + context conditioning) is fundamentally poor at *shared* state. Registers give diffusion world-models a place to *think* that isn't the frame stream itself — a step toward multi-agent, multi-view interactive world models.

## 4. Predictive Divergence Masks

**Authors:** Xiangxin Zhou, Jiarui Yao, Penghui Qi, Bowen Ping, Jiaqi Tang, Haonan Wang, Tianyu Pang — *Tencent Hunyuan / UIUC / NUS*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.10848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10848) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.10848) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.10848)
**Topics:** `RL`, `post-training`

### TL;DR

PPO-style trust-region masks in LLM RL have two independent decisions: **proximity** ("are we outside the trust region?") and **direction** ("would this update push us further out?"). DPPO already upgraded the proximity criterion from a sampled importance ratio to a full behavior↔training divergence. This paper fixes the mismatched direction criterion, deriving a **closed-form first-order predictor** of the divergence change for softmax policies and masking tokens by its sign.

### Key idea

Under a softmax policy, the divergence's next-step change splits into a **local term** at the sampled token (exactly the ratio-based signal PPO already uses) plus a **global term** from softmax normalization over the whole vocabulary. Keeping only the local term reproduces PPO; keeping both makes the direction criterion actually track the divergence. Since rollout engines expose only top-K logprobs, two lightweight tail estimators (aggregated / uniform) close the vocabulary gap.

### Main finding

The predictive direction criterion is empirically better aligned with realized divergence change than the sampled-ratio criterion; improves training stability and effectiveness of divergence-mask methods across model scales and precision settings, especially under training/inference mismatch and stale rollouts.

### Why it matters

Practical LLM RL is inherently off-policy (rollout engine ≠ training stack; batches are stale). The paper makes the mask *coherent* with the divergence it defends, and does so with negligible overhead — a clean drop-in for anyone using DPPO-style masks or worrying about PPO clipping in modern async pipelines.

## 5. ReOPD

**Authors:** Baohao Liao, Hanze Dong, Christof Monz, Xinxing Xu, Li Dong, Furu Wei — *Microsoft Research / University of Amsterdam*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.04763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04763) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.04763) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.04763)
**Topics:** `post-training`, `distillation`, `agents`

### TL;DR

**Replayed-Prefix On-Policy Distillation (ReOPD)** speeds up multi-turn on-policy distillation for agentic tasks by reusing pre-collected teacher trajectories as *replayed prefixes*: the student acts at selected steps, the teacher supplies dense per-step supervision from cached rollouts, and no fresh environment interactions are needed during student training.

### Key idea

Multi-turn on-policy distillation faces a **prefix trap** — pushing histories toward the student's distribution makes the prefixes relevant to the student, but drifts them into regions where the teacher's targets are unreliable. ReOPD reframes prefix choice as a *reliability-aware distribution design* and implements it with a step-decaying schedule that emphasizes earlier, lower-shift prefixes.

### Main finding

Matches or improves fully-online OPD accuracy on math-with-Python and search environments, uses **zero tool calls** during student training, and is at least **4× faster per rollout** than OPD. Turns costly agent-environment interaction into a reusable offline resource.

### Why it matters

Distilling long-horizon agents was gated on running the environment for every rollout. ReOPD decouples student training from live environments, letting one expensive teacher-rollout collection amortize across many student runs — a scalable path for cross-tool / cross-task distillation.

## 6. Experience Distillation

**Authors:** Chenhui Gou, Haoqin Tu, Yunhao Fang, Jianfei Cai, Hamid Rezatofighi — *Affiliation not disclosed on HF page*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.21051](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21051) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.21051) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.21051)
**Topics:** `agents`, `distillation`, `post-training`

### TL;DR

An implementation of **Experience Distillation**: keep in-context-learning's environment-sample efficiency, but internalize the trial-and-error trajectory into weights so gains survive after the context is dropped. Requires no additional environment interaction beyond the initial ICL rollouts.

### Key idea

ICL from an agent's own interaction history is highly sample-efficient but ephemeral — remove the context and the improvement vanishes. Experience Distillation is context distillation applied to *interaction histories*: freeze the environment interactions, then distill the ICL policy into the base weights.

### Main finding

Across 749 software-engineering tasks and six text-adventure games, retains at least **64.8%** of the ICL gains after removing context (vs. **3.8%** for direct SFT on the same trajectories). Matches classical RL baselines with at least **9.6× fewer environment samples**.

### Why it matters

Environment interaction is often the binding cost in agent training (long rollouts, expensive tools, human feedback). Experience Distillation gives a cheap route to *durable* learning from a small, expensive interaction budget — a strong complement to on-policy RL, not a replacement.

## 7. OpenForge RL

**Authors:** Xiao Yu, Baolin Peng, Ruize Xu, Hao Zou, Qianhui Wu, Hao Cheng, Wenlin Yao, Nikhil Singh, Zhou Yu, Jianfeng Gao — *Columbia / Dartmouth / Microsoft Research*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.21557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21557) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.21557) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.21557)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `systems`

### TL;DR

An open-source SFT/RL framework that trains **inside real inference harnesses** (Claude Code, Codex, OpenClaw) rather than through re-implemented training-time stubs. The system natively expresses stateful, multi-process harness inference so end-to-end agent training doesn't require a bespoke harness clone in the trainer.

### Key idea

Existing open training stacks flatten multi-turn tool-use into a single-process rollout — losing state, subprocess lifetime, and the harness's own scheduling logic. OpenForge RL treats the production harness as the rollout engine and layers SFT/RL updates on top, so trained agents inherit exactly the interaction pattern they'll be deployed with.

### Main finding

Trains agents end-to-end across diverse environments while keeping harness-native behavior; the paper positions the system as the missing open equivalent of what closed-source frontier labs use to train Claude-Code-class agents.

### Why it matters

The train/deploy gap for agents is often *harness-shaped* — the RL rollouts use a fake harness that doesn't match production. Closing that gap is prerequisite for open reproduction of harness-native agent training, and for research on how harness design interacts with post-training.

## 8. NOOA

**Authors:** Paul Furgale, Severin Klingler, James Nolan, Matt Staats, Gaia Di Lorenzo, Elisa Martinez Abad, Christian Schüller, Razvan Dinu, Alessio Devoto, Pascal Berard, Gal Kaplun, Elad Sarafian, Riccardo Roveri, Leon Derczynski, Ricardo Silveira Cabral — *NVIDIA*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.20709](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20709) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.20709) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.20709)
**Topics:** `agents`

### TL;DR

A model-agnostic Python framework where an **agent is a Python object**: methods become tools, fields become state, docstrings become prompts, type annotations become schema contracts. Evaluated on SWE-bench Verified, Terminal-Bench 2.0, and ARC-AGI-3.

### Key idea

Collapse the four-way split of prompt templates, tool schemas, callback code, and workflow graphs into one Python object. Reflection generates the schema; docstrings supply intent; state lives on the instance — so agent code reads like ordinary Python, and versioning / testing come free from the language.

### Main finding

Competitive results on major coding-agent benchmarks with a substantially simpler API surface than framework-heavy alternatives. Six model-facing design ideas are documented as the paper's contribution beyond the API.

### Why it matters

Framework fragmentation has been a real drag on agent research: prompt code, tool schemas, and orchestration graphs get out of sync. If the OO framing catches on it lowers the cost of one-off agent experiments and may shape how downstream RL/eval scaffolding is structured.

## 9. Evolving User Intent

**Authors:** Jihoon Tack, Philippe Laban, Jennifer Neville — *Microsoft Research*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.20734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20734) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.20734) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.20734)
**Topics:** `eval`, `agents`

### TL;DR

A framework that turns any static single-turn benchmark into a multi-turn evaluation where the user's intent is disclosed incrementally, revised, and sometimes redirected mid-conversation. Existing evaluation protocols are preserved verbatim — no new annotation. Across many tasks and model families, strong static performance does not transfer; substantial drops appear in the evolving-intent setting.

### Key idea

Wrap the original prompt in a scripted user that reveals intent over turns and can *change its mind*. The eval remains the original metric on the same final task, so drops isolate the intent-tracking failure mode rather than a task-difficulty confound.

### Main finding

Consistent, cross-family accuracy drops when static benchmarks are lifted into the evolving-intent regime — including on tasks the same model handles cleanly when the intent is spelled out up front.

### Why it matters

Most agent evals still measure single-turn task performance. This paper documents a *systematic* capability gap invisible to those evals, and gives a re-usable protocol for turning any existing benchmark into an evolving-intent probe.

## 10. WorkBuddy Bench

**Authors:** Siqi Cai, Shaopeng Chen, Xiang Fei, Yong Mao, Zihan Xu, Zhiheng Lyu, Zhijian Shao, Yuchen Shi, Shuwen Zhang, Chaofan Qiu, et al. — *Tencent Youtu Lab / Keen Security Lab / Workbuddy / Yunding Security Lab*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.20911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20911) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.20911) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.20911)
**Topics:** `eval`, `agents`

### TL;DR

A multi-domain coding-agent benchmark whose contamination resistance rests on *task construction*, not secrecy. Every task is reverse-engineered from a real commit / PR / business scenario and rewritten as a short, colloquial, role-played request, so the prompt is not recoverable by searching the underlying issue. Four subsets — repo-level engineering, front-end, office/business workflows, and red-/blue-team security — are packaged in a uniform task-directory format and run on both CodeBuddy Code and Claude Code harnesses.

### Key idea

Contamination-resistance-by-construction: rather than gating the dataset behind an eval server, distance every task's *surface form* from any web-indexable trace. The dataset (tasks, environments, tests, references) ships fully open and is versioned; reproducibility is the retention story, not secrecy.

### Main finding

Cross-model leaderboard across several families. Per-subset scoring instruments differ enough that no single "suite average" is reported — the paper's stance is that meaningful comparison lives inside each subset, not across.

### Why it matters

The benchmark community keeps re-learning that closed test sets leak. WorkBuddy Bench makes the case that surface-form obfuscation + versioning is a more durable contamination story than secrecy, and provides a template for future open-and-honest evals.

## 11. FinanceComplexQA

**Authors:** Not surfaced on HF page — *Affiliation not disclosed*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.19238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19238) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.19238) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.19238)
**Topics:** `eval`, `agents`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

Bilingual (Chinese/English) benchmark of **2,026 expert-level questions over 1,009 industrial-grade financial documents** across nine task categories and eight financial domains. Generated via **Finance-LaTeX**, a multi-agent pipeline that plans evidence, drafts LaTeX documents, and runs LLM / MCP / human verification. Evaluated with an Agent-as-a-Judge protocol covering accuracy, numerical correctness, evidence coverage, faithfulness, and cost/latency.

### Key idea

Two design choices differentiate it from prior finance QA: (1) **dual-context reasoning** — every question requires both explicit document evidence and implicit domain knowledge (margin interpretation, debt pressure, accounting relations); (2) **cross-layout reasoning** — answers force joint reading of prose, tables, charts, and forms preserved as layout, not flattened.

### Main finding

Layout-preserving retrieval (PageIndex) beats lightweight RAG by ~7–10 points; Claude Code with Sonnet 5 tops the leaderboard (76.01% Chinese, 69.39% English) but at 22–50s and 31–46k tokens per question. Numeric drift, evidence omission, and layout confusion are the dominant failure modes across systems.

### Why it matters

Financial reasoning is a natural stress test for agentic systems: high stakes, dense tables, real domain knowledge. FinanceComplexQA gives a reproducible, domain-grounded, cost-annotated eval — including cost/latency as first-class metrics — that other agent benchmarks tend to ignore.

## 12. SpatialGen

**Authors:** Not surfaced on HF page — *Affiliation not disclosed*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.21072](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21072) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.21072) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.21072)
**Topics:** `eval`, `multimodal`, `diffusion`

### TL;DR

Argues that spatial reasoning benchmarks systematically penalize image-generation models by demanding textual/coordinate answers. Introduces **ProVisE** (Protocolized Visual Evaluation), which lets image-generation models answer spatial tasks with *constrained visual outputs* that parse into the original benchmark metrics, and **SpatialGen-Bench** — 470 samples across 14 subtasks organized into four capability levels.

### Key idea

Match the answer interface to the model. When the answer is naturally a pointer, a mask, a depth field, or a drawn path, force text-only outputs and you conflate spatial cognition with translation ability. ProVisE keeps the *task* fixed but swaps in an inspectable-pixel answer that a parser converts back to the benchmark's numerical form.

### Main finding

Complementary strengths surface: image-generation models excel where answers are inspectable pixel states (depth, occupancy); text-output VLMs win when the task requires transformations beyond visible evidence. An agentic protocol-construction step transfers ProVisE across six external spatial benchmarks.

### Why it matters

Cleanly separates "does the model represent space" from "can the model *narrate* space." Puts image generators — often absent from spatial-reasoning leaderboards — into a fair comparison with text-output VLMs.

## 13. K12-KGraph

**Authors:** Qihan Lin, Zhaoyang Han, Xiaochen Ma, Zhen Hao Wong, Meiyi Qiang, Linzhuang Sun, Wentao Zhang — *Peking University / IAAR-Shanghai / OriginHub Technology / Zhongguancun Academy*
**Links:** [arXiv:2605.09635](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09635) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2605.09635) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.09635)
**Topics:** `data`, `eval`

### TL;DR

A curriculum-aligned knowledge graph extracted from Chinese K-12 textbooks (math, physics, chemistry, biology), plus a **23,640-question K12-Bench** probing *curriculum understanding* (not just exam correctness) and a **7,335-sample K12-Train** SFT set. Even Gemini-3-Flash reaches only 57% exact match on K12-Bench; domain-specific supervision from K12-Train beats eight mainstream instruction-tuning datasets at equal budgets.

### Key idea

Existing educational benchmarks test *whether a model answers exam questions*; K12-Bench tests whether it has actually internalized the underlying curriculum graph — concept dependencies, chapter structure, prerequisite ordering — and derives SFT data directly from that graph.

### Main finding

Curriculum-graph-grounded supervision generalizes better *per sample* than generic instruction data — a strong data-quality-over-quantity result for educational domains.

### Why it matters

Points at a broader recipe: build a *structured domain knowledge graph* first, then generate SFT data from it. Applicable well beyond K-12 wherever curricula or concept hierarchies exist.

## 14. GraphVid

**Authors:** Vedant Shah, Onkar Susladkar, Tushar Prakash, Kiet Nguyen, Tianjio Yu, Adheesh Juvekar, Muntasir Waheed, Ismini Lourentzou — *PLAN Lab, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.21580](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21580) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.21580) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.21580)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `multimodal`

### TL;DR

A graph-conditioned image-to-video diffusion model where multi-object interactions are specified as **interaction graphs** (nodes = subjects, edges = relations) instead of drawn trajectories. Trained on **GraphVid-Bench**, a large-scale interaction-centric video dataset with structured relational annotations.

### Key idea

Replace pixel-level trajectory control (fragile under occlusion, tedious for users) with **structured semantic control**: a small interaction graph specifies who does what to whom, letting the model handle the actual pixel motion.

### Main finding

With substantially less training data and fewer trainable parameters than Motion-I2V, GraphVid still reduces FID by up to **39.9%** and FVD by **37.6%**, and improves PSNR from 9.87→15.98 and SSIM from 0.38→0.61. Structured semantic interfaces beat dense motion controls on interaction-heavy scenes.

### Why it matters

Controllable video generation is bottlenecked on user interface as much as on model quality. Graph conditioning offers a cheap, unambiguous specification channel that composes better with LLM-generated intent than trajectory sketches do.
