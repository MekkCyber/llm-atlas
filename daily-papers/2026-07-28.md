## 1. Kimi K3: Open Frontier Intelligence

**Authors:** Kimi Team (380+ contributors) — *Moonshot AI*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.24653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24653) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.24653) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.24653)
**Topics:** `LLM`, `MoE`, `multimodal`, `RL`, `reasoning`, `agents`

### TL;DR

Moonshot's frontier successor to Kimi K2 / k1.5. A 2.8T-parameter Mixture-of-Experts with **104B activated parameters**, **native vision** input, and a **1M-token context window**, positioned as a fully-open competitor to closed frontier systems. The report bundles architecture, pretraining data, post-training RL, and agentic evaluation into one release.

### Key idea

Scale MoE another turn (2.8T total, ~104B active — much sparser than K2 while keeping the active budget high enough for reasoning), fold vision natively into the same backbone rather than bolting on a projection stage, and stretch context to 1M with long-context-aware attention and inference infra. Post-training extends the Kimi long-CoT-RL playbook (introduced in k1.5) to agentic tool-use trajectories.

### Main finding

Frontier-level scores on math, code, and multimodal benchmarks according to the report, with the headline being that a **fully-open** MoE at this scale matches closed systems while remaining serveable via community MoE stacks. Explicit emphasis on cost per token via aggressive expert sparsity.

### Why it matters

If the numbers hold, Kimi K3 is the first *open* release above the 100B-active MoE frontier with native vision + 1M context. It hands the community a checkpoint that stresses every part of the training and serving stack — MoE routing, long-context attention, FP8 training, MTP, agentic RL, and reasoning post-training — and forces closed labs to keep the model-quality margin narrow.

## 2. StateAct: Program State, before Pixels, for Long-Horizon Computer-Use Agents

**Authors:** Yan Yang, Xiangru Jian, Ziyang Luo, Zirui Zhao, Yutong Dai, Ziji Shi, Hanshu Yan, Jun Hao Liew, Silvio Savarese, Junnan Li — *Salesforce AI Research*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.22798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22798) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.22798) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.22798)
**Topics:** `agents`, `computer-use`, `eval`

### TL;DR

A computer-use harness that ditches the "always look at pixels" default. StateAct's main agent works over program state directly through code (files, DOM, app backends), and only spins up a GUI screenshot-and-click subagent for the small minority of subgoals that actually need vision — 28 of 108 tasks and 1.1% of main-agent steps. A separate *finish gate* verifies the saved artifact structurally.

### Key idea

*State-grounding*: treat the underlying program state as the primary channel and pixels as a fallback. Grounding actions and verification in state (rather than screenshots) makes the failure mode "the agent thought wrong" instead of "the agent misread the screen," and shifts the difficulty from perception to reasoning.

### Main finding

On **OSWorld 2.0**, StateAct pushes Claude Opus 4.8 from **20.6% → 26.9%** binary success and **54.8% → 61.6%** partial success, at roughly **9× lower cost per task** than the same model driven by screenshots. A code-only variant with no GUI subagent only reaches 45.9% partial — worse than the screenshot baseline — so the hybrid is essential.

### Why it matters

Computer-use agents have been vision-first by default because the DOM is often not exposed. StateAct argues that when program state *is* reachable (files, application APIs, DOM), it dominates screenshots on both cost and accuracy, and pixels should be reserved for the rare visual-only subgoals. This reframes the computer-use stack around state access rather than model perception.

## 3. The Physics of Multi-Turn Long-Horizon Planning

**Authors:** Tianyi Men, Zhuoran Jin, Kang Liu, Jun Zhao — *Institute of Automation, CAS / UCAS*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.24720](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24720) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.24720) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.24720)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `distillation`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

A controlled sandbox study of *how* multi-turn long-horizon planning ability is acquired, spanning pre-training, SFT, and on-policy agentic distillation with single and multiple teachers. The environment lets the authors dial task length, data quality, planning knowledge, and planning patterns, so they can isolate what each training stage contributes.

### Key idea

Rather than reading planning ability off of Internet-trained models (where confounds pile up), build a controlled multi-turn environment and probe planning as a function of training stage. Introduce **single- and multi-teacher on-policy agentic distillation** as a post-training regime that pushes on-policy student trajectories toward one or several teacher policies.

### Main finding

They report crisp separations between what pre-training vs SFT vs on-policy distillation contribute, and show that multi-teacher agentic distillation stabilizes long-horizon planning across task lengths where single-teacher distillation collapses. (Concrete numbers not in the abstract.)

### Why it matters

Agentic RL / distillation is where the field is trending, but analyses have mostly been end-to-end evaluations on ad-hoc benchmarks. A controlled "physics" study lets us reason about *which* stage to fix when long-horizon planning breaks, and gives a template other groups can reuse.

## 4. Multi-Agent Protocol Distillation in Agentic Search (MAPD)

**Authors:** Jiangwang Chen, Zixin Song, Shuaiyu Zhou, Chunji Lv, Hank Wu, Kailin Jiang, Jinyang Wu, Bohan Yu, Chenxi Zhou — *BIT, ECNU, USTC, Tsinghua, UCAS*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.24280](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24280) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.24280) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.24280)
**Topics:** `agents`, `distillation`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

A distillation recipe that shrinks the gap between closed-source agentic search (deep-research-style tools) and open-source models, without touching teacher logits. MAPD introduces a **style-normalized intermediate protocol** — a structured trace that captures the teacher's tool-use decisions in a canonical form — and trains the student on protocol-level supervision.

### Key idea

The distribution gap between proprietary and open-source agents is largely a *style* gap in how tool calls and reasoning are interleaved. MAPD normalizes trajectories into a shared protocol before matching, sidesteps the need for teacher logits, and turns multi-teacher distillation into a well-posed supervised problem over a structured intermediate representation.

### Main finding

The protocol-distilled open-source student closes much of the agentic-search gap to the proprietary teachers on knowledge-intensive multi-step retrieval tasks, with cost the paper's typical open-source-friendly training pipeline. (Concrete numbers not in the extracted abstract.)

### Why it matters

Directly matching hidden proprietary policies has been the ceiling on open agentic-search quality. MAPD suggests that the informative signal is the *shape of the trace*, not the token-level distribution, which makes closed→open distillation practical when you only have API access to the teacher.

## 5. JarvisHub: An Open Harness for Canvas-Native Multimodal Creative Agents

**Authors:** Yunlong Lin, Zixu Lin, Zhaohu Xing + 20 contributors — *academic consortium (CUHK / Sea AI Lab / …)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.23588](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23588) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.23588) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.23588)
**Topics:** `agents`, `multimodal`, `systems`

### TL;DR

An open agent harness for long-horizon multimodal creative work (images, videos, audio, storyboards, slides) built around a shared **editable canvas** rather than a chat log. The canvas is at once the workspace, the agent's external memory, and the shared action space; multimodal artifacts, versions, dependencies, and human feedback are typed nodes and links.

### Key idea

Three-layer architecture (canvas state → protocol bridge → agent runtime) that treats a canvas as the *primary state representation* for a creative agent. Prompts, drafts, alternatives, tool actions, evaluation signals, and human feedback all live on the canvas as typed nodes, which lets the agent inspect and revise them like any other piece of state.

### Main finding

JarvisHub gives an *inspectable, editable, human-steerable* creative agent — the paper's contribution is the harness itself and the argument that canvas-native state beats chat-native and node-workflow alternatives for long-horizon creative work. No headline benchmark; comparison is qualitative and architectural.

### Why it matters

Commercial creative-agent tools (Sora-style suites, canvas apps, agentic editors) have been closed. JarvisHub gives the research community an *open* target that captures the "long-horizon, mixed-modality, version-aware" workload where chat-based agent harnesses fall over, and makes it possible to study how creative agents represent context.

## 6. Rethinking Classifier-Free Guidance in On-Policy Diffusion Distillation (PDM)

**Authors:** Bingnan Li, Haozhe Wang, Haozhong Xiong, Fangtai Wu, Jinpeng Yu, Yang Shi, Jiaming Liu, Ruihua Huang — *Qwen / Alibaba*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.24731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24731) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.24731) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.24731)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `distillation`

### TL;DR

Diagnoses a failure mode of naive on-policy diffusion distillation under classifier-free guidance (CFG) — the composed guided prediction is **under-identified at the branch level**, so positive- and negative-branch errors compensate. When the teacher's negative branch has privileged conditioning the student can't access, this compensation breaks and the negative-branch error grows. The paper's fix, **Positive-Direction Matching (PDM)**, constrains the positive prediction and the CFG conditional direction separately.

### Key idea

Split the on-policy distillation objective into two branch-aware losses: (1) match the teacher's positive (conditional) prediction directly, (2) match the *direction* of the CFG update (positive minus negative) rather than matching the composed velocity. This eliminates the "positive down, negative up" pathology (Negative Branch Asymmetry, NBA).

### Main finding

On dense-to-sparse video control, naive guided matching is highly sensitive to inference guidance scales; PDM is robust across scales and transfers knowledge from teacher to student more effectively. Concrete quality-scale curves are in the paper.

### Why it matters

On-policy distillation is the dominant recipe for producing few-step diffusion students from strong teachers. CFG asymmetries have been an unlabeled source of instability. Naming and fixing NBA gives practitioners a debuggable knob, and the fix is a small change to the loss rather than a new architecture.

## 7. Sol-Attn: On-the-Fly Attention Sparsification for Video Generation

**Authors:** Haopeng Li, Yitong Li, Junsong Chen, Tian Ye, Haozhe Liu, Jincheng Yu, Duomin Wang, Ruihua Zhang, Zeke Xie, Enze Xie, Song Han — *HKUST(GZ) / MIT / NVIDIA (implied by co-authors)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.24027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24027) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.24027) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.24027)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `efficient-inference`, `video`

### TL;DR

Attention is the dominant inference cost in diffusion transformers for video, because token sequences get long. Sol-Attn does **on-the-fly attention sparsification**: at each step it thresholds attention blocks dynamically (no offline calibration, no per-model training) and skips the sub-threshold blocks. End-to-end **2.1× / 2.3×** speedups for video generation / editing while preserving visual quality.

### Key idea

Block-level attention thresholding that adapts per-step and per-layer, so the sparsity pattern reflects the *current* query/key structure rather than a static calibrated mask. This avoids the retrain-or-calibrate step other sparse-attention methods need, and slots into an existing DiT video pipeline unchanged.

### Main finding

**2.1× end-to-end video-generation speedup** and **2.3× editing speedup**, with visual quality preserved on standard prompts. No fine-tuning of the base model; no calibration set.

### Why it matters

Video-DiT inference is the current bottleneck for shipping generative video products. A drop-in, training-free 2× brings serving cost in line with image generation and makes iterative video editing far cheaper. Sits alongside vLLM's paged-attention / speculative-decoding ideas but for the *generative* diffusion side rather than autoregressive LLMs.

## 8. OmniVAE: Audio-Video VAE with Cross-Modal Alignment

**Authors:** Jun Zhan, Chen Yang, Yitian Gong, Donghua Yu, Kuangwei Chen, Wenbo Zhang, Kexin Huang, Qi Luo, Zhe Xu, Ying Zhu, + 15 others — *affiliations not disclosed on HF page*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.23855](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23855) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.23855) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.23855)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `diffusion`, `audio`

### TL;DR

A jointly-trained audio-video VAE that produces *aligned* latent spaces for the two modalities, so a downstream generative model doesn't have to learn cross-modal sync from scratch. Beyond reconstruction, OmniVAE adds a **segment-level audio-video contrastive loss** and distills features from pretrained per-modality semantic encoders into each latent stream.

### Key idea

Two auxiliary objectives on top of the standard VAE reconstruction loss: (1) segment-level contrastive alignment that pulls temporally-aligned audio and video segments together in latent space, (2) feature distillation from strong per-modality encoders so each latent stream inherits their downstream learnability. The result is a shared latent geometry that behaves as an audio-video *joint* prior for text-to-A/V models.

### Main finding

Both auxiliary objectives independently improve latent learnability, and the combined recipe translates into **higher generation quality and more accurate cross-modal synchronization** on downstream text-to-audio-video. (Specific FVD/CLIP-style numbers in the paper.)

### Why it matters

Audio-video generation has been the *last-mile* problem for generative video systems, gated by cross-modal sync. Building the alignment into the VAE — rather than asking a downstream diffusion model to learn it end-to-end — is a much cleaner factoring, and mirrors the "align first, generate second" pattern that made CLIP-conditioned diffusion work.

## 9. dRAE: Representation Autoencoder with Hyper-Spherical Codes

**Authors:** Tianren Ma, Lin Long, Chuyan Chen, Mu Zhang, Junbo Zhao, Tong Zhang, Qixiang Ye — *UCAS / Zhejiang / PKU / Ant Group*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.22148](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22148) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.22148) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.22148)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `tokenization`, `quantization`

### TL;DR

Discrete visual tokens that don't collapse. dRAE places codewords on a **hypersphere** (constrained-norm codes) and reports **100% codebook utilization** while scaling the vocabulary to **131,072 tokens**, matching or beating VQ / FSQ-family tokenizers at both understanding and generation tasks.

### Key idea

Replace unconstrained Euclidean codebook vectors with codewords lying on a unit hypersphere, which turns quantization into an angular lookup and removes the "unused low-norm regions" that drive VQ codebook collapse. Combine with a simplified training pipeline (no perplexity boosters, no ad hoc dead-code resets).

### Main finding

100% utilization at up to 131K codes, monotone improvement of downstream understanding + generation quality as vocabulary grows, and consistent gains over standard VQ / FSQ tokenizers at the same code count.

### Why it matters

Visual tokenizers are one of the few remaining serious weaknesses in unified multimodal LLMs — codebook collapse forces designers to either use tiny vocabularies or hack the training. Hyper-spherical codes give a clean geometric reason for utilization, and 131K tokens is enough that you can start thinking about vision codes as first-class "words" alongside a language tokenizer.

## 10. WorldDiT: Unified Diffusion Architecture for World and Action Modeling

**Authors:** Bagel team — *contact research@bagel.com; full author list not disclosed*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.23909](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23909) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.23909) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.23909)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `agents`, `world-models`

### TL;DR

A single diffusion transformer that both **predicts continuous action chunks** and **models future camera-frame pixels**, without a large pretrained VLM as the action backbone. On four LIBERO suites it lands on the reported Pareto frontier of total parameters vs mean success — a strong sub-billion baseline for scaling studies.

### Key idea

One DiT jointly denoises (a) an action chunk (continuous action tokens over the next K steps) and (b) normalized RGB patch targets for future frames. Coupling action generation and visual world modeling in one network — rather than routing actions through a separately-trained VLM — closes the modality gap and gives a compact, unified target for training.

### Main finding

On LIBERO Long / Spatial / Object / Goal, WorldDiT reaches the reported Pareto frontier for parameter count vs mean success, with all four suites reported (many strong baselines only report a subset). Sub-billion parameters.

### Why it matters

The dominant recipe for VLA has been "big VLM + light action head." WorldDiT argues that a unified diffusion transformer, sized for the actual action-and-frame problem, is a serious alternative — cheap enough to iterate on, and strong enough to be the baseline other groups compare to.

## 11. UltraViT: Latency-Optimized On-device Vision Encoder for LVLMs

**Authors:** Adrian Bulat, Alberto Baldrati, Anestis Zaganidis, Yassine Ouali, Hyeonuk Kim, Georgios Tzimiropoulos — *Samsung AI Cambridge / TU Iași / QMUL*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.23373](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23373) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.23373) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.23373)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

A vision encoder for LVLMs designed against **real on-device latency** rather than FLOPs. Pyramidal architecture with heterogeneous spatial mixers picked at the macro-block level, plus a two-stage generative pretraining recipe (dense distillation, then supervision from a capacity-mixed frozen LLM). Roughly **1.7× faster** than SOTA baselines while matching or beating them on downstream LVLM eval.

### Key idea

Design the encoder against actual measured device latency (not GFLOP proxies), and use *generative* pretraining — direct next-token supervision from a frozen LLM — instead of contrastive / SSL, because the encoder's real downstream job is to feed an LLM, not to align to text.

### Main finding

**~1.7× on-device speedup** vs the strongest encoder-centric baselines while establishing new SOTA for efficient LVLM encoding on their eval suite.

### Why it matters

On-device multimodal models are gated by the encoder, not the LLM (which is usually a lightweight decoder). UltraViT makes explicit that the encoder's pretraining objective should match its deployment objective — generative supervision from a frozen LLM is far more useful than a general CLIP embedding when the encoder will only ever feed one.

### Knowledge graph integration

## 12. TILT: Improving Compositional Generation in Diffusion Models with a Model-Intrinsic Reward

**Authors:** Anonymous / affiliation not disclosed on HF page
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.21606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21606) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.21606) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.21606)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `reward-alignment`

### TL;DR

A training-free method for improving compositional prompt-following in diffusion models. TILT defines a **model-intrinsic reward** — no external classifier or reward model — that favors samples where all requested concepts jointly appear, and steers sampling toward a **KL-constrained tilted target distribution** with a closed-form update.

### Key idea

Frame compositional failures as *overlap-mode failures* between joint and single-concept distributions in the model's own likelihood. Define a reward from those internal distributions, plug it into a KL-constrained tilted target, and solve for principled guidance steps at sampling time — combining two natural guidance strategies with a hybrid that balances them.

### Main finding

On T2I-CompBench prompts, TILT improves compositional alignment while preserving image quality, versus prior training-free compositional-guidance methods. Because the reward is intrinsic, there's no external reward model to train or maintain.

### Why it matters

Compositional prompts ("a red cube on top of a blue cylinder next to a green sphere") are one of the most visible weaknesses of modern diffusion. Test-time reward alignment usually needs an external classifier or reward model; showing the base model *already contains* the signal is both cheaper and less brittle.

## 13. Reasoning Denoiser (ReDe): Denoising Reasoning Traces for Hallucination Detection

**Authors:** Junlin Fang, Do Nguyen-Thanh, Xiaogang Xu, Zhen Fang, Sean Du — *NTU / Zhejiang / UTS*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.22098](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22098) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.22098) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.22098)
**Topics:** `reasoning`, `safety`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Long reasoning traces carry signal about whether the model is hallucinating, but they also contain *irrelevant* and *repetitive* steps that drown the signal. ReDe uses **final-answer attention as automatic supervision** to shape step-level representations, so noisy steps become identifiable and can be filtered before feeding the trace into a hallucination detector.

### Key idea

Treat the model's own attention over the final-answer tokens as a self-supervision signal for step relevance. Learn a step-level embedding space in which "noisy" steps (irrelevant / repetitive) separate from informative ones, then drop the noisy steps before running any downstream hallucination detector.

### Main finding

Across multiple reasoning benchmarks, plugging ReDe in front of existing detectors consistently improves detection performance over competitive baselines (confidence-based scores, naive embedding filters).

### Why it matters

As long-CoT models become the default, hallucination detection has to work over increasingly long, noisy traces. A cheap denoising front-end is a very natural piece of the reasoning-monitoring stack, adjacent to CoT-monitoring safety work but pointed at hallucination rather than misuse.

## 14. Codifying the Judge: Scalable Evaluation via Program Distillation (PAJAMA)

**Authors:** Tong Huang, Siyu Qiu, et al. — *University of Wisconsin–Madison*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.22561](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22561) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.22561) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.22561)
**Topics:** `eval`, `LLM-judge`, `rewards`

### TL;DR

Distill an LLM-judge into a **committee of programs** that score candidates directly. Programmatic judges are transparent, inspectable, and free at inference; PAJAMA aggregates their verdicts, escalates low-confidence cases to an LLM, and treats the committee's outputs as a source of cheap reward signal for training.

### Key idea

Replace the per-sample LLM-judge call with a *synthesized program* per rubric axis. A program is deterministic, editable, and API-free. Aggregate a committee of programs into a joint verdict; when confidence is low, fall back to an LLM. As a bonus, distilling a reward model from program verdicts is much cheaper than distilling from proprietary LLM labels.

### Main finding

Programmatic judges **match a 13B LLM judge** across five datasets and four model families; using program outputs as routing signals improves both accuracy and throughput and advances the Pareto frontier. On **RewardBench**, a reward model trained on the programs' verdicts beats one trained on a proprietary LLM's labels at **two orders of magnitude lower API cost**.

### Why it matters

LLM-as-judge is the current default but is expensive, slow, and opaque. Program distillation gives an editable, cheap, fast substitute for the majority of cases, and cracks the "LLM-judge tax" that has been driving up eval costs. Also plugs directly into RLHF-style reward training as a cheap replacement for proprietary preference labels.

## 15. DecoupleMix: Decoupled Ratio Search and Convex Allocation for VLM Data Recipes

**Authors:** Jiahao Xie, Zhongbin Guo, Qianle Wang, Ruiqi Lu, Dongling Xiao, Wanxuan Sun, Cheng Yang — *ByteDance*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.24516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24516) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.24516) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.24516)
**Topics:** `data`, `multimodal`

### TL;DR

Turns VLM data-mixture construction from a heuristic art into a two-stage optimization problem: (1) a single-variable iterative search for **inter-class ratios across capabilities**, (2) a constrained convex optimization with a diversity objective for **intra-class dataset selection**, scored by per-dataset Quality and Difficulty.

### Key idea

Decompose the mixture into inter-class and intra-class sub-problems and solve each with the right tool: line-search where you have one degree of freedom, convex optimization where you have many. Ratios optimized on small proxies **transfer to larger scales without retuning**, which is the property that makes mixture optimization actually practical.

### Main finding

Consistently beats heuristic baselines, transfers ratios from proxies to larger runs, and — with **80B additional multimodal continue-pretraining tokens** — matches strong open-source VLMs trained with substantially larger multimodal budgets.

### Why it matters

VLM data curation has largely been "add the datasets that pass the quality filter and hope." DecoupleMix gives a principled, auditable process for admitting new data and validating claims about it, and — critically — a proxy-to-scale transfer property that saves compute during recipe development.

## 16. FilmBench: A Film-Grade Benchmark for Cinematic Video Generation

**Authors:** Shengyi Wang, Niantong Li, Guangzheng Hu, et al. (30 authors) — *Alibaba / Moku Lab (Hujing Digital) / Beijing Film Academy*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.24241](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24241) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.24241) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.24241)
**Topics:** `eval`, `diffusion`, `video`

### TL;DR

A T2V / R2V benchmark grounded in professional Cinematic Language rather than web prompts and generic image-quality scores. Prompts are reverse-engineered from clips of award-winning films across 20 genres (1,056 of 1,169 prompts are **multi-shot**), the evaluation taxonomy has **3 axes → 12 components → 35 sub-metrics**, and an in-house expert-grade automatic evaluator (FilmOps) reproduces the human ranking at **Spearman ρ = 0.95 (T2V) / 0.96 (R2V)**.

### Key idea

Design the benchmark around what film school teaches, not what web-scraped prompts happen to be: reverse-engineer prompts from real award-winning film clips (with directors and film-academy faculty), align evaluation to professional cinematic axes, and open-source the "Cinematic Language operators" (FilmOps) so the community can plug them into their own evaluators.

### Main finding

Leading T2V (9 models) and R2V (7 models) systems **score well below prior web-style benchmarks**, with two consistent gaps in dynamic aesthetics and a marked drop from single-shot to multi-shot performance that widens for weaker models. The automatic evaluator ranks models like human experts do.

### Why it matters

Video-gen benchmarks have been running out of headroom while professional users still find the outputs unusable for real work. FilmBench reintroduces headroom by shifting the eval axis to *cinematic craft*, and its multi-shot emphasis reflects how film actually works — a much harder target than isolated 5-second clips.
