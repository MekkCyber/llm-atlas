## 1. TurboVLA

**Authors:** Hengyi Xie, Chenfei Yao, Xianjin Wu, Xuanyang Xi, Yiping Tang, Di Xu, Yingying Zhu, Dingkang Liang, Xiang Bai, Han Ding — *Huazhong U. of Science and Technology / Huawei*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.27205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27205) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.27205) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.27205)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `VLA`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

A vision-language-action model that ditches the "shove pixels through an LLM" recipe. Vision and language are encoded independently and cross-talk through a lightweight bidirectional interaction module; a compact decoder emits continuous action chunks. Result: a 0.2B model that runs at 32 Hz on a single RTX 4090 with <1 GB VRAM.

### Key idea

Replace the standard V→L→A pathway (project pixels into the LLM's residual stream, decode actions from LLM hidden states) with a direct V+L→A mapping. Independent encoders for vision and language + bidirectional lightweight fusion + a compact action-chunk decoder — no LLM in the hot loop.

### Main finding

97.7% average success on LIBERO with 0.2B params, 31.2 ms latency, 0.9 GB VRAM on a consumer 4090. Matches or beats VLA policies at several times its size.

### Why it matters

The dominant VLA design pays LLM-inference tax on every control step. TurboVLA shows the LLM was optional as a fusion mechanism — you can get task-conditioned action representations from a much lighter fusion module. Opens VLA deployment to consumer hardware and cheaper edge robots.

## 2. CoRT

**Authors:** Bo-Wen Zhang, Junwei He, Wen Wang, Song-Lin Lv, Wentao Ma, Rongyi Lin, Shuhan Zhong, Lan-Zhe Guo — *Nanjing University consortium*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.25659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25659) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.25659) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.25659)
**Topics:** `RL`, `GRPO`, `credit-assignment`

### TL;DR

GRPO's advantage is broadcast uniformly across every token in a response, even when different rubric criteria target different spans. CoRT rescores the same response under the original rubric prompt and a matched criteria-free prompt; the token-wise log-likelihood contrast becomes a proxy for how much each token depends on the rubric context, which is then used to reweight the signed GRPO advantage.

### Key idea

**Counterfactual replay for token-level credit.** No auxiliary scorer, no separate relevance-learning stage. Just two forward passes on the sampled response — one under the rubric-conditioned prompt, one under a criteria-free variant — and use the per-token Δlog-p as the credit weight. Weights are bounded and response-normalized so the total advantage is preserved.

### Main finding

+4.4 pp average over response-level GRPO across instruction-tuned models and reward granularities; competitive with learned token-level baselines without adding a scorer.

### Why it matters

Rubric-based RL is becoming a default for open-ended tasks (instruction following, writing, safety judgments), but GRPO's flat credit assignment leaves free performance on the table. CoRT gets meaningful token-level credit "for free" from the policy itself. Cleaner than PRM-style scorer training and cheaper than actor-critic.

## 3. HumanCLAW

**Authors:** Jiawei Gu, Shuai Liu, Kairui Hu, Zekun Li, Linjie Li, Chengcheng Tang, Po-Chen Wu, Ivan Shugurov, Lingni Ma, Michael Zollhoefer, Sizhe An, Abhay Mittal, Amy Zhao, Ranjay Krishna, Manling Li, Ziwei Liu, Chuan Guo — *Meta / NTU / UW / Brown / Northwestern*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.27180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27180) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.27180) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.27180)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `eval`, `agents`

### TL;DR

An evaluation framework that separates "what a VLM decides to do" from "whether the low-level controller can execute it." A harnessed VLM issues atomic skill commands; each is expanded into a sub-second full-body motion chunk with real physics. What's measured is the model's moment-to-moment decisions, not motor control noise. Best of 9 SoTA VLMs scores 16.8% on 1,218 long-horizon egocentric episodes.

### Key idea

Decouple decision from execution by giving the VLM a "skill vocabulary" that a physics-aware controller translates into embodied motion. VLM failures are then attributable to decision-making, not tracking loss.

### Main finding

None of nine frontier VLMs breaks 17% success on HumanCLAW-Bench. The failure mode isn't perception ("recognize the target") but **embodied self-awareness** — losing track of one's own body location, whether the goal is reached, whether an obstacle was hit.

### Why it matters

Reframes what's holding back embodied VLM agents. Recognition benchmarks lied by omission — models look far more capable than they are once low-level motor noise stops hiding decision errors. Suggests future embodied training needs proprioceptive/state-tracking signals, not just more perception data.

## 4. DecoEvo

**Authors:** Zixin Song, Junlin Liu, Shuaiyu Zhou, Haiyan Wu, Haihan Shi, Chenxi Zhou, Hanqing Li, Xiao Yang, Da Zhu, Guanjun Jiang, Hai Wan, Xibin Zhao — *Tsinghua / UCAS / PKU / Qwen (Alibaba)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.25675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25675) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.25675) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.25675)
**Topics:** `RL`, `text-space-opt`, `rubrics`

### TL;DR

Text-space optimization (editing external prompts/rubrics rather than weights) breaks when the rubric is fixed *and* breaks differently when the rubric is co-evolved against the solver's own score (the rubric just gets easier). DecoEvo co-evolves solver and rubric-generator skills under **decoupled** objectives: solver gets criterion-level feedback; rubric-generator gets scored on coverage-of-requirements and response-discrimination, both independent of aggregate solver score.

### Key idea

Break the "score → rubric update → better score" feedback loop by evaluating the rubric on properties orthogonal to the solver's current performance. Rubric edits are rewarded for exposing solver weaknesses, not for producing higher solver numbers.

### Main finding

2.8–5.0% relative avg gain over SkillOpt across five benchmarks and three LLM backbones under each benchmark's official evaluation.

### Why it matters

Text-space optimization is a serious alternative to weight training for inspection-required domains — but the "just evolve the rubric" temptation is a scoring-loop failure mode. DecoEvo shows a principled fix: keep evaluator updates on objectives the solver can't game.

## 5. CLBench-V

**Authors:** Lai Wei, Chengqi Li, Jiapeng Li, Ruina Hu, Yue Wang, Weiran Huang — *Shanghai Jiao Tong U. / Zhongguancun Academy / Shanghai Innovation Institute*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.25294](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25294) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.25294) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.25294)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `eval`, `in-context`

### TL;DR

Benchmark for **multimodal in-context learning** decomposed along three axes: context grounding, new-information application, and new-knowledge learning. 3,443 instances across science, finance, long-document, spatial reasoning, and web QA. Best of six recent MLLMs manages 0.2847 overall.

### Key idea

Localize *where* context use breaks down by splitting the ability into three ordered dimensions rather than a single QA score. Uses automated construction + filtering for the newly built portions to keep authoring cost tractable.

### Main finding

Nothing is close to solved. InternVL3.5-30B-A3B leads on grounding + knowledge learning; Qwen3.5-Plus leads on new-information application — no single model dominates. Analysis of judge reliability, context length, and image count suggests failure modes are structural, not just capacity-limited.

### Why it matters

MLLM in-context learning is being treated as "give it images in the prompt and hope" — this benchmark makes the sub-skills legible, which is what the field needs before targeted training data can be built.

## 6. CAST

**Authors:** Yu Wang, Yi-Kai Zhang, Wentao Shi, Ziang Ye, Yuchun Miao, Yueqing Sun, Qi Gu, Xunliang Cai, Lan-Zhe Guo, Han-Jia Ye, Fuli Feng — *USTC / Nanjing U. / Wuhan U. / Meituan*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.25308](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25308) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.25308) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.25308)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `distillation`

### TL;DR

RLVR-trained game agents suffer sparse terminal rewards — nothing tells them which move was the pivotal one. CAST plugs in a game solver (Sokoban / Minesweeper / Rush Hour) as a value oracle: the change in solver value between consecutive states becomes a turn-level advantage the RLVR loop consumes as extra credit. Under a soft-optimal solver assumption, maximizing that solver advantage is equivalent to on-policy distillation from the solver — but requires only scalar values, not teacher logits.

### Key idea

**Solver-value change as turn-level advantage.** Beats PRM/ORM-style value learning by using a domain solver that already knows the value function. The soft-optimal-solver ↔ on-policy-distillation equivalence is what makes it a *training* signal, not just a heuristic reward.

### Main finding

SoTA on Sokoban, Minesweeper, and Rush Hour under in-domain and unseen-difficulty splits. Zero-shot transfer: best average on ALFWorld and WebShop despite training only on the three games.

### Why it matters

Whenever a task has a strong external solver but the LLM needs to learn to *interact*, this shape of teacher recovers dense credit without the usual PRM training burden. Likely template for coding-with-verifiers, math-with-Lean, tool-use-with-simulators.

- [../post-training/reasoning/prm.md](../post-training/reasoning/prm.md) — solver-value replaces a learned PRM.

## 7. SkillRise

**Authors:** Zhiyuan Yao, Yuxin Chen, Zhengxi Lu, Zishan Xu, Yueqing Sun, Yifu Guo, Yuquan Lu, Zhengzhou Cai, Kangning Zhang, Zhuowen Han, Zihan Wang, Ziang Ye, Qi Gu, Xunliang Cai, Weiwen Liu, Yongliang Shen — *Zhejiang U. / NUS / SJTU / Meituan*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.26784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26784) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.26784) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.26784)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `skills`

### TL;DR

Agentic RL usually treats tasks as IID episodes. SkillRise organizes related instances into progressively challenging sequences and has one policy alternate between (a) solving a task and (b) curating a skill document passed to the next task. Credit is decoupled — solving gets current-task outcome, curation gets discounted downstream outcomes.

### Key idea

**Cross-task skill curation as a policy action.** One agent, one document, and two objectives: solve, then leave the shed better than you found it. Downstream-discounted return trains the curation half without needing a separate skill-extractor.

### Main finding

+2.3 to +8.5 pp Pass@1 over the strongest baseline on ALFWorld, WebShop, ScienceWorld. Curation trained across distinct tasks also helps repeated attempts on the same task (transfer both ways). Test-time performance scales with sequence length even under one-attempt-per-task.

### Why it matters

Points at agent RL that *accumulates* rather than resetting each episode, without the complexity of multi-stage skill-extraction pipelines. Simpler, faster, and shows genuine skill transfer rather than same-task rehearsal.

## 8. MindForge

**Authors:** Yihao Chen, Shi Chang, Khaled Chawa, Feng Lin, Boyuan Chen, Shaowei Wang, Ahmed E. Hassan — *Queen's University / Concordia / Manitoba (based on Hassan / Wang / Chen affiliations)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.27146](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27146) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.27146) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.27146)
**Topics:** `agents`, `SE`, `distillation`

### TL;DR

Frontier coding agents solve <1% of ProgramBench (build-from-scratch SE tasks). MindForge auto-converts open-source CLI programs into **source-free environments** — the agent sees only a compiled reference executable and its docs — then generates program-synthesis trajectories with GLM-5.2 as teacher. Fine-tuning Qwen3.6-27B on the traces lifts ProgramBench from 37.98% → 49.51% and improves on all seven unseen SE benchmarks.

### Key idea

**Source-free environments as scalable SE training data.** The reference binary + docs alone provide a behavioral oracle without leaking implementation, so the agent must actually *engineer*. Enables cheap environment generation from OSS repos disjoint from the eval set.

### Main finding

Fine-tuned Qwen3.6-27B: matches substantially larger frontier models on ProgramBench and posts absolute gains of +31.0 on RepoZero-C2Rust, +14.2 on DeepSWE, +10.7/+4.6 on NL2Repo-Bench, and 5–6 pp on SWE-bench Verified / Pro / Multilingual.

### Why it matters

Answers "where does long-horizon SE training data come from" with a scalable pipeline. Reference-binary supervision generalizes across the SE life cycle (not just bug-fix) and doesn't depend on hand-curated tasks.

## 9. SpecFirst

**Authors:** Yihao Chen, Shi Chang, Khaled Chawa, Feng Lin, Boyuan Chen, Shaowei Wang, Ahmed E. Hassan — *(same team as MindForge)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.27167](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27167) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.27167) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.27167)
**Topics:** `agents`, `SE`

### TL;DR

Companion to MindForge. Argues coding-agent pipelines conflate "read the docs", "probe the binary", and "write the code" — so agents under-probe and let early misreads propagate. SpecFirst forces a two-stage loop: a **spec agent** probes the binary and emits a structured behavioral specification; a **code-synthesis agent** then implements against that spec.

### Key idea

Requirements-engineering-as-a-phase inside an agent scaffold. The spec is a stable behavioral reference the coder can consult without re-deriving from raw traces.

### Main finding

+6.9 to +21.3 pp test-pass on all 200 ProgramBench instances across four models and two families. Binary-exploration coverage up 9.4–18.5 pp. Behavioral analysis shows coding starts earlier and is more sustained.

### Why it matters

Suggests agent scaffolds should mirror the SE lifecycle explicitly rather than hoping one-loop planning covers it. The spec artifact is inspectable and reusable — a natural handoff point for tool-augmented review or multi-agent workflows.

**Builds on / relates to existing pages:**

## 10. Open-Ended AI Research

**Authors:** Peter Kirgis, Sayash Kapoor, Andrew Schwartz, Stephan Rabanser, David Africa, Konstantinos Voudouris, Viet Nguyen, Toby Pilditch, Magda Dubois, Harry Coppock, Cozmin Ududec, Nitya Nadgir, Matilda Orona, Tilman Bayer, Derrick Chan-Sew, Yue Ling, Abhishek Shetty, Helen Toner, Gillian Hadfield, Seth Lazar, Steve Newman, Shoshannah Tekofsky, +2 — *METR / Cambridge / consortium*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.27191](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27191) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.27191) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.27191)
**Topics:** `agents`, `eval`, `AI-R&D`

### TL;DR

Introduces **shadow evaluations**: give an agent the central open-ended research question from an unpublished paper, let it work for six days with thousands of dollars of compute, then have the paper's actual authors grade the output. Ran on two unpublished NeurIPS 2026 submissions. Agents finished all the engineering unaided, but both papers were rejected by their own authors as "no substantive progress on the research question."

### Key idea

Break the false dichotomy between narrow verifiable benchmarks (which exclude open-ended research) and blind peer review (which is overstretched and noisy). Author-graded shadow evals give ground-truth authorial judgment on real papers.

### Main finding

Frontier agents can execute AI-R&D engineering end-to-end but fail on judgment (what's publishable), creativity (redesign around a broken hypothesis), backtracking (dead-end escape), resource awareness, and instruction drift. Robustness check with a second model + scaffold reproduces the same failures.

### Why it matters

The strongest empirical counterweight yet to explosive-AI-R&D-progress forecasts. Also gives the field a reusable evaluation shape — expert-authored, unpublished, open-ended, adversarial to model memorization.

## 12. Memory for LLMs

**Authors:** Sining Zhoubian, Dan Zhang, Evgeny Kharlamov, Jie Tang — *Tsinghua / NUS / Bosch AI*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.25380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25380) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.25380) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.25380)
**Topics:** `survey`, `architectures`, `memory`

### TL;DR

Survey of memory in LLMs along three orthogonal axes: **representation** (implicit vs explicit), **update dynamics** (offline vs online), and **persistence** (short-term vs long-term). Formalizes write / route / state-transition / consolidation mechanisms and reviews hybrid architectures.

### Key idea

Architecture-centric axes rather than product categories (RAG vs KV cache vs finetuning). Makes it possible to see e.g. RAG and SSM state as points in the same space — one is offline-explicit-long, the other online-implicit-short.

### Main finding

Not a numeric result — the contribution is the taxonomy plus a review of hybrid designs and system-level efficiency tradeoffs. Also proposes multi-dimensional eval principles.

### Why it matters

Memory papers land in many disconnected literatures (retrieval, state-space models, KV cache, LoRA, agent memory). This gives everyone a shared vocabulary. Good candidate to anchor an llm-atlas memory taxonomy across `architectures/`, `inference/`, and `agents/`.

### Knowledge graph integration

## 14. CADENCE

**Authors:** *(author list not exposed by HF's page)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.16955](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16955) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.16955) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.16955)
**Topics:** `reasoning`, `distillation`, `KL`

### TL;DR

On-policy distillation of reasoning from a larger teacher into a compact student, fixing three named failure modes: cold-start collapse, state-agnostic KL scheduling, and binary reward sparsity. CADENCE stacks six components — DRIFT (per-token forward-KL / reverse-KL mixture), COVA (coverage-adaptive schedule), FTB (forking-token boost), CCD (dense proximity rewards), LAP (brevity-preferential reinforcement), EMR (entropy-matching calibration), BSD (bootstrapped self-distillation).

### Key idea

**Per-token convex KL surrogate.** Forward-KL and reverse-KL are stacked with a state-dependent weight, so mode-seeking and mode-covering can each dominate where they should. Everything else — coverage-adaptive schedule, forking-token boost, brevity, entropy calibration, self-distillation — is a targeted patch for a specific pathology of on-policy distillation.

### Main finding

Distills a 0.5B student to 69.8% GSM8K pass@1 from a 1.5B teacher (closing 63.2% of the gap) and to 72.1% with a 3B teacher. +4.4 pp over matched-compute baselines on GSM8K and MATH-500. Runs on a single Apple Mac Studio.

### Why it matters

Reasoning-distillation results usually plateau because generic KL doesn't respect where the student and teacher genuinely disagree. Per-token-scheduled KL is the right conceptual object. The Mac-Studio-scale result argues small-model reasoning is now bandwidth-limited on training-signal *shape*, not compute.

## 15. StealthBench

**Authors:** Adrian Wood — *Independent security researcher*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.26314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26314) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.26314) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.26314)
**Topics:** `safety`, `agents`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Benchmark for **operational stealth** in autonomous offensive-security agents. 11 hand-verified OPSEC incidents from real bug-bounty / red-team trajectories, expanded into 14 dockerized scenarios where agents *do* find real vulnerabilities but blow their cover — leaking credentials in public uploads, deleting production resources to prove access, force-adding uninvolved users. Scored by a 3-LLM judge panel via Safe Success Rate, Stealth@Solve, and Reckless Solve Rate.

*Borderline: included because agent offensive-security safety is a dual-use eval that squarely fits the safety folder.*

### Key idea

**Task success ≠ safe success.** Agents that finish an offensive task but leave loud fingerprints are dangerous, not competent. Multi-dimensional OPSEC scoring (6 dimensions) forces the question separately from task completion.

### Main finding

No model exceeds 54% Safe Success Rate. OPSEC failures are systematic across model families — not a per-model quirk.

### Why it matters

As offensive-security agents proliferate on both sides (blue-team automation, red-team automation), stealth becomes a first-order safety property. Also a template for other domains where "safe success" splits cleanly from raw task success (medical, legal, ops).
