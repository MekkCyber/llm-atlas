## 1. Training Long-Horizon Search Agents via Answer-Backtracked Credit Assignment (ABC) {#1-abc}

**Authors:** Yijun Lu, Rui Ye, Jiajun Wang, Yuwen Du, Tian Jin, Songhua Liu, Siheng Chen — *Shanghai Jiao Tong University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.05102](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05102) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05102) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.05102)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `credit-assignment`

### TL;DR

Long-horizon web/search agents get one reward at the end of a trajectory, so SFT and RL treat every step (search, retrieve, verify, integrate) the same — useful and useless actions get identical gradients. ABC replaces the sparse trajectory-level signal with dense per-step supervision by backtracking from the final answer to score which intermediate actions actually helped, rewarding useful steps even inside *failed* trajectories.

### Key idea

Answer-Backtracked Credit Assignment: after a rollout, use the final answer as a probe to re-score each step's contribution — was the retrieved evidence used, did the verification catch anything, did integration re-use earlier findings. That per-step credit becomes the SFT weight / RL advantage, converting outcome-only rewards into token/action-level signal without a learned value function.

### Main finding

Substantially better long-horizon search accuracy than uniform SFT and outcome-only RL baselines. The framework rescues gradient from otherwise-wasted failed trajectories, improving sample efficiency for expensive multi-step rollouts.

### Why it matters

Agent RL keeps hitting the same wall: sparse outcome rewards + long trajectories = terrible credit assignment. Answer-backtracking is a cheap, verifier-free alternative to process reward models — it uses the trajectory itself as the annotator and works whenever the answer is checkable.

## 2. ToolArtist: Tool-Using Unified Multimodal Models for Agentic Image Generation {#2-toolartist}

**Authors:** Xiaomin Yu, Zhongxiang Sun, Fengwei Teng, Chengwei Qin, Xiaobin Hu, Jun Xu, Shuicheng Yan — *RUC, HKUST(GZ), NUS, UCD*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.04436](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04436) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.04436) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.04436)
**Topics:** `agents`, `multimodal`, `RL`, `image-gen`

### TL;DR

Post-trains a Unified Multimodal Model into an image-generation *agent*: one policy interleaves reasoning, external tool calls (search), and native image generation. Training is SFT on teacher trajectories (with the image-tool concealed but its outputs kept) followed by Reason-Act-Draw GRPO, a GRPO variant with joint intent- and quality-rewards.

### Key idea

RAD-GRPO — GRPO adapted so a single policy emits reasoning tokens, tool calls, and native image tokens, and the reward decomposes into (a) *intent* fidelity to the user request and (b) image *quality*. Concealing the image-generation tool during SFT trajectory relabeling teaches the student to internalize the drawing step rather than always delegating.

### Main finding

Placing the whole open-world image-generation loop under one agentic policy beats both fixed pipelines and partially agent-controlled baselines on their benchmarks; they release the RL infrastructure and training data.

### Why it matters

Most "agentic image generation" work bolts an LLM planner on top of a frozen T2I model. ToolArtist argues the planner and generator should be the *same* model, trained end-to-end with RL — this is the natural extension of unified multimodal models (Chameleon, Show-o, Janus) into the agent regime.

## 3. Towards Physics of Multimodal Pretraining {#3-physics-of-multimodal-pretraining}

**Authors:** Shengbang Tong, David Fan, Minghao Chen, Philip Torr, Filippos Kokkinos, Mike Lewis — *FAIR at Meta, Reality Labs, University of Oxford*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.05000](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05000) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05000) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.05000)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `pre-training`, `MoE`, `scaling-laws`

### TL;DR

Meta-FAIR runs a controlled sweep across ~all interesting axes of unified multimodal pretraining — modality mixture, architecture, integration timing, tokenizer choice — and distills four findings ("Knowledge Flow", "Synergy vs. Competition", "Early Unification", "Recipes"), then validates on 13.5B MoE models trained on 2T tokens.

### Key idea

Treat multimodal pretraining like a physics experiment: hold everything else fixed and vary one axis at a time. Key architectural finding: *shared* attention and normalization with *modality-specific* FFN layers is the sweet spot for synergy; unifying vision and language from step 0 beats staged training (avoiding "vision laziness"); efficient recipes hit strong generation quality at 5% of naive compute budgets.

### Main finding

At the 13.5B-MoE / 2T-token scale, their derived recipes hold: early unification + shared-attn-shared-norm + modality-specific FFN + calibrated data complexity → strong generative multimodal performance, ~20× compute-efficient over naive baselines.

### Why it matters

Multimodal pretraining has been an alchemy of tokenizer choices and ablation folklore (BLIP, Chameleon, Janus). This paper is the first to lay out mechanism-level design axes with controlled evidence — the "Chinchilla moment" for unified multimodal training.

## 4. The Personalization Mirage: How LLMs Fabricate User Profiles {#4-personalization-mirage}

**Authors:** Yushi Sun, Yanjie Zhang, Rui Sheng — *(affiliation not shown)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.04570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04570) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.04570) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.04570)
**Topics:** `safety`, `eval`, `memory`, `personalization`

### TL;DR

Introduces MirageBench: 150 personas × 6 personalization tasks × 143,616 judged claims across 12 models. Finds that persistent-memory LLMs over-infer user attributes 35–49% of the time — every model tested does it — and that models' *self-reported* over-inference is negatively rank-correlated with their measured over-inference (ρ=−0.60). Trusting a model's self-audit picks the worst offenders.

### Key idea

**Self-Monitoring Inversion**: at the model-comparison level, self-reported OI ranks models the wrong way. Within a single model, self-audit still discriminates its own good vs. bad claims (AUROC 0.58–0.83), but across models the signal flips. Actionable design implication: memory-quality controls must use external verifiers, not model introspection.

### Main finding

Over 143k judged claims, cross-model mean over-inference is 41.6%. Model self-scores anti-rank the offenders. Attributes accumulate roughly linearly across a multi-turn pilot with little revision — silent staleness.

### Why it matters

Every serious deployment of persistent-memory LLMs (ChatGPT Memory, Claude Projects, Copilot Memory) is now on the hook for this failure mode. The result also formalizes a broader lesson: LLM self-critique correlates within-model but inverts across-model — LLM-as-judge with self-comparison is broken by construction.

## 5. OneDayAgent: A Long-Horizon Harness for Autonomous Agents {#5-onedayagent}

**Authors:** Xinyuan Fang, Jintian Zhang, Zhengke Gui, Huajun Chen, Ningyu Zhang — *Zhejiang University, Ant Group*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.05013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05013) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05013) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.05013)
**Topics:** `agents`, `harness`, `eval`

### TL;DR

A "harness" architecture that wraps an open-ended user request in a managed execution process: bounded subtask decomposition + execution-memory management under context pressure + verify/repair on the final deliverable. Reports SOTA on the AgentIF-OneDay benchmark (104 tasks) with GLM-5.2 backend (0.821), generalizes across 5 backends without per-model tuning.

### Key idea

Separate the *harness* (control-flow scaffolding) from the *model* (reasoning engine). The harness handles the three failure modes prior work treats individually — goal drift, state loss, context overflow — jointly, then a verify-and-repair pass fixes the final artifact. The whole thing runs across 5 model families without retraining.

### Main finding

0.821 overall score on AgentIF-OneDay with GLM-5.2 — new SOTA. Zero-tuning transfer across five backend LLMs (three families) with distinct execution styles under the same workflow.

### Why it matters

Agent frameworks are still mostly bespoke — this argues that a shared harness abstraction (subtask + memory + verify) actually generalizes across models. If it holds, agent research can start iterating on harnesses and models independently.

## 6. Toward Skill-Native LLMs: Skill Entropy for Long-Horizon Reasoning {#6-skill-entropy}

**Authors:** Yinghui He, Ling Yang, Jiarui Liu, Yongjin Yang, Lechen Zhang, Yingcheng Wu, Zhenfei Yin, Mengdi Wang, Sanjeev Arora — *Princeton, CMU, U. Toronto, UIUC, Stanford, Oxford*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.05139](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05139) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05139) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.05139)
**Topics:** `reasoning`, `RL`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Frames "cross-skill long-horizon" as an open reasoning frontier: tasks whose steps require *different* skills chained together. Proposes **Skill Entropy** as a measurable difficulty axis, builds Skill²-Bench (558 skills × 9 domains) around it, and turns entropy into an RL reward. Skill-Entropy RL improves Qwen3-4B from 34.4% → 68.4% on their benchmark.

### Key idea

Have the model predict, for each reasoning step, both the answer *and* the skill it invoked. Reward = step correctness + alignment between predicted skill-sequence and gold skill-sequence. Ordinary CoT collapses to a single skill; explicit skill supervision teaches switching.

### Main finding

Qwen3-4B-Instruct: 34.4% → 68.4% on Skill²-Bench. Qwen3-1.7B: 14.6% → 40.1%. Works with off-the-shelf training data (OpenR1-Math) — skill labels are a *reusable* training signal, not a bespoke annotation.

### Why it matters

RLVR papers optimize a single scalar reward on the answer. This adds a *structure* reward — the sequence of skills used — which unlocks measurably harder multi-skill tasks and hints at a general recipe for compositional reasoning.

## 7. GDPevo: Evaluating Agent Self-Evolution on Real Business Tasks {#7-gdpevo}

**Authors:** Zhihao Liu, Xiang Qu, Chenxu Liu, Yifei Liu, Yanke Yu, Jingzhe Xu, Xuejun Wu, Buyue Qian, Xi Chen, Yaowei Zheng, Junhao Hu — *PrismShadow, New York University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.03764](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03764) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.03764) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.03764)
**Topics:** `agents`, `eval`, `data`

### TL;DR

An evolution-native benchmark for agents that persist state across tasks. **Rule hybridization** decomposes each enterprise workflow (CRM, ERP, finance, healthcare, legal, data) into atomic business rules, splits them across training vs. held-out test tasks, and re-combines them so test-time gains must come from training experience — not contamination. 120 tasks V1, expandable to 240 within 2 days.

### Key idea

Rule hybridization: atomic rules become the unit of transfer. Training tasks use rule subsets; held-out tests recombine subsets not seen together. This makes "did self-evolution help?" a *causally* answerable question rather than a contamination-hazy one.

### Main finding

Self-evolution consistently lifts held-out accuracy by up to **16.44 pp**, but the best evolved agents remain far below the fully-informed oracle at **91.6%** — self-evolution is *real* but *underexploited*.

### Why it matters

Every "the agent learned to do X better" claim in the wild is undermined by contamination. Rule-hybridization gives the field a template for contamination-resistant benchmarks that scale automatically — the same recipe applies to any composable-rule domain (agents, tool APIs, spec compliance).

## 8. SA-OPD: Spurious-Signal-Aware On-Policy Distillation {#8-sa-opd}

**Authors:** Yinuo Jiang, Yongjie Ye, Zhou Tao, Xiang Zhuang, Qiang Zhang, Huajun Chen, Tiankai Li — *(affiliations 1-3, not fully labeled)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.03632](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03632) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.03632) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.03632)
**Topics:** `distillation`, `post-training`, `VLM`

### TL;DR

On-Policy Distillation (OPD) has a hidden failure mode: some teacher tokens carry huge gradient but are driven by *language priors* or *formatting conventions*, not by the input. SA-OPD detects these "spurious" tokens with an input-groundedness proxy and filters exactly those where input-groundedness is low *and* teacher-student divergence is high, preventing high-impact spurious updates.

### Key idea

Two-axis filter: **input-groundedness** (does this token depend on the input?) × **divergence magnitude** (would this update be large?). Only reject tokens that are simultaneously ungrounded *and* high-impact. Preserves the dense-signal advantage of OPD while removing its worst updates.

### Main finding

Consistently beats vanilla OPD and prior selective-OPD baselines on both LLM and VLM settings. Establishes input-groundedness as the missing axis in selective-OPD design.

### Why it matters

OPD is emerging as the standard "get dense signal without a reward model" post-training tool (Qwen, Meituan, DeepSeek internal papers). Any recipe using OPD without checking for prior-driven tokens is bleeding signal quality — this gives the field a mechanism to fix that.

## 10. Recursive Synthetic Terminal Tasks (RST) {#10-rst}

**Authors:** Yucheng Shi, Zongxia Li, Ruhan Wang, Anhao Li, Zixun Huang, Junyao Yang, Lei Ke, Ninghao Liu, Haitao Mi, Leowei Liang — *Tencent HY LLM Frontier + multi-university*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.05466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05466) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05466) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.05466)
**Topics:** `agents`, `data`, `SFT`, `RL`, `terminal`

### TL;DR

Long-horizon terminal-agent training data costs $100s–$1000s per task because instruction, environment, reference solution, and verifier must stay mutually consistent. RST bootstraps: seed tasks are extended, re-verified in a fresh sandbox, and re-used as seeds for the next round. 15 rounds → **37,484 tasks at ~$0.05 each**, with pass@4 for DeepSeek-V4-Pro dropping from 90% (R₁) to 2.5% (R₁₅).

### Key idea

Recursive verified synthesis: (extend reference solution) → (re-align verifier + instruction to new workflow) → (validate in fresh sandbox) → (accepted tasks become next-round seeds). Verification prevents drift; recursion increases difficulty; sandbox freshness prevents "solutions that only work for a specific state".

### Main finding

At R₁₅: median solution grows 67 → 374 lines; median commands 40 → 244; DeepSeek-V4-Pro pass@4 drops 90% → 2.5%. SFT on RST trajectories lifts Qwen3.5-27B by up to 10 pts on Terminal-Bench 2 / Hard / Long-Horizon; agentic PPO adds another jump (49.44% / 32.00% / 22.07%). Recursion shows *no ceiling* at 15 rounds.

### Why it matters

Long-horizon terminal / coding-agent training data is the current bottleneck. RST offers a scaling knob — arbitrarily hard tasks at cents per task — and demonstrates the loop is stable through 15 rounds. This is a template for other verifiable-agent domains (software engineering, web navigation, tool-use).

## 11. RSTG: Distill Where You Fail — Adaptive Teacher Guidance for Negative RL-Groups {#11-rstg}

**Authors:** Zhuowen Han, Jinwei Xiao, Zhengxi Lu, Renren Jin, Zhiyuan Yao, Yuxin Liu, Hongyan Hao, Yueqing Sun, Yu Yang, Qi Gu, Xunliang Cai, Deyi Xiong — *TJUNLP Lab, Tianjin University · Meituan Longcat*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.00782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00782) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.00782) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.00782)
**Topics:** `RL`, `distillation`, `GRPO`, `RLVR`

### TL;DR

GRPO dies silently when every response in a group gets the same reward — no gradient signal at all. Naively stacking OPD on GRPO to rescue this fails three ways (over-fitting to teacher, killed exploration, asymmetric advantage). RSTG restricts OPD to *negative zero-variance* prompts only, weights by teacher confidence, targets only high-entropy / high-divergence *tokens*, and adds SFT on teacher-correct trajectories to inject positive gradient where RL has none. **+4.02% math, +3.05% code** over naive GRPO+OPD.

### Key idea

Three surgical restrictions: (1) sample-level: OPD only where GRPO's group all failed (no signal to preserve), (2) token-level: OPD only on tokens where student is uncertain *or* diverges hard, (3) global augmentation: SFT on teacher-correct rollouts fills the positive-gradient gap. Each restriction addresses one of the three failure modes.

### Main finding

+4.02 pp on math and +3.05 pp on code over naive GRPO + OPD baseline. Preserves exploration (unlike naive stacking) while capturing OPD's dense-signal upside.

### Why it matters

The zero-variance failure of GRPO is the single most-cited operational pain point in RLVR pipelines (DeepSeek, Meituan, Zhipu). RSTG is the cleanest published recipe for rescuing it without giving up GRPO's simplicity. Expect this pattern in every subsequent GRPO-based post-training report.

## 12. When Memory Lies: Spatial Memory Staleness in VLM Agents {#12-when-memory-lies}

**Authors:** Yushi Sun, Yanjie Zhang — *(affiliation not shown)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.04574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04574) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.04574) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.04574)
**Topics:** `agents`, `multimodal`, `safety`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Memory-augmented VLM agents rely on persistent spatial knowledge that goes stale as the environment changes. In a dynamic FrozenLake testbed (1,800 detection runs, 12,000 navigation episodes), text-mode staleness detection does *not* imply visual grounding — the same models can flag staleness from text but score vision F1 as low as **0.067** on the identical grids. Trusting raw memory more than doubles agent death rate vs. no-memory baseline.

### Key idea

Separate the *staleness-detection* task from the *downstream-navigation* task and pair them. This exposes the gap: a model may correctly say "this memory entry is now wrong" from text and yet act on the same wrong entry when the input is visual. Auditing helps but doesn't close the gap without reliable visual grounding.

### Main finding

Vision F1 ranges from 0.887 down to 0.067 across models — text solvability ≠ visual grounding. GPT-4o with raw memory dies >2× more than no-memory baseline. Read-time filtering helps in text mode, but visual filtering does not on the tested grid size.

### Why it matters

Memory + tools is the shipping direction for agent products; this shows the memory layer is a *safety* liability, not just a quality one. Names visual grounding under memory-observation conflict as the concrete open problem for the field.

## 13. SKILL-KD: Contrastive Skill Distillation for LLM Agents {#13-skill-kd}

**Authors:** Qiming Shi, Yibo Dou, Jiawen Zhu, Yulong Tao, Linbo Jin, Zhaolu Kang, Yunfan Zhou, Di Weng — *(affiliation not shown)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.28048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28048) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.28048) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.28048)
**Topics:** `agents`, `distillation`, `skills`

### TL;DR

Skill acquisition for LLM agents usually distills teacher demonstrations into memory-like summaries — often too implicit for a weaker student. SKILL-KD instead takes a student failure and a teacher success on the *same task*, extracts their actionable *discrepancy* as a textual **skill patch**, re-runs the student to test it, and iteratively refines. **Drift-Aware Skill Consolidation** decides per-patch whether to add, delete/modify, or skip a rule.

### Key idea

Skills as an explicit distillation medium, not experience summaries. Contrast (student trajectory ↔ teacher trajectory) → patch → verify by re-run → consolidate against edit history. This turns skill libraries into version-controlled patches rather than accretive memory dumps.

### Main finding

Across 5 agent benchmarks and 2 student settings, SKILL-KD beats fixed-model adaptation baselines. Frozen students benefit — no student parameter updates needed.

### Why it matters

Prompt-time skill libraries (Voyager, SkillWeaver style) accumulate silently and drift. SKILL-KD gives the framework a *diff-and-consolidate* discipline that mirrors how humans use lint rules — this could become the shape of frozen-model agent adaptation.

## 14. PIMiner: An Agentic System for Automatic Prompt Injection Red Teaming {#14-piminer}

**Authors:** Yanting Wang, Chenlong Yin, Runpeng Geng, Jinyuan Jia — *Pennsylvania State University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.05108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05108) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05108) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.05108)
**Topics:** `safety`, `agents`, `red-team`, `prompt-injection`

### TL;DR

Existing RL-trained prompt-injection attackers generalize poorly to new targets. PIMiner is a *strategy-library* agent that builds a reusable library during training (over sequences of (dataset, target model) pairs) and at test time transfers to unseen targets **without additional training** and with only **~10 queries per sample**. 76.2% ASR on Gemini-2.5-Pro, 61.9% on GPT-5.1, 42.9% on Claude-Sonnet-4.5 (IPIArena).

### Key idea

Frame red-teaming as a strategy-library problem, not an RL-per-target problem. Train once to accumulate transferable strategies; at test time it's a retrieval + adaptation loop over ~10 queries. The library, not the weights, is the reusable artifact.

### Main finding

IPIArena: 76.2% / 61.9% / 42.9% ASR against Gemini-2.5-Pro / GPT-5.1 / Claude-Sonnet-4.5. AgentDojo: 86.7% / 53.3% / 40.0%. Roughly frontier-model-independent method, only ~10 queries per test sample.

### Why it matters

Automatic prompt-injection red-teaming has been model-specific and expensive. PIMiner turns it into a library-transfer problem that scales to new targets cheaply — this reshapes how model providers should think about pre-release injection testing.

## 15. What AI Red-Team Evaluations Can and Cannot Prove {#15-red-team-evidential-ceiling}

**Author:** Bandana Kaur — *APIsec Research Labs*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.21735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21735) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.21735) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.21735)
**Topics:** `safety`, `eval`, `theory`, `red-team`

### TL;DR

Derives the **evidential ceiling** of a red-team evaluation in closed form under a fixed testing budget. Above a computable harm rate, a modest clean-sheet benchmark *proves* safety for that category to a stated evidentiary standard; below it, no passive benchmark of feasible size can. The cross-over rate falls as 1/n and extends to adaptive/automated red teaming. An audit of 8 existing suites finds most adequate for high-frequency harms but *orders of magnitude short* for rare catastrophic ones.

### Key idea

Belief-move ceiling: for any evaluation procedure with hypothesis-conditioned elicitation rates, there is a closed-form maximum belief update possible from a fixed budget. Discrimination between hypotheses — not attack success alone — determines evidential worth. This gives red-teaming a real evidentiary calculus.

### Main finding

Above the crossover harm rate, a null result is *stronger* evidence of safety than a single failure is of danger. Below it, current benchmarks are several orders of magnitude short of what would be needed for rare-catastrophic-harm claims. Auditing 8 popular safety suites: adequate for common harms, inadequate for rare ones.

### Why it matters

Safety evaluations get quoted as if they were probability statements ("X model passes Y"). This paper gives the field a way to say *exactly which propositions* an eval supports and which it doesn't. Should become a standard citation in any published safety case.
