## 1. AgentOPSD

**Authors:** Zi-Han Wang, Zhengxi Lu, Zhiyuan Yao, Jinyang Wu, Jie Wu, Zhengzhou Cai, Yueqing Sun, Ziang Ye, Linji Hao, Qi Gu, Xunliang Cai, Yongliang Shen, Yujiu Yang — *Tsinghua / Zhejiang U. / Meituan*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.05987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05987) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05987) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.05987)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

Turn-level credit assignment for long-horizon agent RL that never touches a critic. AgentOPSD watches the log-probability gap between a teacher and student policy on each token, aggregates it into turn-level "evidence," and updates a Bayesian belief over which turns were pivotal — in log-odds space, recursively. That belief becomes a reweighting on top of GRPO-style trajectory advantages, so sparse outcome rewards finally reach the few turns that actually mattered.

### Key idea

Recursive Bayesian belief updates over turn-level pivotalness, driven by *token-level* teacher-student log-prob gaps. Concretely: (1) sum token-level KL-flavored evidence within a turn, (2) update `logit(p_pivotal)` by that turn's marginal evidence, (3) reweight the trajectory-level advantage by the marginal revision between consecutive belief states. Critic-free, no extra rollouts, drops into any policy optimizer.

### Main finding

Beats vanilla GRPO and strong self-distillation baselines on ALFWorld (89.1% success with Qwen2.5-7B), WebShop, and Search-QA at both 3B and 7B scales. Ablations show both the turn-level aggregation and the recursive (history-dependent) belief updates carry weight — replacing either with i.i.d. or trajectory-mean signal hurts.

### Why it matters

Agentic RL keeps hitting the same wall: outcome-only rewards can't tell you *which* of 30 tool calls sunk the run. Existing fixes (learned critics, per-turn reward models, MCTS-style advantage shaping) either add compute or need extra rollouts. AgentOPSD gets a principled per-turn credit signal essentially for free by piggybacking on the self-distillation signal, which is a nice fit for the current agentic-RL default (GRPO + verifiable rewards).

## 2. OSReward

**Authors:** Qiushi Sun, Kanzhi Cheng, Yian Wang, Bowen Yang, Hang Yan, Liheng Chen, Fangzhi Xu, Zichen Ding, et al. — *The University of Hong Kong (OS-Copilot)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.28609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28609) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.28609) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.28609)
**Topics:** `agents`, `eval`, `RM`

### TL;DR

A large, human-annotated benchmark for the "VLM-as-judge" setup used across CUA (computer-use agent) evaluation, data curation, and RL. Trajectories span multiple backbones and platforms; annotations are multi-stage human ground truth. Findings: even frontier VLM judges share a *systematic leniency bias* (they call failed runs successes), and the reliable ones are too expensive to run at RL scale. To fix that, the paper releases OS-Shepherd-100K (an open trajectory-judgment corpus) and OS-Shepherd 9B/35B, open reward models trained on it that match commercial judges at 30–60× lower cost.

### Key idea

Two-part contribution: (1) OSReward + OSReward-Hard + OSReward-Multi, a standardized evaluation harness that grades VLM judges on real CUA trajectories with human verdicts; (2) OS-Shepherd, an open VLM reward model trained on reasoning-annotated trajectory judgments so that RL-scale CUA training doesn't have to pay frontier VLM prices per rollout.

### Main finding

State-of-the-art VLM judges fall short of an idealized judge and consistently over-approve failures. OS-Shepherd 9B / 35B match commercial judge accuracy on OSReward at 30–60× lower cost, closing the affordability gap for CUA RL.

### Why it matters

Computer-use RL depends on being able to verify episodes cheaply. Right now the field is quietly leaning on frontier VLM judges whose failure modes are undocumented; OSReward makes those failure modes measurable and offers a cheap open replacement. This is a prerequisite for scaling CUA RL from research demos to real training runs.

## 3. EnvACE

**Authors:** Zishan Xu, Zhiyuan Yao, Yuxin Chen, Yifu Guo, Zhengxi Lu, Yuquan Lu, Jinyang Huang, Yan Xu, Yasheng Wang, Weinan Zhang, Xingshan Zeng, Weiwen Liu — *Shanghai Jiao Tong U. / Tencent*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.06197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06197) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.06197) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.06197)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `world-model`

### TL;DR

Trains an agent to play both roles at once — first emit a tool call, then predict the environment's response to that call, then continue conditioned on the predicted response. Both roles are jointly optimized against the same outcome reward. The policy ends up carrying an internalized *world model* that removes the need to touch a real (or simulated) environment during training, and at test time can be used for "private rehearsals" before actually executing.

### Key idea

**World rehearsal.** Interleave `act(tool_call)` and `rehearse(response)` inside a single autoregressive rollout, with task-success reward flowing back through *both* the action tokens and the rehearsed-response tokens. The response tokens are shaped by the same RL objective as the actions, so the model is directly incentivized to imagine responses that lead to a correct decision.

### Main finding

Beats environment-scaling baselines across BFCL-v4, τ²-Bench, VitaBench, and FinMCP-Bench. Test-time rehearsal-before-execute gives additional gains under a modest rehearsal budget with zero extra external interaction. Gains transfer across model scales.

### Why it matters

The bottleneck in agentic RL right now is *environment construction*: real environments are expensive to stand up and simulators drift from reality. EnvACE turns that constraint into a modeling problem inside the policy itself — the LLM becomes its own environment. If it holds up, this is a route to scaling agent training past the number of well-instrumented environments you can afford.

## 4. GST-Bench

**Authors:** Kaixiang Huang, Heng Dong Huang, Fang, Junting Chen, Junjie Zhu, Yonghang Chen, Zhiyu Zhang, Wei Li — *ByteDance Seed / Zhejiang U. / NUS*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.05747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05747) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05747) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.05747)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `eval`

### TL;DR

A video-based benchmark that probes whether VLMs actually build a *global* 3D understanding of a scene from egocentric video, or just answer from what's currently in-frame. 2,762 human-verified questions over 6,790 minutes of synthetic video; tasks include reasoning about viewpoints that never appeared in the input, and converting egocentric observations to top-down maps.

### Key idea

Force VLMs to answer questions from **viewpoints not present** in the video (novel-view reasoning) and to produce **top-down** representations from purely egocentric input. This directly targets whether the model built a persistent spatial model of the scene rather than doing per-frame retrieval.

### Main finding

The strongest zero-shot model reaches only 42.68 vs. a human score of 79.08. Failure-mode analysis: proprietary models struggle with cross-frame integration; open-source models are limited both in perception and reasoning.

### Why it matters

Spatial reasoning is the current soft target on the VLM roadmap — everyone's shipping agentic vision systems but the ability to hold a scene in mind across frames is still weak. GST-Bench gives an explicit metric for it, and its "novel viewpoint" framing is harder to game than object-count or grounding benchmarks.

## 5. ChronoVision

**Authors:** Yifan Shen, Jian Xu, Boyi Li, Yuner Zhang, Tianjiao Yu, Bingxuan Li, Houze Yang, Rushi Wang, Xu Cao — *affiliations not shown on HF page*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.05631](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05631) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05631) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.05631)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `reasoning`, `RL`

### TL;DR

MLLM training recipe for temporal visual reasoning that treats "reasoning" as *predicting the final visual state directly*, not as a text CoT. During SFT, a Reconstructive Visual Head predicts the latent representation of the transformed state and an ROI-Attention module focuses on task-relevant regions. Post-training is RL with a composite reward that scores outcome correctness, latent-process alignment, and unsupervised visual focus.

### Key idea

Skip the "describe the transformation in words" step: attach a visual head that decodes the *latent* of the final-state image, and shape it with an ROI attention module keyed by semantic span queries. Then RL-tune with a composite reward that includes an implicit process-grounding signal (latent-space alignment) alongside outcome correctness.

### Main finding

74.8% in-domain and 71.6% out-of-domain on the new Vbvr-VQA benchmark (video reasoning cast as image ordering); 55.0% on IntPhys2 out of the box. Both are SOTA at report time.

### Why it matters

Video/visual reasoning benchmarks keep exposing that MLLMs paraphrase captions instead of tracking state. ChronoVision's move — grade the model on the latent it predicts, not the sentence it generates — is a concrete way to break out of that failure mode.

## 6. UniME-R1

**Authors:** Zelong Sun, Jun Wang, Kaicheng Yang, Tiancheng Gu, Ziyong Feng, Zhiwu Lu — *DeepGlint*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.06060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06060) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.06060) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.06060)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `retrieval`, `RL`

### TL;DR

Retrieval-augmented reasoning where the reasoning is conditioned on *what the retriever misunderstood*, not just on the query. An embedder + adviser pair: the adviser inspects the top-k candidates, identifies the confused cue, and either reranks (if the target is in the top-k) or emits a Retrieval-Centric Chain-of-Thought (RC-CoT) to steer a full-corpus re-retrieval with a dual-mode embedder. Trained end-to-end with hard-negative mining and retrieval-oriented RL.

### Key idea

RC-CoT: chain-of-thought whose input is the *initial retrieval result*, not just the query. The adviser is aligned to retrieval outcomes via SFT + retrieval-oriented RL, so it learns to produce CoTs that actually change the retrieval, not CoTs that merely re-explain the query.

### Main finding

Consistent gains over strong baselines on MMEB-V2 and a wide set of multimodal retrieval benchmarks.

### Why it matters

"Query rewriting via CoT" has been a standard trick since GraphRAG-era systems, but it's always conditioned on the query alone. This paper's simple relocation — condition on the *retrieval failure* instead — is a well-motivated fix and is trained with RL against the actual retrieval metric, which is where most CoT rewriters give up.

## 7. HarnessOpt-Bench

**Authors:** Apaar Shanker, Yash Maurya, Shehab Yasser, Vijay S. Kalmath, Veronica Chatrath, Yuan (Emily) Xue — *Scale AI*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.06301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06301) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.06301) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.06301)
**Topics:** `agents`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Benchmark for a slightly meta question: how good are frontier LLMs at iteratively improving the *harness* around another LLM (prompts, tools, control flow, memory, orchestration code) under a fixed evaluation budget. Five models × four tasks × two harnesses (shared + native).

### Key idea

Separate "coding harness the optimizer runs on" from "coding harness the target agent uses," and evaluate the optimizer's ability to close the gap between them by editing prompts/tools/orchestration. Fixed eval budget forces the optimizer to *choose* its experiments rather than brute-force.

### Main finding

Optimizer-model choice matters more than the coding-framework choice. The paper's headline: harness quality is bounded more by the LLM you use to optimize the harness than by the framework it edits.

### Why it matters

"AutoML for agents" is a live research direction (DSPy, TextGrad, Reflexion-style self-improvement) but there's no shared benchmark for the *optimizer* side. HarnessOpt-Bench fills a gap and reframes the question — from "which framework wins" to "which model is the best AutoML-for-agents driver."

## 8. DataSpace

Benchmark for "data agents" — LLM agents that answer natural-language questions over a heterogeneous local workspace (CSV, JSON, SQLite, Markdown, PDF, video) by producing a verifiable *tabular* answer. 410 cross-language tasks, 7,439 artifacts, 15.01 GB total. "Data analyst agents" are the largest concrete near-term deployment of tool-using LLMs; every enterprise wants one. Verifiable-tabular grading is the right primitive — it sidesteps the "did the answer read fluently" trap that plagued earlier data-QA benchmarks.

## 9. On-Policy Delta Distillation

**Authors:** *Authors not shown on HF page.* Model family: Qwen3 (multilingual math).
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.05802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05802) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05802) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.05802)
**Topics:** `post-training`, `distillation`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

On-Policy Distillation (OPD) has been pitched as an alternative to RL for LLM post-training but wasn't well studied across languages. This paper introduces OPD² — On-Policy *Delta* Distillation — which uses the *log-probability gap between a post-trained teacher and its base model* as the learning signal instead of the teacher's raw distribution. Studied on English, Korean, Japanese math reasoning with Qwen3.

### Key idea

Distill the *delta* (`log p_teacher − log p_teacher_base`) rather than `log p_teacher`. That delta isolates *what post-training added*, filtering out base-model preferences the student already has. On-policy: student's own rollouts are the samples the delta is scored on.

### Main finding

OPD² consistently beats OPD across English / Korean / Japanese math benchmarks, with the largest gains in Korean and Japanese. Also narrows the English–Korean gap. Bonus finding: English-only OPD leaks into Korean and Japanese performance too, but it shifts responses *toward English* — so target-language data still matters for target-language responses.

### Why it matters

Distillation is quietly re-emerging as a serious RL alternative in post-training (Qwen3, R1-Distill, etc.). "Delta distillation" is a simple, general lever that respects the base model as a prior. The multilingual leakage finding is a practical warning against monolingual distillation shortcuts.

## 10. Nemotron Greek

**Authors:** Ayoub Kirouane, Christos Petrocheilos + ~23 contributors — *NVIDIA*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.05138](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05138) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05138) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.05138)
**Topics:** `data`, `retrieval`, `RAG`

### TL;DR

End-to-end language adaptation of the Nemotron retrieval + reader stack to Modern Greek across legal / energy / financial / medical domains: corpus mining, synthetic supervision, embedder fine-tuning, cross-encoder reranker adaptation, and a LoRA-tuned MoE reader (Nemotron 30B-A3B) for grounded generation. Ships HERA, the first large Greek RAG benchmark.

### Key idea

Treat "adapt the full RAG stack" as a single pipeline rather than swapping one component: corpus mining → synthetic pairs → dense embedder fine-tune → reranker adapt → reader LoRA. Every stage has a Greek-specific fix; the paper documents where each layer breaks and what it takes to close the gap to BM25.

### Main finding

BM25 beats off-the-shelf multilingual dense retrievers on specialist Greek corpora out of the box. After fine-tuning on 65,773 Greek pairs, Nemotron 1B embedder jumps nDCG@10 from 0.362 → 0.835 (and now beats BM25 in-domain). LoRA-tuning the 30B-A3B MoE reader moves judged answer correctness 29.4% → 66.9% with big faithfulness and citation-quality wins.

### Why it matters

Case-studies-in-depth of adapting a full RAG stack to an under-served language are rare; most papers change one component. The BM25-beats-dense finding for specialist corpora also cuts against the common assumption that dense retrieval is a strict improvement.

## 11. Activity Frames

**Authors:** *Not disclosed on HF page.*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.05784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05784) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05784) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.05784)
**Topics:** `agents`, `memory`

### TL;DR

Deterministic (zero-model) pipeline that compiles passively-captured screen activity into typed "activity frames" — bounded episodes carrying application, site, timing, input volume, and evidence pointers back to raw rows. The output is byte-identical, cacheable, and auditable; no LLM in the compilation loop. Then it plugs in as prompt-ready context for a downstream computer-use agent's memory.

### Key idea

Skip the LLM summarizer for agent memory of past user actions: run a deterministic activity-segmentation compiler that outputs a canonical typed record. Deterministic outputs are cacheable and don't drift, which is exactly what a memory layer wants.

### Main finding

Compiler shrinks a day of raw screen capture into a prompt-ready block **86× smaller in 68 ms**. Downstream agent reading the compiled block hits **98.4% accuracy** (Wilson 91.7–99.7%) on questions about the day, vs. 66–80% for an LLM summary of the same capture. A mid-tier model on the compiled block matches a frontier model on the raw capture.

### Why it matters

There's growing consensus that computer-use agents should learn from what the user *did*, not just what they *said*. Every recent proposal (Rewind-style memory, workflow induction) uses an LLM summarizer for that. This paper is a nice existence proof that a deterministic compiler beats the LLM summarizer both on cost and on downstream accuracy — good news for auditability and privacy, since the compiler is fully inspectable.

## 12. PaDoc

**Authors:** Hao Yu, Jiabo Zhan, Kang Liu, Linnan Zhao, Dongxu Yue, Rui Chen, Jinglin Wang, Chong Sun, Chen Li, Jing Lyu, Chun Yuan — *Tsinghua (SIGS) + industry co-authors*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.06146](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06146) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.06146) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.06146)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

End-to-end document parser that decodes multiple layout regions *in parallel* instead of one long autoregressive sequence. A layout head predicts the region tree first; regional content is then decoded concurrently, conditioned on the layout.

### Key idea

Layout-first, content-in-parallel. Break the "serialize the whole page into one AR stream" bottleneck by decomposing the output along the natural region tree of the page, then decoding each region's content concurrently under a shared encoder.

### Main finding

91.1 layout F1 on OmniDocBench Full; 94.24 Overall (top-tier among end-to-end parsers); best Text Edit (0.038) and Formula CDM (95.59). Speed benefits from parallel decoding.

### Why it matters

Document parsing at scale (invoices, forms, scientific PDFs) hits the same wall as long-form generation — the decoder is serial. Structured outputs like documents *have* an exploitable branching structure, and PaDoc uses it. Similar in spirit to speculative decoding / diffusion-style parallel generation, but grounded in document layout rather than draft models.

## 13. KVAE

**Authors:** Andrey Shutkin, Denis Parkhomenko, Ivan Kirillov, Kirill Chernyshev, Kirill Malakhov, Ilia Vasiliev, Ilia Trushkin, Valeriya Kobenko, David Chikovani, Alexander Ivanov, Azat Saginbaev, Egor Silvestrov, Ivan Mikheev, Konstantin Zakharov — *Kandinsky Lab*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.05798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05798) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05798) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.05798)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `multimodal`, `tokenizer`

### TL;DR

A family of latent-diffusion tokenizers spanning audio, image, and video, all designed to feed downstream text-conditioned generative stacks: KVAE-Audio (48 kHz continuous, 50 Hz × 64 ch latent), KVAE-3D (two causal video variants at 4×16×16 and 4×8×8 compression), and KVAE-2D (image, 8× spatial, 32 channels).

### Key idea

Ship *matched* tokenizers across modalities with consistent latent shapes / compression ratios, so downstream text-to-anything diffusion transformers can share architecture across audio/image/video. The audio variant is causal & continuous full-band; the video variants are causal 3D VAEs.

### Main finding

Report-style release: tokenizer quality and compression ratios documented per modality; positioned as drop-in components for text-conditioned generation stacks.

### Why it matters

The tokenizer is the ceiling for a latent diffusion stack — its compression ratio, latent geometry, and reconstruction quality set what downstream diffusion has to work with. Shared, cross-modal tokenizer families are becoming the norm for any-to-any generation (Emu3, MoVQGAN, etc.); KVAE is the Kandinsky team's public entry.

## 14. MameLoshnLM

**Authors:** Uri Katz, Omer Goldman, Tomasz Limisiewicz, Reut Tsarfaty, Noah A. Smith — *Bar-Ilan / U. Cambridge / U. Washington / AI2*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.05850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05850) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05850) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.05850)
**Topics:** `data`, `eval`, `low-resource`

### TL;DR

The first open 8B LM built specifically for Yiddish: continue-pretrains Llama 3.1 8B on Oytser, a hand-curated Yiddish corpus (web-native + literary), and evaluates on Kashes, a Yiddish multi-task benchmark (translation, linguistic analysis, IE, understanding). Argues that standard multilingual corpora are actively harmful for languages like Yiddish because they include large amounts of noisy / machine-translated / misclassified text.

### Key idea

Corpus quality *dominates* raw multilingual scale for low-resource languages. A tightly curated 8B continue-pretrain on Oytser beats off-the-shelf multilingual baselines and, importantly, captures lexical / morphological patterns that noisy multilingual corpora smear over.

### Main finding

MameLoshnLM outperforms open baselines of similar scale on Kashes across translation / linguistic analysis / IE / understanding. Qualitative analysis: it captures language-defining patterns that generic multilingual models miss.

### Why it matters

The "just add more languages to the corpus" answer keeps proving inadequate; noisy multilingual data actively degrades genuinely low-resource languages. MameLoshnLM is a concrete template — corpus curation + continued pretraining + purpose-built benchmark — for other historically-rich, digitally-underrepresented languages.
