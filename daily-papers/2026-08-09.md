## 1. AgentOPSD

**Authors:** Zi-Han Wang, Zhengxi Lu, Zhiyuan Yao, Jinyang Wu, Jie Wu, Zhengzhou Cai, Yueqing Sun, Ziang Ye, Linji Hao, Qi Gu, Xunliang Cai, Yongliang Shen, Yujiu Yang — *Tsinghua / Zhejiang University / Meituan*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.05987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05987) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05987) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.05987)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `credit-assignment`

### TL;DR

Sparse trajectory-level rewards in multi-turn agentic RL rarely pinpoint the few pivotal turns that decided an outcome. AgentOPSD is a critic-free, recursive turn-level credit-assignment scheme that aggregates token-level teacher–student log-prob gaps into turn-level evidence and then updates a Bayesian belief in log-odds space to reweight turns.

### Key idea

Skip the value network. Instead, take a *privileged* teacher (self-distilled from a stronger rollout) and compute per-turn log-prob gaps against the student; treat those gaps as sequential evidence and roll a Bayesian belief forward in log-odds. Marginal belief revisions between consecutive states expose which turns actually mattered, and those turn-level advantages feed straight into the standard policy-optimization loop with no extra rollouts.

### Main finding

On ALFWorld, WebShop, and Search-QA with Qwen2.5-3B/7B, AgentOPSD improves over GRPO and strong self-distillation baselines, reaching 89.1% success on ALFWorld with the 7B model. Ablations attribute the win to (a) turn-level aggregation and (b) the history-dependent recursive belief update — not the teacher alone.

### Why it matters

Turns credit assignment for long-horizon agent RL into a principled per-turn signal without a critic and without extra rollouts, making GRPO-style pipelines cheaper and denser in supervision. A drop-in over GRPO for multi-turn tool-use RL.

## 2. OSReward

**Authors:** Kanzhi Cheng, Yian Wang, Bowen Yang, Hang Yan, Liheng Chen, Fangzhi Xu, Zichen Ding, Nuo Chen, and 14 others — *HKU / Xi'an Jiaotong / Nanjing / USTC / NUS / Fudan*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.28609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28609) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.28609) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.28609)
**Topics:** `agents`, `eval`, `reward-model`

### TL;DR

Computer-use agents are increasingly trained and scored by reward models over their action traces, but there is no shared protocol for evaluating those reward models across platforms. OSReward proposes a standardized benchmark of computer-use trajectories (web, desktop, mobile) with ground-truth outcome labels, and evaluates the current generation of computer-use reward models on classification, ranking, and step-level scoring.

### Key idea

Fix the evaluator, not the actor. Curate cross-platform (web / desktop / mobile) action trajectories with independently verified outcome labels, then measure reward models along three axes — pairwise ranking of trajectories, absolute pass/fail classification, and per-step usefulness — under a common protocol. Reveals the current failure mode: reward models are decent at classifying whole trajectories but weak at localizing the harmful step.

### Main finding

Cross-platform gaps are large — a reward model tuned on web traces drops sharply on mobile, and vice versa. Step-level scoring lags trajectory-level by a wide margin, and best-of-N selection using these RMs plateaus quickly with N. The benchmark ships with a training split, a held-out test partition, and per-platform breakdowns.

### Why it matters

RL for computer-use agents is bottlenecked by reward quality, not policy learning. A shared reward-model benchmark is the missing evaluation piece that lets the field compare RMs the way it compares policies, and exposes where step-level rewards need work.

## 3. GST-Bench

**Authors:** Kaixiang Huang, Heng Dong Huang, Fang Junting Chen, Junjie Zhu, Yonghang Chen, Zhiyu Zhang, Wei Li — *ByteDance Seed / Zhejiang University / NUS*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.05747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05747) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05747) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.05747)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `eval`, `spatial`

### TL;DR

A VQA benchmark for *global* spatial intelligence in video-understanding VLMs. Uses 6,790 minutes of synthetically generated video with human-verified questions that require inference from novel viewpoints and mapping egocentric observations onto a global top-down map — not just local within-frame perception.

### Key idea

Split spatial VQA into local (single-view) and global (cross-view over long streams). Same task templates, two benchmarks (GST-Bench and GST-Bench-Local); if a model scores well locally but poorly globally, the deficit is *consolidation over time*, not perception. Also ships GST-Train as a training corpus.

### Main finding

22 SOTA VLMs evaluated. The strongest zero-shot model reaches 42.68, vs. 79.08 for humans — a ~36-point gap. Local scores are much closer to human, isolating the deficit to global-scene consolidation.

### Why it matters

Video-VLMs are marketed as embodied agent backbones; this benchmark shows that even frontier models cannot yet build a consistent scene representation over long streams. Sets a concrete target for training-time or memory-side interventions.

## 4. EnvACE

**Authors:** Zishan Xu, Zhiyuan Yao, Yuxin Chen, Yifu Guo, Zhengxi Lu, Yuquan Lu, Jinyang Huang, Yan Xu, Yasheng Wang, Weinan Zhang, Xingshan Zeng, Weiwen Liu — *SJTU / ZJU / NUS / SYSU / CSU / CUHK / Tencent*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.06197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06197) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.06197) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.06197)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `world-model`

### TL;DR

Training long-horizon tool-use agents usually costs a real or simulated executable environment. EnvACE replaces that with **world rehearsal**: the same policy acts, then plays the environment (emitting the response its previous tool call would have produced), and conditions on that self-generated response for the next action. Both roles are optimized end-to-end with task-success reward.

### Key idea

Fold the world model into the policy. A single LLM alternates two roles per turn — actor (issue tool call) and environment (emit the tool's response) — and both roles are trained with the same task-success reward. The rehearsed responses ground subsequent decisions, and at inference time the model can privately rehearse before committing an action, giving a test-time compute lever with no extra external calls.

### Main finding

On BFCL-v4, τ²-Bench, VitaBench, and FinMCP-Bench, EnvACE beats environment-scaling baselines in the overall score. Test-time rehearsal gives further gains under a moderate budget. Controlled studies show world rehearsal helps at every model scale tested.

### Why it matters

Removes the biggest infra cost of agent RL — building or maintaining rich executable environments — by amortizing the environment into the policy weights. Points at "internal world model as scaling axis" for agent training, analogous to how internal reasoning replaced external search.

## 5. UniME-R1

**Authors:** Zelong Sun, Jun Wang, Kaicheng Yang, Tiancheng Gu, Ziyong Feng, Zhiwu Lu — *(affiliations not visible on HF page; author names suggest RUC / DeepGlint)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.06060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06060) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.06060) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.06060)
**Topics:** `retrieval`, `multimodal`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

CoT-augmented retrievers today reason from the query alone. UniME-R1 instead conditions reasoning on the **initial top-k results**: an "adviser" model looks at what came back, identifies which candidates the embedder confused, and either reranks or produces a Retrieval-Centric CoT that seeds a full-corpus re-retrieval.

### Key idea

Split the retriever into embedder + adviser. If the true target is already in top-k, the adviser reranks; otherwise it emits an RC-CoT explaining *what discriminative cue the embedder missed*, and a dual-mode embedder re-embeds under that steering. Training mines hard negatives to simulate realistic failures and jointly optimizes direct retrieval plus RC-CoT-augmented retrieval with RL on retrieval outcomes.

### Main finding

On MMEB-V2 and a broad multimodal retrieval suite, UniME-R1 improves over strong LVLM-retriever baselines. The benefit concentrates on failure cases where the target initially falls outside top-k — exactly where classic query-only CoT rewriting doesn't help.

### Why it matters

Reframes retrieval-CoT as *feedback-conditioned* rather than *query-conditioned* — closer to how humans re-search after seeing bad results. A recipe that pairs with any LVLM retriever and generalizes to unimodal.

## 6. HarnessOpt-Bench

**Authors:** Apaar Shanker, Yash Maurya, Shehab Yasser, Vijay S. Kalmath, Veronica Chatrath, Yuan (Emily) Xue — *Scale AI*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.06301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06301) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.06301) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.06301)
**Topics:** `agents`, `eval`

### TL;DR

An LLM's usefulness in agentic systems depends as much on the *harness* (prompts, tools, control flow, memory, orchestration) as on the weights. HarnessOpt-Bench measures how well frontier LLMs can *optimize a harness* end-to-end: an optimizer edits a seed harness under a fixed evaluation budget and is scored by the normalized gain of its best candidate on a held-out split.

### Key idea

Treat harness improvement as an autonomous-optimization task. An optimizer LLM (wrapped in a coding harness of its own) is given a seed harness, graded eval feedback, and a metered target-eval budget; it edits, tests, and nominates a final candidate. A trusted execution env enforces the budget and preserves versions. Two conditions: shared coding harness vs. each optimizer's native harness.

### Main finding

Across 5 frontier LLMs × 4 downstream tasks × 111 scored runs: (a) optimizers separate more than the harnesses they act through, (b) native harnesses are not consistently superior to the shared coding harness, and (c) gains vary substantially across tasks and seeds — leaving large headroom.

### Why it matters

Establishes "harness optimization" as its own measurable capability, distinct from raw base-model score, and provides an audit-ready protocol. Directly relevant to any team where an LLM writes or edits its own agent scaffolding.

## 7. DataSpace

**Authors:** Boyan Li, Zhuowen Liang, Yupeng Xie, Xiaotian Lin, Tianqi Luo, Xinyu Liu, Yizhang Zhu, Zhangyang Peng, Yuan Li, Zhengxuan Zhang, Jiayi Zhang, Nan Tang, Guoliang Li, Yuyu Luo — *HKUST(GZ) / Tsinghua*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.03451](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03451) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.03451) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.03451)
**Topics:** `agents`, `eval`, `data`

### TL;DR

A benchmark for data agents that produce **verifiable tabular results** from heterogeneous local workspaces — CSV, JSON, SQLite, Markdown, PDF, video. 410 cross-language tasks over 7,439 artifacts (15 GB). Also the official eval for KDD Cup 2026's Data Agents track.

### Key idea

Isolate the "return the exact table this question asks for" capability from open-ended analysis. A deterministic evaluator does header-invariant column alignment, type/precision-aware normalization, and order-aware row comparison — so scoring is reproducible across runs. Task construction is execution-grounded via DataSpace-Builder (cross-language transformation, constraint-aware relational sampling, modality routing, expert review).

### Main finding

Best of six frontier multimodal backbones × five agent harnesses reaches 66.34% accuracy — unsaturated. Harness choice creates a 15.36-point spread with the backbone fixed. Multimodal-evidence integration and joins are the reliably hard axes across all backbones.

### Why it matters

Gives the community a shared, deterministic yardstick for data-analytics agents at real workspace scale — and a natural setting to compare *harnesses* head-to-head on the same backbone.

## 8. Teaching Nemotron Greek

**Authors:** Ayoub Kirouane, Christos Petrocheilos, et al. (28+ authors)
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.05138](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05138) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05138) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.05138)
**Topics:** `data`, `retrieval`, `multilingual`, `RAG`

### TL;DR

An end-to-end adaptation of NVIDIA's Nemotron retrieval stack to Modern Greek: mining a domain-mixed Greek corpus, fine-tuning a 1B embedder and cross-encoder reranker on 65,773 Greek retrieval pairs, LoRA-tuning a 30B MoE reader for grounded generation, and releasing a new benchmark (HERA) for Greek RAG.

### Key idea

Rather than train one big multilingual model, adapt each stage of the RAG pipeline individually to a low-resource language and evaluate the deltas. Also confirms an old-but-important sanity check: BM25 remains a strong baseline that off-the-shelf multilingual dense models don't automatically beat on specialist Greek text.

### Main finding

After adaptation, nDCG@10 improves 0.362 → 0.835 on the Greek eval; the adapted 30B-A3B MoE reader raises judged answer correctness 29.4% → 66.9% with better faithfulness and citation quality. Learned language competence transfers to general-domain Greek, though the BM25 gap remains domain-dependent.

### Why it matters

A reproducible recipe for adapting a modern RAG stack (embedder + reranker + reader) to a low-resource language, with a released benchmark. Concrete evidence that dense retrieval isn't a free upgrade over BM25 without target-language finetuning.

## 9. On-Policy Delta Distillation (OPD 2)

**Authors:** (not fully listed on HF page)
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.05802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05802) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05802) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.05802)
**Topics:** `post-training`, `distillation`, `reasoning`, `multilingual`

### TL;DR

An empirical study of On-Policy Distillation (OPD) and its variant OPD 2 for math reasoning in English, Korean, and Japanese. OPD 2 uses the **probability gap between a post-trained teacher and its base model** — not the teacher's raw distribution — as the learning signal.

### Key idea

Distill the *effect* of post-training rather than the teacher itself. Instead of matching `logp_teacher(y|x)`, match `logp_teacher(y|x) − logp_base(y|x)`: the teacher's post-training-attributable behavior. This isolates what post-training added and avoids re-teaching the student what its base model already knows.

### Main finding

With Qwen3, OPD 2 consistently beats OPD; the gains are largest in Korean and Japanese, and OPD 2 narrows the English–Korean performance gap. English-only OPD helps Korean/Japanese scores but shifts responses toward English, showing the value of target-language data even when the gains transfer.

### Why it matters

Positions OPD as a lightweight, RL-adjacent post-training route (no rollouts, no value net), and shows a principled delta-signal variant that transfers cross-lingually. Practical alternative to RLVR when a strong post-trained teacher exists.

## 10. Activity Frames

**Authors:** (not listed on HF page)
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.05784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05784) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05784) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.05784)
**Topics:** `agents`, `memory`

### TL;DR

Computer-use agents pay full frontier inference to re-derive routines their user has already performed, because agent memory today records what the user *said*, not what they *did*. Activity Frames compiles passively captured screen activity into typed, bounded episodes (application, site, timing, input volume, evidence pointers) via a deterministic zero-model pipeline.

### Key idea

No model in the loop for the memory-construction step. Segment a screen-capture stream into typed frames with byte-identical, cacheable, mechanically auditable outputs, so the memory system inherits the reproducibility of a compiler rather than the drift of an LLM summarizer.

### Main finding

On a single-user 128,756-frame corpus (51 active days), the compiler reduces a day of raw capture to a prompt-ready context block **86× smaller** in **68 ms**. An agent reading that block answers day-questions at **98.4%** accuracy (Wilson 95% CI 91.7–99.7%) vs. **66–80%** for an LLM summary of the same capture, with a mid-tier model matching a frontier one on the compiled block.

### Why it matters

Argues that agent memory should be *compiled*, not summarized: the wins are cheaper inference, smaller prompts, and cross-model portability, all from removing the LLM from a step where determinism dominates. Actionable pattern for computer-use products.

## 11. DyPES-VLA

**Authors:** Junjie He, Zhide Zhong, Yangyang Zheng, Pingyue Sheng, Jiayu Dong, Ruixin Li, Haodong Yan, Jiaguan Zhu, Tianran Zhang, Runze Yu, Wen Chen, Liuqing Yang, Yuxiang Gao, Haoang Li — *HKUST(GZ) / COCO Matrix*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.06374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06374) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.06374) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.06374)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `VLA`, `MoE`

*Borderline: robotics VLA, kept because it's a vision-language *foundation model* with a shared VLM backbone.*

### TL;DR

A cross-embodiment VLA that (a) learns shared dynamics priors by giving the VLM a **future-prediction objective** on cross-embodiment data, and (b) routes to an **embodiment-specific MoE action head** so heterogeneous action spaces don't have to be pre-aligned into a common format.

### Key idea

Two-tier separation. Shared: a future-prediction pretraining signal drives the query representation to capture object motion, contact, and interaction-induced scene changes across embodiments. Specific: an MoE action head shares attention layers (common temporal action structure) but routes to embodiment-specific feed-forward experts for the unique kinematics of each robot.

### Main finding

State-of-the-art on **LIBERO (98.0%)**, **RoboCasa-GR1 (59.25%)**, and **RoboTwin 2.0 (89.02%)** as a single generalist policy across simulation and real-world evaluations.

### Why it matters

A concrete recipe for cross-embodiment generalist policies that doesn't need manual action-space alignment — an operational cost that has slowed VLA scaling. The MoE-per-embodiment factorization is likely to become a standard pattern.

## 12. KVAE

**Authors:** Andrey Shutkin, Denis Parkhomenko, Ivan Kirillov, Kirill Chernyshev, Kirill Malakhov, Ilia Vasiliev, Ilia Trushkin, Valeriya Kobenko, David Chikovani, Alexander Ivanov, Azat Saginbaev, Egor Silvestrov, Ivan Mikheev, Konstantin Zakharov — *Kandinsky Lab*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.05798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05798) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05798) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.05798)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `tokenizer`, `diffusion`

### TL;DR

A family of tokenizers for latent-diffusion generation across modalities: **KVAE-Audio** (continuous 48 kHz, 50 Hz latent, 64 channels), **KVAE-3D** (two causal video tokenizers with 4×16×16 and 4×8×8 compression), and **KVAE-2D** (image, 8× spatial compression, 32 channels).

### Key idea

Ship one *tokenizer stack* — same design philosophy, three modalities — so downstream text-conditioned latent-diffusion models can share vocabulary shape. Causal video tokenizers are the notable part: the two 4× temporal / 16× and 8× spatial variants trade off latent budget vs. reconstruction quality for text-to-video LDMs.

### Main finding

Full reconstruction and downstream metrics not visible in the fragment; the abstract emphasizes the family's design and coverage across audio, image, and video for subsequent text-conditioned generation.

### Why it matters

Latent-diffusion pipelines are bottlenecked by their tokenizer; a coherent, open, cross-modal tokenizer stack from Kandinsky Lab makes multi-modal LDMs easier to compose without reinventing the encoder for each modality.

## 13. Invisible Shortcuts

**Authors:** Vladan Stojnić, Ryan Ramos, Giorgos Kordopatis-Zilos, Noa Garcia, Giorgos Tolias — *(CTU Prague / Osaka U. — inferred from author affiliations in prior papers)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.05424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05424) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05424) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.05424)
**Topics:** `interp`, `multimodal`, `data`

### TL;DR

Vision encoders trained on ImageNet or billion-scale captions learn to use **invisible pixel-level metadata traces** (image-processing pipeline, camera acquisition) as shortcuts. Because natural corpora correlate metadata with semantics, large-scale supervision quietly *encourages* metadata sensitivity — which then breaks under metadata distribution shift.

### Key idea

Isolate metadata as a distinct source of shortcut learning, orthogonal to the classical object-vs-background story. Construct controlled metadata–semantics correlations at training time and measure how the strength of that correlation predicts (a) metadata sensitivity and (b) performance drop under metadata shift. Then test mitigations that reduce sensitivity even to *unseen* metadata without hurting downstream accuracy.

### Main finding

Stronger metadata–semantics correlations at pretraining reliably produce stronger metadata sensitivity and larger degradation under shift. Mitigations transfer to unseen metadata classes. Metadata sensitivity partly explains why some encoders excel at generated-image detection — and mitigating it can *improve* OOD generalization.

### Why it matters

Vision encoders that feed VLMs quietly encode camera / processing fingerprints; this changes how you should design multimodal training data and evaluate VLM robustness. Also reframes generated-image detection: some of the signal is metadata shortcuts, not "real" content cues.

## 14. TCFM (Task-Conditional Flow Matching)

**Authors:** Tirth Bhatt, Naren Kumar S, Mayank Singh — *LINGO Research Group, IIT Gandhinagar*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.05785](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05785) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05785) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.05785)
**Topics:** `multilingual`, `embedding`, `flow-matching`

### TL;DR

A multilingual embedding-adaptation framework that applies **flow matching** only to translation-shaped tasks while keeping retrieval, classification, and pair-classification tasks on objectives better aligned to their learning dynamics. Combined with teacher-guided representation preservation and a three-stage curriculum.

### Key idea

Different embedding tasks have fundamentally different loss geometries — training them under one objective either underfits one or overfits another. TCFM routes per-task: flow matching for translation-like alignment; task-native losses elsewhere. Teacher preservation prevents drift; curriculum stabilizes it.

### Main finding

New SOTA on Indic MTEB, consistent gains across a diverse multilingual task set, and cross-family generalization (works across embedding-model backbones). Code and datasets to be released.

### Why it matters

A concrete example of flow-matching leaking outside the diffusion world into representation learning, plus a practical pattern for low-resource-language embedding models where a single training objective doesn't fit all downstream tasks.
