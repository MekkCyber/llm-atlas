## 1. MatrAIx

**Authors:** Xiaomin Li, Yuexing Hao *(organizers)* + ~90 contributors — *39-institution consortium (affiliations superscripted, top-line institutions not disclosed on HF page)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.04205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04205) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.04205) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.04205)
**Topics:** `eval`, `agents`, `data`, `simulated-users`

### TL;DR

A population-scale simulated-user evaluation infrastructure for AI products. Persona 8B is a bank of 8.3 billion persona records over a 1,290-dimensional categorical schema; an open coreset of ~1M personas mixes 599,847 human-grounded profiles with 400,000 synthetic ones. Personas are dropped into four interactive environments (Survey, Chatbot, Web, App) and 1,010 tasks across 25+ domains, powered by frontier LLMs, to run product experiments that capture how heterogeneous users actually react.

### Key idea

Two moves: (1) generate personas from a **dependency graph** that preserves inter-attribute correlations (so a "cost-conscious retiree" doesn't also declare frontier-model preferences), plus a human-grounded arm for realism; (2) evaluate not on static prompts but by dropping persona agents into live playgrounds — with the persona model instantiated as Claude Opus 4.8 / GPT 5.5 / Claude Haiku 4.5 — and recording behavioral signals like price sensitivity, tolerance for latency, and follow-through after AI failure.

### Main finding

Across 18,189 trials on eight representative tasks, persona adherence measured on ten behavioral attributes was expressed-or-correctly-suppressed in **91.5% of trials** (366/400 in a controlled study). The full 8B-persona pool, the 1M coreset, and the four evaluation environments are released as an end-to-end infra stack.

### Why it matters

Human user studies are the gold standard but don't scale; offline benchmarks scale but abstract away user diversity. MatrAIx tries to bridge the gap for AI-product evaluation — an "npm for user studies" — and pushes eval infrastructure past static text prompts into interactive, heterogeneous, multi-turn behavior. If persona fidelity holds up under independent scrutiny, it changes how AI products are red-teamed, priced, and rolled out.

## 2. SFT vs RL Multitask

**Authors:** Kejian Zhu, Zhuoran Jin, Shangqing Tu, Hongbang Yuan, Yushi Bai, Kang Liu, Juanzi Li, Jun Zhao — *CAS / Tsinghua (inferred from co-authorship pattern; affiliations not printed on HF page)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.03573](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03573) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.03573) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.03573)
**Topics:** `RL`, `SFT`, `post-training`, `multi-task`

### TL;DR

An empirical + theoretical account of why SFT tears itself apart across tasks while RL doesn't. Under multi-stage training, SFT gradients interfere destructively across tasks; RL updates stay sparse and approximately orthogonal, so tasks coexist. The authors trace this to *variance-limited* interference in RL (bounded by advantage-normalized gradient variance) versus *norm-limited* interference in SFT (scaling with raw gradient magnitude), and use the insight to propose Parallel-RL — a paradigm that decouples per-task training.

### Key idea

Cast multi-task LLM training as a gradient-interference problem. Two mechanisms limit interference:
- **SFT:** interference bound scales with gradient norm → high-magnitude updates on one task overwrite parameters used by another (norm-limited).
- **RL (PPO/GRPO-style):** advantage normalization + on-policy sampling drive per-task gradient variance small → near-orthogonal updates (variance-limited).

Parallel-RL exploits this by training multiple task heads in parallel without the interference-driven task-mixing gymnastics SFT would need.

### Main finding

Parameter-level analysis shows RL post-training induces sparse and approximately orthogonal weight updates across tasks — quantitatively cleaner than SFT's dense, overlapping updates. Parallel-RL delivers efficiency and flexibility gains over a monolithic multi-stage SFT recipe.

### Why it matters

This is a mechanistic explanation for a widely-observed but under-theorized fact: RL post-training is oddly good at composing capabilities that SFT can't hold together. If Parallel-RL scales, it argues for pipelining specialized RL runs and merging late, instead of the current dominant recipe of stage-by-stage SFT → RL on ever-larger mixed batches.

## 3. Environment Distributions

**Authors:** Kejian Zhu, Zhuoran Jin, Dongqi Huang, Hongbang Yuan, Yupu Hao, Kang Liu, Jun Zhao — *Institute of Automation, Chinese Academy of Sciences / UCAS*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.03571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03571) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.03571) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.03571)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `multimodal`, `curriculum`

### TL;DR

More environments ≠ better agents. The paper diagnoses what's wrong with the "just scale environments" trend for multimodal agent training and proposes two components to fix it: Ability-aware Environment Selection (AES) for diversity, and Hierarchical Difficulty Curriculum (HDC) with two difficulty axes — harness weakening and state-scale progression.

### Key idea

Treat the environment pool the way we treat the *data mixture*: pick for diversity along ability dimensions rather than raw count, then structure the training order as a curriculum with explicit difficulty levers. Difficulty is separated into (1) reducing scaffolding ("harness weakening") and (2) growing the state space the agent must navigate.

### Main finding

Both AES and HDC yield measurable multimodal-agent training gains over an environment-count-only scaling baseline (specific numbers in the paper). The negative result — that scaling environment count alone plateaus — is itself the headline.

### Why it matters

Multimodal agent training is at the "just add more environments" phase. This paper argues that phase is ending and curriculum + diversity will start to matter more than raw environment count — the same trajectory data-curation went through after Chinchilla.

## 4. Round-Trip Consistency

**Authors:** Alexander Scheinker — *affiliation not shown on HF page*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.00675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00675) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.00675) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.00675)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `world-models`, `self-verification`

*Borderline: included because bidirectional diffusion + a self-supervised rollout-error signal is transferrable to video/latent-diffusion world models, not just physical systems.*

### TL;DR

Train a single conditional latent diffusion model that can step a dynamical system forward *or* backward in time via a direction flag. Because forward-then-backward must return to the start, the round-trip discrepancy 𝒞ᵢ becomes a measurement-free proxy for the unobserved rollout error — no ensembles, no ground truth, no held-out data, at the cost of one extra rollout.

### Key idea

A single bidirectional diffusion sampler (direction as a conditioning flag) gives you a *self-check*: any state reachable by rolling out i steps must round-trip back after i backward steps. Squared drift on that round-trip is your uncertainty estimate for that trajectory at deployment time.

### Main finding

The round-trip discrepancy correlates with the (normally unobservable) forward-rollout error, so a deployed model can flag its own drift without any external oracle. Cost is one additional backward rollout per uncertainty query.

### Why it matters

Uncertainty for autoregressive generative rollouts is a chronic problem — usually addressed with ensembles (expensive) or held-out data (unavailable at inference). Bidirectional consistency gives a cheap, model-native answer that translates naturally to video world models and any latent-diffusion generator that supports time-reversal.

## 5. StreamArena

**Authors:** Guankai Li, Yinghao Zhu, Shijian Wang, Sitong Wu, Shaozuo Yu, Meng Chu, Yuan Lu, Jiaya Jia — *Xiaohongshu Inc. / HKU / CUHK / HKUST*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.05703](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05703) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05703) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.05703)
**Topics:** `agents`, `multimodal`, `eval`, `video`

### TL;DR

A benchmark plus a system for hour-scale, interactive, streaming video understanding. StreamArena is 243 full-length videos (avg 88.8 min) + 3,646 open-ended QA pairs that probe real-time perception, historical retrospection, proactive interaction, and multimodal tool use. StreamMind is a two-tier architecture: latency-critical frontend workers handle live interaction while backend workers asynchronously build persistent multimodal memory and do historical recall / external search.

### Key idea

Diagnose why prior streaming-video-agent benchmarks are broken: minimal baselines that see only the last four frames win, and multiple-choice options leak language shortcuts. Fix both — hour-long videos, open-ended answers — then propose a system that splits agent work into a synchronous "live" tier and an asynchronous "memory + retrieval" tier so long-horizon evidence survives.

### Main finding

Under StreamArena, single-tier baselines expose real tradeoffs: recent-frame agents lose distant events, text-caching agents lose visual evidence, memory-compressing agents lose fine details. StreamMind's asynchronous-persistent-memory tier lifts all four capability axes and cuts query-to-answer latency by reusing prebuilt state.

### Why it matters

Streaming video is where the multimodal + agent frontier meets its context-length wall. If interactive video assistants are going to work at meeting or lecture length, they need architecture that separates latency-critical response from expensive long-horizon reasoning — StreamMind is a concrete first attempt.

## 6. Skaling

**Authors:** Mathurin Videau, Badr Youbi-Idrissi, David Lopez-Paz, Kartik Ahuja — *FAIR at Meta*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.07222](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07222) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.07222) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.07222)
**Topics:** `scaling-laws`, `pre-training`, `compute-optimal`

### TL;DR

Standard Chinchilla-style scaling laws systematically over- and underestimate loss in the *data-scarce* and *overtraining* extremes because they treat model size and data as independent. Skaling adds a single interaction exponent coupling capacity and data, cutting MAPE by **1.5–3×** across both interpolation and extrapolation regimes. Paired with a sparse low-compute grid, it hits full-grid extrapolation quality with **~10× less compute** than a uniform sweep.

### Key idea

Rewrite the scaling-law functional form so N (model) and D (data) enter with a coupling exponent rather than as separable factors. The coupling is what Kaplan-era laws implicitly captured and Chinchilla-era laws dropped — Skaling reintroduces it explicitly with one added parameter.

### Main finding

- MAPE reduced 1.5–3× vs. Chinchilla-style baselines, in both interpolation and extrapolation.
- With a sparse-grid restricted-compute sweep, matches full-grid extrapolation using ~10× less compute.

### Why it matters

Every frontier training run bets millions of GPU-hours on a scaling extrapolation. A tighter functional form for those extrapolations — especially in the data-scarce, high-token-per-parameter regime that's now dominant — directly cuts the risk of "we trained a model at the wrong point on the frontier." Also a rare positive signal for cheap-experiment sweeps: hit the sparse low-compute corner and let the law extrapolate.

## 7. Modular TTT

**Authors:** Bohao Tang, Zhen Qin, Yuqi Pan, Zheng Li, Pengfei Liu, Ya Zhang — *Shanghai Jiao Tong University / Shanghai Innovation Institute / ByteDance Seed*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.07110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07110) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.07110) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.07110)
**Topics:** `architectures`, `TTT`, `sequence-modeling`

### TL;DR

Test-time training (TTT) views sequence modeling as an online-learning problem over "fast weights." Existing TTT variants each hard-code their inner learner — Modular TTT exposes the fast-weight network, loss, learning rate, weight decay, and normalization as a directed acyclic graph, and lets you swap any component. Systematic ablations then say what actually matters.

### Key idea

Cast the TTT inner learner as a DAG of primitives (train-view forward, train-view backward, causal query-view), and expose every design axis explicitly rather than baking them into a single variant. This lets the same code path realize DeltaNet, Gated DeltaNet, and every hybrid in between.

### Main finding

Ablations reveal:
- Small LR init, weight decay, and a **single-layer nonlinearity** help.
- MSE and inner-product losses are near-equivalent.
- **Deeper fast-weight nets and normalization hurt** (they cause activation blow-up).
- Residual connections and gating add almost nothing.
The best resulting variant, trained at 410M and 1.45B on 100B tokens, matches Gated DeltaNet on loss and downstream benchmarks.

### Why it matters

TTT is the hot successor to state-space and linear-attention families for long context. But the space is a swarm of one-off variants. A common substrate + honest ablations is what the community has been missing — this is the equivalent of what nanoGPT did for pre-LN transformers, applied to the TTT family.

## 8. ReASearch

**Authors:** Junbo Li, Boyi Liu, Canwen Xu, Yite Wang, Yuxiong He, Zhangyang Wang, Qiang Liu, Zhewei Yao — *UT Austin / Snowflake*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.06714](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06714) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.06714) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.06714)
**Topics:** `agents`, `reasoning`, `RL`, `optimization`

### TL;DR

Existing systems for optimizing prompts / programs / ML workflows use an explicit outer-loop controller (evolutionary search, bandits, textual-gradient methods). ReASearch replaces the controller with a single reasoning-driven tool-using agent that itself decides what to evaluate, how to diagnose failures, which edits to make, and when to verify or restart. Across 14 tasks it's competitive with or beats specialized optimizers by **2–40%**, and improves the human best-known solution on Circle Packing.

### Key idea

Internalize the outer-loop search policy inside the agent's reasoning trace instead of an external controller. The agent has persistent memory, domain-specific tools, and a shared scaffold across three domains (prompts, programs, ML workflows). Search behaviors — proposal, diagnosis, budget allocation, restart — emerge from the reasoning rather than being coded in.

### Main finding

- Gains of 2–40% over strong domain-specific baselines across 14 tasks.
- Discovers improved solutions on Circle Packing (a classical combinatorial-geometry problem where prior best-known is human).
- Same scaffold — no per-domain controller — handles prompts, programs, and ML workflows.

### Why it matters

Automating the outer loop of ML/prompt/program search has been controller-shaped: bandits, evolutionary, textual gradients. ReASearch is a data point that a strong reasoning agent + tools can absorb the outer loop as *behavior*, blurring the line between "optimizer" and "agent." If it generalizes, the specialized-optimizer ecosystem starts to look like the pre-LLM manual-feature-engineering ecosystem: replaceable by a good enough agent.

## 9. SA-PPG + RailCap

**Authors:** Ruijie Hou, Yueyang Jiao, Zhao Wang, Yingming Li — *Zhejiang University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.07341](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07341) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.07341) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.07341)
**Topics:** `eval`, `data`, `contamination`

### TL;DR

The prevailing metric for "did we mitigate benchmark contamination?" — G-AP (Gap of Aggregate Performance) — is fundamentally flawed: it averages before differencing, so per-question over- and under-suppression cancel, and it weights every question uniformly, letting gaming push probabilities onto the clean model's hotspots. SA-PPG replaces it with a stratified per-question probability gap. RailCap then does the mitigation itself — during generation, whenever a sample falls back onto the greedy trajectory, cap the next token to the runner-up until the response distribution disperses enough.

### Key idea

Two independent contributions on the same axis:
- **Evaluation (SA-PPG):** estimate each question's solve probability by sampling, difference against a clean model per-question, and aggregate within groups stratified by the clean model's solve probability.
- **Mitigation (RailCap):** intervene at decode time rather than in a pre-estimation step — cap greedy-drift tokens to the runner-up when the trajectory looks memorized.

### Main finding

Prior mitigation strategies' "restoration" is substantially **overestimated** under SA-PPG. RailCap attains the lowest SA-PPG across multiple contaminated models and benchmarks, without needing an accurate pre-estimate of where contamination lives.

### Why it matters

Contamination mitigation has been evaluated with a metric that flatters it. If SA-PPG becomes the new bar, most published "we can restore contaminated models" claims will need to be re-checked. RailCap's decode-time intervention also side-steps the fragile "first estimate where contamination is" pipeline that everything before it relied on.

## 10. Efficient KD

**Authors:** Bakbergen Ryskulov, Iker García-Ferrero, David Montero, David Jansen, Ali Hashemi, Jezabel R. Garcia, Antonio Tiene, Román Orús — *Multiverse Computing*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.03796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03796) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.03796) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.03796)
**Topics:** `distillation`, `fine-tuning`, `systems`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

Two orthogonal systems-side improvements to LLM knowledge distillation: (1) **offline KD** — cache the teacher's top-K logits once, train the student against the cache, matching online distillation loss while freeing teacher memory and reaching ~29% faster iterations / 41% higher throughput on one H200; (2) a **fused, chunked KL loss** that never materializes the full vocab-sized logit tensor, making peak memory linear in sequence length and letting you train at 32,768-token context on one GPU.

### Key idea

Distillation's bottleneck isn't the loss shape, it's the *systems layout*. Top-K teacher logits change during online training but are extremely cheap to precompute and cache — offline distillation exploits that. And the naïve KL loss materializes a `(seq × vocab)` tensor per step; a fused chunked kernel keeps that off-chip, unlocking 4× the context on the same GPU.

### Main finding

- Offline KD matches online KD's loss at ~29% faster iterations, ~41% higher single-GPU throughput.
- Fused chunked KL: linear-in-seqlen peak memory, verified 4K → 256K tokens on an isolated head-only kernel benchmark; enables 32,768-token training context on a single GPU.
- Code released: https://github.com/CompactifAI/Full-Chunked-KL-Loss.

### Why it matters

Small models are how most orgs ship LLMs, and distillation is how you get them. Cutting the distillation-training cost by half and lifting the context ceiling by 4× is a direct multiplier on how many ablations, distillation runs, and long-context healing passes a team can afford — and thus on final small-model quality.

## 11. AudioRubrics

**Authors:** Fangxu Yu, Tao Feng, Dehai Min, Zinan Lin, Weijia Xu, Michael Xu, Philip S. Yu, Ge Liu, Tianyi Zhou — *UMD / UIUC / UIC / Microsoft Research / MBZUAI*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.02831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02831) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.02831) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.02831)
**Topics:** `RL`, `RLVR`, `multimodal`, `audio`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

Outcome rewards let a model reach the audio answer without attending to the audio; process rewards rely on hand-crafted, fixed criteria that don't adapt to each question or the current policy. AudioRubrics synthesizes **per-sample rubrics from the raw waveform**, then, conditioned on the model's own rollouts, regenerates and reweights criteria per group — a self-evolving, audio-grounded reward that keeps targeting the current policy's weaknesses.

### Key idea

Two-stage per-sample rubric: (1) generate initial rubric criteria from the waveform itself, so the reward is anchored in acoustic evidence rather than symbolic labels; (2) after seeing the current policy's rollouts, regenerate + reweight the rubric so criteria the policy already satisfies deflate and criteria it fails inflate — the reward evolves with the policy.

### Main finding

Across three audio-reasoning benchmarks, AudioRubrics substantially outperforms both open-source and training-based baselines. Gains **scale with the capability of the rubric generator/judge** (better judge → better reward), and the resulting policy converges to a *stable* reasoning length, avoiding both degenerate collapse and unbounded growth.

### Why it matters

The static-rubric trap — hand-designed process rewards that saturate as the policy improves — is a real ceiling on RLVR in domains where you can't just check a final answer. Making rubrics *per-sample* and *evolving with the policy* is a template applicable well beyond audio (video reasoning, tool use, agentic RL). Reasoning-length stability without penalty terms is a bonus.

## 12. Activation Oracles

**Authors:** Tobias Bersia, Tatiana Gaintseva — *BAISH / Queen Mary University of London*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.23379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23379) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.23379) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.23379)
**Topics:** `interp`, `safety`, `probes`

### TL;DR

Activation Oracles (AOs) are LMs trained to answer natural-language questions about another model's activations — a flexible "read hidden state" interface. In a controlled Taboo Word Guessing setup where subject models learn to internally use a hidden concept while suppressing overt mention, fine-tuning an oracle *on that concept* doesn't produce a specialist reader — it produces a concept-specific **anti-reader** that selectively fails to recover the exact concept it was trained on. LogitLens and layer-ablation show the target is still decodable inside the oracle; the failure is in the readout pathway.

### Key idea

Behavioral leakage (does the subject let the concept slip?), representation-level decodability (does a probe recover it?), and AO-verbalizability (does an oracle name it?) are *not* the same thing — they empirically come apart. Fine-tuned AOs can learn "don't verbalize this concept" as a reporting bias even when the concept is decodable.

### Main finding

- Fine-tuned AOs become *anti-readers* on their training concept.
- The concept remains decodable inside the oracle via LogitLens.
- Layer ablations localize the failure to the readout pathway, not the internal representation.

### Why it matters

Learned interpretability interfaces (AOs, natural-language probes) are being pitched as reliability tools. This paper is a red flag: the interface can silently learn to lie in ways the underlying representation doesn't. It matters both for interp research and for any safety pipeline that leans on oracle-style introspection.

## 13. Referential Dangling

**Authors:** Zhengpei Hu, Kai Li, Dapeng Fu, Xuechao Zou, Yuanhao Tang, Yue Li, Tengfei Cao, Jianqiang Huang — *affiliations not shown on HF page*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.04569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04569) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.04569) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.04569)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`, `long-context`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Hard prompt compression scores tokens / sentences / chunks independently and keeps the top under a budget. The paper identifies a paradigm-level failure: **independent scoring can split dependent-evidence pairs** — the compressor keeps the sentence containing the answer while deleting the sentence that defines the entity the answer refers to. On three multi-hop QA datasets at compression ratio 0.30, Beaver (Qwen3-0.6B-embedding chunk ranking) leaves the answer path incomplete in **34–54% of bridge examples**.

### Key idea

Independent-scoring compression treats the corpus as a bag of chunks and can only guess which chunks are semantically dependent. When a bridge entity is defined in chunk A and used in chunk B, keeping only the higher-scoring one severs the reference — *referential dangling*. This is a paradigm-level flaw, not a bug in any one compressor.

### Main finding

- Referential dangling rate 34–54% on bridge examples at compression 0.30 for Beaver.
- The failure mode is shared across independent-scoring compressors, not a one-off issue.

### Why it matters

Prompt compression is now standard in production long-context deployment. If a large fraction of multi-hop questions silently become unanswerable because of dangling references, downstream QA quality is being quietly cratered. It motivates dependency-aware or graph-aware compression as the next paradigm.

## 14. PrivacyPeek

**Authors:** Mingxuan Zhang, Jiahui Han, Dadi Guo, Songze Li, Guanchu Wang, Na Zou, Dongrui Liu, Xia Hu — *multi-institution, not disclosed on HF page*
**Links:** [arXiv:2606.00152](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00152) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.00152) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.00152)
**Topics:** `agents`, `safety`, `eval`, `privacy`

### TL;DR

Existing agent-privacy benchmarks audit what the agent *outputs* or *acts on*. PrivacyPeek audits the earlier stage: what data the agent *acquires* into its context via tool calls, whether or not it's ever surfaced. 1,182 cases across 7 acquisition behaviors and 16 domains; 10 agents from 4 model families; probe-elicitation follow-ups measure disclosure of case-specific sensitive targets from the retained context.

### Key idea

Two-stage audit:
- **Acquisition Inspection:** inspect the tool-call trajectory (both invoked tools and returned data) to detect when the agent pulls sensitive info beyond the task scope.
- **Probe Elicitation:** send a follow-up probe and measure disclosure of a case-specific sensitive target from the retained context — because over-acquired data is one prompt-injection or careless action away from leaking.

### Main finding

- Unnecessary sensitive-data acquisition is **widespread** across 10 agents / 4 model families.
- Task-completion capability positively correlates with acquisition-stage leakage.
- Prompt-level defenses reduce only a small fraction; most leakage is unmitigated.

### Why it matters

Once sensitive data is in the agent's context, it's exposed to every subsequent step, tool call, and injection. Auditing only the *output* leaves a large attack surface unmeasured. This is one of the first clean formalizations of "context-window is the privacy surface" for tool-using agents.

## 15. DCAS

**Authors:** Kishanthan Thangarajah, Boyuan Chen, Ahmed E. Hassan, et al. — *Centre for Software Excellence*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.06113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06113) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.06113) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.06113)
**Topics:** `agents`, `fine-tuning`, `eval`, `cli-agents`

### TL;DR

Open-model CLI software-engineering agents are almost all fine-tuned on OpenHands-collected trajectories, and they degrade sharply when deployed under any non-training scaffold — a fine-tuning-induced scaffold-lock. DCAS is an API-interception layer that routes traffic between any CLI scaffold and any backend model without touching the scaffold, enabling cross-scaffold eval and planning-aware trajectory collection. A controlled intervention isolates *planning quality* as the load-bearing scaffold-specific behavior, and a small DCAS-collected planning-aware fine-tune generalizes across scaffolds.

### Key idea

Distinguish two senses of "planning" that current scaffolds bake in:
- **Explicit planning:** a first-class pre-execution plan artifact.
- **Implicit planning:** structural conventions that shape execution throughout the loop.

Both are currently the scaffold's responsibility, so a model fine-tuned under one scaffold internalizes that scaffold's planning style and breaks under others. DCAS lets you attribute the failure and then collect training data that internalizes planning as a *model* capability rather than a scaffold artifact.

### Main finding

- Cross-scaffold plan-source intervention: gains from a better plan **exceed** the cross-scaffold degradation, confirming planning is the load-bearing axis.
- A model fine-tuned on a small DCAS-collected planning-aware trajectory set under one scaffold generalizes to non-training scaffolds.
- The two senses of planning are empirically **separable** in training data.

### Why it matters

Open coding-agent models have converged on one training scaffold and drift when deployed anywhere else. This paper diagnoses the drift precisely, gives the community an infra layer to collect cross-scaffold trajectories, and shows that "move planning from scaffold to model" is a real training axis — which matters for anyone deploying open-model coding agents outside OpenHands.
