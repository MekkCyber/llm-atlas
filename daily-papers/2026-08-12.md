## 1. ComBodied Agents

**Authors:** Qianggang Ding, Xingyao Wang, Rui Feng, Zhibin Wang, Feixiang Wang, Kelong Mao, Hao Sun, Zhiyao Luo, Jiankai Tang, Lei Li, Jiadong Guo, Minheng Ni, Weicong Lin, Chenxi Yang, Hongxiang Gao, Zhenghua Chen, Yang Bai, Min Wu, Jun Cheng, Huazhu Fu, Dacheng Tao, Bang Liu — *Mila / A*STAR / Tsinghua / NUS / Renmin / Oxford / Cambridge / … (22-author consortium)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.10915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10915) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.10915) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.10915)
**Topics:** `agents`, `position`, `multimodal`

### TL;DR

A position paper introducing "ComBodied Agents": a unified paradigm for human-centered agentic AI that treats an evolving individual's state as the primary object of modeling and intervention rather than either software transformations (digital agents) or physical transformations (embodied agents). Proposes a closed loop of event-based multimodal perception → longitudinal memory → Personal World Models → admissible-intervention policy, with agency-preservation metrics and consent/uncertainty/reversibility constraints.

### Key idea

Fuse the strengths of *digital* and *embodied* agents around a **Personal World Model** — a purpose-bounded, uncertainty-aware, user-correctable representation of a specific human — and have the agent select interventions that maximize *sustained human benefit* rather than engagement or automation. Tools, sensors, wearables, robots, and human services are demoted to action channels.

### Main finding

Framework paper — no benchmarks. The synthesis contribution: unifies fragmented lines of work (personal assistants, health agents, AI companions, adaptive HCI) into one closed-loop specification and proposes scenario-centered evaluation, agency-preservation metrics, and governance directions.

### Why it matters

Reframes what "long-horizon agent" evaluation means: instead of task-benchmark scores in a static environment, evaluate an agent's cumulative effect on a modeled person over months. If the framing catches on, agent-training pipelines have to grow explicit human-state models and uncertainty-aware intervention policies, which is a very different shape from today's tool-calling RL loops.

*Borderline: included as a position/survey paper because it maps a whole design space the agents/ folder currently doesn't cover.*

## 2. Co-Evolution in Agentic Systems

**Authors:** Qing Zong, Jiayu Liu, Junhao Shen, Zecong Tang, Linsi Wu, Yuxuan Liu, Rui Wang, Zhaowei Wang, Weiqi Wang, Cheng Qian, Xiusi Chen, Yangqiu Song — *HKUST / UIUC / CUHK / HKU / Peking University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.10299](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10299) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.10299) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.10299)
**Topics:** `agents`, `survey`, `RL`

### TL;DR

A survey of *co-evolution* in agentic systems — how multiple agents and their environment impose adaptive pressure on each other after deployment. Proposes a three-stage taxonomy: **Agent–Agent** co-evolution (adversarial / collaborative / organizational), **Agent–Environment** co-evolution (adaptive tasks, feedback, interaction spaces), and **Meta** co-evolution (the evolution mechanism itself becomes evolvable).

### Key idea

Argue that single-entity self-evolution is inherently bounded by a static learning context and that lifting that ceiling requires multi-component adaptation loops. The three-stage staircase (Agent–Agent → Agent–Environment → Meta) sheds successive layers of human-engineered constraint.

### Main finding

Survey — no experiments. Contribution is the taxonomy and a mapping of existing papers into the three stages, plus explicit open problems: evaluating open-ended co-evolution, scaling to many components, and keeping increasingly autonomous evolutionary processes safe.

### Why it matters

Names a research area that's been growing organically (self-play RL, agent-vs-agent debate, environment generation) and gives it structure. The Meta layer overlaps with autoML / self-improving agents, and the safety-of-open-ended-evolution framing connects it to alignment work.

## 3. Beyond Pixels

**Authors:** Zihao Liu, Xiaolong Shen, Zhenglin Zhou, Ruijie Quan, Yi Yang — *Zhejiang University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.10744](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10744) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.10744) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.10744)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `video`, `4D-generation`

### TL;DR

**Latent-to-4D**: skip RGB and predict explicit dynamic 3D scenes ("4D") directly from the **final denoised latents** of a pretrained video diffusion transformer, using a small 4D decoder that shares the video model's VAE. A single ~1K-clip-trained checkpoint transfers unchanged across multiple video DiTs in the same VAE family.

### Key idea

Existing 4D generation either decodes RGB and reconstructs geometry (distribution mismatch, error propagation) or bolts a geometry head onto one specific video generator (breaks when the generator changes). The paper argues the *shared VAE* latent space is the right reusable interface — align a video latent with the 4D decoder's token grid and refine with frame-wise and global spatiotemporal attention.

### Main finding

On Text4D-200 and I4D-200, Latent-to-4D beats matched same-latent Wan+4RC cascades in projection-based DINO-F1 by **+2.88–3.45** and **+5.81** points respectively, and wins human preference on geometry, temporal stability, and overall quality. A single checkpoint transfers across multiple video DTs sharing the VAE with no retraining.

### Why it matters

Standardizes a **VAE-shared latent interface** as the composability contract between video generation and downstream 3D/4D heads. If that pattern generalizes, the pretrained video model becomes a plug-in feature extractor for geometry, editing, physics, and other structured outputs — no one-off retraining per pair of models.

## 4. Reference-Free MMT

**Authors:** Chris Han, Pengzhi Gao, Pei Fu, Jian Luan — *Xiaomi Inc.*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.10812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10812) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.10812) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.10812)
**Topics:** `RL`, `GRPO`, `machine-translation`

### TL;DR

Post-train an open multilingual translation model (MiLMMT-46, 46 languages) with **GRPO** using a reference-free reward: average two reference-free quality-estimation models (COMET-QE style) with a **language-identification gate**, then **linearly interpolate** the SFT and RL checkpoints to get the final model. Also compares on-policy distillation as an alternative and finds it saturates at the RL frontier.

### Key idea

Two moves that stand alone: (1) the reward is *reference-free* (no parallel corpus at RL time) but gated by a language-ID model — if the translation drifts to the wrong language, the reward is zeroed; (2) the shipped model is a **linear interpolation of the SFT and RL checkpoints**, which recovers general capability the RL run partially lost.

### Main finding

Across 46 languages, MiLMMT-46-v1.0 beats its own SFT baseline and open peers (Seed-X, HY-MT2, TranslateGemma) and leads reference-free scores against Google Translate, Gemini 3 Pro, and GPT-5. On-policy distillation reaches but does not surpass the RL+interpolation frontier.

### Why it matters

Two-for-one: shows GRPO scales cleanly to multilingual MT when the reward is a QE model with a language-ID gate (avoiding the common "translate then hack the metric in the wrong language" failure), and gives more evidence that **SFT⇄RL checkpoint interpolation** is a cheap, portable recovery for capability loss under RL.

## 5. AdvFD

**Authors:** Mingju Gao, Jingkai Zhou, Kun Gai, Changqian Yu, Hao Tang — *Peking University / KlingAI Research*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.11205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11205) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.11205) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.11205)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `adversarial`, `post-training`

### TL;DR

**Adversarial Fréchet Distance (AdvFD)**: a generator post-training loss for visual generation that replaces the static pretrained feature space in a Fréchet-distance loss with a **calibrated adversarially learned representation**, mitigating "Fréchet hacking" (metric goes up, quality does not).

### Key idea

Existing FD-losses compute Fréchet distance between real and generated distributions in a *fixed* pretrained feature space (e.g. Inception). The generator can then overfit to that specific view — improving the target FD without improving quality. AdvFD trains an **adversarial** feature space in parallel that keeps changing what "distribution mismatch" is measured over, closing the loophole.

### Main finding

Presented as a general-purpose distribution-level objective on top of the standard diffusion / flow-matching sample-level loss. Concrete numbers weren't in the abstract snippet, but the framing is: **improves visual quality where fixed-FD losses stagnate** and prevents cross-feature-space Fréchet drift.

### Why it matters

Diffusion post-training increasingly relies on distribution-level objectives (FD/CMMD-style) on top of the base score/flow loss. AdvFD proposes a principled fix for the pretrained-space monoculture that has been quietly damaging results — a fully adversarial-vs-static schism revived for the diffusion era.

**Suggested new pages:**
- `multimodal/advfd.md` *(depth)* — the adversarial Fréchet-space post-training loss for visual generators.
- `multimodal/_generator-post-training-losses.md` *(taxonomy)* — Inception-FD, DINO-FD, CMMD, AdvFD; when to use each.

## 6. Mendel Gödel Machine

**Authors:** Changzhi Liu, Yilun Liu, Sikuan Yan, Volker Tresp, Yunpu Ma — *UESTC / LMU Munich / Munich Center for Machine Learning*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.07645](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07645) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.07645) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.07645)
**Topics:** `agents`, `self-improvement`, `code`

### TL;DR

Extends the Gödel-Machine-style "agent rewrites its own source code" loop with **two new mutation operators** drawn from Mendelian genetics: (a) **reaction-norm mutation** — edit an agent using its trajectories on *multiple tasks simultaneously* to preserve context; (b) **cross-lineage hybridization** — edit an agent using a *reference agent's* trajectory on the same task. Both exploit the archive of past attempts rather than a single failure trajectory.

### Key idea

Move from single-trajectory clonal self-modification (the current default in self-improving coding agents) to **comparative signals** across the archive: contrast an agent's own multi-task behavior, and contrast against a reference lineage's behavior on the same task. The archive stops being just a store of past programs and becomes a source of population-level gradient.

### Main finding

Ablation-style improvements over single-trajectory baselines on coding-agent self-improvement (specific benchmark numbers not fully surfaced in the abstract). The claim is that the two new operators are complementary and jointly outperform single-trajectory mutation.

### Why it matters

Self-improving coding agents (Darwin-Gödel Machine and friends) are currently sample-inefficient because each self-edit only sees one failure trajectory. Comparative operators are a low-cost way to raise the effective signal from an existing archive — likely to be adopted widely if it holds up in reproduction.

## 7. VibeLifeBench

**Authors:** Z.Y., Qionglin Qiu, S.L., Lei Huang, Lingxiao Du, Fanqing Meng, Hanjing Li, Qiguang Chen, Ethan Qin, XingYu, Mengkang Hu, Xiang Cheng — *(multi-affiliation, not fully disclosed on HF page)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.10875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10875) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.10875) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.10875)
**Topics:** `agents`, `eval`

### TL;DR

A benchmark of **200 multi-week everyday-life tasks** across ten domains that explicitly tests three capabilities most agent benchmarks skip: **proactivity** (act on a virtual clock without being prompted), **environment persistence** (state changes autonomously), and **long-horizon coherence** (weeks of interaction). Uses 22 mock service backends with 288 tools and 12,261 fine-grained scoring checks.

### Key idea

Move agent evaluation off the "single-turn task with reactive tool calls" template into a **persistent, autonomously-evolving virtual world** where the agent must decide *when* to act, not just *what* to do. Fine-grained scoring lets partial credit reflect intermediate correctness.

### Main finding

Strongest frontier model achieves only **32.5% average performance**, exposing systematic gaps in proactive perception, persistent state maintenance, and long-horizon consistency. Failures cluster around proactivity — agents wait to be told.

### Why it matters

Everyone is shipping "personal assistant" and "always-on agent" products, and none of the standard benchmarks measure whether such an agent actually surfaces things at the right moment. VibeLifeBench targets that gap and gives a bar to beat.

## 8. Ex-Omni-2D

**Authors:** Haoyu Zhang, Zhipeng Li, Xiaoying Tang, Tianshu Yu, Yiwen Guo — *(affiliations not disclosed on HF page)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.10720](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10720) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.10720) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.10720)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `diffusion`, `distillation`

### TL;DR

An omni-modal dialogue framework that jointly produces **coordinated text, personalized speech, and reference-conditioned video** with a *Visual Thought Plan* (predicts scene, emotion, and motion), a **shared multi-codebook speech unit** as an acoustic-temporal interface across modalities, and a **distilled streaming student** video generator that runs at ~1.293× real-time factor.

### Key idea

Two design moves: (1) a *plan* modality — the model first emits a compact scene/emotion/motion plan that conditions both the speech and video branches, keeping them coordinated; (2) treating multi-codebook speech units as a *shared acoustic-temporal interface* that both audio and video generation subscribe to.

### Main finding

Real-time-factor of ~1.293× (near-live streaming generation) with jointly coherent text + speech + video output. Distillation from a slower teacher enables the streaming student.

### Why it matters

Native omni-modal dialogue (input and output including video) is one of the frontier product bets, and the "plan modality that gates modality-specific generators" pattern gives a coherent template — cleaner than trying to force one autoregressive transformer to jointly emit all modalities.

## 9. Decoding-Level Taboo

**Authors:** Tadanobu Chuyo Kamijo, Ori Rottenstreich, Javier Conde, Gonzalo Martínez, Pedro Reviriego — *(UPM, Technion — from author affiliations elsewhere)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.09900](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09900) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.09900) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.09900)
**Topics:** `safety`, `eval`, `decoding`

### TL;DR

**Taboo** is a zero-prompt diagnostic: intervene directly in logit space at runtime by dynamically masking the primary candidate token at word boundaries, forcing the model into *circumlocution*. Measures off-nominal-path robustness — the divergence between benchmark scores and real deployment where safety/format constraints regularly push a model off its optimized generation corridor.

### Key idea

Rather than stress-testing via adversarial prompts, apply the stress at the **decoding step**: hide the model's most-favored token and see whether it can still produce a coherent answer via alternate phrasings. Larger and better-aligned models circumlocute more successfully.

### Main finding

Across open-weight model families, off-path robustness improves with both parameter scale and post-training instruction alignment. Also proposed as a generator of synthetic-diverse datasets and as an audit tool for runtime safety guardrails.

### Why it matters

Existing LLM evals almost all measure nominal-path performance, but deployment routinely pushes models off that path via system prompts, constrained decoding, and safety filters. A decoding-level stress test that requires no prompt engineering is a portable, reproducible primitive for measuring the gap.

## 10. SkillZip

**Authors:** Xiaofan Bai, Hongqiang Lin, Chao Liu, Yantao Zhang, Xuan Jin, Xipeng Cao, Yuhong Li — *(affiliations not disclosed on HF page)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.11079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11079) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.11079) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.11079)
**Topics:** `agents`, `context-compression`

### TL;DR

An **evaluation-free** method for compressing the accumulated "skills" of a self-evolving agent. Formalizes compression as a typed **minimum-description-length** objective over a skill contract + residual, with a hard coverage constraint on every trigger, workflow edge, tool requirement, obligation, and output field.

### Key idea

Generic prompt compression is unsafe here — a skill has name/description that gate applicability, workflow that controls execution, tool contracts, and rare-but-essential exceptions. SkillZip's "*explain once, reference many*" formulation states a repeated rule once at its scope, factors repeated action sequences into a shared procedure, and keeps only the differences as explicit exceptions.

### Main finding

Runs in a **one-shot** mode (single structured extraction call + deterministic optimization) or a **continual Zip-on-Write** mode that folds in each self-evolution patch without replaying tasks. Reported to outperform baselines on compression ratio, generalizability, and cost overhead across the paper's benchmark suite.

### Why it matters

Self-evolving agents accumulate skills that inflate the context window on every subsequent call, which is now the dominant cost driver for long-running agents. An evaluation-free structural compressor removes the rollout tax of prior compression methods and unblocks continual-learning agent deployments.

## 11. Not Worth Another Token

**Authors:** Harshitha Kolukuluru, Reshma Ashok, Kirat Arora, Evan William Ciccarelli, Nischal Ashok Kumar, Lunyiu Nie, Franck Dernoncourt, Samyadeep Basu, Ryan A. Rossi, Nedim Lipka — *UT Austin / Adobe Research / UMass Amherst*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.08389](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08389) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.08389) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.08389)
**Topics:** `agents`, `efficient-inference`, `RAG`

### TL;DR

Systematically compares **context-pruning strategies** for long-horizon deep-research agents across three pipeline stages (pre-retrieval, post-retrieval, pre-synthesis) using both lightweight heuristics and a learned value model. Finds that *stage placement* dominates *scoring rule* — early pruning wins.

### Key idea

Frame agent context management as **marginal value estimation**: each additional piece of evidence has declining marginal value, so prune before it enters the pipeline whenever possible. The stage of pruning matters far more than the specific scoring function.

### Main finding

Lightweight heuristics at the **pre-retrieval** stage cut token usage by **up to 73%** with little quality loss. Learned pruning is competitive on selected trade-offs but no single method dominates on quality, efficiency, and faithfulness simultaneously.

### Why it matters

Deep-research agent budgets are dominated by prompt tokens, and the field has been reaching for learned pruners; this paper shows the low-hanging fruit is where you prune, not how. Practical, actionable guidance for anyone shipping a research-agent product.

## 12. InSight-doc

**Authors:** Kaican Li, Weiyan Xie, Lewei Yao, Jiannan Wu, Lanqing Hong, Yongxiang Huang, Nevin L. Zhang — *HKUST / Huawei*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.10628](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10628) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.10628) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.10628)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `agents`, `RL`

### TL;DR

An agentic long-document VLM that treats **visual resolution as an adaptive reasoning-time resource** — starts at low resolution and selectively zooms into high-resolution regions for finer evidence, no external retriever. Trained with SFT (17.9K trajectories) + RL (19.2K hard examples) for region-level zoom-in.

### Key idea

Replace passive full-page encoding with an **active-perception** loop: the model decides which regions to re-encode at higher resolution based on the current question and its intermediate reasoning. Zoom trajectories are the SFT/RL signal.

### Main finding

InSight-doc-8B improves the baseline by **+4.3–16.4 accuracy points** on document VQA benchmarks. On long documents it reduces hallucination by **>40%** and inference latency by **41–68%** while maintaining an accuracy lead.

### Why it matters

Long-document VLMs currently either encode every page at full resolution (expensive, context-rot-prone) or bolt on a retriever (adds a whole subsystem). Active perception with region zoom is a cleaner middle path that also generates natural RL signals.

## 13. DSAgentBench

**Authors:** Mizanur Rahman, Mohammed Saidul Islam, Ridwan Mahbub, Md Tahmid Rahman Laskar, Shafiq Joty, Enamul Hoque Prince — *York University / Nanyang Technological University / Salesforce AI Research*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.10366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10366) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.10366) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.10366)
**Topics:** `agents`, `eval`, `code`

### TL;DR

A benchmark of **275 end-to-end data-science tasks** in *real computer environments* — notebooks, IDEs, terminals, browsers, and databases — with deterministic evaluators that assess analytical correctness, visual outputs, and model performance (not just code execution). ### Key idea

Move agent evaluation from "run a script that hits an oracle" to **run in a real OS with real tools** and grade the resulting artifacts (plots, models, reports). Determinism comes from the evaluator, not from the environment.

### Main finding

Even the strongest evaluated agent — **Claude-4.6-Sonnet — reaches only 56.7%** task success, while all open-source agents stay **below 1%**. The gap between closed and open agent stacks is enormous on real-computer workflows.

### Why it matters

Every serious "AI data scientist" product claim needs a benchmark like this. DSAgentBench also flags how far behind open-source agents still are on long-horizon computer-use tasks — a useful stake in the ground for the next round of open agent training.

## 14. Power Law Graph Attention

**Authors:** Burc Gokden — *Fromthesky Research Labs LLC*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.10288](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10288) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.10288) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.10288)
**Topics:** `architectures`, `attention`

### TL;DR

Introduces **PLGA** (Power Law Graph Attention), an attention variant used in the **PLDR-LLM** family that replaces SDPA's fixed bilinear form with a *learned input-generated* bilinear operator built from a positive tensor via elementwise power laws. Proves PLGA contains SDPA exactly (at $G_{\text{LM}} = I$) and shows an *inference-collapse* theorem where deductive outputs become input-invariant and inference reduces to generalized SDPA with a constant operator.

### TL;DR

*Borderline: included because it's an architectural attention variant with formal claims and Lean-checked proof cores — the graph currently has no page on attention-variant families beyond MHA/MLA.*

### Key idea

Generalize SDPA by making the bilinear form $Q^\top W K$ into a *learned, input-dependent* operator $G_{\text{LM}}$ built from a strictly positive tensor $A_{\text{LM}}$ via elementwise power laws. Formal claims are labeled theorem / conditional theorem / measurement / conjecture with selected proof cores machine-checked in Lean 4.

### Main finding

Empirical: relative fluctuations of $10^{-6}$ or below in the inference-collapse regime; block and sequential scoring select identical answers and agree on TruthfulQA probability-mass within $5 \times 10^{-5}$ per item. Theoretical: a Perron–Frobenius structure on $A_{\text{LM}}$ and a NOTEARS-style DAG regularizer.

### Why it matters

The formal framing (typed theorem / measurement / conjecture with Lean-checked cores) is unusual for LLM architecture papers and may become a template. Also gives a concrete "input-dependent attention operator" contact point with graph attention and mixture-of-attention lineages.

## 15. CoinRAG

**Authors:** Gyuwan Kim, Cheoneum Park, Tao Yang — *UC Santa Barbara*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.07458](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07458) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.07458) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.07458)
**Topics:** `inference`, `KV-cache`, `RAG`

### TL;DR

**CoinRAG** replaces chunk-level KV-cache reuse in long-context RAG with **contextualized nugget-level** reuse: two-stage retrieval identifies query-relevant *semantic units* inside chunks, then their sliced KV representations are assembled with a chunk-level context. Cuts prefill cost while improving answer quality.

### Key idea

Chunk-level KV cache reuse ships a lot of redundant / noisy tokens. Nugget-level reuse ships only the query-relevant slices and reassembles them into a compact contextual representation — *"assemble small nuggets to accumulate a larger value"*. Two-stage retrieval (chunk → nugget) is the key mechanism.

### Main finding

On LongBench multi-hop QA: **+5.3% average relative F1** and a new Pareto frontier under a standard fast-prefill latency budget, beating chunk-cache-reuse baselines on both efficiency and answer quality.

### Why it matters

KV cache reuse is currently the fastest-growing knob for RAG-heavy deployments (long context + repeated documents). Moving from chunk-level to semantic-nugget-level is the next natural granularity step and directly informs how KV caches should be structured on the serving side.
