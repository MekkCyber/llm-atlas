## 1. OpenART

**Authors:** Yunhao Chen, Xin Wang, Yixu Wang, Yi Liu, Jie Li, Yan Teng, Xingjun Ma, Xia Hu, Yu-Gang Jiang — *Fudan University / Shanghai AI Lab*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.00677](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00677) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.00677) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.00677)
**Topics:** `safety`, `agents`, `eval`, `red-team`

### TL;DR

An open-ended arena that red-teams tool-using LLM agents by *evolving the environment*, not the instruction. OpenART ships 10k+ validated stateful tasks over 50 domains and 500k tools, and a black-box attacker — EMHA — that walks a hypergraph of authorized state transitions to find safety failures. Across 75 agent/model configurations the pooled Attack Success Rate hits **85.0%**.

### Key idea

Fix the user's task and only mutate the *environment state* the agent perceives across turns. EMHA (Evolutionary Markov Hypergraph Attack) is a black-box, feedback-driven policy that picks state transitions from a hypergraph of allowed operations — no model weights touched. Because attacks work at the state layer, they compose across long tool-use trajectories (median 97 calls).

### Main finding

- 10,004 validated stateful scenarios, 500k+ tools, 50 domains.
- EMHA vs. instruction-only evolution: gap grows from ~2% on easy envs to **>17%** on the hardest, i.e. environment evolution *increasingly* dominates as complexity rises.
- Runtime implementation (sandbox, memory, planner) explains a large share of safety variance — beyond the underlying LLM.

### Why it matters

Static single-turn jailbreak benchmarks are essentially saturated for agents; long-horizon environmental attack surfaces are what production deployments actually face. OpenART pushes agent safety evaluation into the regime where the *state*, not the prompt, is the adversary.

## 2. Spark-to-Paper

**Authors:** Zhuoyang Qian, Biao Wu, Yiran Wang, Chris D Yan, Desan Dai, Liangwei Zheng, Jin Jiang, Junsheng Zhang, Wenhao Wang — *Multi-lab consortium*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.11924](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11924) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.11924) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.11924)
**Topics:** `agents`, `LLM`, `eval`

### TL;DR

An end-to-end research-paper-writing agent packaged as **thirteen composable skills** on top of an existing coding assistant — no bespoke agent runtime. It plans experiments before observing results, revises manuscript claims against measured evidence, and bounds a novel "Self-Refutation Loop" failure mode. Costs **$8.10 and 3.2 hours per manuscript** on average.

### Key idea

Two separations do the heavy lifting: (1) model judgment vs. deterministic ops (compiled checks, plotting code, citation validators), and (2) experiment planning vs. reporting — the required evidence is committed *before* results come in, so claims must be revised, not fitted post-hoc. The system then bolts on deterministic integrity checks + self-critique to keep repeated experiments from spiraling into rejecting their own objective.

### Main finding

- **99.5% citation validity**, **96.4% figure editability** across 8 controlled research topics.
- Ablation: fabrication detection jumps from **14% (single-pass draft) → 92%** with the full integrity + review stack; adversarial review reaches 74% precision.
- Full run: 11.9M tokens, $8.10, 3.2 h on average.

### Why it matters

Autonomous paper-writing has been demoed but never plausibly reliable. Framing it as skills-on-a-coding-assistant instead of a separate agent platform is the interesting design move: it argues that most "agentic workflows" people are building bespoke runtimes for should just be skills in whatever coding tool the researcher already uses.

## 3. AI4AI at Test-Time

**Authors:** Cheng Qian, Wenting Zhao, Liangwei Yang, Heng Wang, Jielin Qiu, Heng Ji, Silvio Savarese, Huan Wang, Shelby Heinecke — *Salesforce AI Research / UIUC*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.12307](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12307) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.12307) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.12307)
**Topics:** `LLM`, `agents`, `distillation`, `test-time`

### TL;DR

Can a strong model transfer its capability to a weak one *without any weight updates*? Yes — by letting the strong model iteratively author an **inference-time harness** (code, routing, format enforcement) that wraps the weaker target. On four Theory-of-Mind benchmarks, average target performance jumps **0.49 → 0.91**, roughly doubling.

### Key idea

Strong-to-weak *scaffolding*. Each round the builder LLM uses a 5% validation slice to critique and rewrite a harness that the target LLM will execute the full test set through. Gains come from **offloading unstable reasoning into deterministic code, benchmark-specific routing, and strict answer-format enforcement** — not from more sampling or longer chains.

### Main finding

- Average target model: 0.49 → 0.91 across four ToM benchmarks.
- Builder reasoning effort improves harness quality monotonically.
- Weaker targets get the largest gains; platform effects are modest.

### Why it matters

If test-time scaffolding matches (or exceeds) traditional weight-level distillation for capability transfer, then "how to deploy a smaller model" becomes a *code generation problem* the frontier model solves once, not a training run. Complements — rather than replaces — parameter-level distillation.

## 5. SkillZip

**Authors:** Xingyu Tan, Xiaoyang Wang, Qing Liu, Xiwei Xu, Xin Yuan, Liming Zhu, Wenjie Zhang — *UNSW / CSIRO Data61*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.05604](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05604) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.05604) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.05604)
**Topics:** `agents`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

Compresses agent-skill libraries so they fit in a limited context budget without breaking procedural dependencies. SkillZip operates on **section-level graphs** of skills and preserves *procedural contracts* — the verifier-reachability and dependency links that make a skill actually runnable. Achieves **3.46× compression** while keeping high preservation up to **100k skills**.

### Key idea

Treat a skill library as a graph of code+doc sections with contract edges (inputs, outputs, invoked verifiers, prerequisite skills). Then compress at the section level while enforcing that every kept contract still resolves. This turns skill loading from "paste every relevant markdown blob" into a graph-sparsification problem with hard reachability constraints.

### Main finding

- 3.46× compression ratio with high preservation of verifier reachability and cross-skill dependencies.
- Scales to libraries of 100k skills — the regime real coding assistants will need.

### Why it matters

Every serious agent framework (Claude Skills, Cursor rules, custom skill packs) will hit the "skill library outgrows context" wall. Structured contract-preserving compression is the sensible response and this looks like an early clean formulation of that problem.

## 6. NCP-Bench

**Authors:** Yingpeng Ma, Jianhao Yan, Bei Shi, Ka Hou Kam, Runnan Wang, Xuebo Liu, Yulong Chen, Yue Zhang, Derek F. Wong — *Westlake / U. Macau / HIT-Shenzhen*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.08160](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08160) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.08160) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.08160)
**Topics:** `eval`, `agents`, `LLM`

### TL;DR

Frames long-horizon consistency as **Narrative Commitment Preservation (NCP)** and ships a 100-environment benchmark derived from movie synopses to measure it. Even GPT-5.2 preserves only **42% of commitments** after 20 turns, and fact-conflict rates run **40–68%** across state-of-the-art models.

### Key idea

Each environment is a structured narrative spec — trajectory, commitments, and initial facts — that a checker can programmatically verify at every turn. The player agent tries to break the story; the narrator agent tries to keep it consistent. That gives an *automated* long-horizon eval that doesn't collapse into vibes-based grading.

### Main finding

- 100 narrative environments, up to 100-turn interactions, automatic commitment/fact-conflict checkers.
- GPT-5.2 tops the leaderboard at just 42% commitment survival after 20 turns.
- Only *isolated* runs satisfy all achievement commitments within the 100-turn limit — the class of problem is basically open.

### Why it matters

Long-horizon consistency is now the biggest gap between agent demos and agent products. NCP-Bench turns that gap into a number you can push, which is the precondition for it becoming a training signal.

## 7. AutoWorldModel-Bench

**Links:** [arXiv:2608.11216](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11216) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.11216) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.11216)
**Topics:** `eval`, `agents`, `world-model`

### TL;DR

A closed-loop benchmark where a frontier coding agent (Codex-5.4, Claude Opus 4.6) must **autonomously improve a world-model starter kit** under fixed compute. Structured-state (not pixel) worlds across 8 game environments let iteration cycles stay short. 63/64 sessions produced measurable improvements; **91%** made non-trivial architectural or objective changes.

### Key idea

Instead of humans doing world-model research, hand the entire methodology loop — architecture change, training run, evaluation, revise — to a coding agent inside a fixed compute envelope, and score the *rate* and *depth* of research-oriented modifications the agent produces.

### Main finding

- 8 environments, structured-state representation, closed-loop.
- 63/64 sessions improve the baseline; **91%** perform substantive research-style edits, not just hyperparameter twiddles.

### Why it matters

Automated ML-research benchmarks are proliferating (MLE-Bench, MLR-Bench, RE-Bench). AutoWorldModel-Bench narrows the frame to a domain where the *research method* has no dominant recipe, which is where autonomous scientific agents earn their claim.

## 9. ToolHazard

**Authors:** Yutao Mou, Pengfei Yang, Zhe Yin, Zhangchi Xue, Xiaotian Luan, Dingyao Yu, Tong Zhang, Shikun Zhang, Wei Ye — *Peking U.*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.11878](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11878) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.11878) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.11878)
**Topics:** `safety`, `agents`, `eval`

### TL;DR

A framework that *manufactures* adversarial tool-use environments at scale: Environment Simulator + Attacker Agent + User Simulator jointly build executable environments, locate injection points, and craft targeted payloads. Alignment data mined from the pipeline improves security on downstream benchmarks *without* hurting benign tool-use performance.

### Key idea

Three-way loop: (1) simulate a realistic tool-execution environment, (2) an attacker agent probes and finds indirect-prompt-injection footholds, (3) a user simulator drives realistic queries. What used to require hand-crafted honeypots becomes a generation problem, and the resulting attack trajectories double as alignment training data.

### Main finding

Testing exposes substantial vulnerabilities across current LLM agents, with attack success sensitive to injection **timing and position** in the trajectory. Alignment fine-tuning on ToolHazard-generated data hardens agents on standard security benchmarks while preserving benign task performance.

### Why it matters

Indirect prompt injection is the dominant real-world agent threat and the field's evaluation is chronically manual and biased toward known environments. Auto-generated environments + injection payloads is the same move that unlocked scalable jailbreak evaluation two years ago.

## 10. Latent Dynamics Reasoning (LDR)

**Authors:** Haodong Li, Shaoteng Liu, Tianyu Wang, Chongjian Ge, Sihui Ji, Jiahan Zhang, Xin Lin, Haolin Lu, Zhe Lin, Manmohan Chandraker — *UPenn / Adobe / NVIDIA*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.09926](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09926) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.09926) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.09926)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `video`, `world-model`

### TL;DR

Video diffusion models fit pixels but don't model how pixels *evolve*. LDR treats latent transitions as an explicit kinematic integration step and only regresses the higher-order **residuals**. Result: **>20× smaller** in- vs. out-of-distribution error gap on physics benchmarks vs. video-diffusion baselines, at a fraction of the parameters and compute.

### Key idea

Split the next-latent prediction into a physics-motivated kinematic prior (position + velocity integration in latent space) plus a learned residual for higher-order dynamics. The prior handles the low-order part exactly; the network only has to learn what the prior can't.

### Main finding

>20× improvement in extrapolation-vs-interpolation error gap on the paper's physics benchmarks, with fewer parameters and faster inference than baseline video diffusion.

### Why it matters

The "video model = world model" claim keeps failing at out-of-distribution physics. Baking integration into the architecture — rather than hoping enough data teaches it — is the plausible route to video generators that are actually predictive.

## 11. The Illusion of Visual Tool-Use

**Authors:** Zhiheng Wang, Bo Peng, Lai Wei, Chaochao Lu — *Shanghai AI Lab / Fudan*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.06270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06270) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.06270) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.06270)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `eval`, `interp`

### TL;DR

A causal audit of the "thinking-with-images" paradigm — VLMs that crop, zoom, and re-examine images while reasoning. The paper argues aggregate accuracy hides two failure modes: **the returned visual observation has no causal effect on the answer**, and **it does have effect but the scheduling of the tool call is wrong**.

### Key idea

Instead of measuring only accuracy, use causal interventions (swap observations, ablate tool outputs) to check whether visual tool-use actually mediates the model's answer. The distinction matters because both patterns raise aggregate scores while offering no real capability gain.

### Main finding

Despite aggregate accuracy improvements from crop-and-zoom, most measured scenarios show **no causal effectiveness** of the visual tool-use — either the observation is ignored, or the schedule of when to call the tool is broken.

### Why it matters

Casts serious doubt on a growing family of "agentic VLM" papers whose gains may be reward-hacking style shortcuts. The methodology (causal audits of tool-use) is exportable to text agents too — where the same "used tool, ignored result" failure is very likely lurking.

## 12. Simplax

**Authors:** Jinya Sakurai, Patrick Pynadath, Satoshi Hayakawa, Jaehong Yoon, Xulei Yang, Nancy F. Chen, Xun Xu — *A*STAR / NTU / U. Tokyo*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.10615](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10615) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.10615) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.10615)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `LLM`

### TL;DR

Uniform discrete diffusion, upgraded. Simplax augments each corrupted categorical state with a coupled **Dirichlet/simplex-valued** variable that preserves the underlying uniform diffusion marginal but yields a Rao–Blackwellized reverse-bridge objective and a richer sampler. Improves perplexity–entropy tradeoff on OpenWebText and, when trained only on 30-clue Sudoku, achieves the best accuracy across all clue densities including the 17-clue uniquely-solvable regime.

### Key idea

Instead of changing the corruption process (which would break comparability with existing discrete diffusion), *couple* the categorical state with an auxiliary continuous simplex variable via an exact Dirichlet–categorical construction. Training becomes a Rao–Blackwellized bridge objective; inference uses a stochastic simplex-aware reverse sampler. Denoiser input stays discrete.

### Main finding

- Better perplexity–entropy tradeoff on unconditional OpenWebText.
- Sudoku model trained *only* on 30-clue puzzles achieves highest accuracy across all clue densities including 17-clue, and highest validity in unconditional generation.

### Why it matters

Discrete diffusion is emerging as a serious contender to autoregressive text generation. Simplax shows the training objective and reverse process can be enriched without disturbing the underlying corruption kernel — an under-explored axis of the design space.

## 13. 3PO — Parameter Exploration for RLVR

**Authors:** Vatsal Venkatkrishna, Nico Daheim, Iryna Gurevych — *UKP Lab, TU Darmstadt*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.09805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09805) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.09805) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.09805)
**Topics:** `RL`, `post-training`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

Perturbed Parameter Policy Optimization (3PO) does exploration in *parameter space*, not action space — each rollout samples a slightly-perturbed policy rather than a hotter token distribution. On OLMo and Qwen2.5-Math it improves over standard GRPO at comparable compute and reduces problematic rollouts.

### Key idea

Cast the RLVR policy as a *distribution over parameters* (variational learning). Each training step samples policies from that distribution and rolls each out. Exploration is decoupled from token-level entropy tricks (which distort the answer distribution) and instead lives in weight-space uncertainty — a much more RL-native place for it.

### Main finding

- Outperforms GRPO on OLMo and Qwen2.5-Math with comparable computational cost.
- Fewer rollouts get stuck in problematic modes (a common GRPO failure late in training).

### Why it matters

Everyone's RLVR loop looks the same on the outside — the interesting research directions are (a) the reward, (b) the rollout diversity. Parameter-space exploration attacks (b) without touching (a) and slots in as a drop-in over GRPO.

## 14. Agent Safety Should Be a Runtime Contract

**Authors:** Albus W. Ng, Yi Han, Jusheng Zhang, Wenhao Wang — *(independent + industry)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.11274](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11274) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.11274) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.11274)
**Topics:** `safety`, `agents`

### TL;DR

A position paper arguing agent safety cannot be a *training* property — it has to be a **runtime contract** with (a) preventive gates (sandboxes, permission checks, filters) and (b) *evidential* requirements (a verifiable chain proving each successful completion). Grounded in an audit of 52 documented safety incidents. The unit of safety, they argue, is the **trajectory-with-checkable-evidence**, not the model.

### Key idea

Formalize an "Agent Trajectory Schema and Evidence Chain": every action a deployed agent takes must be paired with mechanically checkable evidence of both authorization and completion. This shifts the safety guarantee from "the model was aligned" to "the runtime observed and can prove what happened."

### Main finding

- 52 documented safety incidents surveyed.
- Analysis of publication imbalance: training-time safety research vastly outpaces deployment/runtime safety research.
- Formal Trajectory Schema + Evidence Chain framework as the proposed alternative.

### Why it matters

Agents in production keep failing in ways that no amount of RLHF can prevent — permissions were too broad, an action wasn't logged, a tool result wasn't validated. Framing safety as a runtime contract with evidence chains is a mature response and neatly complements the mostly-training-time safety literature this repo covers.

## 15. ReRound

**Authors:** He-Yen Hsieh, H. T. Kung — *Harvard SEAS*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.11045](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11045) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.11045) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.11045)
**Topics:** `quantization`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

**Calibration-free** post-training quantization that fixes round-to-nearest's midpoint ambiguity. ReRound trains a **conditional diffusion model** over continuous weight reconstructions to guide rounding decisions on weights sitting near quantization interval midpoints; a tolerance metric decides which weights go through diffusion vs. plain RTN. Improves over standard RTN at 3-bit and 4-bit while staying competitive with calibration-based methods and adding **zero inference overhead**.

### Key idea

RTN's failure mode is deterministic ambiguity at bin midpoints: two adjacent representable values are equally bad, and a bad tie-break compounds across a layer. ReRound uses a small conditional diffusion model to *reconstruct* what the true weight distribution would look like, then picks the rounding side and selects the reconstruction whose singular-value spectrum matches the original best.

### Main finding

- Consistent improvements over standard RTN at 3-bit and 4-bit for smaller LLMs.
- Competitive with calibration-dependent methods (GPTQ / AWQ family) *without* a calibration set.
- No inference-time cost — the diffusion model is used only at quantization time.

### Key idea for the KG

This slots directly under quantization/. The graph already has FP8 and a number-formats taxonomy but no PTQ / rounding depth files.

## 16. NeuPAT

**Authors:** Jiayue Jin, Jingwei Zhang, Chen Wang, Jing Liu, Longteng Guo — *ICT, Chinese Academy of Sciences*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.08107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08107) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.08107) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.08107)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `fine-tuning`

### TL;DR

Multimodal expansion of an LLM often destroys its language ability. NeuPAT measures **per-neuron plasticity** during multimodal SFT and applies neuron-wise update constraints — protect language-sensitive neurons, permit large updates on the ones that adapt well to multimodal input. Recovers **94.5%** of the language-capability degradation from vanilla tuning while keeping comparable multimodal performance.

### Key idea

Neurons in the pretrained LLM are *heterogeneously plastic* under multimodal fine-tuning. Estimate a small probe of adaptation patterns per neuron, then set update-magnitude constraints that scale inversely with language sensitivity. Architecture-agnostic and lightweight.

### Main finding

- 94.5% of language regression from vanilla multimodal SFT recovered across 11 language benchmarks.
- Comparable multimodal downstream performance across diverse LLM families.

### Why it matters

The catastrophic-forgetting tax on multimodal expansion is one of the least-discussed but most consequential drawbacks of building VLMs by grafting a vision encoder onto a strong LLM. A neuron-level plasticity budget is a much sharper knob than the usual layer-freeze or LoRA-only defaults.
