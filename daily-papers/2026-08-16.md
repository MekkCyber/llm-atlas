## 1. LLMRouter

**Authors:** Tao Feng, Fangxu Yu, Haozhen Zhang, Zhongjie Dai, Liangqi Yuan, Zijie Lei, Weizhi Zhang, Kunlun Zhu, Haodong Yue, Keyang Xuan, Ge Liu, Jiaxuan You — *UIUC / NTU / UMD / Purdue / UIC*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.06867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06867) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.06867) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.06867)
**Topics:** `eval`, `inference`, `systems`

### TL;DR

A unifying formulation of LLM routing (which model answers this query at what cost?) as a five-component sequential decision process — context encoder, model encoder, scoring function, decision rule, learning signal — together with an open-source library implementing 16+ routers and a benchmark (xRouteBench) that spans generic, memory-augmented, multimodal, time-series and personalized routing scenarios.

### Key idea

Frame router design as swappable modules with a shared interface, then automate supervision construction by running every candidate model over each benchmark and scoring the response/cost surface. Every family — binary quality prediction, cost-aware cascades, graph routers, agentic routers — falls out as an instantiation of the same five slots, and users can add a new router by writing only a routing method and a loss.

### Main finding

Learned routers beat the best fixed-model baseline by **14.6% relatively** across xRouteBench. Router rankings flip toward lightweight designs under tighter cost budgets, and user-conditioned routing (personalization) delivers consistent gains over the strongest un-personalized router.

### Why it matters

Prior routing papers used incompatible codebases and one-off benchmarks, making claims un-comparable. Standardizing the abstraction turns router design into a modular research problem — plug in a new scoring function or decision rule and get an apples-to-apples number — and gives production teams a reference for which router to reach for at a given budget.

## 3. Alaya-EVOKE

**Authors:** Yuanyang Yin, Gongxuan Wang, Yifan Zhan, Chuanhao Li, Kaipeng Zhang, Feng Zhao — *Shanghai AI Lab (consortium)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.13546](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13546) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.13546) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.13546)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `video`, `multimodal`

### TL;DR

An interactive video world model that decouples scene memory from generation by keeping geometry in an external, camera-indexed **world-state bank**. Only view-relevant slices are retrieved into the generator, enabling open-ended, continuously evolving playback while supervision cost stays linear in the visible window.

### Key idea

Instead of forcing the diffusion student to memorize the entire world in its weights or KV cache, EVOKE keeps persistent geometry in an external bank keyed by camera pose. A redesigned teacher pipeline generates supervision aligned with what the student can actually see, so training scales linearly with visible frames — hence "linear-scaling supervision to endless world."

### Main finding

Strong results on multiple long-horizon world-model benchmarks with a compact three-step student, materially improving out-of-sight consistency and cutting the divergence between short-window and long-horizon rollouts.

### Why it matters

Long-horizon consistency is the wall between "impressive video demo" and "usable simulator." Externalizing world state is a clean architectural answer that decouples memory footprint from generation quality — a template other interactive world models are likely to copy.

## 4. DreamX-Phi 1.0

**Authors:** DreamX Team; Rui Chen, Xiangxiang Chu, Geng Li, Jifan Li, Qingfeng Shi, Datao Tang, Jing Tang, Jun Wang, Pengfei Zhang — *DreamX Team*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.13489](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13489) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.13489) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.13489)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `video`, `agents`, `multimodal`

### TL;DR

An action-conditioned video world model for robotic manipulation. Given an observation, a language instruction, and a sequence of end-effector poses + gripper states, DreamX-Phi predicts the resulting future observations. Combines PRoPE-style geometric encoding, a depth branch, SAM3-mask supervision, and a frozen V-JEPA teacher for object consistency; then distilled for efficient deployment.

### Key idea

Attack manipulation-video fidelity from three angles simultaneously: (1) PRoPE geometric positional encoding gives the network a sharper prior over 6-DoF arm control, (2) a depth branch stabilizes scene geometry, and (3) SAM3 masks + a frozen V-JEPA teacher keep manipulated objects identity-consistent across frames.

### Main finding

**1st place on WorldArena 2.0 Track 1** (predicting rollouts from actions) and **2nd on Track 2**. A student model distilled from the full pipeline sustains the accuracy at inference-friendly cost.

### Why it matters

Interpretable, geometrically-grounded conditioning + object-identity teachers is a repeatable recipe for closing the sim-to-real gap in manipulation video models. The result strengthens the case for using video world models as data engines and world-simulators for VLA training.

## 5. DarwinX

**Authors:** Yifan Zhang, Yutong Dai, Juntao Tan, Luyu Yang, Rishi Mullur, Thai Hoang, Zhiyuan Hu, James Zhu, Phil Mui, Silvio Savarese, Ran Xu, Zeyuan Chen — *Salesforce AI Research / Agentforce*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.07545](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07545) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.07545) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.07545)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Treats agent self-improvement as **evolutionary search over harnesses** (prompts + tools + control flow) with the underlying model frozen. Introduces a "preserve-and-extend contract" that only admits variants which extend coverage without regressing existing tasks, keeps an archive of alternative lineages for recombination, and drives fitness purely from each benchmark's own verifier — no gold solutions, no hand-picked winners.

### Key idea

Single-lineage self-improvement is path-dependent: local wins often regress other tasks. DarwinX runs a *population* of harnesses, mutates them from failure / teacher / self-derived evidence through one edit interface, and enforces monotonic coverage. Recombination across the archive lets promising branches mix.

### Main finding

Across four benchmarks, one loop adds ≈17 points on average. Terminal-Bench 2.1: +7.7 to **83.2%** on a matched base and **84.7%** on a stronger base (verified frontier). TerminalWorld held-out: **68.3%**, beating every off-the-shelf agent. WebArena-Infinity real-task pass@1: **43.5% → 93.0%** audit-clean. A Terminal-Bench harness transferred unchanged to SWE-bench Verified.

### Why it matters

Reframes "agent improvement" from training the model to evolving the surrounding harness — a much cheaper unit of iteration that also generalizes (it survived task/verifier/base-model swaps). This is what AutoDesign (paper #7) and SKILLER (paper #9) implicitly bet on; DarwinX gives the strongest evidence yet that harness-level search compounds.

## 6. PlayWorld

**Authors:** Kaixin Ding, Xi Chen, Minghong Cai, Zhiyuan Xu, Yiyang Wang, Yuxiang Lu, Junyi Li, Shuyang Chen, Yuan Gao, Xin Tao, Pengfei Wan, Hengshuang Zhao — *Kling / HKU / consortium*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.13552](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13552) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.13552) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.13552)
**Topics:** `eval`, `diffusion`, `agents`, `video`

### TL;DR

A benchmark that evaluates interactive video world models by letting a multimodal *agent player* pursue long-horizon objectives (e.g. "turn 360° and see if the scene remains consistent") instead of prescribing a fixed action sequence. 171 scenarios, four core dimensions: geometry consistency, interaction fidelity, out-of-sight evolution, insight evolution.

### Key idea

Fixed-action evaluation over-punishes models whose optimal control differs from the reference: two systems can pursue the same objective with very different action sequences. Replacing the scripted actor with an agent that *actively tries to achieve an objective* makes the comparison fair, and separates "renders frames well" from "acts as a world."

### Main finding

Across 9 state-of-the-art world models, none reliably support long-horizon interactive objectives. The dominant failure modes are spatial-consistency drift and lack of persistent state evolution — exactly the axes Alaya-EVOKE (paper #3) targets with external world state.

### Why it matters

Establishes a standard for evaluating *interactive* rather than *cinematic* video generation. Puts hard numbers on the failure modes everyone qualitatively suspected, and gives the DreamX-Phi / EVOKE line of work a shared scoreboard.

## 7. AutoDesign

**Authors:** Yaxin Luo, Haobin Jiang, Jialv Zou, Xu Huang, Wenhao Yan, Haodong Li, Zhengrong Yue, Jing Li, Xiaofu Chen, Xiaohan Zhao, Jiacheng Liu, Jiacheng Cui, Zhiqiang Shen, Xiaotong Li — *MBZUAI / consortium*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.13560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13560) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.13560) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.13560)
**Topics:** `agents`, `multimodal`, `eval`

### TL;DR

A meta-optimization framework for **long-horizon agentic design** (turning multimodal source material into a structured, condensed output — the running example is academic posters). AutoDesign learns and iteratively refines a *design harness* aligned with human design priors, so the harness itself improves as it processes more inputs.

### Key idea

Static agent harnesses fall short for open-ended design tasks: they cannot capture the recursive human "sketch → critique → revise" loop. AutoDesign layers a meta-optimizer above the design harness that ingests critique + priors + past executions and rewrites the harness for the next input, giving a design analogue of self-improving code agents.

### Main finding

Achieves **78.32 PosterBench** and raises the average PosterBench score from **54.99 → 67.39 (+12.4 points)** across multiple configurations — sizable margins for a domain where harness quality was previously the ceiling.

### Why it matters

Extends the "evolve the harness, not the weights" thesis (DarwinX) to a very different task family (multimodal creative composition) and confirms the pattern is not benchmark-specific. Together with DarwinX and SKILLER, harness-level optimization is emerging as its own subfield.

## 8. Spatial Memory Agent

**Authors:** Haokai Zhang, Yuhang Ding, Yunshu Zhou, Xinze Du, Shengtao Zhang, Zhiyue Zhao, Yuling Xi, Hao Chen — *(academic consortium)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.12743](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12743) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.12743) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.12743)
**Topics:** `agents`, `multimodal`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Improves the spatial reasoning of a **frozen** VLM agent by giving it a runtime memory of past verified spatial experiences distilled into reusable "lessons," each scored by a **Transfer Reliability Score (TRS)**. At inference time, lessons are retrieved by semantic filter plus similarity–TRS ranking and injected into the prompt to guide the frozen model.

### Key idea

Third path between SFT / RL and external tool-calling: parameter-update-free self-evolution. Instead of updating weights or calling depth/3D tools, SMA converts verifier-scored rollouts into compact natural-language lessons, calibrates each lesson's TRS from later retrieval outcomes, and treats deployment as read-only retrieval + prompt injection.

### Main finding

Across 5 spatial benchmarks × 4 base VLMs, SMA is the best macro-average method for every base, and wins the majority of 20 individual evaluations — without any weight update or tool call at inference.

### Why it matters

Adds a concrete evidence point for **procedural memory** as an alternative to fine-tuning for narrow capability gains. Composable with fine-tuning underneath; also directly relevant to the "harness evolution" line since retrieval-guided harnesses are one lightweight form of self-improvement.

## 9. SKILLER

**Authors:** Chenhao Dang, Siyuan Xiong, Conghui He, Weijia Li — *Shanghai AI Lab / SYSU*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.10538](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10538) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.10538) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.10538)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `SFT`

### TL;DR

Automatically generates reusable **agent skills** (packaged procedural knowledge in a standardized format) for *small* language models by transmitting RL signals through natural language rather than gradients — a language-level analogue of RLVR that lets a small model accumulate a stable skill library from its own successes and failures.

### Key idea

RL for agent skills doesn't have to update parameters: a verifier's reward can be summarized as a natural-language critique, and the model's "policy update" becomes a rewrite of its skill instructions. This makes it feasible on small models where full RL is expensive and unstable.

### Main finding

On Qwen-9B, gains of **+4.3 to +20.4 points** across 5 benchmarks; on Qwen-4B, **+1.8 to +13.3 points**. On single-skill tasks, the 4B and 9B models match closed-source frontier performance.

### Why it matters

Extends the "language-level RL / self-editing" family to *skill libraries* rather than isolated prompts, and shows it particularly helps small models — critical for on-device / edge agents where full RLVR is out of budget.

## 10. Rhetoric Reward-Hack

**Authors:** Ming Li, Chenguang Wang, Xirui Li, Xinyue Zeng, Dianqi Li, Peng Shi, Dawei Zhou, Tianyi Zhou — *(academic consortium)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.08975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08975) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.08975) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.08975)
**Topics:** `safety`, `eval`, `LLM-judge`

### TL;DR

Constructs 4,200 controlled full-paper rewrites derived from 120 anonymized ICLR 2026 submissions, then measures how LLM reviewers react to rhetorical shifts (evidence framing, novelty positioning, hedging, etc.) with scientific content held constant. Diagnoses reward hacking by rhetoric — even under strict-review protocols.

### Key idea

Isolate rhetoric from content: two LLM rewriters transform each submission along six rhetorical dimensions in opposing directions, five LLM reviewers score them under standard *and* strict protocols. The design cleanly attributes score deltas to *how* things are said rather than *what* is said.

### Main finding

Evidence framing and novelty positioning yield the strongest sign-flipping effects on AI-reviewer scores; iterative rewriting amplifies them, and stricter protocols only partly mitigate. In short: LLM peer-reviewers are demonstrably rhetoric-hackable.

### Why it matters

The peer-review-with-LLMs pipeline is being deployed live at major venues. This paper gives conference chairs a quantitative case that the current protocols admit an adversarial channel, and hands the LLM-judge community a controlled corpus for measuring rhetorical robustness.

## 11. Massive Activations HLA

**Authors:** Zunhai Su, Bohan Sun, Xialie Zhuang, Shuibai Zhang, He Xiao, Jing Xiong, Hengyuan Zhang, Zhongzhu Zhou, Tiantian Zhang, Ngai Wong, Chuan-Wei Kuo — *HKU / consortium*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.12149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12149) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.12149) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.12149)
**Topics:** `interp`, `architectures`, `LLM`, `quantization`

### TL;DR

First systematic study of **massive activations** (MAs) in hybrid linear-attention LLMs. Discovers two architecture-aligned morphologies: **pre-attention spikes (PAS)** — MAs consistently spike right before every full-attention layer — and **inter-spike plateaus (ISP)** — MAs persist through intervening linear-attention layers, connecting successive PAS.

### Key idea

MA behavior in hybrid models is structured by *where* full-attention layers sit in the stack, not by data. As full-attention density rises, PAS connect through ISP and recover the stable MA morphology of dense-attention LLMs — placing hybrids on a continuum with full-attention models. A shared "write-sink-cancel" lifecycle explains both morphologies via when cancellation happens.

### Main finding

The pattern recurs across 5 linear-attention architectures, 6 hybridization configs, 5 data domains, and models spanning 1.2B → 397B. Controlled 1.3B pre-training shows both morphologies emerge early; full-attention output gating attenuates magnitudes without eliminating the layerwise pattern.

### Why it matters

Massive activations are the main obstacle to aggressive quantization; understanding *why they appear where they do* in hybrids gives quantization designers concrete rules (e.g. protect the pre-attention layer, expect plateaus in linear-attention blocks) rather than empirical heuristics.

## 12. Full-bandwidth Transformer

**Authors:** Xi Wang, Ziyang Cai, Zheng Zhan, Harry Dong, Ying Fan, Gustavo de Rosa, Tim Pearce, John Langford — *Microsoft Research*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.08888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08888) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.08888) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.08888)
**Topics:** `architectures`, `LLM`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

An autoregressive transformer that widens the **vertical** feedback channel between decoding steps: at each step, the previous top-layer hidden state is fused with the next input embedding through a gated linear unit before re-entering the bottom of the stack. In standard transformers only the *sampled token* is fed back; here the full hidden state joins it.

### Key idea

A transformer computes on two axes — horizontal across tokens (dense attention) and vertical through depth (residual stream). The vertical channel between consecutive decoding steps is currently 1 token wide; the paper argues this is a needless bottleneck and proposes a minimal GLU-gated fusion of the previous top hidden state with the new input embedding, adding constant per-step cost.

### Main finding

Consistent perplexity / downstream improvements over standard autoregressive transformers of matched size — the "latent feedback" costs one small GLU per step but recovers signal the sampling operation currently throws away.

### Why it matters

If the improvement holds at scale, this is an appealingly cheap architectural change: no new parameters per layer, no attention modification, no training-time incompatibility. It also connects to the recurrent-memory work (Maglev, this digest) by widening the recurrent state — but does so without abandoning parallel training.

### Knowledge graph integration

**Builds on / relates to existing pages:**

## 13. Maglev

**Authors:** Bo Liu, Qiang Liu — *UT Austin*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.02870](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02870) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.02870) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.02870)
**Topics:** `architectures`, `LLM`, `long-context`

### TL;DR

A recurrent transformer variant with **fixed-size sliding memory**: a prefiller (full attention) produces per-position memory targets; the decoder uses sliding-window attention with recurrent K/V injection to predict the next token. A memory-consistency loss aligns prefiller and decoder so training stays parallelizable.

### Key idea

Split the model into (a) a prefiller that has global context and produces memory K/V targets, and (b) a decoder that only sees a sliding window plus injected recurrent K/V. This gives fixed-memory inference (like classical RNNs) while preserving transformer-scale parallel training via the consistency loss.

### Main finding

Improved validation loss and downstream pretraining benchmark scores vs. plain sliding-window attention and latent recurrent transformers. Parameter sharing between prefiller and decoder further reduces memory footprint.

### Why it matters

Directly attacks the long-context memory problem without giving up either O(1) inference or transformer training throughput. Sits alongside Full-bandwidth Transformer as another "widen the recurrent channel" architectural line — but with a very different mechanism (K/V injection vs. hidden-state fusion).

## 14. Gambit (Thought-Level Beam Search)

**Authors:** Lijie Yang, Hongyin Luo, Jiawei Zhao, Tri Dao, Ravi Netravali — *Princeton (with MIT/Meta collaborators)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.08020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08020) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.08020) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.08020)
**Topics:** `reasoning`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

An inference-time search algorithm for large reasoning models that operates at **thought granularity** rather than token: at each thought boundary, keep the top-K promising traces (beam) and prune the rest, dynamically concentrating compute where reasoning looks fruitful. Cheap alternative to full tree search.

### Key idea

Both parallel sampling and MCTS misallocate compute — parallel sampling wastes it uniformly, MCTS spends it on expansion overhead. Thought-level beam search sits between them: prune per thought (not per token or per rollout), so wasted branches are killed early but the algorithm stays lightweight.

### Main finding

Up to **+6.7 points on HMMT-24** and **+3.3 points on AIME-25** while reducing token consumption by **68.5%** vs. parallel sampling.

### Why it matters

Test-time compute is the primary lever for reasoning-model performance, but it is currently spent naïvely. Gambit is a drop-in scheduler-level change that keeps most of the accuracy of aggressive search at a fraction of the tokens — an easy win for hosted reasoning services.

## 15. CaRL (Knowing When to Quit)

**Authors:** Xinyan Guan, Jiali Zeng, Chunlei Xin, Yaojie Lu, Hongyu Lin, Xianpei Han, Le Sun, Fandong Meng — *CASIA / Tencent*
**Links:** [arXiv:2607.29211](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29211) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.29211) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.29211)
**Topics:** `reasoning`, `RL`, `safety`

### TL;DR

Trains LLMs to **abort futile reasoning** — refuse instead of grinding out plausible-sounding-but-wrong derivations on tasks beyond their capability. Introduces **Capability-aligned Reinforcement Learning (CaRL)** which reward-shapes to prefer refusal over futile reasoning, and adds *hindsight refusal augmentation* that converts observed failures into new refusal supervision.

### Key idea

Futile reasoning is dangerous because the output looks confident. Rather than trying to make the model *solve* out-of-capability problems, CaRL teaches it to detect and abort them: reward = high for correct answers, high for refusals on unsolvable problems, low for confidently-wrong long derivations. Hindsight augmentation converts each rollout that ended in a wrong answer into a training example labeled "should have refused."

### Main finding

Model learns to distinguish tasks inside vs. outside its capability boundary; refusal rate on unsolvable problems rises without collapsing performance on solvable ones. Reduces confidently-wrong long CoT — the failure mode that most misleads users.

### Why it matters

Sits at the intersection of reasoning and safety: long-CoT RL currently rewards *any* answer with tokens attached, giving models a strong incentive to fabricate. CaRL is a concrete alignment-of-effort mechanism, and pairs naturally with existing hindsight-style relabeling in the reasoning-RL stack.
