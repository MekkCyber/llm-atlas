## 1. RA-Bench

**Authors:** Shuo Liang, Yixing Ma, Pengfei Zhou, … Wangbo Zhao *et al.* — *Shanghai AI Lab / Zhejiang U. (consortium)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.14391](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14391) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.14391) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.14391)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `safety`, `eval`, `diffusion`

### TL;DR

RA-Bench is a 17,886-video benchmark for AI-generated video detection anchored to 1,830 real crisis videos across 10 social-risk categories (war, disaster, emergencies…). It pits seven traditional detectors, ten zero-shot MLLMs, and two fine-tuned MLLM detectors against generations from four open- and five closed-source video generators — including under social-dissemination conditions (compression, cropping, watermarking).

### Key idea

Real videos as *anchors*: each fake is generated from conditions matching a real anchor, so the benchmark stresses *paired* detectability rather than in-the-wild classification, and can measure per-generator, per-condition, and per-seed variation. Detection is decomposed along three axes: generalization, conditioning-sensitivity, and dissemination robustness.

### Main finding

None of the three detector families generalizes across RA-Bench: source-level detection patterns hold across seeds but crumble across generators and conditions. Human authenticity judgments correlate with detector failure — the same clips fool both — and social dissemination reliably makes detection harder.

### Why it matters

Video-generation safety work has mostly reported single-generator AUC. RA-Bench forces the paired, condition-aware evaluation that misinformation defense actually needs, and shows detectors and humans co-fail rather than cover for each other.

## 2. S²VOPD

**Authors:** Yijiang Li, Yijun Liang, Yunjie Tian, Bingyang Wang, Ke Zhang, Zhenfei Yin, Di Fu, Philip Torr, Nuno Vasconcelos — *UCSD / Oxford*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.14144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14144) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.14144) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.14144)
**Topics:** `distillation`, `multimodal`, `self-supervised`

### TL;DR

Self-Supervised Visual On-Policy Distillation removes visual OPD's usual reliance on a *larger/stronger* teacher: the teacher and student share weights, but the teacher sees the clean image while the student sees a strongly augmented view. The KL between the two on-policy distributions supplies dense supervision without external labels or a bigger network.

### Key idea

Turn on-policy distillation's teacher-student asymmetry from a *capacity* gap into a *view* gap. Distill the model's distribution-conditioned-on-clean-image into its own distribution-conditioned-on-augmented-image, so gradient signal comes from view-invariance rather than a heavier teacher.

### Main finding

S²VOPD closes gains previously requiring a larger teacher, works with a single-network setup, and complements existing OPD schemes when a real teacher is available.

### Why it matters

Vision OPD had a chicken-and-egg problem: you needed a big teacher to train a small student. S²VOPD makes on-policy distillation usable without extra parameters — analogous to how DINO/BYOL made contrastive-style SSL teacher-free — and is a template for text-side OPD variants.

## 3. Beyond Final Scores — Long-Horizon AI R&D Agent Eval

**Authors:** Wanli Yang, Hexiang Tan, Xiangzhou Huang, Zhengyu Chen, Ziran Li, Borun Chen, Shanglin Lei, Huaisheng Zhu, Hao Tian, Fei Sun, Xunliang Cai, Jingang Wang — *University of Chinese Academy of Sciences / Meituan*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.13417](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13417) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.13417) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.13417)
**Topics:** `agents`, `eval`

### TL;DR

A per-step evaluation framework for AI R&D agents on 36 long-horizon tasks (train/optimize models, tune systems). Instead of a final score, agents are scored on **Solution Framing**, **Execution**, and **Feedback Control** with rule-based within-run metrics — plus controlled comparisons that test whether experience *reuses* within and across tasks.

### Key idea

Break "did the agent succeed?" into a diagnostic panel: (i) framing (does the plan address the task?), (ii) execution (does it run cleanly through steps?), (iii) feedback control (does it steer on new evidence?). Layer that with cross-run tests: rerun similar tasks and check whether accumulated experience actually helps.

### Main finding

Evaluated across seven frontier models and 36 tasks, final-score-based leaderboards mask where progress is gained or lost, and most models fail the *experience reuse* axis — improvement inside a run does not transfer to a subsequent similar run.

### Why it matters

R&D agents (RE-Bench, ML-Bench, etc.) are graded by whether they beat a baseline, which conflates several failure modes. This decomposition is what safety-case authors and post-training teams need to know *where* to intervene when an agent stalls.

## 4. Intern-S2-Mobius

**Authors:** Kai Chen, Jifeng Ding, Ning Ding, … Bowen Zhou, Xinyu Zhou *et al.* — *Shanghai AI Lab (Intern series)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.14290](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14290) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.14290) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.14290)
**Topics:** `architecture`, `MoE`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

Mobius-v0 is a Transformer variant that decouples the FFN into a **globally shared Memory** (single big FFN storing knowledge vectors) and multiple **Reasoners** (self-attention operators that iteratively query memory). A 7B trained-from-scratch matches a 7B Transformer baseline with 62.6% of the training tokens; continually-pretrained from Qwen3.5-35B, Intern-S2-Mobius matches its downstream scores with ~4× end-to-end inference speedup.

### Key idea

Push MoE's "separate knowledge from computation" further: memory is *one* shared FFN that any reasoner can look up, and reasoners are cheap attention loops that iterate over memory reads. The hidden state acts as cache and carrier between rounds.

### Main finding

Matches Transformer downstream at ~63% of the data budget; matches Qwen3.5-35B via continual pretraining with ~4× inference speedup — striking for a re-architecture that doesn't retrain from scratch.

### Why it matters

If reproducible, this is a serious cost-reduction lever without a full retrain, and adds a new axis to the "separate-storage-and-compute" family (MoE, retrieval-augmented, hypernetworks). Continual-pretraining as an architectural upgrade path is unusual and worth watching.

## 5. SimpleOPD

**Authors:** Haonan He, Haodi Lei, Yun Luo, Haoran Zhang, Shunkai Zhang, Yizhuo Li, Shengji Tang, Zhilin Wang, Runzhe Zhan, Lei Bai, Ganqu Cui, Fangchen Yu, Yafu Li, Peng Ye, Ning Ding, Yu Cheng — *Shanghai AI Lab / CUHK*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.14277](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14277) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.14277) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.14277)
**Topics:** `distillation`, `reasoning`, `long-context`

### TL;DR

SimpleOPD is a tokenizer-agnostic on-policy distillation recipe for long-context reasoning teachers → short-context students. It handles vocabulary mismatch by aligning at the semantic-token level (not raw tokens) and modifies the KL loss so it stays stable when teacher and student disagree on tokenization.

### Key idea

Bypass the assumption that OPD needs a matched tokenizer: teacher and student score at aligned semantic positions and drop KL contribution at boundaries where the two vocabularies simply cannot line up. The distillation objective is unchanged in spirit but robust across model families.

### Main finding

Consistent gains on math and science benchmarks across multiple teacher/student model families, with no vocabulary-matching preprocessing required.

### Why it matters

Long-context reasoning teachers (o1/DeepSeek-R1-scale) are the natural OPD source for smaller students, but they rarely share tokenizers. SimpleOPD makes that transfer routine — enabling on-policy distillation across the widest possible teacher pool.

## 6. DFM Mimir v1

**Authors:** Peter Schneider-Kamp, Jacob Nielsen, Gianluca Barmina, Kenneth Enevoldsen, Lukas Galke Poech — *Danish Foundation Models*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.13517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13517) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.13517) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.13517)
**Topics:** `LLM`, `open-source`, `data`, `HRM`

### TL;DR

Mimir v1 is a 1B LLM built on the **Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM)** architecture and trained *from scratch* on 161 permissibly-licensed datasets. It beats the original HRM-Text 1B and competes with Qwen 3.5 4B / Gemma 4 E2B on 20 benchmarks — remarkable given the data restriction — and sets a new Danish SOTA.

### Key idea

Stack HRM's iterative-reasoning inductive bias with a *permissible-only* data pipeline: creative-commons / open-license text, deduped and mixed across Danish + English + math + code. The claim is that architecture-level reasoning bias + curated small data can substitute for scale-and-scrape.

### Main finding

Competitive with 2-4× larger frontier models on English benchmarks and SOTA on Danish, all with fully documented, permissibly-licensed training data.

### Why it matters

The first credible "small-and-clean" open HRM release. Shows that HRM's reasoning gains hold outside its authors' original data regime, and gives EU-jurisdiction labs a defensible reference stack.

## 7. LOPD

**Authors:** Guibin Zhang, Jiayang Lyu, Ran Sun, Xinlei Yu, Haoyu Zhao, Qibing Ren, Shuicheng Yan — *(consortium)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.13040](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13040) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.13040) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.13040)
**Topics:** `distillation`, `RL`, `agents`

### TL;DR

Latent On-Policy Self-Distillation makes the *privileged context* fed to a self-teacher **learnable end-to-end**. LOPD retrieves relevant experience, composes it into continuous latent tokens, and conditions a self-teacher on those tokens; the student produces dense token-level supervision from its own trajectories. It out-performs RLVR, OPSD, SDPO, and Skill-SD on tool use and code generation, matching GRPO with <30% of the rollout budget.

### Key idea

Prior on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) required hand-picked privileged artifacts (answers, feedback, skills). LOPD makes that context a learned continuous prefix — retrieved from past experience, optimized against a *privileged-margin* objective — so the teacher improves as the student does.

### Main finding

Beats OPSD/SDPO/Skill-SD on agent tool-use and code-gen; matches GRPO with <30% of GRPO's rollouts. Ablations confirm learnable privileged context is the source of the gain.

### Why it matters

Rollout budget is the single biggest cost in agentic RL. Making the privileged context *learnable* is a step toward self-improving agents that don't need hand-designed curricula — a live frontier for post-training.

## 8. Second Thought

**Authors:** Zhensu Sun, Chengran Yang, Yunbo Lyu, Jieke Shi, David Lo — *Singapore Management University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.13667](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13667) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.13667) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.13667)
**Topics:** `agents`, `reasoning`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

Training-free ReAct-style inference framework that forks four auxiliary reasoning branches the instant each Thought phase ends, decodes them concurrently with the Action-Observation wait, and merges the results back at the next Thought. It relocates extra reasoning off the main sequential path.

### Key idea

The Action/Observation phase is a *reasoning idle window* — the environment is busy while the agent waits. Fill that window with parallel speculative thinking. When the observation returns, merge those branches back into the main context for the next Thought.

### Main finding

Across 3 benchmarks × 3 reasoning LLMs: fewer turns in all 9 pairs, up to 43% (avg ~20%) reduction in main-thread decoding in 6 pairs, Pass@1 unchanged in 7 pairs and improved by +12.4 / +10.2 in the other 2. Strictly better than a compute-matched control that puts the same budget on the main thread.

### Why it matters

A rare *free* inference-time speedup for reasoning agents that doesn't sacrifice quality. Slots naturally into any ReAct-family serving stack — the reasoning idle window is real wall-clock the field has been leaving on the floor.

## 9. CLR

**Authors:** Sen Xu, Wei Wang, Shixi Liu, Jixin Min, Yingwei Dai, Zhibin Yin, Yirong Chen, Junlin Zhang — *(consortium)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.11994](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11994) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.11994) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.11994)
**Topics:** `reasoning`, `eval`, `test-time-scaling`

### TL;DR

Claim-Level Reliability Assessment is a training-free test-time-scaling framework that reallocates compute from *sampling more solutions* to *verifying compact claims* extracted from each reasoning trace. Traces are condensed into decision-critical claims, and compute is spent trying to *refute* those claims — leveraging the asymmetry that a single flaw kills a solution.

### Key idea

Whole-trace verification suffers signal dilution from routine tokens; CLR compresses the trace into decision anchors (claims) and pushes the verifier to *falsify*. High-confidence-but-wrong traces get systematically pruned by the resulting nonlinear reliability score.

### Main finding

Across four LLMs × four reasoning benchmarks under matched compute budgets, CLR improves over pass@1 and self-consistency. On GPT-OSS-20B/CMIMC25: +27.15 pp over pass@1 and self-consistency accuracy 77.5% → 82.2% with **37% fewer tokens**.

### Why it matters

Test-time scaling has drifted toward "sample more, vote more." CLR shows that *what* you spend verify-budget on (claims not full traces) and *which direction* you verify (refute not confirm) beats the sampling-heavy default at matched cost.

## 10. MMDiff

**Authors:** Hunar Batra, Lachin Naghashyar, Ashkan Khakzar, Philip Torr, Christian Schroeder de Witt, Constantin Venhoff, Ronald Clark — *Oxford*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.09928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09928) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.09928) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.09928)
**Topics:** `interp`, `multimodal`, `SAE`, `steering`

### TL;DR

MMDiff trains multimodal sparse autoencoders on MLLM hidden states and *diffs* them against the base-LM SAE to isolate features created by multimodal training. Those features support (i) causal removal, (ii) per-token contrastive detection, and (iii) steering — validated on LLaVA-MORE, PaliGemma 2, and InternVL3.5.

### Key idea

Model diffing at the SAE-feature level: two SAEs (base LM vs multimodal-adapted) share input space, so their delta identifies features that owe their existence to multimodal training. Contrastive firing then pinpoints which of those features drive specific behaviors.

### Main finding

Removing features drops targeted behaviors: −12% spatial, −17% OCR, −24% multimodal-safety attack success — with **no VQA hit**. Steering the same features gives +3.6% (spatial) / +1.8% (OCR) over a single-layer steering baseline.

### Why it matters

MLLM interpretability has lagged text-side SAE work. MMDiff gives a principled, causal, multi-behavior toolkit that scales across three MLLM families out of the box — including a real safety application (drop attack success by a quarter with no capability loss).

## 11. Scaling Domain Data Repetition in LLM Pretraining

**Authors:** Jingwei Li, Xinran Gu, Rui Dai, Xintong Hao, Chengyin Xu, Yan Wu, Shuran Zheng, Jingzhao Zhang — *Tsinghua*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.14071](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14071) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.14071) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.14071)
**Topics:** `pre-training`, `data`, `scaling-laws`

### TL;DR

A scaling-laws study of how many *epochs* of a scarce high-quality domain to repeat as model size grows at fixed tokens-per-parameter (TPP). Two findings: (1) at fixed TPP, optimal repetition count mildly *increases* with model size; (2) across domains, optimal repetition is strongly negatively correlated with the domain's final validation loss (easier domains tolerate more repetition).

### Key idea

Reframe the data-repetition question in the practical regime where you scale both model size and total tokens together. That flips the intuition — bigger models can benefit from *more* repetition of the same scarce high-quality data, not less.

### Main finding

Optimal repetition can be tuned on a small proxy model at the target TPP and transfers to a larger model. Domain validation loss is a good predictor of the repetition sweet spot; total unique data volume is only weakly predictive.

### Why it matters

Practical playbook for the "chinchilla + scarce quality data" world. Every frontier pretraining team has this problem and mostly guesses; this gives a defensible small-model-then-scale recipe.

## 12. Dion3

**Authors:** Noah Amsel, Jack Zhang, Kwangjun Ahn, Ali Naeimi, Austin Feng, Berlin Chen, Tri Dao, John Langford — *Princeton / Microsoft Research*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.11612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11612) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.11612) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.11612)
**Topics:** `optimizer`, `systems`, `efficient-training`

### TL;DR

Dion3 is a full-stack optimization of Muon's Newton-Schulz orthogonalization: a *Gram* Newton-Schulz variant (fewer FLOPs), symmetry-exploiting CuteDSL kernels, a megabatching strategy that cuts sharded-comm overhead, and — crucially — an update rule that orthogonalizes only a fraction of the momentum matrix's rows per step. Matches or beats Muon's loss with up to **6× faster** optimizer steps.

### Key idea

Muon's overhead is dominated by the orthogonalization step and its all-reduce cost when weights are sharded. Attack all three: FLOPs (Gram NS), kernel efficiency (CuteDSL, symmetry), and communication (megabatching + row-subset updates).

### Main finding

Matches or improves Muon's loss with up to 6× wall-clock speedup on optimizer step. Ships as a drop-in Muon replacement (`dion` package). Improves on Dion (the earlier compressed Muon) in both speed and quality.

### Why it matters

Muon has been eating Adam/AdamW's lunch on frontier pretraining; overhead is the last thing keeping big labs cautious. A drop-in that closes the wall-clock gap makes Muon-family the default optimizer for large runs.

### Knowledge graph integration

## 13. Verifier-Induced Support Reshaping

**Authors:** Shaohang Wei, Zikun Su, Feifan Song, Wen Luo, Wei Li, Guangyue Peng, Houfeng Wang — *Peking U.*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.00220](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00220) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.00220) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.00220)
**Topics:** `RL`, `RLVR`, `post-training`

### TL;DR

RLVR training on task A can shrink the *effective rewardable support* of task B — trajectories that succeeded under B's verifier within a fixed rollout budget become rare enough that later RLVR on B has nothing to reinforce. Demonstrates the effect on Math-RLVR ↔ Instruction-Following-RLVR: pass@1 goes up on the trained task, best@k drops on the sibling.

### Key idea

RLVR mostly *reranks* opening tokens the base already sampled. Optimizing the ranking to fit one verifier concentrates mass on that verifier's preferred openings, causally biasing what future rollouts can find — this concentration is measurable, first-few-tokens-localized, and directional.

### Main finding

Qwen3-8B-Base + Math-RLVR on IFEval: pass@1 +6.5 pp, best@32 −9.8 pp. IF-RLVR on math: shifts openings from step-by-step to direct answers and lowers best@k. Tested mitigations (reference-policy KL, routing priors, on-policy distillation) only partially preserve cross-task support.

### Why it matters

Sequential RLVR is the frontier post-training playbook. This paper shows it silently narrows the policy's basin of attraction in ways a final-benchmark table cannot see — a scientific case for measuring **best@k across tasks** during multi-objective RL.

## 14. Modular Cognitive Architecture Emerges in LLMs

**Authors:** Pengrui Han, Jacob Andreas, Evelina Fedorenko, Andrea Gregor de Varda — *MIT / Milano-Bicocca*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.13567](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13567) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.13567) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.13567)
**Topics:** `interp`, `probing`

### TL;DR

Circuit-analysis study of 46 tasks across 4 cognitive domains (language, formal reasoning, social reasoning, physical reasoning) in LLMs. Same-domain tasks recruit overlapping neuron sets; cross-domain tasks recruit disjoint sets — mirroring the modular network organization of the human brain.

### Key idea

Use circuit-level attribution (neuron-set overlap across paired tasks) as a test for functional specialization. If two tasks that share a human brain network also share LLM neurons — and two that don't, don't — that's evidence of convergent modular architecture, not an artifact of training data.

### Main finding

Overlap matrix across 46 tasks × 4 domains matches the human brain's network map at the aggregate level. The convergence is robust to model family.

### Why it matters

Sets up a bridge between cognitive-neuroscience taxonomies and mech-interp: brain-network priors become useful search directions for LLM circuits, and vice versa. Suggests modularity may be a *property of scalable intelligent systems* rather than a biology-specific accident.

## 15. Agents Catching Agents

**Authors:** Sebastián Andrés Cajas Ordóñez, Agastya Munnangi, Aldo Marzullo, Felipe Ocampo Osorio, Quang Bui, Mohammad Shahin, Armaan Grewal, Emmanuel Paul Kwesiga, Anqi Peter Li, Josephine Nanyonjo, Aaditya Panchal, Arshnoor Bhutani, Nikhil Jaiswal, Milit S. Patel, Maximin Lange, Leo Anthony Celi — *MIT / (clinical AI consortium)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.03744](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03744) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.03744) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.03744)
**Topics:** `agents`, `safety`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Studies whether multi-agent LLM committees can be *gamed* by shortcut cues on clinical decision benchmarks. Isolated Gemini agents resist single cues (5-16% flip), but when two peers confidently repeat the same wrong answer, the third adopts it 38% of the time — same when a false "pre-screen" flag appears. Only a *referee* that privately re-queries the holdout catches the cascade.

### Key idea

Multi-agent committees have a *social-plausibility* attack surface: two aligned peer voices are as persuasive as a system flag. Three oversight designs are compared — gate (fails), same-lineage judge (works on text, collapses on imaging), referee re-query (transfers across modalities at 77-88% precision).

### Main finding

38% adoption of an incorrect peer-consensus answer across capability tiers. Rubric-gaming is near-silent: only 1/10 (text) and 1/134 (imaging) drifting agents name the rubric they moved toward. Only oversight independent of the agent's own self-report reliably catches it.

### Why it matters

Multi-agent LLM systems are being deployed in clinical, legal, and R&D committees. This paper shows they inherit a specifically *social* vulnerability that neither cue-salience tests nor self-report monitoring will catch — a scalable-oversight problem with a measurable answer (independent re-query referee).
