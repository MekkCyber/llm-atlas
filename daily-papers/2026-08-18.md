## 1. MOSS-VL Technical Report

**Authors:** Pengyu Wang, Chenkun Tan, Shaojun Zhou, Qirui Zhou, Yanxin Chen, … Xipeng Qiu (30+ authors) — *Fudan University / Shanghai Innovation Institute / MOSI Intelligence*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.15045](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15045) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.15045) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.15045)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `VLM`, `real-time`, `streaming`

### TL;DR

MOSS-VL is an open vision–language model family that treats **real-time perception-while-speaking** as a first-class capability rather than a bolt-on. The decoder ingests vision through gated cross-attention (so incoming frames don't fight the text KV cache), a synthesized interaction corpus supervises *when to speak, when to stay silent, when to revise*, and all real-time-specific training is compressed into a single light final stage on top of a strong offline foundation.

### Key idea

Three co-designed choices: (i) **vision arrives via gated cross-attention** into the decoder rather than being concatenated as tokens, freeing the model to generate while frames stream in; (ii) a **synthesized interaction corpus** turns the "when to speak" problem into a supervised label rather than an emergent behavior; (iii) a **staged curriculum** — heavy offline VLM pretraining, then a small final stage that adds real-time controls — so the offline capability doesn't have to be relearned.

### Main finding

Presented as a technical report — the paper's contribution is the recipe and the released open-weight family. The authors position it against streaming VLMs that pay quality tax to add real-time turn-taking; here the offline foundation stays intact and the real-time stage is cheap.

### Why it matters

Most open VLMs today are turn-based: you send a full image plus a prompt, they answer, done. Real-time voice+vision agents (screen-share assistants, live-video helpers) need "speak while seeing" as a native capability. MOSS-VL is one of the first open recipes for that stack.

## 2. StateM

**Authors:** Ziheng Qin, Yaxin Lu, Zhangyang Atlas Wang, Kai Wang — *Affiliation not disclosed on HF page*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.15089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15089) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.15089) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.15089)
**Topics:** `agents`, `eval`, `harness`, `long-horizon`

### TL;DR

StateM is a runtime "harness" that wraps an unchanged LLM agent with **durable state, phase-local context, checked transitions, recoverable runbooks, and versioned procedural practices** — an argument that most long-horizon agent failures are harness failures, not model failures. On Terminal-Bench 2.1, StateM lifts GPT-5.5 xhigh from the 83.1% reference to 92.1%, matching a much larger model, and reaches 95.3% raw accuracy at the top of the leaderboard.

### Key idea

Don't train — **scale the harness**. Explicitly model agent execution as a state machine with (1) durable state slots the agent must read/write, (2) phase-local context windows that hide irrelevant history, (3) checked transitions that reject illegal moves, (4) recoverable runbooks so a crashed step resumes rather than restarts, and (5) procedural practices that are versioned and human-inspectable.

### Main finding

- GPT-5.5 xhigh: 83.1% → **92.1%** on Terminal-Bench 2.1 without weight changes.
- Reaches **95.3%** raw accuracy overall — new SOTA on the benchmark.
- One reported configuration hits frontier performance for a **$15 run**.

### Why it matters

Reframes the "long-horizon agent" problem as a systems problem: the same model becomes qualitatively more capable with better runtime scaffolding. Sharpens the case that agent leaderboards without harness disclosure are close to meaningless.

## 3. HarnessEval-W

**Authors:** Weiliang Chen, Haowen Sun, Jun Gao, Jiawei Chi, … Fangfu Liu (30+ authors) — *Multi-institution consortium*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.16859](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16859) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.16859) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.16859)
**Topics:** `eval`, `agents`, `multimodal`, `world-models`

### TL;DR

Judging a world-model rollout means checking whether physics, causality, and world state evolve correctly — the kind of judgement that scalar metrics erase. HarnessEval-W runs the eval itself as an agentic pipeline: a **parent agent decomposes each case into measurable subproblems, spawns tool-equipped sub-agents**, and returns not just a score but a **verifiable evidence tree** justifying it. Applied to 18 world models over 330 cases with high alignment to human preference.

### Key idea

Move from a fixed rubric to a **hierarchical evaluation agent**. Each rollout is inspected by a parent agent that (i) reads the evaluation question, (ii) decomposes it into subproblems (physics plausibility, causal consistency, object persistence, …), (iii) spawns a specialized sub-agent per subproblem with tailored context and tools, and (iv) aggregates evidence into a final verdict — everything logged as an inspectable reasoning chain.

### Main finding

Applied to **18 world models over 330 cases**; verdicts closely align with human preference while producing per-rollout, per-subproblem diagnostics. Released as a *live* benchmark that grows as world models grow.

### Why it matters

Bench-as-agent is a general pattern. Any judgement task with a rich reasoning chain (world models, agent trajectories, long-form generation) is a candidate. It also directly generalizes the "LLM-as-a-judge" trend into a hierarchical, tool-using judge.

## 4. SA-MRPO

**Authors:** Yifei Chen, Haichao Zhang, Haozheng Luo, Xander Wu, Jie Ni, Yun Fu, Nuno Vasconcelos, Yijiang Li — *University of Florida / UCSD / Northeastern*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.16072](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16072) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.16072) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.16072)
**Topics:** `RL`, `post-training`, `reasoning`, `multi-reward`

### TL;DR

Standard multi-reward RL post-training scalarizes rewards with a **fixed weighted sum** *before* group-wise standardization (as in GRPO), which throws away information: two rollouts with very different reward profiles can end up with identical advantages, and gradient budget keeps flowing into already-saturated objectives. **SA-MRPO** standardizes each reward independently and adaptively down-weights objectives that batch-level statistics show are already saturated.

### Key idea

Two edits to the GRPO advantage:
1. **Per-objective group-wise standardization** — each reward gets its own z-score, so rollout profiles stay distinguishable.
2. **Saturation-aware reweighting** — batch-level estimator of how "solved" each objective is → smaller effective weight on rewards near their ceiling, more headroom for the rest.

### Main finding

Method paper. Empirically maintains performance on already-satisfied objectives while reallocating optimization toward the ones with remaining headroom — the pathology (identical advantages on distinct rollouts, wasted gradient on saturated rewards) is what's most cleanly demonstrated.

### Why it matters

Multi-reward RL is now the default for reasoning + safety + format + code post-training. If the community keeps stacking rewards, "which advantage estimator" matters more than "which base RL algorithm." SA-MRPO is a drop-in advantage change with no rollout-cost overhead.

## 5. ClawGym II

**Authors:** Huatong Song, Fei Bai, Ming Yang, Renyuan Li, … Ji-Rong Wen (20 authors) — *Renmin University of China*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.16798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16798) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.16798) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.16798)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `harness`, `sandbox`

### TL;DR

A unified **black-box RL** framework for training general agents through *arbitrary* harnesses: the training loop treats the harness as an opaque environment it can only step, not introspect. Combines **sandbox execution, trajectory reconstruction, and mix-harness training** so a single policy learns across many different harness contracts without per-harness engineering.

### Key idea

Train agents like you'd train a browser agent — **step the environment, don't own it**. Sandboxed execution isolates each rollout; a trajectory-reconstruction module rebuilds a coherent (state, action, reward) sequence from whatever the harness happens to expose; and mix-harness training samples across many different harnesses per batch so the policy generalizes rather than overfitting to one execution frame.

### Main finding

System-level paper. The pitch is *stability at scale* across heterogeneous harnesses — treating the harness as black-box removes the biggest source of pipeline breakage when a new agent framework lands.

### Why it matters

Ties directly to the StateM story above: harness variety is now the dominant source of agent-training complexity. A black-box, harness-agnostic RL framework is the training-side analogue of harness scaling.

## 7. An Empirical Study of Training Pixel-Space Text-to-Image Diffusion Models

**Authors:** Zanyi Wang, Mingzhe Zheng, Xiangpeng Yang, Huanqia Cai, Aiming Hao, Yuming Jiang, Peng Gao, Harry Yang, Steven Hoi — *Alibaba Token Hub / Alibaba Group / HKUST / Nanjing U. / UCSD*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.16887](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16887) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.16887) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.16887)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `T2I`, `training-recipe`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

Large-scale **pixel-space** T2I diffusion is famously slow to converge vs latent-space, so most modern systems ship as latent diffusion. This paper's recipe: **pretrain in latent space, then transition to pixel space during post-training** — a "latent-to-pixel" curriculum. With careful choices for weight init, data composition, prediction target, decoder architecture, and noise schedule, pixel-space models match or exceed latent counterparts while delivering **3.18–4.75× end-to-end inference speedups**.

### Key idea

Split the training budget: use latent diffusion for the expensive generative-prior acquisition phase (its native strength), then **hand the priors off to a pixel-space model in post-training**. Then systematically ablate the transition-time choices — how to initialize weights, what data mix to use, which prediction target (ε vs v vs x0), which decoder, which noise schedule — until pixel matches latent quality with a fraction of the inference cost.

### Main finding

- Pixel-space models **match or exceed** latent-space counterparts on the reported T2I benchmarks after the latent-to-pixel transition recipe.
- **3.18–4.75×** end-to-end inference speedups (no VAE decoder at inference time is the biggest factor).

### Why it matters

Latent diffusion won on training compute, not inference cost. If pixel-space matches quality with a straight ~4× speedup and no VAE artifacts, expect the frontier T2I stack to migrate — especially for interactive image editing and video, where VAE decoders dominate latency.

## 8. Agentic Transaction

**Authors:** Zhaoyan Sun, Xiaoxiao Wang, Guoliang Li — *Tsinghua University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.13900](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13900) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.13900) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.13900)
**Topics:** `agents`, `systems`, `reliability`, `long-horizon`

### TL;DR

Reinterprets **ACID** (atomicity, consistency, isolation, durability) for LLM agent systems. Long-horizon agent workflows fail like partially-applied database transactions — half-completed side effects leave the world inconsistent. **Agentic Transaction** wraps agent execution in a semantic transaction manager that gives *Semantic* ACID guarantees over multi-step tool calls, and reports a 10.6% improvement over comparable systems.

### Key idea

Map database primitives onto agent execution:
- **Semantic atomicity** — if the workflow can't finish, its already-applied side effects are semantically rolled back (not just deleted state, but tool-level compensation).
- **Semantic consistency** — invariants over external systems are checked at boundaries.
- **Semantic isolation** — parallel agent workflows don't interfere.
- **Semantic durability** — committed effects survive crashes.

### Main finding

**+10.6%** over comparable agent systems on the paper's long-horizon workflow benchmarks. First-class treatment of rollback + isolation for agent tool calls.

### Why it matters

Agents are being deployed against real systems (email, calendars, code repos, payments) where partial failure is worse than no attempt. ACID-for-agents is the missing systems abstraction between "the agent tried" and "the world is consistent."

## 9. How Do Agents Fail on AutoResearch

**Authors:** Yanlin Fei, Nazhou Liu, Xinmiao Yu, Shaolong Chen, Lei Li, Rahul Thapa, Madalina Ciobanu, Qingqing Mao, Ritankar Das — *Prentis AI / Titan Research Labs*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.14905](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14905) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.14905) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.14905)
**Topics:** `agents`, `eval`, `reasoning`, `research-agents`

### TL;DR

**AutoResearchEval**: 100 real-world frontier research tasks across 7 scientific domains, evaluated end-to-end through the *full* research workflow rather than one-shot QA. From 800 agent trajectories across 8 harness × model combinations the authors derive an **AutoResearch Failure Taxonomy** of 45 patterns, with the headline diagnosis that **current agents lack a metacognitive loop** — they don't check what they produced against what was asked, and don't revise when findings don't match.

### Key idea

Evaluate not the answer but **the whole workflow**: question formulation → literature search → experiment plan → execution → analysis → write-up. Log 800 trajectories, cluster failure modes into 45 patterns, then organize them into a taxonomy that separates *knowledge failures* from *procedural failures* from *metacognitive failures*.

### Main finding

- **100 tasks × 7 domains × 8 harness/model combos → 800 trajectories.**
- **45 distinct failure patterns** organized into the AutoResearch Failure Taxonomy.
- Central diagnosis: absent metacognitive loop — no self-check, no revision when outputs don't align with the goal.

### Why it matters

Every "agentic scientist" demo skips the failure catalogue. This paper is the failure catalogue. It also gives a concrete axis (metacognitive loops / self-checking) that training and harness work can now target.

## 10. Large Discovery Models

**Authors:** Zhongwei Yu, Yan Song, Xue Yan, Anjie Liu, Xingyu Lu, Yihang Chen, Huichi Zhou, Siyuan Guo, Luoyang Sun, Sihan Chen, Xiangning Yu, Jun Wang — *Affiliation not disclosed*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.15669](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15669) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.15669) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.15669)
**Topics:** `RL`, `search`, `generative`, `open-ended`

### TL;DR

A general recipe for **model-based open-ended search** over expensive black-box objectives: pair a generative model (proposes candidate designs) with a **Bayesian non-parametric reward surrogate** (predicts performance + uncertainty). The surrogate steers the generator toward high-uncertainty, high-value regions, and updates online as experimental results land. Applied to neural-net training, antibody design, and molecular optimization with substantial gains across the three.

### Key idea

Treat discovery as a **generator + surrogate** loop:
1. Generator proposes a candidate design (network hyperparams, protein sequence, molecule).
2. Bayesian non-parametric surrogate scores it with a calibrated uncertainty.
3. Acquisition combines predicted value and uncertainty to pick what to actually evaluate.
4. Real evaluation returns → surrogate updates → next round.

### Main finding

- **2.4×** greater reduction in validation BPB (LM training as the black-box objective).
- **-18.2%** binding energy on antibody design.
- **>60%** relative gain on molecular multi-objective optimization.

### Why it matters

The "AI-for-science" story often skips the search algorithm entirely. LDM shows that pairing a generative proposer with a *calibrated* surrogate is where the real gains live — and the recipe is model-agnostic enough to apply to LM hyperparameter search and RL curriculum design.

## 11. GenRouter

Frame agentic image generation as a routing problem: given a prompt, pick from a library of workflows (fast text-to-image, LoRA-styled generation, edit-first, multi-model ensemble, …). ### Main finding

- **>95% cost reduction, 65% latency reduction** vs the static heavyweight pipeline.
- Superior visual alignment on the reported benchmarks.

### Why it matters

Model routing is emerging as its own capability — for LLMs (Portkey, RouteLLM), for coding agents, and now for image generation. GenRouter is a concrete recipe for the image-gen slice with a live self-improvement loop.

## 12. R³-Bench

**Topics:** `eval`, `reasoning`, `agents`, `budget-allocation`

### TL;DR

Reasoning models are usually benchmarked one-problem-at-a-time — but real deployments have to *share a compute budget across many problems at once*. **R³-Bench** evaluates six-problem suites under a shared inference budget and finds a stark gap: across 72 main-table cells the "oracle" (best per-problem allocation) matches or exceeds the current-practice mean **in all 72** and is strictly higher in **71**. No single allocation policy dominates across math / competitive programming / abstract reasoning.

### Key idea

Change the evaluation frame from **per-problem** to **per-suite-under-budget**: give the model a suite of six problems and one shared thinking budget, force it to allocate. Report contest-mean (what the model actually does) vs oracle-mean (what an oracle allocation would achieve) — the gap *is* the diagnosis.

### Main finding

- **6-problem suites, shared budgets, 6 models, 72 cells** = the main table.
- Oracle ≥ contest in **72/72**, strictly greater in **71/72**.
- **No allocation policy dominates** across the three domains.

### Why it matters

If you deploy a reasoning model as a batch API or as an agent making many child calls, budget allocation across sub-problems is the dominant cost lever. R³-Bench isolates that skill and shows current models are visibly bad at it.

## 13. HarmProfile

**Authors:** Zhouyuan Ma, Yutao Wu, Hanxun Huang, Xiang Zheng, Xiao Liu, Yixin Cao, Zuxuan Wu, Xingjun Ma, Yu-Gang Jiang — *Fudan University (Y-G Jiang group)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.14577](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14577) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.14577) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.14577)
**Topics:** `safety`, `eval`, `red-teaming`

### TL;DR

**HarmProfile** is an **80,000-artifact** dataset built from 23 frontier LLMs across 13 model families, organized into **15 harm categories and 57 subcategories**. Beyond a single "safe/unsafe" score, it *profiles* each model — showing that both **harmfulness and diversity of harmful outputs grow with capability**, and that distinct models exhibit distinct risk shapes.

### Key idea

Move from a scalar "refusal rate" to a **distributional harm profile** per model: for a fixed elicitation set, log every produced artifact, taxonomize into fine-grained categories, and produce a per-model *profile* rather than a single number. Reveals that two models with similar aggregate safety scores can have very different failure shapes.

### Main finding

- **80,000+ validated harmful artifacts** across **23 frontier LLMs, 13 families**.
- Fine-grained taxonomy: **15 categories, 57 subcategories**.
- Both **harmfulness and diversity increase with capability**.

### Why it matters

Aggregate refusal rates hide the shape of the risk. Distributional profiles give safety teams and regulators the granularity they need to target red-teaming and to tell models apart on the axes that matter for their deployment.

## 14. Gathered, Not Admitted

**Authors:** Parsa Mazaheri — *UC Santa Cruz*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.15022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15022) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.15022) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.15022)
**Topics:** `interp`, `mech-interp`, `attention`, `probing`

### TL;DR

Challenges the "**admission**" hypothesis that a latent variable sits at the query position and a gate lets it be read. Using **Jacobian lenses** on open-weight models, the paper argues instead that **attention *gathers*** the variable into a readable position within a specific mid-depth window — with two hard edges: below the window the variable doesn't survive, above it gets destroyed. Also shows that shifting readout logits is a **poor proxy** for actual component usage.

### Key idea

Two claims, both operationalized with Jacobian lenses:
1. **Gathering, not admission.** Attention transports a latent quantity into a verbalizable position, concentrated in a mid-depth window (transport at least **17×** above anywhere shallower).
2. **Readout shifts are misleading.** Three components can move readouts within **12%** of each other while differing **7.4×** in behavioral impact — so "does this component change the logit" is not the same question as "does this component do the work."

### Main finding

Concept lens visibility rises by **+0.050 [+0.045, +0.057]** under demand; the gathering window has measurable survival-below and destruction-above edges. Cleanly separates *readability* from *usage* as two distinct claims about a component.

### Why it matters

If readout-based interpretability is measuring something *different* from behavioral usage, a large chunk of the mech-interp literature needs revisiting. The gathering-window story also gives a concrete geometric picture of where "verbalizable" state lives.

## 15. DumpsterCluster

**Authors:** Cheuk Hang Lau, Ilia Shumailov, Yiren Zhao — *University of Cambridge / University of Oxford / Quettaflop AI*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.14614](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14614) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.14614) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.14614)
**Topics:** `inference`, `systems`, `sustainability`, `pipeline-parallel`

### TL;DR

*Borderline: included because inference-systems + sustainability sits inside the "how we serve" scope even though the paper's contribution is largely empirical.*
A 128-GPU cluster built from second-hand datacenter V100s at **$22K vs $600K** new. With pipeline-parallel tuning it serves LLaMA-70B at competitive throughput — but the environmental picture is the other half: carbon per token is **~4×** higher for 8B models and **>40×** for 70B vs current hardware on a standard grid. Repurposing only wins environmentally under cheap renewables.

### Key idea

Two coupled experiments: (i) how do you actually make a heterogeneous, retired-GPU cluster serve a 70B-class model at reasonable throughput (answer: pipeline-parallel with careful stage balancing and asynchronous scheduling); (ii) once serving works, what is the carbon accounting? — surfacing that hardware-repurposing is a compute-per-dollar win but not automatically a compute-per-CO₂ win.

### Main finding

- **$22K** cluster vs **$600K** new-hardware equivalent.
- Serves LLaMA-70B with pipeline-parallel at competitive throughput.
- Carbon per token: **~4×** for 8B, **>40×** for 70B under standard grid conditions.

### Why it matters

The default "reuse old hardware for LLM inference" narrative is mostly correct on capex and mostly wrong on carbon. Gives operators an explicit break-even lens (grid mix and load factor) rather than an unqualified endorsement.
