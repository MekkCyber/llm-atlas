## 1. Zetta ζ: An Efficient Closed-Loop Embodied Harness for Self-Evolving Physical Intelligence

**Authors:** Xin Ding, Liang Mi, Mingzhe Huang, Zixuan Wang, Chao Zhang, Zixu Hao, Fu Chen, Xiangyu Li, Yikai Zheng, Yaoyu Guo, Weijun Wang, Kun Li, Hao Wu, Yunxin Liu, Ting Cao — *(institutional affiliation not disclosed on the arXiv abstract page)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.16590](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16590) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.16590) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.16590)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `robotics-fm`

### TL;DR

Zetta is a closed-loop embodied-agent harness that keeps a frozen base policy but evolves code-based **runtime critics** and **recovery skills** on-line while the robot acts. Three timescale-separated loops (action-frequency governance, per-rollout critic/recovery proposal, validation-gated skill update) let the harness react to state changes faster than the base agent can be re-prompted.

### Key idea

Rather than reflect *after* an episode ends, generate small executable "critic" and "recovery" snippets that guard specific sub-skills and validate them before promoting them into the skill library. A separate infrastructure layer, **Z-Infra**, decouples agent logic from heterogeneous execution resources so rollouts stream cheaply.

### Main finding

State-of-the-art on **LIBERO-Pro (90.8%)** and **RoboCasa (93.6%)** with an **11.1× inference speedup**; success keeps scaling with self-exploration experience, learned skills transfer zero-shot, and clear "aha moments" (spontaneous recovery behaviours) emerge.

### Why it matters

Establishes a scaling axis for embodied agents that is orthogonal to base-model size — you spend rollout compute rather than training compute. Bridges the "agent framework" and "policy learning" traditions and gives VLA models a tractable path to online improvement without retraining.

## 2. Co-RL: Unsupervised Reasoning Emerges from Diverse Cohort in Multi-agent RL

**Authors:** Yunhao Yang, Yuexin Bian, Yunjie Tian, Di Fu, Tianjin Huang, Yuanyuan Shi, Ziang Xiao, Nuno Vasconcelos, Yijiang Li — *UC San Diego / Johns Hopkins*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.17253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17253) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.17253) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.17253)
**Topics:** `RL`, `reasoning`, `multimodal`

### TL;DR

Co-RL trains a **cohort of decoupled models** (no shared parameters) simultaneously with RL, where each model's reward comes from its **peers' agreement/disagreement** rather than any ground-truth verifier. Reasoning ability emerges purely from cohort diversity.

### Key idea

Replace the verifiable reward in RLVR with a peer-derived signal: models vote/critique on each other's answers, and a policy is reinforced when its output survives peer scrutiny. Diversity across the cohort keeps the reward informative and blocks collapse.

### Main finding

Consistently beats base models and prior label-free approaches, matches or surpasses supervised RLVR on several benchmarks: **+3.0–8.6% on text reasoning** and **+2.3–7.2% on multimodal reasoning** — with **zero ground-truth labels**.

### Why it matters

If peer-reward RL can match RLVR on tasks with verifiers, it plausibly extends RL post-training to domains where no verifier exists (open-ended reasoning, creative writing, dialog quality) — the biggest open bottleneck after RLVR.

## 3. OmniScientist: An Omni-Modal Omni-Discipline AI Scientist

**Links:** [arXiv:2608.13558](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13558) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.13558) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.13558)
**Topics:** `agents`, `multimodal`, `eval`

### TL;DR

An AI-scientist harness that ingests **heterogeneous raw evidence** (images, signals, audio, video, 3-D structures) through a perception layer feeding three specialised agents (hypothesis, execution, manuscript). Evaluated across five discipline families to argue that lifecycle-wide multimodal perception — not just text — is required for grounded scientific discovery.

### Key idea

Split the AI-scientist loop into perception → hypothesis → execution → writing, and force perception to handle raw modality-native artifacts (images, signals, 3-D) rather than pre-digested text summaries.

### Main finding

Empirically the modality-native perception layer is the bottleneck for cross-discipline benchmarks: text-only pipelines lose evidence-grounding as soon as data leave the language modality.

### Why it matters

Reframes AI-scientist evaluation from a text-benchmark game to a **grounded-perception** game, pushing the field toward truly multimodal automated research pipelines.

## 4. SPADE: Self-Play in Adaptive Synthetic Executable Environments

**Authors:** Bo Liu, Simon Yu, Yiding Jiang, Ao Qu, Andrew Zhao, Zichen Liu, Junsu Kim, Zijian Zhou, Seungone Kim, Tongzheng Ren, Mickel Liu, Hanfei Yu, Zhaorun Chen, Weiyan Shi, Paul Pu Liang, Luke Zettlemoyer, Yejin Choi, Natasha Jaques — *UW / CMU / MIT / Meta*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.19197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19197) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.19197) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.19197)
**Topics:** `RL`, `agents`, `self-play`

### TL;DR

A self-play RL framework where a **single LLM plays two roles**: an *Environment Designer* that writes complete long-horizon training environments as executable code with an OpenAI Gym-style `reset()`/`step()` interface, and a *Reasoning Agent* that learns to act inside them. The environment pool grows adaptively as the agent scales.

### Key idea

Treat "environments" themselves as generated artifacts. Because the designer emits executable code with a standard Gym API, every generated task is automatically verifiable and can be dropped into any existing RL training loop.

### Main finding

Compared to hand-curated, static, or frozen-verifier training pools, SPADE keeps the goal distribution matched to the learner's ability and continues to improve rather than saturating — a scalable substitute for the finite pool problem.

### Why it matters

Attacks the "we're running out of RL tasks" bottleneck in agent training. If self-generated Gym environments hold up, RL post-training becomes an axis you can scale with compute rather than annotation.

## 5. Training Leaves Traces: Centered Residual Signatures for Language Model Lineage Verification

**Authors:** Aman Singh Thakur, Rayan Khoury — *Independent*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.14929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14929) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.14929) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.14929)
**Topics:** `interp`, `safety`

### TL;DR

A **data-free, white-box** method for deciding whether two open-weight checkpoints share ancestry. It extracts a *centered residual signature* per model and scores pairs symmetrically. Near-perfect separation of related vs unrelated models, and robust to weight-space laundering (permutation, sign flips, low-rank noise).

### Key idea

Even after fine-tuning, quantization, pruning, or merging, systematic residual structure inherited from the ancestor's training run survives. The signature is a per-checkpoint fingerprint that is invariant under common laundering transforms.

### Main finding

Correctly identifies lineage across the public LLaMA-2 family with near-perfect ROC. Survives common weight-laundering pipelines that break naive weight-similarity checks.

### Why it matters

Provides a practical **provenance tool** for open weights: enforcing licenses, detecting undisclosed base models, and auditing model families without needing training data or activations.

**Topics:** `eval`, `agents`

### TL;DR

FM-Bench evaluates whether LLM agents stay effective over **20 simulated football-management seasons** (~340–400 decision points, 26 tools per agent) in both solo and arena tracks. Fifteen frontier models were run.

### Why it matters

Reveals a capability gap invisible to short-horizon benchmarks. Gives the field a controlled way to study long-horizon reasoning and strategic behaviour without touching real markets.

## 7. Looped Language Models Improve Compositional Tool Calling

**Authors:** Andrei Cristian Popescu, Haitz Sáez de Ocáriz Borde, Pietro Liò — *Cambridge*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.18171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18171) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.18171) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.18171)
**Topics:** `agents`, `architecture`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

Applies **looped language models** (weight-shared recurrent transformer layers, variable inference-time depth) to compositional tool use. Under matched SFT recipes, recurrent computation reliably helps multi-step tool chains, while single-call API tasks see smaller and more model-dependent gains.

### Key idea

Recurrent depth at inference is a knob for tool-use accuracy. Adaptive inference — spending extra loops only on inputs that need them — dominates static-depth choices on the compute/quality frontier.

### Main finding

On API-Bank, BFCL, and NESTful, accuracy on **multi-step** tool use rises monotonically with recurrent depth. Adaptive inference reaches the same quality with much less compute than always looping to max depth.

### Why it matters

Suggests looped/recurrent architectures are a natural fit for **agentic** LLMs where step counts vary wildly per task. Positions inference-time depth as a first-class efficiency lever alongside speculative decoding and prefix caching.

## 8. The More Popular, The Harder to Forget: Adaptive Popularity for LLM Unlearning

**Authors:** Anna Borisiuk, Andrey Savchenko, Alexander Panchenko, Elena Tutubalina — *AIRI / Skoltech*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.14229](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14229) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.14229) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.14229)
**Topics:** `safety`, `unlearning`

### TL;DR

**AdaPop** replaces uniform unlearning gradients with **popularity-weighted** ones: high-frequency facts get exponentially larger penalties, low-frequency ones smaller. A token-confidence signal plus automatic penalty tuning holds catastrophic-forgetting damage down.

### Key idea

Popular pretraining facts are memorised more deeply and resist standard unlearning objectives longer. Scaling the unlearning loss by an estimated popularity/frequency term equalises effective removal strength across items.

### Main finding

Substantially reduced leakage of unlearned content vs baselines across multiple model families and unlearning benchmarks, with representations of forgotten items diverging further from originals than under uniform losses.

### Why it matters

A pragmatic knob for right-to-be-forgotten and copyright-removal workflows: acknowledges that "forget one fact" is not a single objective, it's a distribution over difficulties.

### Why it matters

Provides a repeatable recipe for **structured synthetic-data scaling** in an under-covered domain (creative writing) where existing corpora are narrow and evaluations are unreliable.

### TL;DR

**HOTFIXR** diagnoses per-language competency gaps and generates *targeted* synthetic data to patch them. Reports +6.2% in-distribution, +3.7% reduction in catastrophic forgetting, and +7.1% on out-of-distribution languages.

## 12. Bounded Agents: Delegation Security for Multi-Agent AI Systems

**Authors:** Xabier Muruaga — *Independent*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.15888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15888) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.15888) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.15888)
**Topics:** `safety`, `agents`

### TL;DR

The **Agentic Principal Chain (APC)** enforces delegation security across tool/agent calls by tracking who delegated what to whom via six authorization checks per request. In evaluation, drops **AgentDojo exfiltration from 75–100% to 0%** across all four domains and **blocks all 544 InjecAgent data-stealing cases**, with an 8.6–13.9 pp utility hit in some settings.

### Key idea

Move authorization from a static permission set at session start to a **dynamic principal chain** that is verified at every action, closing loopholes that appear when agents delegate to sub-agents or tools mid-session.

### Main finding

Near-total elimination of two well-known agent-exfiltration benchmarks with measurable but bounded utility loss — the first end-to-end delegation-security substrate for LLM agents.

### Why it matters

Agent systems today implicitly assume flat authorization; APC gives the field a concrete substrate for principled delegation, which is a prerequisite for any real-world deployment carrying private user data.

## 13. Temporal Multi-Signal Fusion for Token-Level Hallucination Detection

**Authors:** Igor Itkin — *Independent*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.18115](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18115) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.18115) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.18115)
**Topics:** `safety`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Instead of scoring each generated token independently, treat hallucinations as **temporally extended spans** and label them with a BiGRU over a **33-dimensional feature stream** (text statistics + NLI entailment + LM surprisal). No access to model internals required; works on closed-source models.

### Key idea

Confident-but-wrong tokens are the failure mode of independent per-token detectors. Fusing multi-source signals *and* reading them as a sequence lets evidence propagate from confident positions to ambiguous neighbours in a span.

### Main finding

**AUC 0.840 on RAGTruth**, +11 points over a per-token logistic-regression baseline (p = 0.002). The same 0.845 ceiling recurs across BiGRU, Mamba, and attention backbones — the bottleneck is the feature set, not the sequence model. Under 4% AUC drop on OOD generator families.

### Why it matters

A black-box, model-agnostic hallucination detector that generalises across generators — exactly the shape a production safety filter needs.

## 14. SemComp-Bench: Benchmarking Semantic Task Completion in Video Generation

**Authors:** Keyu Tu, Zhuowei Chen, Mengqi Huang, Yuxin Wang, Jiahao Zhu, Zhendong Mao, Yongdong Zhang — *USTC / DeepGlint*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.17426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17426) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.17426) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.17426)
**Topics:** `eval`, `diffusion`, `multimodal`

### TL;DR

Reframes video-generation evaluation from perceptual quality to **outcome-oriented task completion**: does the produced clip actually achieve the requested outcome while staying semantically grounded in the reference image and instruction? Six-domain dataset (SemComp-Data), VLM-graded binary questions, and Outcome-Achievement + Generation-Reliability scores.

### Key idea

Fidelity metrics reward pretty videos, not correct videos. Score generation with a VLM asking outcome questions, and measure two axes: was the outcome achieved, and did it come from the right causes?

### Main finding

Current SOTA video generators struggle to maintain task-relevant semantic grounding while achieving intended outcomes — the two axes trade off, exposing a gap perceptual metrics hide.

### Why it matters

Reorients video-generation research toward *usefulness* rather than *appearance* — a natural next step after Sora/VEO/Runway-style quality races.
