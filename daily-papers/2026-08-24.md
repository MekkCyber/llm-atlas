## 1. Let's Scale Step by Step (µP MoE HP Transfer) {#1-mup-moe-hp-transfer}

**Authors:** Nayeon Kim, Hojin Lee, Yunju Bak, Jaesun Park, Boseop Kim — *Korean industry team (affiliation not disclosed on arXiv)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.20061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20061) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.20061) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.20061)
**Topics:** `MoE`, `scaling-laws`, `optimizer`, `MLA`

### TL;DR

A two-step recipe for choosing the learning rate of huge Mixture-of-Experts models without doing a huge sweep: first use a Maximal Update Parameterization (µP) adapted for MoE + MLA + Muon so that the optimal LR transfers across model *widths*, then fit a scaling law across *token budgets* to extrapolate to trillion-token horizons. Validated on a 155B-total / 17B-active foundation model trained from scratch.

### Key idea

Take µP — the parameterization family that makes optimal LR width-invariant — and adapt it to the *modern* MoE ingredient stack: routed experts, Multi-head Latent Attention, and the Muon optimizer. Then run a small proxy at multiple token budgets, fit a linear scaling law of optimal LR against `log(tokens)`, and extrapolate to the full run's horizon.

### Main finding

Optimal LRs derived from a small proxy extrapolate to 10-trillion-token horizons with R² = 0.95. Their 155B/17B MoE trained with the extrapolated LR converges stably without further sweeps.

### Why it matters

Hyperparameter sweeps at frontier MoE scale are prohibitively expensive; every big-lab tech report describes some ad-hoc "we picked LR by scaling from a smaller run" story. This makes that step principled and predictable — µP-style transfer stitched together with a token-axis scaling law, worked out for MLA + Muon.

## 2. InfinityEdit {#2-infinityedit}

**Authors:** Yunze Tong, Mushui Liu, Canyu Zhao, Shiyi Zhang, Didi Zhu, Peng Zhang, Wanggui He, Jinlong Liu, Ying Chen, Hao Jiang, Pipei Huang, Bo Zheng — *Alibaba / Zhejiang University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.20910](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20910) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.20910) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.20910)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `video`, `adapter`

### TL;DR

Recasts instruction-based video editing for open-ended, *streaming* video: given a preceding segment plus an edit request, generate the next segment that continues the stream under the edit. A lightweight adapter with three attention modules bolts onto a pretrained streaming video generator so edits can be applied on-the-fly.

### Key idea

The adapter has three attention modules — history cross-attention (denoising frames conditioned on incoming input frames), temporal causal self-attention (past-to-future only), and edit cross-attention (injecting the edit instruction). It's activated only in the chunk where a new edit arrives; subsequent chunks fall back to the vanilla model with a reset anchor frame, preserving the base model's unbounded-generation ability.

### Main finding

Faithfully continues an ongoing video stream under sequential edit instructions and stays stable across unbounded chunks — where prior in-place video editors fail because they assume a fixed input clip.

### Why it matters

Real-world video editing use cases (live restyle, apply-camera-move to an ongoing shot) look nothing like the in-place assumption of most academic pipelines. Formalizes "infinite video editing" as a task and provides a plug-in adapter recipe that keeps the base generator intact.

## 3. ParaTempo {#3-paratempo}

**Authors:** Xuteng Zhang, Wenhao Zeng, Xiaodong Gu, Chao Hu, Haotian Lin, Yuling Shi, Min Wang, Beijun Shen — *Shanghai Jiao Tong University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.16425](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16425) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.16425) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.16425)
**Topics:** `reasoning`, `efficient-inference`, `parallel-reasoning`

### TL;DR

Training-free framework for asynchronous parallel reasoning. Each branch is periodically probed for its tentative answer distribution; a *temporal confidence* signal — how sharply recent probes concentrate on a dominant answer — drives pruning of low-confidence branches, early retirement of committed ones, and forking of new branches with the freed compute.

### Key idea

Instead of relying on final-answer voting or noisy per-token confidence, sample a probability distribution over answers *periodically inside* each branch. Temporal confidence measures the branch-local sharpness of these repeated probes. All control decisions (prune, retire, fork, global stop) fall out of this one signal — no synchronization between branches needed.

### Main finding

On math and science reasoning benchmarks: 21.8–32.2% lower average latency and 18.1–30.3% fewer total tokens vs standard parallel-reasoning baselines, with competitive accuracy. Temporal confidence is more predictive of future branch convergence than token-level or instantaneous signals.

### Why it matters

Test-time compute scaling via parallel branches is the dominant knob for pushing reasoning accuracy; the wall-clock and token cost, however, are what makes it prohibitive in serving. Getting a training-free, branch-local dial on that budget is the sort of thing that plugs immediately into inference frameworks.

## 4. OmniAssistBench {#4-omniassistbench}

**Authors:** Xianyun Sun, Chaoyou Fu, Zhengye Zhang, Feiyang Duan, Qingyuan Cao, Yonghui Niu, Sihang Yuan, Ge Zhang, Caifeng Shan — *NJU / Alibaba (affiliations inferred)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.21360](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21360) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.21360) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.21360)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `eval`, `agents`

### TL;DR

An interactive benchmark for omni-modal LLMs acting as real-time video assistants. To sidestep the "diverging interaction paths" problem, the dataset is built by *reverse-engineering* existing internet videos: deduce a plausible user goal, segment the video into multi-turn clips, and score whether the model can steer a user along the exact same route.

### Key idea

Real interactive assistant videos are rare and single-user, so a static benchmark can't capture branching interactions. OmniAssistBench provides predefined priors (derived from the source video) and evaluates whether the model *follows the same route*, which lets a static dataset score a fundamentally interactive task.

### Main finding

Gemini-3-Pro tops the leaderboard at 66.4/100; the strongest open model, Qwen3-Omni-Instruct, reaches 51.2. Models struggle with visual prompts (e.g., hand gestures), fail to maintain multi-turn history, and often respond before the target event actually occurs.

### Why it matters

There's a rush to release "omni" assistants (voice + video + text end-to-end), but there's essentially no interaction-level benchmark that stresses their assistant-style behavior over turns. Provides a concrete diagnostic that separates surface understanding from useful assistant behavior.

## 5. PatternEval + PatternRL (Hybrid-Thinking MLLMs) {#5-patterneval}

**Authors:** Xinming Wang, Weinong Wang, Hongming Yang, Yansong Lin, Zheng Ruan, Shangpin Peng, Qiming Peng, Nan Qiao, Fengyuan Lu, Guoqing Ma, Marito Li, Songyang Zhang, Saiyong Yang, Han Hu, Yonglong Tian, Xu-Yao Zhang — *Tencent / Meituan (inferred)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.12781](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12781) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.12781) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.12781)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `eval`, `RL`

### TL;DR

Hybrid-thinking MLLMs (those that alternate between deliberative and quick-response modes) frequently degrade in response *behavior* — CoT leakage into the answer, response repetition, contradictions, "performative" reasoning — even when correctness looks fine. PatternEval measures these failures on 2,415 multimodal prompts; PatternRL adds pattern-specific penalties in RL to mitigate them.

### Key idea

Move beyond correctness-only evaluation of hybrid-thinking models: enumerate four recurrent response-pattern failures (CoT leakage, repetition, logical contradiction, performative reasoning) and reward against them explicitly. PatternRM is a response-level reward model trained on these labels; PatternRL wraps it into RL with pattern-specific penalty terms.

### Main finding

Response-pattern failures are widespread across providers; non-thinking mode fails much more often than thinking mode, causing systematic mode-mismatch. Training Qwen3-VL-4B/8B with PatternRL cuts these failures with only a marginal accuracy trade-off.

### Why it matters

As hybrid-thinking becomes the default UI (Claude thinking-mode, DeepSeek-R1 non-reasoning switch, Qwen3's `enable_thinking`), the gap between "gets the right answer" and "delivers a decent response" is exactly the alignment story users see. This gives a concrete diagnostic + a working RL recipe.

## 6. On-Policy Distillation: Two Sides {#6-on-policy-distillation}

**Authors:** Zhaoyi Li, Deyang Kong, Yuan Wei, Evan Yang, Ranran Shen, Mahardika Krisna Ihsani, Ming Yang, Wei Zhang, Chuan Hao, Jian Yang, Ran Tao, Bryan Dai, Shikun Zhang, Wei Ye, Ying Wei, Defu Lian — *Peking U. / NTU (inferred)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.16647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16647) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.16647) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.16647)
**Topics:** `distillation`, `post-training`, `generalization`

### TL;DR

Controlled study of on-policy distillation (OPD) — supervising the student on trajectories sampled from *its own* policy. OPD transfers the teacher's *reasoning behavior* rather than its answers on particular problems; the transfer strength depends heavily on whether teacher and student share origin (same base model family), and mixing multiple teachers produces a mixture-dependent seesaw across capabilities.

### Key idea

Vary one generalization factor at a time — in-domain shift, cross-domain transfer, and multi-teacher settings — and isolate what OPD actually transfers. The controlling variable turns out to be origin: same-origin teacher-student pairs generalize broadly (languages, reasoning horizons, other domains); cross-origin pairs mostly overfit the trained distribution.

### Main finding

Training difficulty barely matters — even problems the teacher can't solve are useful. Same-origin OPD generalizes strongly; cross-origin OPD does not. Multi-teacher OPD trades capabilities off against each other in ways determined by the mixture, because per-prompt routing cannot confine each teacher's influence.

### Why it matters

Distillation is the dominant technique for producing small "reasoner" models (DeepSeek-R1-distill, Qwen3-distill, …). This paper is the first careful account of *when* it generalizes and why multi-teacher recipes often fail to compound.

## 7. UniSpace {#7-unispace}

**Authors:** Anonymous — *Affiliation not disclosed on arXiv*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.08676](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08676) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.08676) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.08676)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `MoE`, `architecture`

### TL;DR

Unifies understanding, generation, and editing in a single visual representation space built from a *frozen* pretrained semantic ViT. Introduces Patch Reparameterization — a reconstruction-aware patch embedding added alongside the original semantic pathway — so the frozen ViT blocks can carry both semantic abstractions and pixel-level detail. Scales the recipe into UniSpace, an 8B Mixture-of-Transformer-Experts model.

### Key idea

The bottleneck in unifying vision-language and image generation is that the *final tokens* of a semantic ViT discard fine-grained detail. The paper shows the frozen ViT blocks themselves aren't the problem — the patch parameterization is. Patch Reparameterization adds a separate reconstruction-oriented patch embedding into the same frozen blocks; the shared representation then handles understanding, generation, and editing without a separate VAE pathway.

### Main finding

The reparameterized ViT preserves multimodal understanding while enabling high-fidelity image reconstruction. Scaled to an 8B MoT-Experts model, UniSpace supports text-to-image generation and instruction-based image editing in one visual space.

### Why it matters

Every major unified-model attempt (Chameleon, Show-o, VAR, Janus, Emu3, …) has argued about whether to have a separate VAE/tokenizer for pixels or share one representation with the LLM. UniSpace makes the case that a frozen semantic ViT is enough — the *patch embedding* is the real hinge — and produces an 8B system to back the claim.

## 8. SparsePR {#8-sparspr}

**Authors:** Pardis Taghavi, Reza Langari, Gaurav Pandey — *Texas A&M University (inferred)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.18484](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18484) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.18484) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.18484)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`, `sparse-attention`, `video`

### TL;DR

Training-free sparse-attention operator for video generators and world models. Combines Response-Coupled Partitioning (routing queries by their K/V response, not by index adjacency) with Probe-Fitted Residual Reconstruction (an affine correction learned per call from a small set of exact query rows). Preserves generation quality while executing at ~25% of the dense attention pair budget.

### Key idea

Row-wise attention concentration alone doesn't yield a working sparse operator — queries sharing a route may cover disjoint K/V, and retained attention mass doesn't determine post-softmax error. SparsePR (i) *chooses* the partition by clustering sampled query-key responses so co-routed queries cover a shared support, then (ii) *repairs* the resulting sparse-vs-dense gap with a call-specific affine correction fit from a probe set of exact rows.

### Main finding

Across four video generation and world models: attention-reconstruction error consistently reduced vs prior training-free sparse attention. Preserves generation quality at 22.0–26.0% executed-pair density, delivering 1.48× to 2.61× end-to-end speedups.

### Why it matters

Video diffusion transformers spend most of their compute on attention because context lengths are enormous (frames × spatial tokens). Training-free sparse operators are the only way to accelerate deployed video models without retraining; SparsePR raises the bar on how much you can cut without visible degradation.

## 9. AgentMercury {#9-agentmercury}

**Authors:** Minbyul Jeong, Chanwoong Yoon — *KAIST / Naver (inferred)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.20634](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20634) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.20634) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.20634)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `data`

### TL;DR

Scalable framework for synthesizing *executable environments* for agent RL. Instead of building an environment per benchmark task, AgentMercury instantiates a persistent business "world" with entities, services, tools, state, and cross-service invariants — and lets tasks emerge from the world. Generated 4,783 executable environments across 14 industries and 50 countries.

### Key idea

Flip the environment-construction target: not a task-per-environment, but a *world-per-environment* with executable cross-service invariants. Diverse tasks and trajectories emerge naturally from the world. And crucially: the construction process itself is a learnable capability — fine-tuning on construction traces lifts held-out authoring success from 3.3% to 83.3%.

### Main finding

RL on these business-oriented environments improves Qwen3.5-4B from 12.3 → 15.7 on EnterpriseOps-GYM and — surprisingly — from 45.9 → 56.0 on AIME26, showing cross-domain transfer even though environments were not benchmark-targeted.

### Why it matters

The big open question in agent RL is where the environments come from. Manual construction doesn't scale; benchmark-targeted synthesis overfits. Scenario-grounded worlds provide a scalable path — and making world construction itself a trained skill closes the loop.

## 10. Daedalus-150M {#10-daedalus-150m}

**Authors:** Anonymous — *Affiliation not disclosed on arXiv*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.20210](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20210) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.20210) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.20210)
**Topics:** `architecture`, `efficient-inference`, `SLM`, `quantization`

### TL;DR

Small language model whose architecture was *designed for* CPU inference from day one — single-user, one token at a time, 4-bit weights, no GPU. Keeps full attention in only 6 of 18 blocks; the other 12 use short convolutions with a fixed 2-timestep memory, so two-thirds of the network never re-reads a growing KV cache.

### Key idea

Reverse the standard SLM workflow. Instead of scaling down a transformer and then squeezing it onto a CPU, fix the deployment target first and pick a block mix that respects it. Convolutional blocks with fixed short memory eliminate KV re-reads for most of the network; attention blocks are used only where cross-token context truly matters.

### Main finding

Trained from scratch on 59.9B tokens, Daedalus-150M scores 47.31 on a fixed five-task benchmark bar of 42.20 — beating GPT-2 124M, Pythia-160M, OPT-125M, GPT-neo-125M (all trained on 3–6× more data), and exceeding MobileLLM-125M's published score despite that model seeing a trillion tokens. Val bits-per-byte 0.8685.

### Why it matters

The narrative "just quantize a small transformer for the edge" is challenged by an architecture chosen for the target. Also a data-point that CPU-first architecture beats data-scale for SLM CPU deployment.

## 11. Llama-Mobile (2.7-bit VLM Quantization) {#11-llama-mobile}

**Authors:** Luka Ribar, Jeevan Bhoot, Douglas Orr — *Graphcore (inferred)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.21134](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21134) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.21134) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.21134)
**Topics:** `quantization`, `multimodal`, `mobile`

### TL;DR

Quantization pipeline for VLMs that uses *the model itself* to generate calibration data — no access to the training set required — plus a novel 2.7-bit-per-parameter weight format optimized for efficient Arm-CPU execution. Compresses Llama 3.2 11B Vision Instruct to 3.7 GB with 8-bit activations while preserving VQA performance.

### Key idea

Two moves. (i) Self-generated calibration data — the model produces the calibration prompts and completions, so no training-set dependence, which matters when the training set of a VLM is not available. (ii) A 2.7-bit weight format tailored to Arm CPU instruction paths for efficient execution.

### Main finding

Llama 3.2 11B Vision Instruct → 3.7 GB, 8-bit activations, preserved strong performance on standard visual QA benchmarks. Runs on mobile-class Arm CPUs.

### Why it matters

Below-3-bit is the frontier where quantization stops being a stopgap and starts changing what fits on-device. Also stakes a claim on a specific format (2.7 bit) whose exact instruction-level economics matter for mobile deployment.

## 12. CLEAR {#12-clear}

**Authors:** Chengxiao Wang, Enyi Jiang, Xiaojing Liao, Sanmi Koyejo — *Indiana U. / Stanford (inferred)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.21278](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21278) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.21278) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.21278)
**Topics:** `safety`, `LoRA`, `alignment`

### TL;DR

Conditional safety adapter: a lightweight hidden-state gate that continuously controls the activation strength of a *safety LoRA*. On benign prompts the LoRA stays largely off; on harmful ones it engages. Trims the utility tax that globally-applied SFT or standard LoRA safety tuning imposes.

### Key idea

Rather than tuning the frozen backbone globally (SFT) or applying a fixed low-rank correction (LoRA) that fires equally on every input, learn a gate over hidden states that decides *how much* of the safety adapter to apply per input. Benign inputs get near-identity behavior; harmful ones activate the safety correction.

### Main finding

Improved HarmBench robustness with substantially less utility degradation than SFT or standard LoRA baselines.

### Why it matters

Safety tuning almost always makes models dumber (overrefusal, style-shift, capability loss). A per-input conditional activation of the safety correction is a cleaner architectural answer than post-hoc jailbreak filters or global refusal training.

## 13. Graph Engineering in the Era of LLM Agents {#13-graph-engineering}

**Authors:** Yuyuan Feng, Zhishang Xiang, … Qinggang Zhang (35 authors) — *Jilin U. / consortium*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.21156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21156) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.21156) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.21156)
**Topics:** `agents`, `survey`

### TL;DR

*Borderline: included because it names and organizes the agent-engineering-paradigm space (Prompt, Context, Harness, Loop, and now Graph Engineering) — the taxonomy itself is a useful anchor for `agents/` even if the paper is a survey.* Argues that individual-agent optimization runs out at complex, long-horizon tasks; proposes "Graph Engineering" — explicit, evolving graph structures over tasks/agents/state — as the next paradigm for system-level agent intelligence.

### Key idea

Frame the last two years of agent tooling as a paradigm ladder: Prompt Engineering → Context Engineering → Harness Engineering → Loop Engineering → Graph Engineering. Graph Engineering makes tasks, agents, and system states first-class *graph* objects with explicit dependencies and evolving dynamics.

### Main finding

A structured survey plus reference implementations, released at github.com/DEEP-JLU/Awesome-Graph-Engineering.

### Why it matters

Even if you disagree with the "Graph Engineering" branding, the paradigm ladder is a useful mental model for `agents/` and gives concrete language for what changes as you go from a single agent to a coordinated multi-agent system.
