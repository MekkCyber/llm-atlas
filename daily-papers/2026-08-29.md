## 1. RLHEV: Agentic Game Development as a Verifiable Trajectory Data Engine {#1-rlhev}

**Authors:** Pengfei Zhou, Hexin Wang, Zhengfeiyang Zhang, Yixing Ma, Zhenglin Wan, Kaipeng Zhang, Wangbo Zhao, Yang You — *National University of Singapore*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.25518](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25518) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.25518) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.25518)
**Topics:** `RL`, `world-models`, `agents`, `data`

### TL;DR

Spatial/world-model generation is stuck on fuzzy proxies like CLIP scores that give lousy RL signal. The paper argues game engines are the missing analog of the compiler for code agents: an engine can *execute* a scene, cheaply verifying collision, physics, navigability and playability, while the human developer's accept/reject supplies the global reward. RLHEV bakes both signals into an RL post-training loop that scales world models via a recursive data engine rather than via more crawled video.

### Key idea

Reinforcement Learning with Human-Engine Verification (RLHEV): frame each generated scene as an *executable specification*. The engine returns dense automatic rewards (physics-consistent? navigable? playable within bounds?); the developer accept/reject signal on the same scenes provides an implicit human preference layer. A long-horizon game-dev trajectory dataset is emitted as a byproduct, feeding subsequent RL rounds.

### Main finding

Positioned as a paradigm paper: game-engine-verified RL produces cleaner reward gradients than CLIP-style proxies, and the data engine emits trajectories with grounded outcomes that scale world-model training without needing more raw video (concrete numbers to be validated once figures are posted).

### Why it matters

Suggests the same trick that unlocked reasoning RL — an executable verifier (compiler/tests) — is what's missing for spatial generative models. If it works, it's a template: any generative domain with a runnable simulator (physics, chemistry, games) gets an RLVR-shaped path.

## 2. PAWBench: How Far Are We from Probabilistically Aligned World Modeling? {#2-pawbench}

**Authors:** Yuandong Pu, Le Zhuo, Sayak Paul, Gabriel Jorge Menezes, Avram Đorđević, Shiyang Li, Yifan Zhou, Bin Fu, Wenlong Zhang, Junjun He, Yu Qiao, Yihao Liu, Jingbo Xing, Xi Chen — *Shanghai AI Lab / HuggingFace / academic consortium*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.27345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27345) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.27345) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.27345)
**Topics:** `eval`, `world-models`, `diffusion`

### TL;DR

Video "world models" are usually judged one rollout at a time (does this clip look plausible?), which conflates *distributional* alignment with *individual* plausibility. PAWBench formalizes probabilistic alignment — a world model should reproduce the *distribution* of physically valid futures under repeated sampling — and benchmarks 11 current systems across 50 physical scenarios. None of them consistently match reference outcome distributions.

### Key idea

PAWEval: replay each scenario many times, convert the video rollouts into empirical outcome distributions, and score them against a reference distribution (rather than picking the "best-looking" single clip). Probes whether language prompts, initial noise seeds, or targeted training reshape the model's predictive distribution.

### Main finding

Across 50 scenarios × 11 systems, **no model consistently recovers reference probabilities** while also covering the full range of valid behaviors. Modulating prompts / noise / training changes distributions but doesn't close the gap. A distribution-level evaluation gap is quantified for the first time.

### Why it matters

Reframes the "video-as-world-model" claim: matching a single trajectory is not enough — a genuine world model has to be a calibrated *sampler* of futures. The benchmark gives the field a target beyond FVD-style plausibility metrics.

## 3. TTPO: Test-Time Policy Optimization {#3-ttpo}

**Authors:** Aozhe Wang, Zhengxi Lu, Jianze Wang, Shangke Lv, Ying Liu, Weiming Lu, Jun Xiao, Yueting Zhuang, Hua Yang, Qianglong Chen, Yongliang Shen — *Zhejiang University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.27448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27448) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.27448) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.27448)
**Topics:** `RL`, `reasoning`, `test-time-training`, `post-training`

### TL;DR

Test-time training with majority-vote pseudo-labels is fragile: one wrong vote poisons the teacher for every token. TTPO exploits an asymmetric empirical fact — rollouts that *disagree* with the pseudo-label are usually wrong regardless of whether the vote itself is right. It distills agreeing rollouts via on-policy self-distillation while pushing disagreeing rollouts down with grouped RL, and adds token-level gating so distillation ignores already-converged tokens and RL only penalizes *confident* errors.

### Key idea

Two-branch asymmetric objective on unlabeled prompts: (a) OPSD-style distillation on rollouts that agree with the majority vote, downweighted where token-level confidence has plateaued; (b) grouped-RL penalty on rollouts that disagree with the vote, applied only to confidently-wrong tokens. Majority-vote routing tightens as the model improves — a self-supervised bootstrap.

### Main finding

Without any labels, TTPO **matches label-supervised OPSD on five competition-level math benchmarks**. Raises Qwen3-1.7B from 38.0 → 45.2% in the TTT setting, +25.2% to +36.4% in the "no-thinking" regime, with strong cross-task generalization.

### Why it matters

Turns unlabeled test-time data into a reliable training signal for reasoning without a verifier or a reward model — a route to keep improving models on distribution shift after they ship, without human labels.

## 4. Self-OPD: On-Policy Distillation for Flow Matching without Teacher {#4-self-opd}

**Authors:** Shiyi Zhang, Mushui Liu, Yunze Tong, Wanggui He, Siyu Zou, Jinlong Liu, Yunlong Yu, Jian Song, Hao Jiang, Pipei Huang, Bo Zheng — *Zhejiang University / Alibaba*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.26872](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26872) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.26872) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.26872)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `RL`, `distillation`, `flow-matching`

### TL;DR

On-policy distillation for flow-matching normally requires training a task-specific teacher and then distilling the student against it. Self-OPD kills the teacher: at each timestep it branches the deterministic next-state prediction into K stochastic SDE candidates, scores each against a deterministic self-reference baseline, and pushes the velocity field toward high-advantage branches and away from low-advantage ones — GRPO-style but for flow-matching velocity fields.

### Key idea

Group Relative Policy Optimization ported to flow matching: K SDE branches per timestep, normalized advantages against a deterministic self-baseline, all-branch pull-push velocity update with direction-aware attenuation and SDE-variance normalization. For multi-objective alignment, scores are fused at the **reward level** (rather than field level), avoiding cross-objective gradient conflict — the paper flags this as the empirically dominant choice.

### Main finding

Beats prior RL/OPD baselines on single-reward and mixed-reward image benchmarks *without task-specific teachers*, both saving the teacher-training cost and removing teacher/student distributional mismatch.

### Why it matters

Extends the GRPO recipe from text to continuous generative processes. Removes the "train a teacher per task" bottleneck that has held back OPD for diffusion/flow-matching alignment.

## 5. What Makes Good Agentic Data? An ACE Lens {#5-ace}

**Authors:** Xingshan Zeng, Zishan Xu, Boju Zhang, Yuzhou Wu, Lingzhi Wang, Jianghao Lin, Liangyou Li, Yasheng Wang, Lifeng Shang, Xin Jiang, Weinan Zhang, Yong Yu, Qun Liu, Weiwen Liu — *Huawei Noah's Ark Lab / SJTU*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.27260](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27260) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.27260) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.27260)
**Topics:** `agents`, `data`, `survey`

### TL;DR

Cross-domain agent training papers describe data generation in wildly different vocabularies, making it hard to see the underlying mechanism. This paper factors the whole agentic-data-generation literature into a two-level framework called **ACE** (Allocation / Candidate construction / Evaluation-and-selection) and reorganizes prior work under it, arguing the real problem isn't generating *more* data but continually *allocating* valid, informative, non-redundant experience as agents and environments evolve.

### Key idea

ACE separates three often-conflated stages: **Allocation** (what tasks/environments/turns to spend budget on), **Candidate construction** (how trajectories are actually produced — teacher rollouts, self-play, tree search, human seeds), and **Evaluation & selection** (verification, deduplication, curriculum filtering). The lens exposes that many "agent data recipes" fix two stages and vary the third.

### Main finding

Meta-contribution rather than a single-number result: a shared taxonomy that unifies otherwise-heterogeneous agentic-data pipelines, plus a diagnosis that continual allocation (not brute-force scale) is the current bottleneck.

### Why it matters

Gives the field a common vocabulary. Once you can say "this method varies the *candidate construction* under fixed *allocation* and *evaluation*," it becomes possible to compare methods head-to-head and to identify unexplored corners of the design space.

## 6. TaoLive Digital Avatar Agent — Training Agents to Evolve with Their Harness {#6-taolive}

**Authors:** Yuhan Sun, Wenhao Lin, Yongdong Luo, Yibo Hu, Meiguang Jin, Junfeng Ma, Weihang Pan, Jiaxin Zhao, Zulong Chen — *TaoLive AIGC LLM Team (Alibaba)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.15763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15763) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.15763) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.15763)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `SFT`, `tech-report`

### TL;DR

Production tech report on the TaoLive AI streamer. The system pairs a compact latency-friendly model with an **evolvable harness** (skills, hooks, prompts, tools updated independently of weights) — but compact models overfit to a fixed harness. The fix is **Harness-Aware Training** (HAT): explicitly train the small model to remain robust as the surrounding harness is swapped and updated between rollouts.

### Key idea

Treat the harness itself as an environment axis in training: sample harness configurations, generate SFT+RL data under each, and train the model with a distribution *over* harnesses rather than a single fixed one. This buys the latency of a small model without paying the usual overfitting cost when the harness inevitably changes in production.

### Main finding

Post-HAT small models retain strong product-QA performance and generalization while meeting real-time latency targets that large-model configurations miss — with room to update prompts/tools/skills between deploys without retraining weights.

### Why it matters

An honest look at the "small model + rich scaffold" pattern that most agent products actually ship. Names the failure mode (harness overfit) and gives a training-time fix that's compatible with continual harness iteration.

## 7. GameWAM: A World Action Model for Video Games {#7-gamewam}

**Authors:** Yuncheng Guo, Zhanqiu Zhang, Yiwen Guo, Weijia Li — *Sun Yat-sen University / Ant Group*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.26200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26200) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.26200) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.26200)
**Topics:** `world-models`, `multimodal`, `agents`

### TL;DR

Game agents map perception → action but lack explicit dynamics; interactive video world models predict futures conditioned on actions but aren't policies. **World-Action Models** unify both. GameWAM is the first WAM for native closed-loop gameplay + GUI: it jointly generates future visual frames and keyboard/mouse trajectories through parallel visual and action generative processes with **block-causal conditioning** and **flow matching**.

### Key idea

Two coupled flow-matching heads: one over visual futures, one over executable native-control trajectories, share block-causal conditioning. A per-step **gameplay/GUI mode predictor** dispatches to mode-specific action distributions with continuous-action normalization. **Block-cycle control** predicts beyond a committed horizon, executes only a short action prefix, then replans — long-horizon planning without long-horizon commitment.

### Main finding

Competitive task success with **fewer executed native actions** than baseline game agents. Also identifies **LASI (Low-Frequency Action Source Imprinting)**: the low-frequency components of the sampled action source systematically steer coarse camera motion even under fixed conditioning — a new source-sensitivity failure mode in generative control.

### Why it matters

Consolidates "policy" and "world model" into one architecture, and gives the community a named failure mode (LASI) for generative controllers that's likely to generalize past games.

## 8. PILOT in the Loop: Live Self-Improvement for Long-Horizon Agents {#8-pilot}

**Authors:** Yang Xiao, Yusong Sun, Haoyi Wu, Wenyang Hui, Wen Da, Zhaokai Luo, Mu Chuan, Yao Hu, Wenjie Li, Chengyue Jiang — *ShanghaiTech / Xiaohongshu*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.26530](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26530) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.26530) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.26530)
**Topics:** `agents`, `self-improvement`

### TL;DR

Most agent self-improvement runs post-hoc — after an episode ends. PILOT adds a **supervisor-worker loop** that steers the *active* run and distills lessons learned mid-episode into reusable skills that the worker can immediately pick up.

### Key idea

A supervisor process reads worker traces as they stream, redirects the active run when it detects wasted budget or repeated errors, and simultaneously compresses new procedural know-how into a growing skill library. Skills produced during a run become tools inside that same run.

### Main finding

Substantial performance gains across long-horizon agent benchmarks with **reduced token usage** — the supervisor pays for itself by preventing wasted rollouts, and skill compression amortizes across future subtasks.

### Why it matters

The current gap in agent stacks is that skill libraries update between runs, not during them. Live steering + live skill compilation closes that loop and cuts the cost of long tasks.

## 9. Zero-WAM: In-Context World-Action Modeling from Human Videos {#9-zero-wam}

**Authors:** Jiaming Zhou, Qihang Zhang, Gangwei Xu, Cunxin Fan, Yujie Zhao, Ruilin Wang, Yiming Luo, Shuai Yang, Xing Zhu, Yujun Shen, Junwei Liang, Yinghao Xu — *HKUST(GZ) / Ant Group*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.26103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26103) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.26103) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.26103)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `ICL`, `robotics`

*Borderline: robotics-flavored VLA — included because the technique is in-context learning over videos, a mechanism transferable beyond robots.*

### TL;DR

Instead of expressing a new manipulation task through language, condition a video-action model on a **human demo video** as the in-context specification. Zero-WAM is a causal video-action model that executes tasks never seen at training time by *following* the demo video's visual cues about how the task should unfold.

### Key idea

Bring in-context learning to embodied control: the "prompt" is a video clip of a human doing the task, not a language description. A causal joint video+action architecture attends to the demo clip while emitting an executable action trajectory in the target scene.

### Main finding

Zero-shot cross-task manipulation improves over language-conditioned VLA baselines, with the video prompt providing richer intent than natural language for physical tasks.

### Why it matters

If natural task specification for physical tasks is *a human video*, then ICL for embodiment doesn't need language at all — a substrate shift for how VLAs are deployed and adapted.

## 10. Understanding Evolution Strategies for LLM Reasoning: Broader Reasoning Coverage than GRPO {#10-es-vs-grpo}

**Authors:** Yunpeng Ba, Zhi Zheng, Yue Xie, Jiaqing Li, Xialiang Tong, Tao Zhong, Mingxuan Yuan, Zhichao Lu, Xuyang Wu, Zhenkun Wang — *City University of Hong Kong / Huawei Noah's Ark*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.27351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27351) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.27351) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.27351)
**Topics:** `RL`, `reasoning`, `post-training`

### TL;DR

Evolution Strategies (ES) had been treated as a memory-efficient-but-weaker cousin of GRPO for LLM reasoning post-training. This paper reframes ES as a **distinct paradigm** with a genuine strength: broader reasoning *coverage* (Pass@K). It shows both theoretically and empirically that ES avoids GRPO's entropy collapse, and proposes a **sequential GRPO→ES** recipe that keeps GRPO's Pass@1 gains while inheriting ES's Pass@K gains.

### Key idea

Verifier-projected Jensen-Shannon diversity across the ES population is a lower bound on Pass@K. GRPO's on-policy update collapses entropy quickly; ES's population-based perturbation does not, keeping the model exploring. A held-out study also finds that despite large whole-model parameter drift, ES gains come from a **sparse subset of large-magnitude updates** — large parameter movement need not imply broad functional change or catastrophic forgetting.

### Main finding

ES improves Pass@1 *while* achieving higher Pass@K than GRPO on the same reasoning benchmarks; sequential GRPO→ES dominates either alone. Also: larger LLMs need smaller ES populations, contra the usual "bigger model = bigger population" reflex.

### Why it matters

Positions ES as a *complement* to GRPO rather than a fallback for memory-constrained shops. Suggests a two-stage post-training default: GRPO for Pass@1, ES for coverage.

## 11. WikiSkill: Compiling Agent Experience into Persistent Knowledge {#11-wikiskill}

**Authors:** Liyan Tang, Cyrus Rashtchian, Chun-Sung Ferng, Andrew Tomkins, Da-Cheng Juan, Tu Vu — *Google / Virginia Tech*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.27454](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27454) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.27454) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.27454)
**Topics:** `agents`, `memory`, `skills`

### TL;DR

Automatic skill discovery for agents typically loses the *insights* behind each skill — they stay stuck in optimization logs. WikiSkill co-evolves skills with a **persistent knowledge base (wiki)**: raw execution experience feeds insights into the wiki, and subsequent skill updates read from and add to it, giving skill evolution a compounding memory rather than a per-iteration reset.

### Key idea

Three separated stores: **raw execution traces**, **compiled knowledge wiki**, and **executable skills**. A consolidation step distills lessons from traces into wiki articles; a skill-update step conditions on the wiki and prior skills. Every generation of skills stands on cumulative knowledge, not just the last iteration's traces.

### Main finding

Skills improve monotonically across iterations, with the wiki serving as both a memory buffer and a debugging surface — insights persist even when a specific skill is deprecated or replaced.

### Why it matters

Agent memory has been shallow (retrieval over traces, one-shot summarization). WikiSkill is a design pattern for making the *lessons* durable and reusable across the whole system, not just retained inside one skill.

## 12. CritICL: Inference-Time Weak-to-Strong Generalization from Failure Modes {#12-criticl}

**Authors:** Yufan Wu, Yinghui He, Zhengyi Hu, Lang Wei, Ruichen Li, Qifan Yang, Ting Zhu — *Zhejiang University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.27455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27455) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.27455) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.27455)
**Topics:** `reasoning`, `test-time-compute`, `ICL`

### TL;DR

Test-time scaling usually spends compute on repeated generation or external verifiers. CritICL takes a different angle: LLM failure modes are *structured* across model scales within the same family, so **failures of a weaker sibling** are a cheap oracle for what to warn a stronger model about. It injects those failures as **critique-based in-context examples**, in two flavors: dynamic (retrieve failures relevant to each input) and static (a global failure-mode profile).

### Key idea

Use a weak model to generate cheap failure trajectories; distill them into critique examples (what went wrong + why); prepend them to the strong model's prompt so the strong model spends less compute on the same class of mistake. No verifier, no reward model, no repeated generation.

### Main finding

Beats standard in-context learning and matches or beats test-time scaling methods **while using significantly fewer generations and lower token cost**.

### Why it matters

Reframes weak-to-strong from a *training-time* alignment tool into an *inference-time* efficiency mechanism, and monetizes the fact that failures within a model family transfer up-scale.

## 13. What Does an Evaluation License? A Commit-Bound Census of Inspect Evals {#13-eval-license}

**Author:** Xi Qin — *(no affiliation listed)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.19269](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19269) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.19269) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.19269)
**Topics:** `eval`, `methodology`, `safety-eval`

*Borderline: methodology paper about eval semantics rather than a new benchmark — included because it fills a real gap in how the graph thinks about eval integrity.*

### TL;DR

An eval artifact (task + scorer + metric) specifies a *forward computation*, but that computation may not **license** the claim attached to it — replaying the claim needs bound historical evidence and semantic grounding that the eval doesn't always carry. The paper formalizes this "claim-replay" gap and audits **all 124 mechanically eligible units in Inspect Evals** at a pinned commit; **110 stop before deterministic inference** for lack of the required historical or semantic binding.

### Key idea

Formal quadruple $(D, F, q, \text{identified set})$: a frozen substrate $D$, a grounded family $F$, a claim query $q$, and the identified set of admissible answers. If any of these are unbound, the eval's forward pass runs but the *claim* it appears to support is unwarranted. Yields typed stops, instability witnesses, and stable substructure rather than one metric.

### Main finding

**110/124 Inspect Evals units** cannot deterministically replay their claim at a pinned commit — the metric runs, but the evidence needed to interpret it is missing or ambiguous. Where execution *does* close, exact values, winners, and pairwise relations separate cleanly by claim resolution and by primary vs. review family.

### Why it matters

Uncomfortable news for the safety-eval community: the number attached to a benchmark result is often a valid computation of an under-specified claim. Suggests eval hygiene needs to move from *reproducing the metric* to *replaying the claim*.
