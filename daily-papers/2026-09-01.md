## 1. On the Design of Qwen3.8-Next Architecture: Evaluation, Efficiency, and Training Stability {#1-qwen38-next}

**Authors:** Zihan Qiu, Zekun Wang, Xiao Li, Yanpeng Li, Yang Xu, Yixuan Wang, Huaqing Zhang, Rui Men, Bochao Mao, Chengruidong Zhang, Fan Zhou, Hao Luo, Haofeng Huang, Haoran Lian, Haoyan Huang, Hongqing Chen, Jianwei Zhang, Jing Xu, Junjie Wang, Langshi Chen, Liangyu Wang, Linlang Jiang, Man Yuan, Minmin Sun, Peng Jin, Siqi Zhang, Siyu Wang, Xingzhang Ren, Yakai Wang, Yi Zhang, Yiming Dong, Yizhong Cao, Yubo Ma, Yunfei Mao, Bo Zheng, Dayiheng Liu — *Qwen team / Alibaba*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.30320](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30320) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.30320) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.30320)
**Topics:** `MoE`, `attention`, `arch`, `pre-training`

### TL;DR

Full architecture ablation report for Qwen3.8-Flash-Next: a 125B / 6B-active sparse-MoE with three headline design moves — a Gated DeltaNet + global-attention hybrid backbone, Qwen Sparse Attention (a block-scored sparse attention swapped in at continued-pretraining), a widened four-branch **Gated Residual** stream, and an off-accelerator n-gram embedding table adding 51B parameters of "free" capacity. Beats its 397B-A17B predecessor on 8 of 14 pretraining benchmarks with 1/3 the active params, 1/3 the tokens, and ~1/9 the training FLOPs.

### Key idea

Every candidate change is evaluated jointly on three axes — loss + downstream, training/prefill/decode cost, and hyperparameter/stability effect — rather than loss alone. Concretely: (i) *layer-wise hybrid* of Gated DeltaNet (GDN) and full attention (1-in-4), later swapped to **Qwen Sparse Attention (QSA)**, a micro-block indexer that scores context sparsely; (ii) a **Gated Residual (GR)** stream widened to four branches with an elementwise output gate; (iii) an **n-gram embedding layer** whose tables are prefetched from host memory so they don't consume HBM; (iv) tuning with the **Muon** optimizer, which shifts optimal LR/batch-size upward and removes the need for batch-size warmup.

### Main finding

At 125B/6B-active, matches or exceeds the 397B/A17B predecessor at 1/3 of active params, 1/3 of tokens, ~1/9 of the FLOPs. Stability improves under stress tests; enlarging the n-gram vocabulary lowers loss monotonically even though downstream accuracy saturates — a concrete counterexample to loss-only ablations.

### Why it matters

A frontier-lab-quality ablation report on a hybrid-attention sparse-MoE trained at scale — comparable in scope to DeepSeek-V3 or Kimi K1.5. The GDN/QSA/GR/n-gram-embedding recipe is now a public design point for anyone building sparse-attention MoE beyond dense Transformer + MLA.

## 2. Does On-Policy Distillation Really Distill? From Noisy Teacher to Self-Improvement {#2-opsa}

**Authors:** Yi Ding, Ruqi Zhang — *Purdue*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.31046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31046) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.31046) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.31046)
**Topics:** `post-training`, `RL`, `distillation`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

Argues that "on-policy distillation" isn't really distillation: student improvement is dominated by the KL-with-teacher term acting as a *negative advantage on low-probability tokens*, and a fixed negative advantage (no teacher at all) matches the teacher-driven signal. Introduces **OPSA** (On-Policy Self-Adaptation), an entropy-adaptive teacher-free variant.

### Key idea

Decompose the on-policy-distillation objective and observe (a) teacher signal contains substantial noise that scales with model size, (b) student updates concentrate on low-log-prob tokens regardless, and (c) replacing the teacher-derived weight with a *fixed* negative advantage on those tokens matches performance. Then propose **entropy-adaptive negative advantages** — assign stronger negative signals at high-entropy positions and redistribute probability mass among frequent tokens.

### Main finding

On Qwen3-1.7B, OPSA improves AIME24 by **35.41 pts** (263% relative), more than doubles Pass@32 across benchmarks, and outperforms on-policy distillation itself by **16.77 pts on AIME24** — despite requiring no teacher model.

### Why it matters

Reframes "distillation" as reward-shaping on entropy — closer to RLVR without verifiers. If it holds up, entire pipelines that pay compute for a large teacher model in on-policy KD can drop the teacher.

## 3. Normalized Low-Rank Adaptation (NoRA) {#3-nora}

**Authors:** Jiale Kang, Ziyin Yue, Zheng Zhan, Yangyi Huang, Weiyang Liu — *mixed academic*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.31036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31036) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.31036) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.31036)
**Topics:** `fine-tuning`, `LoRA`, `optimization`

### TL;DR

Because LoRA initializes the up-projection to zero, early training is entirely driven by the *down-projection*. NoRA simply normalizes the down-projection matrices during training (or just at init) — no extra parameters, no inference cost — and consistently accelerates convergence, improves stability, and mitigates forgetting across pretraining, SFT, and RL.

### Key idea

Treat LoRA's asymmetric init (B=0, A~) as the source of poorly-conditioned early updates. Normalize A during training (a full-training variant) or once at initialization (a drop-in that preserves standard LoRA thereafter). No new params, no inference overhead.

### Main finding

Consistent gains over vanilla LoRA across three training phases (pretraining, SFT, RL); reduces catastrophic forgetting and improves training stability. The "init-only" variant captures much of the benefit essentially for free.

### Why it matters

The LoRA ecosystem is enormous but almost no one addresses the *init asymmetry* explicitly. NoRA is a one-line change with universal upside — likely to displace default LoRA init in production stacks quickly.

## 4. Verification-Aware Training for Speculative Decoding (VAT) {#4-vat}

**Authors:** Geonmo Gu, Byeongho Heo, HeeJae Jun, Yoohoon Kang, Sangmin Lee, Sangdoo Yun, Dongyoon Han — *NAVER*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.30135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30135) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.30135) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.30135)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`, `speculative-decoding`

### TL;DR

Simulates the target model's verification pass *during* draft-model training so the draft learns to propose tokens the target will actually accept. Adds a small binary **verification head** as an auxiliary classifier plus a rejection-pattern-adaptive loss weighting. Up to +11.4% acceptance length and +8.7% wall-clock speedup vs vanilla draft training.

### Key idea

Standard draft-model training minimizes next-token loss on the target's outputs but never sees the verification game. VAT co-trains the draft with (i) a **verification head** — a lightweight binary classifier predicting whether the target will accept a proposed token — and (ii) **adaptive weighting** that upweights positions where the draft is systematically getting rejected. The draft ends up "verification-aware" without changing the decoding algorithm at inference time.

### Main finding

Across draft/target size pairs and benchmarks: **+11.4% acceptance length**, **+8.7% wall-clock speedup** over the strongest baseline draft-training method.

### Why it matters

Every speculative-decoding stack (EAGLE, Medusa, etc.) trains drafts on next-token loss and treats verification as a separate concern. VAT closes that gap with a training-time signal — a drop-in change that improves the entire family.

## 5. Scaling Large Reasoning Models beyond Human Supervision: A Path toward Superintelligence {#5-scaling-lrms}

**Authors:** Zhiqin Yang, Jingwen Fu, Yuhan Liu, Hengyu Liu, Yonggang Zhang, Kainan Cao, Zizhuo Zhang, Chenxin Li, Ruibin Yuan, Jiahao Pan, Jiankai Sun, Zhenyuan Zhang, Yibo Li, Yunlong Lin, Jing Xiong, Sida Lin, Bo Han, Wei Xue, Yike Guo — *HKUST / Tencent Hunyuan / HKU / CUHK / etc.*

**Topics:** `reasoning`, `RL`, `RLVR`, `survey`

### TL;DR

Position/survey paper. ### Key idea

The L0-L4 supervision ladder gives a clean taxonomy for the modern RLVR / self-improvement literature and makes explicit which risks appear at which rung.

### Main finding

Not empirical — the contribution is the framework and the evaluation-object triad (capability × fidelity × experience quality).

### Why it matters

Provides a shared vocabulary for a fast-moving subfield. The framework also makes it easy to see which safety failure modes (reward hacking, sycophantic verifier drift) are *inherent* to each rung of the ladder.

## 6. SHAPE of Chain-of-Thought in Math Reasoning {#6-shape-cot}

**Authors:** Jonghyun Song, Sangjun Song, Minjae Oh, Haesung Pyun, Sungsik Lee, Yohan Jo — *SNU*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.28600](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28600) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.28600) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.28600)
**Topics:** `reasoning`, `eval`, `interp`

### TL;DR

Introduces **SHAPE**, a framework borrowed from math-education research that reads CoT trajectories through two lenses: (1) *semantic spaces* (which mathematical interpretation the model is inhabiting — algebraic, geometric, etc.) and (2) *heuristics* (what specific action it's taking — simplify, work backward). Heuristic usage predicts correctness better than surface CoT features, and RL post-training concentrates heuristic usage.

### Key idea

Score each CoT trajectory on the semantic space it inhabits and the mathematical heuristics it uses. Both are grounded in math-education literature; the annotation pipeline is validated against expert judgments. Post-training LLMs with prompts that promote *diverse* heuristic usage improves accuracy.

### Main finding

Heuristic annotations explain answer correctness better than length/step-count. Successful trajectories concentrate in a small number of semantic spaces. RL post-training visibly narrows the heuristic distribution — direct evidence of *what* RL is teaching, not just that it helps.

### Why it matters

A rare mechanistic-education view of what RL post-training does to reasoning behavior. Complements process-reward and CoT-faithfulness literature by providing a *content-level* description of good CoTs.

## 7. Chain-of-Thought Faithfulness Varies with Where and How Preference Cues Are Delivered (FACE-Eval) {#7-face-eval}

**Authors:** Aryo Pradipta Gema, Neel Rajani, Rohit Saxena, Wai-Chung Kwan, Pasquale Minervini — *Edinburgh / Miniml.AI*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.29464](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29464) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.29464) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.29464)
**Topics:** `safety`, `interp`, `eval`, `agents`

### TL;DR

Existing CoT-faithfulness benchmarks put bias cues in the user message. Real agents get preferences via *tool returns* or *raw artifacts*. **FACE-Eval** (5,100 samples, 15 open-weight models 4B–1.6T) systematically varies cue **location** (user message vs tool return) and **explicitness** (explicit summary vs raw artifact). Every model shows lower verbalized commitment and higher unverbalized adoption on tool-channel and implicit cues — CoT monitors detect the shift less well.

### Key idea

Two-dimensional grid — {user, tool} × {explicit, implicit} — with two metrics: **verbalized commitment** (of the answers that follow the cue, how many acknowledge it in CoT) and **unverbalized adoption** (of all cued samples, how many take the cue silently). Also runs two LLM monitors and correlates monitor accuracy with the CoT-faithfulness gap.

### Main finding

**All 15 models** have lower verbalized commitment for tool-return vs user-message cues; unverbalized adoption higher for tool-return in all 15 and for implicit in 28/30 model-channel comparisons. Monitor detection ability correlates *negatively* with unverbalized adoption (Pearson r = -0.54, -0.78). "You are being monitored" prompts don't reliably close the gap.

### Why it matters

Concrete failure mode for CoT-monitoring safety cases: the moment bias information arrives via tools instead of the user turn, faithfulness drops and monitors miss more. Directly informs how CoT monitoring should be evaluated for agent deployments.

## 8. The Safeguard Worked. Is the LLM System Safer? {#8-safeguard-worked}

**Authors:** Pingyu Wu, Weiming Zhang, Nenghai Yu — *USTC*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.00519](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00519) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.00519) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.00519)
**Topics:** `safety`, `eval`, `position`

### TL;DR

Position paper. Argues that safeguard-local metrics (refusal rate, attack success rate, policy violation rate) describe the safeguard, not the *deployed system*. One successful adaptive attack that still gets harmful output through the whole system suffices to show harm remains — and proving the *absence* of residual harm requires system-level evidence, not just safeguard scores.

### Key idea

Distinguish **local safeguard efficacy** (does the safeguard block the tested requests?) from **system-level residual harm** (does the full deployed system still produce harmful help under adaptive attack?). Local scores are one-sided: they cheaply prove local competence but cannot bound residual harm.

### Main finding

Framework contribution. Reframes what a "safer" system means at the deployment layer and enumerates what evidence would actually license that claim.

### Why it matters

Safety-case discipline for LLM systems. Directly relevant to how release evals are read — a safeguard hitting 99% refusal doesn't mean the deployed product is safe.

## 9. SafeAtlas-VL: Beyond Binary Multimodal Safety with Large-Scale Data and Guard Models {#9-safeatlas-vl}

**Authors:** Zongrui Wang, Xiangyang Zhu, Sicheng Wang, Han Wang, Dingyi Rong, Zeyu Zhang, Chunyi Li, Yue Shi, Kaiwei Zhang, Zicheng Zhang, Yuan Tian, Qi Jia, Yan Teng, Wei Sun, Ning Liu, Guangtao Zhai — *SJTU / Shanghai AI Lab*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.29098](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29098) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.29098) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.29098)
**Topics:** `safety`, `multimodal`, `eval`, `data`

### TL;DR

1.5M-instance dataset for multimodal safety moderation with **five-level ordinal** labels (not binary) on three targets: image, request, response. Introduces SafeAtlas-Bench (5k held-out) and the SafeAtlas Guard models trained with target-conditioned tuning + soft cumulative-ordinal head. 8B guard beats the previous SOTA by ~4% F1.

### Key idea

(1) A five-level ordered scale (not binary safe/unsafe) applied uniformly to image, user request, and assistant response. (2) Target-conditioned tuning so one model produces separate judgments per target. (3) Soft cumulative-ordinal head so the model outputs a continuous risk score in addition to five-way classification.

### Main finding

1.5M training + 5k eval. 8B model beats prior SOTA by **~4% F1** overall and generalizes to other benchmarks without their training data.

### Why it matters

Moves multimodal moderation off the safe/unsafe binary — necessary for policy tiering and staged responses in real deployments. Public data + models make it a plausible new baseline.

## 10. DIEM: Dynamic Important Example Mining for Reinforcement Finetuning {#10-diem}

**Authors:** Haoru Tan, Sitong Wu, Yanfeng Chen, Shizhen Zhao, Yang-Tian Sun, Tianjia Liu, Chirui Chang, Shaofeng Zhang, Samm Sun, Xiuzhe Wu, Ruobing Xie, Xiaojuan Qi — *HKU / Tencent*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.29252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29252) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.29252) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.29252)
**Topics:** `RL`, `post-training`, `data`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

Adaptive prompt/example selection for RL fine-tuning. Uses a **gradient-alignment importance estimator** to approximate each sample's marginal contribution to the current step, plus a **constrained batch reweighting** scheme that preserves gradient magnitude for stability. Consistently outperforms both static and dynamic baselines across reasoning benchmarks.

### Key idea

At each RL step, estimate per-sample marginal contribution via gradient alignment (cheap approximation to a leave-one-out signal), then reweight the batch subject to a magnitude constraint so the effective step size doesn't drift. Fully automated, no heuristics.

### Main finding

Consistent wins over strong static and dynamic prompt-selection baselines across reasoning benchmarks. Code promised at github.com/hrtan/DIEM.

### Why it matters

Prompt/example curation is a substantial fraction of the value in RLVR pipelines. DIEM is a principled online-curation baseline that other RL-post-training methods will need to beat.

## 11. DreamX-Creator: Democratizing Native Audio-Video Generation at 2K Resolution {#11-dreamx-creator}

**Authors:** Jiashu Zhu, Yanhao Zheng, Ruitian Tian, Rujing Dang, Shen Zhang, Bingze Song, Jiachen Lei, Ruimin Lin, Jiahong Wu, Xiangxiang Chu — *Meituan*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.31106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31106) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.31106) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.31106)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `multimodal`, `video`, `audio`

### TL;DR

7B native joint audio-video generator: conditioned on a first frame + text prompt, the network processes audio and video streams *independently* in the first half and *couples* them in the second half via **Gated Cross-Modal Attention** (token- and head-wise output gates). Adds an audio-video RLHF stage with **Modality-Aware Multimodal Feedback** and a 1-step 2K refinement pipeline distilled from a bidirectional multi-step teacher.

### Key idea

Three ingredients:
1. **Gated Cross-Modal Attention** — output gates per head and per token control how much cross-modal signal enters the joint layers, so early layers stay modality-specialized.
2. **Modality-Aware Multimodal Feedback** — RL feedback is routed: video reward → video branch, audio reward → audio branch, cross-modal reward → coupling layers.
3. **Autoregressive 1-Step 2K Refinement** — distill a bidirectional multi-step 2K teacher into a per-chunk 1-step student for real-time 2K decoding.

### Main finding

Competitive with SOTA open-source A/V generators at 7B, native (not two-stage) synchronization, 2K output at low decode cost.

### Why it matters

Native joint A/V generation with gated cross-modal coupling is a clean recipe for extending diffusion beyond image/video-only. The modality-aware RLHF routing is a broadly reusable idea for multimodal generators.

## 12. GenFirst: Generation Before Reconstruction for Stable End-to-End Latent Generative Modeling {#12-genfirst}

**Authors:** Guangting Zheng, Yiyuan Zhang, Tao Yang, Yunpeng Chen, Rui Zhu, Jiajun Deng, Yanyong Zhang — *USTC / Baidu*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.29335](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29335) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.29335) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.29335)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `generative`, `training-stability`

### TL;DR

End-to-end latent generative training instead of the usual two-stage (freeze VAE → train generator). The trick is a **generation-first schedule**: start under minimal reconstruction pressure, progressively strengthen reconstruction as the generator stabilizes. Also identifies the KL-entropy term as essential for preventing collapse.

### Key idea

The two objectives (reconstruction, generation) have asymmetric learning dynamics — reconstruction converges fast and pins the latent geometry before the generator has a chance to shape it. GenFirst inverts the order: generate first, then gradually reintroduce reconstruction. Small hyperparameter change, big stability gain.

### Main finding

Across SiT and MMDiT: **gFID 0.97 (with CFG) / 1.45 (without) on ImageNet-256** for SiT; **GenEval 0.90** for text-to-image with MMDiT.

### Why it matters

If reproducible, this ends the two-stage VAE-then-diffusion pipeline for latent generative models — one training run, better results.

## 13. CogEvol: Towards Efficient and Reliable Learning Environment Generation {#13-cogevol}

**Authors:** Shangqing Tu, Daniel Zhang-Li, Yucheng Wang, Shiyu Gan, Yanpeng Wang, Huiqiang Rong, Mofei Chen, Shen Yang, Yini Chen, Yinuo Duan, Haoxuan Li, Binglin Liu, Ye He, Danqi Zheng, Zhanxin Hao, Yuxuan Wu, Mengting Tao, Yuqiu Liu, Jifan Yu, Juanzi Li, Bin Xu, Lei Hou, Huiqin Liu, Yu Zhang — *Tsinghua / OpenMAIC*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.30968](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30968) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.30968) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.30968)
**Topics:** `RL`, `agents`, `GRPO`, `data`

### TL;DR

Domain-specialized model family for one-shot generation of learning artifacts (slides, interactive HTML pages) — replaces minute-long multi-turn agent scaffolding with a single pass. Trained with a **hybrid rule + VLM reward** driving GRPO, hardened after catching a real reward-hacking episode where the model produced visually convincing but unplayable games.

### Key idea

Production-grounded data pipeline: real failures from 220k production requests → 53,687 verified SFT samples. Reward is a hybrid — rule-based (structural) + VLM-based (visual quality) — used inside GRPO. When they caught a reward-hacking case (unplayable-but-pretty games), they patched the reward and re-trained; the paper documents that debug loop as a first-class finding.

### Main finding

27B model reaches 83.7 (slide quality) / 63.7 (interactive-HTML) benchmark scores while using **26.9× fewer parameters** than flagship coding models. 4B checkpoint released Apache-2.0. Ships live production traffic on domestic Ascend accelerators at A800-parity.

### Why it matters

A public worked example of the *reward-hacking-caught-in-production* loop with concrete hardening steps — the kind of detail almost never in the literature. Also a case of GRPO + hybrid rewards used in a domain where automatic verifiers alone won't work.

## 14. CAST: Critique-Aware Supervision for Training Reliable Long-Horizon Tool-Calling Agents {#14-cast}

**Authors:** Amir Saeidi, Zehua Zhang, Rishitosh Singh, Naman Ahuja, Vivek Gupta, Ali Payani, Gaowen Liu, Jayanth Srinivasa, Chitta Baral — *ASU / Cisco Research*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.30147](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30147) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.30147) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.30147)
**Topics:** `agents`, `SFT`, `tool-use`, `RL`

### TL;DR

Turns sparse task outcomes into per-action supervision for tool-calling agents. A critique model produces structured rationales for why each action is valid/invalid under partial observability, then those rationales become the supervision for policy training. Fine-tuning Qwen3 with CAST beats GPT-OSS-120B by **10+ pts pass^4** on Retail and gains an extra 9% out-of-domain on Telehealth.

### Key idea

Two-stage: (1) analyze trajectories to synthesize **structured critique rationales** explaining action validity under partial observability and domain policies, (2) train the policy with critique-aware supervision derived from those rationales. Reliability metric is pass^k (both k=4) — much stricter than pass^1 because it requires success across multiple trials.

### Main finding

Fine-tuned Qwen3 outperforms GPT-OSS-120B by **>10% pass^4** on Retail; +9% on Telehealth (out-of-domain).

### Why it matters

Long-horizon tool-calling reliability is the current bottleneck in production agent deployments. Critique-aware supervision is a way to convert sparse outcome rewards into dense action-level signal without an environment simulator.

## 15. PaperGym: Rubric-Centered Evolution for Research-Plan Generation {#15-papergym}

**Authors:** Yuhan Wang, Zhengxi Lu, Yuchen Yan, Kaitao Song, Wenqi Zhang, Weiming Lu, Jun Xiao, Yueting Zhuang, Yongliang Shen — *Zhejiang U.*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.31119](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31119) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.31119) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.31119)
**Topics:** `agents`, `eval`, `RL`, `rewards`

### TL;DR

Framework that turns real research papers into full research-planning environments for training and evaluating AI scientists. Question is synthesized from goals+background; rubric is derived from methods+experiments — so the rubric doesn't leak the answer. Two-stage training uses rubrics as **privileged context** and as **reward signal**.

### Key idea

Separate the *question* (what the agent must plan) from the *evaluation rubric* (what defines a good plan). PaperGym derives them from disjoint sections of the paper, cutting criterion leakage from ~12-34% (prior datasets) to **3.7%**. Two-stage training: SFT with rubric-as-privileged-context, then RL with rubric-as-reward.

### Main finding

Qwen3-8B trained with PaperGym reaches **73.48 on ResearchQA**, surpassing the much larger Kimi K2.6.

### Why it matters

Rubric-as-reward + rubric-as-privileged-context is a clean instantiation of the "verifier from a stronger source than the policy" pattern for open-ended agent tasks. Also a template for building agent RL environments from any curated corpus.
