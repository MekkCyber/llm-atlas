## 1. SMELT

**Authors:** Shaowen Wang, Ge Zhang, Kairong Luo, Yuhao Wu, Shaofan Liu, Jiaheng Liu, Wenhao Huang, Shen Yan, Jian Li — *Tsinghua-affiliated group*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.01343](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01343) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.01343) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.01343)
**Topics:** `MoE`, `architecture`, `scaling-laws`

### TL;DR

A compute-matched study of *looped* MoE transformers: iterate a shared block of layers to gain effective depth without new parameters. The recipe **SMELT** (Sparse MoE, middle layers Loop Twice) loops the middle half of layers twice while matching per-token FLOPs, non-embedding parameters, and KV-cache size against an unlooped baseline. Fits Chinchilla-style scaling laws up to 54B non-embedding params and saves 6.8–18.0% of training FLOPs on the compute-optimal frontier.

### Key idea

Prior looped-transformer work confounded "extra depth via looping" with "extra FLOPs from re-executing layers". SMELT strips this out by holding **per-token FLOPs, parameters, and KV cache** fixed against the baseline — so any gain is attributable to the loop, not the compute. The specific choice is to loop only the **middle half** of layers **twice**, leaving early/late layers unshared. Mechanistically the second visit *reduces the attention sink* and reallocates attention mass to content-relevant tokens.

### Main finding

Across four scales up to 54B non-embedding parameters, SMELT beats an unlooped MoE baseline on the compute-optimal frontier, with 6.8–18.0% FLOPs savings for equal loss. Downstream benefit is *larger than validation loss predicts*, biggest on code, and grows with sequence length and in-context example count.

### Why it matters

Looped transformers had a reputation for being a "free depth" trick that fell apart under honest compute accounting. SMELT is the first budget-matched scaling-law study to show looping is a real architectural win for MoE, with a clean mechanistic story (attention-sink reduction). Combined with existing MoE recipes (DeepSeek-MoE, aux-loss-free balancing), it's a plausible next default for open-weight MoE pre-training.

## 2. Switch Distillation

**Authors:** Jacqueline He, Howard Yen, Shuyue Stella Li, Margaret Li, Hanqing Zeng, Yinglong Xia, Benyu Zhang, Zhuokai Zhao, Qiang Zhang, Pang Wei Koh, Luke Zettlemoyer, Wen-tau Yih — *Meta / University of Washington*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.01532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01532) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.01532) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.01532)
**Topics:** `pre-training`, `distillation`, `mid-training`

### TL;DR

Forward-KL knowledge distillation from a stronger post-trained teacher is standard for training smaller LMs, but the paper shows it behaves *differently* during **mid-training** than during pre-training: reasoning still improves, but factual recall gets *slower*. The fix is **Switch Distillation** — route each token: distill when teacher predictive entropy is low (confident), fall back to plain cross-entropy otherwise.

### Key idea

The stage-dependence is traced to two asymmetries: (1) post-trained teachers are more confident on **procedural** tokens than on **knowledge-intensive** ones, and (2) students acquire low-entropy factual knowledge *earlier* in training, so late-stage KD on those tokens overwrites what the student already learned better than the teacher can supervise. Switch Distillation gates on teacher entropy per token — trust the teacher on procedure, trust the ground-truth token on facts.

### Main finding

Vs. plain next-token prediction during mid-training: **1.61–1.71× reasoning gains**, **1.13–1.19× knowledge/commonsense gains**, while preserving **96.7–96.8% of factual recall** (plain forward-KD loses factual recall). Gains persist after post-training. Outperforms existing distillation objectives across teacher sizes.

### Why it matters

Mid-training is the phase everybody is quietly optimizing but rarely publishing on. Switch Distillation is the first clean characterization of *when* forward-KD helps vs. hurts, and a drop-in fix — one entropy threshold, no new networks. Likely to become the default mid-training objective for small-model distillation.

## 3. StudentSim

**Authors:** Ke Yang, Chenglong Wang, Michel Galley, Chandan Singh, Jeevana Priya Inala, ChengXiang Zhai, Jianfeng Gao — *Microsoft Research / UIUC*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.01591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01591) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.01591) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.01591)
**Topics:** `RL`, `reward-modeling`, `agents`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Trains individualized *student simulators* — LLMs that mimic a specific learner's responses and update them under tutor guidance — by pooled training followed by per-student specialization. Ships **StudentSimEval**, a 60-student protocol spanning chess, L2 English writing, and math, with fidelity (F) and guidance-responsiveness (R) metrics. As a proof of concept, uses the simulator as a **reward model** for tutor RL and gets a chess tutor rated better than one trained against a GPT-5.4 simulator.

### Key idea

Two-stage recipe: (1) pool sparse per-student data across the roster to fit a shared simulator, (2) specialize per-student using only that student's records. This resolves the classical tradeoff — state-tracking models fit responses but ignore guidance; role-play models follow guidance but drift from the student. Then use the trained simulator as the **environment + reward signal** for a downstream tutor RL loop.

### Main finding

Beats GPT-5.4 on both metrics in all three domains. Chess: F=0.51, R=0.91 vs 0.23/0.72 (GPT-5.4) and 0.45/0.27 (Maia2). Human experts prefer the tutor trained against StudentSim over a no-RL baseline and a tutor trained against a GPT-5.4-simulator reward.

### Why it matters

A concrete demonstration that *learned environment models* — not off-the-shelf frontier LLMs — are the right reward for RL on tutor / adaptive-system tasks. Same trick generalizes to any personalized-agent domain where labelled user data is small but user-specific behavior matters.

## 4. T-Tech Corporate LLM

**Authors:** Olga Tsymboi, Dmitrii Stoianov, Ramil Latypov, Danil Taranets, Daniil Dryabin, Mikhail Gashkov, Viktor Zelenkovskiy, Aleksandr Fida, Gleb Alektorov, Nikita Gulyakov, Arthur Babkin, Aleksandr Medvedev, Pavel Gein, Anatolii Potapov — *T-Tech*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.01572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01572) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.01572) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.01572)
**Topics:** `RL`, `GRPO`, `model-merging`, `post-training`

### TL;DR

An engineering-first post-training recipe for consolidating >200 internal enterprise apps onto one self-hosted LLM. Rather than jointly optimize all objectives, they train **one GRPO expert per axis** — instruction following, function-calling, task distribution — then merge via two-stage **SLERP**. Beats a ~7× larger baseline on internal Arena (69.6 vs 65.8) and absorbs 50% of platform traffic (116M requests / month).

### Key idea

Joint multi-objective RL suffers cross-domain reward interference (each axis's reward drives a distinct failure mode: semantic collapse, over-calling, verbosity hacking). The recipe *decouples* by training a separate GRPO expert per axis with a domain-tailored reward, then recomposes with two-stage spherical linear interpolation of weights. Production error analysis, not benchmarks, sets which axes exist and how they're measured.

### Main finding

Non-reasoning-mode model beats the ~7× larger baseline: Arena 69.6→65.8, instruction-following 0.85→0.83, function-calling 0.79→0.77, while general dialogue also lifts. Serves 116M requests/month at a fraction of the fleet cost, replacing a fragmented multi-model serving setup.

### Why it matters

A rare *deployment case study* for GRPO — most GRPO papers stop at benchmarks. Two takeaways transferable outside enterprise: (1) per-axis reward isolation avoids the interference tax of one big multi-objective reward, and (2) SLERP weight merging is a viable recomposition primitive for RL-trained experts, not just SFT ones.

### TL;DR

An open-source foundation GUI agent covering mobile, web, and desktop under a unified closed-loop reasoning-action framework. Prior GUI agents overfit to narrow benchmark app sets and rely on brittle reward verification. Positioned as a general-purpose open foundation for GUI agents. Concrete quantitative comparisons not extracted from the abstract; the abstract emphasizes closed-loop deployability and safety-aware controls for consequential actions rather than headline benchmark deltas.

### Why it matters

The GUI-agent field has plateaued because benchmark-tuned models don't survive deployment. UI-Venus-2 is an infrastructure-level answer — *what would you actually need to scale* if you were serious about deployable computer-use agents — and it's open source. Sets a coverage bar peers will need to meet.

## 6. H3-World

**Authors:** Danze Chen, Zeqing Wang, Ziyue Lin, Xingyi Yang, Yeying Jin — *Tencent (via internship)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.01560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01560) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.01560) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.01560)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `video`, `world-model`, `LoRA`

### TL;DR

Turns the 33B **MiniMax-H3** video generator into an interactive world model using natural language as the action interface, no dedicated action modules. Each action is a structured character+camera instruction bound to a temporal video-latent segment via **temporal attention routing**. Reaches effective character/camera control with only 8k gameplay samples, 10k LoRA steps, and 0.199% trainable params.

### Key idea

Large video generators already respond to text — this coarse capability is *sharpened* into precise, temporally grounded control by (a) representing actions as structured character+camera instructions, (b) aligning each with corresponding video-latent time slots, and (c) *temporal attention routing* that restricts each instruction to its intended interval, so control doesn't leak across actions. Reuses learned semantic representations rather than adding a new action head.

### Main finding

Effective character and camera control with minimal footprint: 8,000 gameplay samples, 10,000 LoRA steps, 0.199% of parameters trainable. Preserves the base generator's quality and generalizes to unseen scenarios.

### Why it matters

Suggests interactive world models can be built by **routing existing generators** rather than training bespoke action-conditioned systems. Temporal attention routing itself is a general primitive for aligning discrete symbolic control with continuous latent trajectories — useful outside gaming (e.g., video editing, robot policies over generated video).

## 7. ZimaBlue

**Authors:** Xionghao Wu, Yijun Yang, Shiyang Zhou, Haoze Sun, Jianhui Liu, Songsong Yu, Jiyao Zhang, Wenbo Li, Bo Wang, Guoqing Ma, Lin Song, Renjie Liao, Shenghe Zheng, Wei Tang, Xiaojuan Qi, Yanwei Li, Yuan Zhang, Zhuotao Tian, Haoyang Huang, Nan Duan — *multi-org consortium*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.00188](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00188) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.00188) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.00188)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `VLA`, `world-model`, `pre-training`

### TL;DR

A three-stage recipe for **World Action Models** (WAMs) that scales embodied experience via *action-free* egocentric video. Stages: (1) causal video pre-training on human+robot ego-video, (2) mid-training that grounds visual dynamics in robot trajectories via a unified action representation, (3) target-robot specialization. Slow-Fast asynchronous dual-system deploys at 30 Hz on a single 4090. Scaling to 120,000 hours of video lifts real-robot zero-shot success 36.1% → 77.8%.

### Key idea

Robot trajectories are expensive and narrow; egocentric video is cheap and broad. The recipe converts action-free video into embodied experience by *first* learning generalizable video dynamics, *then* grounding those dynamics in labeled robot data with a **unified action representation** that lets heterogeneous robots share one head. At inference, a *Slow-Fast* split — big slow world model provides representations, small fast head emits actions — makes generative WAMs real-time.

### Main finding

Zero-shot real-robot success climbs from 36.1% (target-robot data alone) to **77.8%** with 120,000 hours of ego-video pre-training. Runs at 30 Hz on RTX 4090. Strongest relative gains on unseen tasks.

### Why it matters

The *scaling-law analogue* for robot learning: like LLMs, generalization needs corpus scale, and the corpus doesn't have to be in-distribution (action-labeled). Sets a template — video pre-train → action-grounded mid-train → deploy — that decouples VLA scaling from the expense of teleoperation.

## 8. Qwen-Drive-1.0

**Authors:** Xin Zhou, Zongchuang Zhao, Zhibo Yang, Mingsheng Li, Humen Zhong, Shuai Bai, Du Chu, Ruizhe Chen, Zhaohai Li, Jun Tang, Qiuyue Wang, Mingkun Yang, Jiazhao Zhang, Dayiheng Liu, Dingkang Liang, Xiang Bai — *Qwen / Alibaba*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.00111](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00111) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.00111) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.00111)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `VLA`, `foundation-model`

### TL;DR

An initial step toward a VL foundation model for autonomous driving. Retains Qwen's VLM architecture; adds an **external BEV perception head** (3D detection + occupancy + BEV map segmentation) and a **Planning Expert** conditioned on shared VLM features. Staged training recipe combines driving supervision with general vision-language data to preserve both.

### Key idea

Rather than build a driving-specific model, keep the pretrained VLM backbone intact and *probe* its 3D-capable representations with an explicit BEV head — the head both delivers 3D perception and serves as an inspectable interface to what the shared representations know. Trajectory planning is a separate expert head over the same features. Preserves general VL capability via a mixed-data staged training recipe.

### Main finding

Strong 3D perception + driving-scene understanding while largely preserving general VLM capability. "Highly competitive" motion-planning across open-loop, pseudo-closed-loop, and closed-loop evaluations; specific numbers not extracted from the abstract.

### Why it matters

A concrete existence proof that a general-purpose VLM can be adapted into a driving foundation model without discarding its general reasoning. Interesting design point: **share representations, specialize heads**, contrasting with vertical driving-only models. Likely to influence how VLA researchers approach multi-domain adaptation.

## 9. UMM Synergy

**Authors:** Penghao Wu, Haiwen Diao, Weichen Fan, Lewei Lu, Dahua Lin, Ziwei Liu — *Shanghai AI Lab / NTU*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.01607](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01607) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.01607) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.01607)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `unified-model`, `architecture`

### TL;DR

An investigation of *when* unified multimodal models (UMMs) actually get synergy between understanding and generation. Finds each objective helps the other at the representation level, but forcing both through the same computation path lets one dominate. A **task-decoupled architecture** — specialize the conflicting visual computation while preserving semantic interaction — avoids this asymmetric degradation. End-to-end UMMs beat matched planner-executor pipelines on complex U+G tasks.

### Key idea

Ablation across three levels (representation, task, system) in a controlled *structurally native* setup with no pretrained vision priors. The finding is nuanced: generation enriches understanding features; understanding sharpens VL alignment for generation; but capacity contention in a fully-shared path causes asymmetric degradation. Fix: split the visual computation for conflicting objectives while keeping the semantic interaction shared.

### Main finding

- Representation: bidirectional benefit, but one objective dominates if forced through the same compute path.
- Task: positive bidirectional transfer for tasks sharing knowledge.
- System: end-to-end UMM > matched planner-executor pipeline on complex U+G tasks.

### Why it matters

Provides a design principle for the increasingly popular native-UMM regime: *don't share everything, share the right thing*. Specifically: share semantics, split the visual computation for conflicting objectives. Refutes the "UMMs are just a unified interface" framing — the value is in the shared knowledge, unlocked by right-sized specialization.

## 10. DroneCATS

**Authors:** Jaewoo Park, Minyoung Lee, Sukmin Seo, Moonbin Yim, Hyunwook Yoon, Dohoon Ryu, Daehee Kim, Myungseo Song, Jihyuk Byun, Seunggyu Chang, Taeho Kil, Jiseob Kim, Bado Lee, Geewook Kim — *NAVER Cloud*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.01404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01404) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.01404) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.01404)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `agents`, `eval`, `VLA`

### TL;DR

A benchmark that drops MLLMs directly into a drone control loop with the entire action space declared *only in the prompt* — no fine-tuning, no function schemas. Tasks: commanding, approaching, tracking, searching, plus multi-drone commanding. Scales models down to 2B to find where things break first. The stark finding: navigation isn't the failing dimension; **protocol discipline** (declaring arrival, emitting correct terminators) is.

### Key idea

Treat the MLLM as the independent variable and the drone as a *swappable* embodiment. The agent architecture is intentionally thin — the model itself must yaw and search, deliberate, and self-declare arrival. The benchmark then diagnoses failures by capability and scale.

### Main finding

Even simple embodied settings are far from solved. Small (2B) open models often navigate more reliably than frontier models but *lose the episode* by declaring arrival prematurely or never. Multi-drone commanding amplifies the divide: small models blindly copy a single coordinate across drones. The gap between deployable-edge and frontier is not spatial reasoning — it's action-protocol discipline.

### Why it matters

Reframes the deployability question for embodied MLLMs. If you're building a small edge VLA, **spending compute on spatial reasoning is a lower ROI than spending it on protocol discipline** (declared-terminator training). Also a rare benchmark that pushes down to 2B for edge-agent inference.

## 11. Safin-1

**Authors:** Ming Zhang, Kaisen Yang, Shu Yu, Ermo Hua, Zhekai Chen, Cheng Jin, Jingnan Zheng, Yi Zhang, Zhongtian Ma, Jiawei Zhou, Sirui Chen, Qiaosheng Zhang, Xiang Wang, Ning Ding, Xia Hu, Bowen Zhou, Youbang Sun, Chaochao Lu — *Shanghai AI Lab (multi-org)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.00092](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00092) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.00092) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.00092)
**Topics:** `safety`, `architecture`, `memory`, `foundation-model`

### TL;DR

Argues safety should be an **intrinsic architectural property**, not a post-hoc behavioral overlay. Introduces **MARCH** (Memory-Anchor Routing across Context History) — a network that maintains structured memory states and content-conditionally retrieves history — then a **Safety State** that persistently specializes the model on safety without repeatedly modifying the backbone.

### Key idea

Frames safety as *state-native and adaptively maintainable*: routed memory states can carry persistent safety capabilities that are invoked through the model's native computation, unifying contextual memory with persistent capability adaptation. Test-time adaptation of the state doesn't touch backbone weights.

### Main keeps

Substantial safety-task improvements via the Safety State, with general capabilities, long-context understanding, retrieval, and efficiency all validated. The authors mark it as an **initial architectural exploration**, not a finished vision.

### Why it matters

If it holds up, this is a template for **architectural safety** — safety-relevant capability plumbed into the model like retrieval or long-context memory, not stitched on with SFT. Interesting overlap with SSM/state-based architectures, agent-memory literature, and constitutional/self-critique training.

## 12. EAL-Bench

**Authors:** Tommaso Cerruti, Mika Okamoto, Ansel Kaplan Erol — *Independent*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.01836](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01836) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.01836) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.01836)
**Topics:** `safety`, `agents`, `memory`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Identifies a new failure class for long-running agents: **endogenous authorization laundering** — the agent's own persistent memory misrepresents evolving permissions, so it grants itself authority the underlying history never permitted, *without any external attack*. Ships EAL-Bench to measure how faithfully memory preserves authorization state and how errors propagate.

### Key idea

Persistent memory is a *policy surface*, not just a performance component. Under incremental updates, memory writers create false authority for up to 50.2% of unauthorized requests; executors then act on that false authority in 98.6% of trials. Two mitigations — source-event backing for stored permissions, and permission changes tracked via bounded event sourcing — reduce laundering but also reject more legitimate actions (safety-utility tradeoff).

### Main finding

Across five writers × two executors × three domains (procurement, cybersec, finance): incremental-update writers laundered up to 50.2% of unauthorized requests; downstream execution rate 98.6%. Both proposed safeguards trade laundering-rate for legitimate-action-rejection.

### Why it matters

Long-horizon agent research has focused on capability and cost; EAL-Bench points at a **fundamentally new class of policy failure** that emerges from the memory substrate itself. Suggests memory-writer training and event-sourcing-style state representations may become standard agent safety hygiene.

## 13. AgentJudgeBench

**Authors:** Abhigya Verma, Amit Kumar Saha, Seganrasan Subramanian, Sai Harshitha Aluru — *Independent*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.26623](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26623) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.26623) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.26623)
**Topics:** `eval`, `agents`, `LLM-judge`

### TL;DR

A 3,808-instance benchmark for **LLM-as-a-judge reliability on agentic tool-calling over workflow DAGs** — a structured evaluation setting distinct from open-ended preference judging. Six DAG topologies × three difficulty tiers, five generators (3B–70B + GPT-5.4), six judges (20B → frontier), paired with/without ground truth.

### Key idea

Judge alignment degrades monotonically with task difficulty and 1.5× faster without ground truth. On hard queries without ground truth, all six judges converge to a **77–82% ceiling regardless of scale** — a structural ceiling driven by task difficulty that model capacity alone cannot overcome. Ground-truth exposure is not uniformly helpful (over-anchoring on GPT-5.4 / Gemini-2.5-Pro).

### Main finding

- No-ground-truth ceiling: 77–82% band across all six judges.
- CoT and judge temperature: negligible effect.
- Structured rubrics: up to +6.5pp, non-uniform.
- With ground truth, QwQ-32B best matches programmatic reference; without, frontier judges lead only marginally.

### Why it matters

Concrete evidence that LLM-judge unreliability on agentic evaluation is **structural, not capability-limited** — a bounded reliability that all judges hit. Guidelines for reliable evaluation follow: use rubrics; don't over-scale the judge; don't blindly trust ground-truth-anchored judges.

## 14. Harness-of-Harness

**Authors:** Haoyang Yan, Min-le Su, Hangfan Zhang, Zhanhao Li, Chen Zhang, Shao Zhang, Yang Chen, Lei Bai, Shuyue Hu — *Shanghai AI Lab*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.01481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01481) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.01481) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.01481)
**Topics:** `agents`, `coding`, `RL`

### TL;DR

A framework that wraps existing coding-agent harnesses (Codex, OpenCode, Pi) in an iterative **planning-coding-testing loop** with continual improvement across days-long runs. Balances repair vs. capability growth, scopes work into small verifiable increments, separates implementation-time testing from independent evaluation, and versions project history. 52.25% average / 82.86% max relative gain over standalone harnesses on three benchmarks.

### Key idea

Rather than build a new agent, treat existing harnesses as **primitives** and orchestrate them across days. Key design constraints: (1) verifiable outputs, not prescribed workflows; (2) small increments over long-form plans; (3) skill/tool exposure grows progressively; (4) versioned history is reused, not recreated; (5) evaluation is independent of implementation-time testing to prevent Goodharting.

### Main finding

Across GameCraft-Bench, FrontierSWE, ProgramBench with three harness-model pairs: **+52.25% average** relative gain, **+82.86% max** after three iterations. Multi-day deployment: 70+ iterations autonomously build a fully playable FPS (storyline, mechanics, visuals, audio).

### Why it matters

The right primitive for autonomous SWE may be **loops over harnesses**, not new agent models. HoH is a template for how to spend agent compute across days without divergence — reusable across harness-model pairs and applicable to any long-horizon deliverable with independent verification.

## 15. InternReviewer

**Authors:** Xuerui Su, Liya Guo, Qizhi Pei, Qipeng Guo, Zhongbo Tian, Lijun Wu, Kai Chen, Zun Wang — *Shanghai AI Lab / Fudan*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.28612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28612) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.28612) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.28612)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `reward-modeling`

### TL;DR

Two agentic-RL-trained scholarly agents (peer-reviewer, rebuttal-writer) with a **unified objective reward system** designed to sidestep subjective LLM-judge bias: reference-anchored semantic alignment + structural compliance + citation-verification against real-time arXiv-retrieval logs.

### Key idea

The novel piece is the reward system: rather than a subjective judge, use *objective* multi-dimensional criteria — semantic alignment against reference reviews, structural compliance to a rubric, and a strict verification that cross-checks every citation the agent produces against its own tool-use log. The last piece is essentially a hallucination-elimination mechanism at reward-time.

### Main finding

Significant improvements in reasoning depth and citation accuracy vs baseline. Concrete numbers not extracted from the abstract.

### Why it matters

An operational recipe for **hallucination-eliminating reward design** in agentic RL: gate the reward on tool-use log verification, not on judge opinion. Transferrable to any research-agent domain where citation authenticity is measurable.
