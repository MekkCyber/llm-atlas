## 1. Compile by Training

**Authors:** Yuntian Deng, Pengyu Nie, Stuart Shieber — *University of Waterloo / Harvard*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.04199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04199) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04199) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.04199)
**Topics:** `LLM`, `distillation`, `adapter`

### TL;DR

Treats a natural-language spec as *source code* for a small neural function. At "compile time" one or more teacher LLMs generate task-specific examples, and those examples train a compact adapter on a small interpreter model. The compiled function then runs standalone — no teacher, no remote API — and can be versioned and composed like ordinary software modules.

### Key idea

Cast prompt-to-function distillation as a compilation pipeline: NL spec → synthetic data (from teachers) → LoRA-style adapter on a compact interpreter. Once emitted, the function has no runtime dependency on the teacher stack.

### Main finding

On FuzzyBench-Hard — a subset where the prior "Program-as-Weights" fast compiler emits zero exact matches — compile-by-training reaches **83.6% semantic accuracy**, at a cost of about a minute of compile time (vs. seconds for the fast compiler). Demoed on a website helper, a language-controlled 3D avatar, and an English↔Claudish translator.

### Why it matters

Frames small-adapter distillation as *software artifacts you can ship*, not model weights you're stuck fine-tuning ad-hoc. If the compile-vs-quality tradeoff generalizes, this becomes a cleaner substitute for repeated frontier-API calls in production glue code.

## 2. Terminal-Universe

**Authors:** Jie Wu, Zhenru Zhang, Beichen Zhang, Xuwu Wang, Yuhui Su, Mouxiang Chen, Peng Wang, Zhihai Wang, Que Shen, Hao Zhou, An Yang, Fei Huang, Yujiu Yang, Dayiheng Liu — *Qwen (Alibaba) / Tsinghua*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.04148](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04148) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04148) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.04148)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `data`

### TL;DR

Terminal agents log tons of trajectories but almost no *runnable environments*. Terminal-Universe reconstructs the environment each trajectory ran in by replaying its file-operation history, then a completion agent fills in missing files/deps. From that reproduced workspace it re-derives the original task and synthesizes new ones — scaling along breadth (cross-workspace queries) and depth (multi-turn user simulation).

### Key idea

Every executed tool call in an existing trajectory (touch, edit, mkdir, delete, …) is a fingerprint of the workspace state before that step. Invert those calls to reconstruct a partial workspace, then close the gaps with a completion agent. Environments become recoverable *from the trajectories that used them*, sidestepping expensive from-scratch environment synthesis.

### Main finding

Applied to public terminal-agent trajectories, produces **37.3k task-sufficient environments**. SFT-ing Qwen3.5-27B on this corpus lifts Terminal-Bench 2.1 by **+11.9 points** single-round and EvoCode-Bench v2 MT@4 by **+13.8 points** multi-round.

### Why it matters

Environment scarcity is the current bottleneck for training verifiable coding agents. Recovering environments *from the training data you already have* changes the economics — every open-source trajectory dump becomes a pipeline stage rather than a dead artifact.

## 3. LLaDA-Image

**Authors:** Chuyan Chen, Haoxing Chen, Kun Chen, Zhenglin Cheng, Long Cui, Ruishan Fang, Zhangxuan Gu, Zhicheng Huang, Zhenzhong Lan, et al. — *Ant Group / Westlake University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.03796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03796) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.03796) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.03796)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `multimodal`, `DiT`

### TL;DR

Open-source 6B DiT trained *from scratch*, paired with a frozen LLaDA2.0-Mini diffusion-LM as its vision-language understanding module. Pipeline is image-only pretraining first (220M samples, 98% real images), then paired-data mid-training. Ships parameter-free RMSNorm, the Muon optimizer, and a 2–4-step Turbo distillation.

### Key idea

Two-stage curriculum for a unified text-to-image model: build a strong *visual prior* on image-only pretraining before ever seeing text pairs. Combined with parameter-free RMSNorm throughout the DiT and Muon as the optimizer, and Turbo distillation for few-step inference.

### Main finding

**53.53 / 53.38** on Qwen-Image-Bench (English / Chinese) — new SoTA among open-source models on both tracks. Turbo variant retains photorealism at 2–4 sampling steps.

### Why it matters

The "learn visual physics first, then bind to text" recipe pushes back on paired-data-first orthodoxy for image DiTs. Releasing weights + code + recipes gives the open ecosystem a real from-scratch reference DiT at 6B.

## 4. BCIT — Conditional Experience Transfer

**Authors:** Wenfeng Feng, Weiqing Li, Abudukelimu Wuerkaixi, Guohua Liu, Yuewei Zhang — *Alibaba Cloud Computing*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.26730](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26730) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.26730) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.26730)
**Topics:** `post-training`, `LLM`

### TL;DR

Autonomous post-training loops accumulate "this update worked" evidence — but once the parent model has drifted, blindly replaying old winners can waste compute or actively hurt. BCIT (Boundary-Calibrated Intervention Transfer) authorizes each candidate update *before* weight-changing training by checking whether prior evidence still applies to the current parent, and does a bounded probe when it can't tell.

### Key idea

Treat every logged post-training result as **effect + context** (parent, data, stage), not a context-free rule. A candidate update is authorized only if applicability conditions on the current state match; hard conflicts veto it; when uncertain, run a bounded training trial to gather fresh evidence.

### Main finding

On a 4B model post-trained across finance reasoning, text-to-SQL, and function calling, BCIT authorizes fewer harmful updates and delivers higher final-model quality *at matched compute* than baseline autonomous-post-training policies.

### Why it matters

Puts a decision-theoretic frame on autonomous fine-tuning pipelines. The unit of interest becomes *credible experience transfer* rather than "run more experiments," which matters as tuning agents become common.

## 5. Random Attention

**Authors:** Heng Wang, Jielin Qiu, Wenting Zhao, Cheng Qian, Liangwei Yang, Jiawei Han, Heng Ji, Silvio Savarese, Shelby Heinecke, Huan Wang — *Salesforce Research / UIUC / Cornell*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.03430](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03430) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.03430) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.03430)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

Reasoning traces blow up the KV cache. Every "smart" cache-eviction method scores tokens by expected future importance and keeps the top-k. This paper strips the scoring: keep the prompt, then evict uniformly at random per head. It matches the best score-based evictor on four models × six reasoning tasks — while running 32–43% faster in vLLM.

### Key idea

The "signal" in importance scores is much weaker than assumed. Once the prompt is protected, uniform random eviction inside each attention head is competitive — no scoring, no bookkeeping.

### Main finding

Matches the strongest baseline evictor across four LLMs and six reasoning benchmarks, at **+32–43% throughput** in vLLM. The absent-scoring result is the load-bearing claim, not an aesthetic detail.

### Why it matters

Kills a common assumption in KV-cache research (that importance scoring earns its keep). Also gives production inference stacks an essentially-free lever for long-CoT decoding.

## 6. LatentPress

**Authors:** Zhengze Zhou, Hejian Sang — *LinkedIn*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.01507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01507) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.01507) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.01507)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`, `LLM`

### TL;DR

Compressed context is usually shipped as text (summaries) or images (OCR-recovered). LatentPress writes conversation histories and long documents directly into continuous soft tokens that the frozen decoder consumes through its input-embedding interface — no text or OCR intermediate at inference time.

### Key idea

A small "writer" adapter (0.1% of decoder params) produces memory tokens at 4–16× compression. The decoder reads them as if they were embeddings of ordinary tokens. Trains only the adapter; the decoder is fully frozen.

### Main finding

LongMemEval: **0.504 acc @ 7.70× compression** vs. 0.490 uncompressed evidence — beats text summaries (0.184) and OCR-based compression (0.312–0.426). Writing is ~10× faster than summarization / OCR; reading is 5–9× faster than raw context.

### Why it matters

Points at a third context-compression channel — machine-facing soft tokens — that skips human-readable intermediates altogether. If it generalizes across decoders, it becomes a plausible substitute for retrieval + summarization pipelines.

## 7. One-Shot On-Policy Distillation

**Authors:** Zixuan Fu, Bingxiang He, Yuxin Zuo, Haohuan Huang, Jinqian Zhang, Ruhang Xiao, Cheng Qian, Qinyu Luo, Huan-ang Gao, Yudong Wang, Zhiyuan Liu, Ning Ding, Chaojun Xiao — *Tsinghua / OpenBMB*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.04172](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04172) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04172) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.04172)
**Topics:** `post-training`, `distillation`, `data`

### TL;DR

On-policy distillation combines student rollouts with token-level teacher supervision. This paper cranks the data axis to its limit: **one training query**. One-shot OPD keeps improving for hundreds of steps and recovers most of a full-data run's gains across tasks and model families.

### Key idea

Diagnose why: a single query generates a wide *state-coverage* trajectory space under on-policy sampling. Introduce a state-coverage metric to quantify that supervision surface, and show it's what drives OPD's gains — not query variety.

### Main finding

Across multiple task domains and model families, one-shot OPD recovers *most* of the full-data OPD improvement — evidence that OPD's data-efficiency ceiling is a rollout diversity ceiling, not a prompt-set ceiling.

### Why it matters

Reframes what "data" means for on-policy distillation: rollout state coverage, not corpus size. Makes OPD dramatically cheaper to run in principle, and points at compute-bound rather than data-bound as the real bottleneck.

## 8. Gated DeltaNet under NVFP4 W4A4

**Authors:** Sergii Kozyrev, Davyd Maiboroda — *Independent*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.04098](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04098) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04098) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.04098)
**Topics:** `quantization`, `architecture`

### TL;DR

Hybrid LLMs pair softmax attention with linear-attention layers like Gated DeltaNet (GDN). This paper W4A4-quantizes all 496 linear layers of a 27B hybrid to NVFP4 and *matches full-precision quality* — a 17.5 GiB checkpoint. It also explains why: recurrence forgets state impulses, block scaling handles outliers, gate projections are naturally robust, and per-token quantization cost stabilizes over long context.

### Key idea

Analyze the linear-attention path specifically. Its recurrent state's exponential forgetting damps quantization noise across tokens; softmax attention would carry the same noise indefinitely. So GDN tolerates aggressive activation quantization *because* it forgets.

### Main finding

**W4A4 NVFP4 across 496 linear layers of a 27B hybrid** matches FP baseline quality. Ships a runnable quantized checkpoint (~17.5 GiB).

### Why it matters

Argues that hybrid attention-recurrence architectures aren't just fast — they're *quantization-friendly by construction*. A concrete point in favor of hybrid over pure softmax for on-device / low-precision serving.

## 9. Puffin-World

**Authors:** Kang Liao, Yihang Luo, Xiao-Ming Wu, Linyi Jin, Size Wu, Chunyu Lin, Yao Zhao, Fei Wang, Wei Li, Chen Change Loy — *NTU S-Lab / MMLab*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.04196](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04196) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04196) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.04196)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `world-model`

### TL;DR

Unified multimodal model that jointly represents three *native* 3D world states — physics (gravity field, latitude), geometry (depth), and appearance (image) — plus an Omni-Camera representation. Trained on Puffin-16M (15M vision-language-camera triplets + 1M trajectories) and used for closed-loop world exploration.

Bind an explicit camera geometry to a unified generative model so that appearance and geometry are decoded jointly for each future view. Physics is propagated forward as a first-class world state, not a rendering side-effect.

### Main finding

Enables interleaved closed-loop tasks (mimic exploration, self-calibrated exploration) that decouple from external offline modules. Fully released weights, code, and dataset.

### Why it matters

Where prior video/world models bolt on cameras and physics via external modules, Puffin-World internalizes them into the generative loop. If the paradigm scales, it argues for a single-net world model over a modular renderer + planner stack.

## 10. CORE — Reranker Distillation for MLLM Embeddings

**Authors:** Tingyu Song, Mingxin Li, Yanzhao Zhang, Dingkun Long, Chu Liu, Pengjun Xie, Yilun Zhao, Shu Wu — *Alibaba Tongyi Lab*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.04083](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04083) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04083) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.04083)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `retrieval`, `distillation`

### TL;DR

MLLM embedding models routinely fail on *compositional* retrieval — scenes with the same objects but different attribute-object bindings. But the same backbone, used as a cross-attentive reranker, resolves those cases. CORE distills the reranker's judgments into the embedding model with a listwise Rank-KL objective.

### Key idea

Synthesize candidate lists spanning five compositional matching levels, then train the embedding model to reproduce the reranker's ranking (Rank-KL) rather than a contrastive positive/negative signal. Listwise supervision uses graded signal that contrastive throws away.

### Main finding

CORE-RERANKER-8B: **82.7% total average**, +10.7 pts over Jina-Reranker. CORE-EMBED-8B: best overall total (0.666) among evaluated embedding models. Improvements transfer to MCMR without hurting COCO/Flickr30K retrieval.

### Why it matters

Puts the "reranker teaches embedder" pattern on a solid footing for the multimodal setting — and offers a concrete alternative to contrastive learning when graded compositional signal is available.

## 11. DRACO — Dynamic Rubrics for Long-Horizon Agents

**Authors:** Shubham Gandhi, Saurabh Goyal, Kiran Kate, Yara Rizk — *IBM Research*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.04094](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04094) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04094) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.04094)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `rewards`

### TL;DR

For long-horizon agents without programmatic verifiers, rubric scoring gives one scalar per trajectory — too coarse for credit assignment. DRACO grows rubrics dynamically to track the current policy's capability, scores them per trajectory, and *redistributes* the scalar across responsible steps in closed form to produce per-step GRPO advantages — no learned attribution module.

### Key idea

Dynamic rubrics = curriculum of judgment criteria that evolve with the policy. Per-step advantage = closed-form redistribution of the trajectory-level rubric scores onto the steps that "activated" each criterion. Feeds directly into GRPO.

### Main finding

AppWorld: **+15.9 pts** over base, **+5.3 pts** over GRPO with the sparse ground-truth reward — *without using verifiers itself*. Transfers OOD: +5.3 pts over base on Tau-Bench, beating ground-truth-reward training and other rubric-based settings.

### Why it matters

For the outcome-blind regime where most real agent tasks live, DRACO is one of the cleanest recipes: dense advantages from a rubric-only signal, no attribution model, and gains that transfer OOD.

## 12. RealSWE

**Authors:** Gyuhyeong Kim, Hyojung Gwon, Jeonghyeon Kim, Kyuhong Shim, Sunjae Lee — *Sungkyunkwan University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.27831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27831) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.27831) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.27831)
**Topics:** `eval`, `agents`

### TL;DR

SWE-bench tasks are curated GitHub issues — long, structured, information-rich. Real user requests are neither. RealSWE decomposes tasks into 381 realistic variants that manipulate *information composition* and *style*, then measures how coding agents degrade under realistic input.

### Key idea

Compositional benchmark: fix the underlying task, vary which pieces of information (behavior spec, motivation, reproduction steps, expected output) are present, and vary linguistic style. Isolate what actually matters vs. what merely tracks in-distribution richness.

### Main finding

Realistic inputs drop resolution rate by **~6.4 pts on average**. Explicit behavior and motivation statements matter a lot; linguistic style barely does.

### Why it matters

Reveals that a chunk of coding-agent performance comes from information richness the deployment setting won't have. Argues for evaluation practice that separates *task difficulty* from *request quality*.

## 13. Environment Evolution for Terminal Agents

**Authors:** Zhiyuan Fan, Tinghao Yu, Yuanjun Cai, Jiang Zhou, Jiangtao Guan, Jincheng Liu, Yun Yang, Dingxin Hu, Zhuo Han, Xing Wu, Feng Zhang, Lilin Wang — *industry consortium*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.04128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04128) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04128) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.04128)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `data`

### TL;DR

Environments synthesized once from scratch become too easy as the policy learns. Prior co-evolution methods re-synthesize based on on-policy weaknesses — but that limits generalization. This paper evolves environments **off-policy** along three difficulty-relevant directions and schedules generations across training, giving continuous learning signal.

### Key idea

Derive three orthogonal difficulty axes from the multi-turn RL objective. Evolve environments generation-by-generation along those axes via a multi-agent harness that doesn't depend on the training policy's rollouts. Result: environments stay ahead of the policy without leaking training signal into environment design.

### Main finding

Environment-evolution rollouts (with Hy4 preview, Claude Opus 5, GPT-5.6 Sol as evolution drivers) yield harder environments. Simple long-horizon RL on those environments: **+14.4 pts** Qwen3.6-27B and **+18.0 pts** Qwen3.6-35B-A3B on Terminal-Bench 2.1.

### Why it matters

Together with Terminal-Universe (#2), stakes out an "environments as first-class training data" viewpoint. Off-policy evolution is a clean cure for the co-evolution generalization problem.

## 14. Visual-Token Allocation in Long-Video MLLMs

**Authors:** Prakhar Khatri — *Independent*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.03820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03820) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.03820) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.03820)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

An hour of video at 1 fps is 3,600 frames but only a small slice fits in an MLLM's visual-token budget. This paper does a controlled study of *how to spend that budget*: which frames to select, how much to spatially compress, and whether to reinvest freed tokens into more frames.

### Key idea

Decompose the token-budget problem into three levers: (a) frame selection, (b) per-frame spatial compression, (c) reinvestment. Study each independently on the same models to isolate their contributions.

### Main finding

Query-conditioned frame selection substantially beats uniform sampling; a classical sparse-approximation selector is competitive with custom neural selectors; spatial compression *plus more frames* is a net win over dense frames alone.

### Why it matters

Cuts through the noise in long-video MLLM papers by testing the three levers under a shared budget. Practical guidance for anyone shipping a long-video model.

## 15. RLVR Narrows the Solution Space

**Authors:** Qiancheng Zhou, Ruizhe Li — *University of Aberdeen*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.29188](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29188) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.29188) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.29188)
**Topics:** `RL`, `reasoning`, `interp`

### TL;DR

RLVR lifts pass@1 but shrinks the *solution space* — test-time scaling stops paying off. This paper localizes where the shrinkage happens: it's at the *entrance* (early decision tokens), not inside the chain of computation. Alternative solutions remain executable; the policy just no longer initiates them. A simple parameter-interpolation intervention restores diversity.

### Key idea

Trace where likelihood mass shifts between the pre- and post-RLVR policy. Almost all of the shift lands on early decision points — the "which strategy to try" tokens. Later computation tokens are approximately unchanged. Intervene by interpolating the policy weights back toward the base model to reintroduce entrance-level diversity.

### Main finding

On Countdown across two model configurations, solution coverage drops by up to **67%** after RLVR. The entrance-locked pattern is broad — seen across benchmarks — but staged training pipelines can prevent it. Parameter interpolation partially restores diversity.

### Why it matters

Names a concrete failure mode of RLVR — collapsed entrance entropy — with a clean localization and a working recovery. Directly relevant to the "pass@k stops improving" phenomenon debated in reasoning-RL papers.
