## 1. NCP-ArchPreview

**Authors:** Intern-NCP Team (Jiaqi Cao, Chiyu Chen, … Dahua Lin, Bowen Zhou, et al.) — *Shanghai AI Lab / SII / SJTU consortium*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.10715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10715) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.10715) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.10715)
**Topics:** `LLM`, `pre-training`, `architecture`

### TL;DR

An 8.9B pretraining run that augments standard next-token prediction with a **Next Concept Prediction (NCP)** objective: the model learns to predict discrete concept codes that span multiple tokens, drawn from a product-quantized codebook built directly from its own hidden states. Concept predictions are fed back into the token stream, so the token head still generates autoregressively. Trained on 5.73T Dolma-3 tokens; matches OLMo-3-7B's final pretraining loss at 51.3% of the tokens.

### Key idea

Two heads, one model. A **Concept Module** predicts the next concept — a discrete code from a self-constructed PQ codebook over hidden states, covering a span longer than one token. Predicted concepts are injected back into the token-level context, so NTP and NCP are jointly optimized end-to-end. Concepts act as a coarser-grained forecasting target that supervises longer-range structure without leaving the autoregressive framework.

### Main finding

Pretraining efficiency: OLMo-3-7B's terminal loss is reached with **~51% of the training tokens**. After a full 5.73T-token run, NCP-ArchPreview beats OLMo-3-7B by **2.45 macro-avg points** downstream, with a **+5.99 GSM8K** delta. Largest latent-space LM demonstrated to date.

### Why it matters

Multi-token / concept prediction has been circling the field for years (Meta's MTP, MEDUSA-style multi-head speculation, LCM). NCP-ArchPreview shows a jointly-learned concept space over hidden states can deliver real pretraining-token savings at 9B scale, not a small-model curiosity — a plausible successor to plain NTP for frontier pretraining.

## 2. SenseNova-U1.5

**Authors:** Haiwen Diao, Jiahao Wang, Chenjing Ding, Hanming Deng, … Dahua Lin (sponsor), Wenxiu Sun, Ziwei Liu (senior leads) — *SenseTime*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.11929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11929) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.11929) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.11929)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `unified`, `MoT`

### TL;DR

SenseTime's 8B **Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)** native unified model. Understands, reasons about, and generates images inside one **encoder-free and VAE-free** stack — no CLIP tower, no VAE latent bridge. Trained with spatially-coherent patch reconstruction, native resolutions up to 4K, and post-trained by multi-expert on-policy distillation across aesthetics, bilingual text rendering, infographic generation, and image editing.

### Key idea

Drop the two "translators" (vision encoder + VAE) that every prior VLM stitched together, and let a single MoT backbone see raw patches on both the input and output paths. Route capabilities through **specialized post-training experts** (aesthetic, bilingual glyph, infographic, editing) then consolidate them back into one policy via on-policy distillation. Patch reconstruction is the training signal that keeps the visual interface spatially coherent.

### Main finding

Across image-fidelity, complex-composition, text-rendering, multi-reference editing, and interleaved generation benchmarks, U1.5 improves over encoder+VAE unified baselines while preserving subject identity, geometry, and unmodified regions during edits. Generalizes to long structured visual instructions despite limited exposure to that data.

### Why it matters

The last year of unified multimodal work has been arguing over how to glue a CLIP-family encoder to a diffusion VAE — U1.5 says the glue itself is the problem. If encoder-free + VAE-free scales, it collapses the multimodal stack from three trained systems to one, and moves visual planning/creation onto the same substrate as text reasoning.

## 3. IMO Gold with Nemotron

**Authors:** Ivan Moshkov, Stephen Ge, George Armstrong, Wei Du, Sadegh Mahdavi, Igor Gitman — *NVIDIA*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.10712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10712) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.10712) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.10712)
**Topics:** `RL`, `reasoning`, `math`

### TL;DR

An **open recipe** — data, code, checkpoints — for training a Nemotron 3 Ultra checkpoint that hits 30/42 on IMO 2026, above the gold-medal threshold. Combines SFT on curated proof traces with an RL loop that generates, verifies, and refines *natural-language* proofs (no formal solver). Ships an accompanying benchmark of 200 novel olympiad problems.

### Key idea

Iterative proof-refinement pipeline entirely in natural language: propose → critique → repair, with a verifier LLM scoring rigor and used as reward signal. RL is applied on top of SFT-warmed proof-writer checkpoints; the pipeline replaces per-step formal proof-checking with model-graded verification, opening olympiad-level RL to shops without a Lean/Isabelle stack.

### Main finding

**30/42 at IMO 2026** — above gold-medal cutoff — with an entirely open weights + data + code release. Companion benchmark of 200 novel olympiad problems for future work.

### Why it matters

Prior IMO-gold results (DeepMind's AlphaProof / AlphaGeometry stack) shipped as closed systems tied to formal environments. An open, verifier-in-the-loop natural-language recipe brings competition math within reach of open-weights labs, and validates model-graded verification as a scalable substitute for symbolic solvers on unformalizable proofs.

## 4. HyQuant

**Authors:** Jiatong Ding, Bingxin Xing, Yu Zhang, Dian Ding, Xiaodong Yi, … Yiming Zhang — *Xiamen U. / SJTU / Xi'an JTU / Tencent Penglai Lab*
**Links:** [arXiv:2608.27875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27875) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.27875) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.27875)
**Topics:** `quantization`, `efficient-inference`, `attention`

### TL;DR

Hybrid-precision quantization scheme for LLM attention: aggressively low-bit for the bulk of KV/attention state, but keeps a **small set of vertical-line tokens** and a **local window** in high precision to absorb outliers. Applies across both prefill and decode; ships fused kernels for the mixed layout.

### Key idea

Instead of the usual smoothing-only approach to outliers, HyQuant splits attention state by *structural role*: (a) global "vertical lines" (tokens that many later tokens attend to heavily) and (b) the recent local window stay in high precision; (c) everything else is compressed to low bits. Both prefill and decode paths are supported with fused ops that fold precision selection into the kernel.

### Main finding

Preserves accuracy across benchmarks at very low bit-widths where uniform-precision attention quantization degrades. Efficient fused kernels enable end-to-end memory/latency gains on both prefill and decode.

### Why it matters

Attention quantization has been the stubborn last mile of LLM quantization — weights and MLP activations are already in 4-bit territory, but attention state degrades fast. Structural (not per-tensor) selection of high-precision tokens is a clean generalization of "keep outliers" for the attention module, useful for long-context serving.

## 5. Negative Self-Distillation

**Authors:** Rongcan Pei, Zhepei Wei, Shuyao Xu, Xinyu Zhu, Wei-Lin Chen, Yu Meng — *UVA*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.11699](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11699) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.11699) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.11699)
**Topics:** `post-training`, `reasoning`, `distillation`

### TL;DR

On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) — teach a student its own privileged-info-conditioned traces — turns out to *hurt* reasoning: it kills the exploratory, self-corrective behaviors the model needs. Negative Self-Distillation (NSD) flips the sign: the model learns to **diverge** from its own flawed traces, rather than imitate confident-but-shortcut traces.

### Key idea

Traditional self-distillation minimizes KL(student ∥ teacher). NSD minimizes KL against *anti-targets*: rollouts the model got wrong become negative teachers, and the policy is pushed away from their token distributions. This preserves calibrated uncertainty and self-correction, which OPSD-style positive distillation actively suppresses.

### Main finding

On complex reasoning benchmarks, NSD reverses the degradation OPSD produces and outperforms both OPSD and vanilla SFT baselines. The paper shows the OPSD failure mode in an additive-decoding gate-activation analysis.

### Why it matters

The "self-improvement loop" has become the default late-stage post-training move (act as your own teacher on privileged solutions). NSD gives the community a warning shot — for hard reasoning, that loop is negative-value — plus a clean fix that fits inside the same on-policy machinery.

## 6. EvoSafeHarness

**Authors:** Nanxi Li, Yingzi Ma, Yulong Cao, Edward Suh, Bo Li, Dawn Song, Chaowei Xiao — *UChicago / NVIDIA / Cornell / Berkeley*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.05903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05903) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.05903) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.05903)
**Topics:** `safety`, `agents`, `red-teaming`

### TL;DR

Automatically synthesizes agent-safety harnesses (both natural-language policy and executable guard code) tailored to a target model + domain. An evolutionary search jointly evolves the policy prose and code logic, tested against red-team attacks. On one benchmark, attack success drops from 45.6% → 10.0%, with better utility than fixed expert defenses.

### Key idea

Two-track co-evolution: candidate harnesses are (policy_text, guard_code) pairs. Attacks and utility tasks are evaluated in a sandbox; fitness combines attack-block rate with task-completion. Mutation edits both the natural-language policy and the executable check logic; crossovers mix policies with guards from different lineages. The result is a per-model, per-domain guardrail that generalizes better than one-size-fits-all defenses.

### Main finding

Attack success rate drops from **45.6% → 10.0%** on one benchmark suite while retaining more utility than baseline defenses. Adaptation-per-domain outperforms hand-crafted general-purpose harnesses.

### Why it matters

Agent guardrails have been either huge frontier-API moderators or hand-coded rulesets. EvoSafeHarness treats guardrail design itself as a search problem, and shows the search can be automated without a human-in-the-loop policy author. Pairs naturally with the recent trend of shipping agent-specific safety cases.

## 7. X-AuT

**Authors:** Haojun Zhang, Yi Zou, Min Chen, Qize Yu, Lianrui Fan, Xini Ding, Hao Li, Shuchang Zhou, Xianming Liu, Shiyu Huang — *XPENG AI*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.11412](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11412) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.11412) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.11412)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `audio-LLM`, `distillation`

### TL;DR

Progressive layer-pruning recipe for the *audio encoder* inside a speech LLM, coupled with **cross-scale distillation** + representation alignment + behavioral probing + LoRA repair. Pruning Qwen3-ASR-0.6B's audio tower from 18 → 16 layers actually *improves* macro-average error (5.61% → 5.27%) across ten CN/EN benchmarks.

### Key idea

Prune the audio encoder incrementally, layer by layer, restoring accuracy after each cut with four repair mechanisms: **(1)** behavioral probes over intermediate acoustic representations, **(2)** representation alignment to the pre-pruned teacher's features, **(3)** **cross-scale distillation** matching multi-resolution audio features, and **(4)** LoRA adapters that absorb residual error. The whole loop stays cheap because only the pruned layer's neighborhood is retrained.

### Main finding

18 → 16 encoder layers, macro-avg word/char error rate **5.61 % → 5.27 %** on ten Chinese-English public ASR benchmarks — *fewer parameters, lower error*, showing over-parameterization in the audio tower of speech LLMs.

### Why it matters

Speech LLMs inherit oversized audio towers from their ASR pretraining. X-AuT gives a task-preserving surgical route to shrink that tower, which is where latency dominates on-device. Cross-scale distillation is a reusable recipe for other multimodal encoders (vision, video).

## 8. Image Tokenizers as Visual Languages

**Authors:** Siting Li, Zhengyang Wang, Simon Shaolei Du, Xi Chen, Yang Liu — *University of Washington / Meta*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.09143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09143) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.09143) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.09143)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `tokenization`, `eval`

### TL;DR

A controlled scaling study of **image tokenizer choice** inside unified multimodal models. Track text-loss, image-loss, and bidirectional-loss separately as training proceeds. Three findings: tokenizers rank differently under each loss; reconstruction quality does *not* predict downstream performance; tokenizer choice measurably affects *text* modeling in joint training.

### Key idea

Treat image tokenizers as the "visual language" of a unified LM and measure them the way we'd measure a text tokenizer: track loss curves per modality across a scaling sweep, on the *same* joint pretraining setup. Compare tokenizers under three prediction objectives (text-only, image-only, bidirectional) so trade-offs surface instead of being averaged away.

### Main finding

- Task-specific losses (text / image / bidirectional) scale differently and can rank tokenizers in different orders on the same backbone.
- Better VAE reconstruction FID does **not** translate to better downstream multimodal task performance.
- Choice of image tokenizer changes the *text* modeling loss in joint training — modalities are not independent under a shared LM head.

### Why it matters

The community has quietly assumed reconstruction = good tokenizer. This is the clearest disproof yet, and it reframes tokenizer selection as a *joint-training* problem, not a standalone codec problem. Rescales conclusions from single-metric tokenizer bakeoffs.

## 9. SpatialBlock

**Authors:** Soohyun Ryu, Sohee Kim, Eunho Yang — *KAIST*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.07064](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07064) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.07064) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.07064)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `data`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

15k **synthetic block-stacking problems** designed to teach LVLMs foundational 3D-from-2D reasoning: viewpoint transformation, occlusion, projection, and structural combination. Small dataset, controlled-color visual anchors, big real-world spatial-task gains.

### Key idea

Isolate the 3D→2D reasoning skill from confounders by generating **fully synthetic** block-stacking configurations with programmable viewpoint and color. Controlled color modulation provides visual anchors that force *anchor-based* rather than holistic-pattern reasoning. Fine-tune LVLMs on this compact SFT corpus and measure carryover to real spatial tasks.

### Main finding

Models fine-tuned on the 15k SpatialBlock corpus improve on real-world spatial-reasoning benchmarks despite the training data being entirely synthetic — evidence that spatial primitives are transferable.

### Why it matters

Spatial reasoning has been a persistent VLM weakness; expensive real-world 3D data collection has been the bottleneck. SpatialBlock demonstrates a cheap synthetic route with real transfer, and provides a controlled testbed for isolating the specific spatial primitives that fail.

## 10. Building Multilingual Bridges

**Authors:** Mehrnaz Mofakhami, Ananya Sahu, Alejandro R. Salamanca, Daniel D'souza, Alexandre Berard, Thomas Euyang, Marzieh Fadaee, Julia Kreutzer — *Cohere / Mila*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.10445](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10445) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.10445) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.10445)
**Topics:** `data`, `reasoning`, `multilingual`

### TL;DR

Reasoning LMs answer in English even when prompted in Swahili. **Tiny Aya L2-Thinker** (3.35B) hits >93 % in-language reasoning across **60 languages** using nothing but careful **data mixing** — no per-language reasoning supervision. Data mixing is the lever; reasoning is language-agnostic behavior once the mix is right.

### Key idea

Cast in-language reasoning as a data-mixture problem, not a supervision problem. Sweep multilingual pretraining/SFT mixtures, characterize which mixtures transfer chain-of-thought across typologically distant languages, and pin the recipe that makes English reasoning traces "port" without needing reasoning traces in the target language.

### Main finding

**>93 % in-language reasoning across 60 languages** at 3.35B parameters. Reasoning transfer is achieved through data mixture alone, without target-language CoT training data.

### Why it matters

Every open-weights reasoning model to date defaults back to English mid-CoT. This paper shifts the frame from "we need CoT data in every language" to "we need the right pretraining mixture", which is achievable with existing corpora. Practical unlock for reasoning-in-Swahili, -Bengali, -Tagalog deployments.

## 11. Beyond Solver Verdicts

**Authors:** Vikash Singh, Debargha Ganguly, Aman Goel, Ali Torkamani, Xiaoxue Han, Joseph Lilien, Ferhat Erata, Vipin Chaudhary — *Case Western Reserve / Yale / others*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.11085](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11085) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.11085) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.11085)
**Topics:** `reward-model`, `math`, `neurosymbolic`

### TL;DR

Names a new failure mode of neurosymbolic autoformalization pipelines — **Verdict-Preserving Unfaithfulness**: a formal translation can be *wrong* about what the natural-language problem meant while still producing the "correct" solver verdict. Fix: distill an offline **Z3-equivalence oracle** into a continuous, reference-free reward model over translations. AUROC 0.961 for reference-equivalence detection; generalizes across translators and formal styles.

### Key idea

Treat autoformalization as a **reward-modeling** problem, not a verdict-matching one. Build a training signal from an offline SMT-based (Z3) equivalence checker between candidate formalizations and gold formalizations, then distill it into an LM that scores reference-equivalence without needing the gold formalization at inference. That reference-free score becomes the reward for RL on the translator.

### Main finding

**0.961 AUROC** for reference-equivalence detection; the reward model generalizes across translators and across formal target styles (SMT-LIB, Lean, etc.).

### Why it matters

Verdict-matching has been the default proxy for autoformalization quality, and it silently rewards models for lucky solver flukes. Generative Verification supplies a principled reward that isn't blind to meaning, which is what any RL loop over autoformalization now needs.

## 12. Recursive Code World Models

**Authors:** Zhiqi Li, Yuxuan Liao, Bo Zhu — *(affiliation not disclosed on HF)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.11499](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11499) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.11499) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.11499)
**Topics:** `agents`, `multimodal`, `code`

*Borderline: included because RCWM sits between "code world model" and "VLM coding agent" — both in-scope threads.*

### TL;DR

Represents 3D scenes as **recursive scene programs** — code that a VLM coding agent can generate, render, and refine against a reference image. A "global-local-global" solver preserves scene-wide geometry while filling in fine-scale structure. Reconstructs a complex 3D world from a single reference image, outperforming prior code-based image-to-scene methods.

### Key idea

Reconstruction as **iterative program synthesis**. A VLM coding agent proposes a recursive scene program, the engine renders it, the agent compares the render against the reference image, edits the program, and recurses. "Global-local-global" reserves the outer passes for global geometry constraints and the inner passes for local detail, so recursion doesn't blow up the whole scene when adding leaves.

### Main finding

Outperforms prior code-based image-to-scene reconstruction methods on complex 3D worlds from a single image, using recursive scene programs plus a VLM refinement loop.

### Why it matters

"Code as world representation" is a young thread — most world models are learned latents. Executable programs give hard structural guarantees (an inserted chair *is* a chair) and interpretability, at the cost of a search loop. Also a clean case study for VLM-driven agentic refinement.

## 13. World in World

**Authors:** Chenxi Song, Yanming Yang, Chi Zhang — *Westlake AGI Lab*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.11548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11548) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.11548) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.11548)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `video`, `world-model`

### TL;DR

A **training-free** control interface over a frozen causal video world model. You inject source frames, target-viewpoint projections, and geometry renderings as visual evidence into the model's own self-attention, and the model re-renders the world along a new camera path — no fine-tuning. Supports camera-controlled rerendering, long-horizon revisiting, and human-motion transfer.

### Key idea

Feed control signals as **extra visual tokens attended to by the world model's causal attention**, rather than as new conditioning heads or a control-adapter. Everything the frozen model needs — where the new viewpoint is, what geometry to keep, which appearance to preserve — is passed as image-shaped evidence, and the causal attention treats it just like history frames.

### Main finding

Camera-controlled video re-rendering across diverse viewpoint changes, with long-horizon revisit and human-motion transfer, all without additional training. Quality is preserved because the base video model is untouched.

### Why it matters

Every controllable video-generation paper this year has trained an adapter. "World in World" argues you can steer a strong world model with attention-time visual evidence alone, similar in spirit to test-time compute for LLMs. If it holds up on longer horizons, it collapses a lot of adapter-training infrastructure.

## 14. TempCloze

**Authors:** Wenqi Pei, Henry Hengyuan Zhao, Yilai Liu, Jiahao Meng, Han Chen, Ziyu Wang, Hongyang Du — *HKU and others*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.01515](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01515) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.01515) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.01515)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `video`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Video-cloze benchmark for Video-LLMs: given a video's beginning and ending, identify the true missing middle among four same-source distractors that share scenes and objects. 1,521 filtered long-take / egocentric videos, distractors probing three axes — *Semantic* (what happens), *Alignment* (when), *Progression* (how). 10 proprietary + 21 open-source Video-LLMs evaluated.

### Key idea

Kill the linguistic shortcut in video benchmarks by keeping distractors on the same source video with shared scenes/objects — the language of every answer is nearly identical, so the model has to *watch*. Decompose the temporal skill into three axes so the failure mode is diagnostic, not an aggregate score.

### Main finding

Across 31 Video-LLMs, **Alignment** (when an event should occur) is the dominant bottleneck: models get plausible semantics and local progression right but lose to distractors along the temporal-alignment axis.

### Why it matters

Every "long-video understanding" claim has been vulnerable to text-only shortcut solutions. TempCloze cleanly isolates the temporal skill and pinpoints Alignment as the failure to target next — a diagnostic that argues for time-aware attention / positional encodings on the video side.

## 15. GLIE

**Authors:** Mohamed Eltahir, Talal Aloushan, Rose Khairoalsendi, Jana Shata, Mohammed Alhassan, Leen Alrehaili, Tanveer Hussain, Naeemullah Khan — *KAUST*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.11808](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11808) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.11808) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.11808)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `retrieval`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

**Generative Late-Interaction Embeddings** — store only *k* representative vectors per page and *regenerate* the full late-interaction embedding set for the top-k candidates at search time. Analysis of embedding geometry shows visual-doc vectors cluster near a low-dim manifold, so a generative decoder can reconstruct the full set from a compressed seed. At **k = 4 vectors/page**, retains ~80 % of uncompressed retrieval performance with negligible training overhead.

### Key idea

Late-interaction retrieval (ColBERT-style) is high-fidelity but storage-heavy: hundreds of vectors per page. GLIE stores a **compressed seed** of *k* vectors and pairs it with a small generative decoder that expands that seed back to the full vector set at query time — only for the top candidates surfaced by seed-level search. Compression exploits the observation that the vector set lies near a low-dimensional manifold.

### Main finding

At k = 4 vectors per page, GLIE retains ~80 % of the uncompressed model's retrieval performance with minimal training overhead — dramatic storage savings at controlled quality loss.

### Why it matters

Visual document retrieval (ColPali-style) has been storage-bound at fleet scale. Moving from *store all vectors* to *store seeds and regenerate on demand* is a structural rearchitecture, and the manifold-geometry justification generalizes to other late-interaction settings (text, cross-modal).
