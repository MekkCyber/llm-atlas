## 1. Vidu S2

**Authors:** Jintao Zhang, Kai Jiang, Fan Bao, Jianfei Chen, Jun Zhu, et al. — *ShengShu / Tsinghua*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.11638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11638) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.11638) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.11638)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `video`, `multimodal`

### TL;DR

Vidu S2 is a real-time video generation stack with two components: **Vidu S2-Avatar** for interactive 720p digital character generation with mid-stream reference swaps, and **Vidu S2-Editing** for live stream-level video editing (style, clothing, character, background replacement). Both components are extended to real-time *spatial* (stereoscopic) video.

### Key idea

Push a diffusion-transformer video backbone toward real-time streaming while allowing conditioning that can change on the fly (a new reference image or edit target dropped in mid-generation). Instruction following is upgraded so operators can drive the avatar toward specific behaviors (dancing, gesture) without retraining.

### Main finding

Real-time 720p avatar generation, real-time video-to-video editing on a live stream, and real-time spatial video synthesis — all from one model family. The paper positions this as the first real-time interactive+editable+spatial video system.

### Why it matters

Real-time video diffusion has been the bottleneck for interactive avatar and stream-editing products. Combining live editing with real-time spatial output opens the design space for VR/AR agentic pipelines that need controllable, viewer-conditioned video without a render farm.

## 2. Atria Dawn

**Authors:** Honglin Guo, Tao Gui, Yicheng Chen, Guanting Dong, Yaojie Lu, Yu-Gang Jiang, Bowen Zhou, et al. (100+ authors) — *Fudan / Shanghai AI Lab / RUC consortium*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.15818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15818) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.15818) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.15818)
**Topics:** `agents`, `LLM`, `RL`, `eval`

### TL;DR

A frontier-scale agentic LLM tech report. Atria Dawn Preview is trained end-to-end for tool-mediated research/engineering workflows via a **Verifiable Experience Pipeline** that grounds tool interactions in executable environments and externally verified outcomes. Beyond the model, the paper reports a human-AI collaboration study on 769 tasks across 56 participants that quantifies where human judgment still dominates the loop.

### Key idea

Train an agent by connecting long-horizon tool trajectories to *executable* environments and to *externally verified* outcomes — not to a fixed reward model — so the training signal is grounded in whether the produced artifact actually runs / actually passes. Wrap this in an end-to-end RL loop over agentic behaviors rather than single-turn preference data.

### Main finding

Across 16 real-world agent benchmarks (research, engineering, digital work), Atria Dawn Preview is competitive with frontier agents and takes the top score on 5 of them. In the 56-participant study, roughly one-third of completed AI-assisted tasks were rated as infeasible without AI, while humans still make most final decisions.

### Why it matters

A tech report that pairs a strong agentic model with a rigorous study of what "agentic" actually shifts in real work. The Verifiable Experience Pipeline is a candidate answer to the reward-hacking problem that has plagued long-horizon agent RL; the human-AI collaboration data is the kind of ground truth the field currently lacks.

## 3. ZGCM-1

**Authors:** Jiyan He, Guang Liang, Yifei Shen, Shuxin Zheng, Tie-Yan Liu, et al. — *Microsoft Research Asia*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.13356](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13356) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.13356) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.13356)
**Topics:** `LLM`, `agents`, `reasoning`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

A **fully open** 7B foundation model with a 256K context window, positioned as a small model that overcomes parametric capacity limits by coupling internal reasoning with external tool use. Architecture mixes sliding-window and full attention; training uses a progressive curriculum across expanding context lengths.

### Key idea

The premise: 7B parameters cannot memorize the web, so the model is trained to *think* internally and *retrieve/act* externally as complementary capabilities rather than as an inference-time bolt-on. Sliding-window attention keeps most layers cheap; full-attention layers handle long-range fusion. Curriculum stages context length upward to bake long-range retrieval and multi-step search into the base model.

### Main finding

At 7B parameters, ZGCM-1 is claimed competitive with substantially larger models on math reasoning and agentic search tasks — a point on the tool-use / parametric-capacity Pareto frontier.

### Why it matters

Small-model + tool-use is the actively contested efficient-agent regime. A fully open 7B checkpoint trained natively for it (not fine-tuned into it) gives the community a clean baseline for the "compact reasoner that searches" hypothesis.

## 4. Dream-RSI

**Links:** [arXiv:2609.14858](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14858) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.14858) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.14858)
**Topics:** `RL`, `agents`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

A framework for recursive self-improvement in which the *exploration strategy itself* is the object being learned. Fixed strategies fail as search spaces scale; naive online strategy learning has delayed feedback over long rollouts. Dream-RSI targets that meta-search problem.

### Key idea

Treat the exploration meta-policy as an evolving world that the agent trains against — an inner loop that adapts to how the outer search space is shifting. The framing separates "what is discovered" from "how discovery is done", and makes the second recursively learnable.

### Main finding

The abstract (available portion) frames scalable recursive self-improvement as a meta-search-space problem and introduces the framework; concrete numbers are behind the paywall in the current fetch, but the paper is one of the top-upvoted RSI submissions of the day.

### Why it matters

Recursive self-improvement is one of the more speculative themes in agent research; making its *exploration* component into a first-class learnable object is a step from hand-tuned meta-heuristics toward RSI that can be studied like any other RL problem.

## 5. PhysBrain 1.5

**Authors:** DeepCybo Team; Yu Bin, Haipeng Cao, Zheng Chang, Kai Chen, et al. — *DeepCybo*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.14973](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14973) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.14973) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.14973)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `VLA`, `agents`

### TL;DR

A unified 8B model that fuses vision-language understanding, end-effector action generation, and future-state prediction into a single autoregressive-next-token stack. Pretraining is entirely from **human interaction videos** with paired semantic/spatial context, recovered motion, and future observations; SFT mixes human demos, robot trajectories, and simulation.

### Key idea

Cast language responses, end-effector motion, and dense visual targets as **three tokenized streams** all optimized with next-token prediction. Instead of a bespoke action head, motion becomes just another discrete sequence. Pretraining from human video sidesteps the robot-data bottleneck.

### Main finding

Averages **72.5** across 28 embodied benchmarks — new open-source SOTA, on par with proprietary GPT-6-Astra / Gemini 3.6 Flash on those benchmarks. Best open-source score on 14 benchmarks. Qualitatively produces end-effector trajectories and spatially-aligned RGB/depth/mask predictions.

### Why it matters

Extends the "VLM as universal tokenizer" thesis from vision+language to vision+language+action+future-state. If the recipe holds, robotics stops needing a separate action modeling stack — the same next-token loss trains the whole loop.

## 6. RSIAgent

**Authors:** Sibo Zhu, Shicheng Fan, Xinyue Wang, Wenyi Wu, Kun Zhou, Biwei Huang — *UCSD*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.15364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15364) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.15364) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.15364)
**Topics:** `agents`, `eval`

### TL;DR

A **training-free** multi-agent RSI framework. Curriculum, actor, and verifier agents cooperate to explore a new environment, validate outcomes, and accumulate reusable causal (action→condition→consequence) memory. Exploration is split into parallel broad recursive exploration and focused deep recursive exploration for hard cases; memory is frozen and reused for downstream tasks with no parameter updates.

### Key idea

Push all of the environment adaptation into a **frozen memory** built by three coordinated agents rather than into weight updates. The broad/deep split addresses the classic exploration tradeoff: parallel breadth discovers structure; sequential depth exposes hidden constraints and boundary cases.

### Main finding

On OSWorld-v2 and Agent's Last Exam, plugging RSIAgent into Kimi-K3 and GLM-5.3 lets those open-source backbones outperform frontier closed-source models including GPT-6 — without touching any weights.

### Why it matters

If curriculum+actor+verifier memory alone can flip the open-vs-closed frontier, environment adaptation is turning into a *retrieval* problem more than a *training* problem for CUAs. Also nicely factored: broad-then-deep is a clean design pattern for agent scaffolds.

## 7. Grouped Value Attention

**Authors:** Vishesh Tripathi, Abhay Kumar, Ramsha Khan — *(small-team hardware group)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.13285](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13285) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.13285) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.13285)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`, `architecture`

### TL;DR

An attention variant that **only caches values** and reconstructs keys on demand via a learned linear map, plus a small shared decoupled-RoPE positional-key channel. At decode the key-reconstruction map is absorbed into the query, so content keys never need to be materialized in cache. ~45–47% smaller persistent KV cache than matched GQA at comparable accuracy at 350M scale.

### Key idea

Standard GQA stores both key and value tensors per group. GVA drops the content-key store, keeps a **grouped value cache** plus a small **positional key** in a decoupled-RoPE slice, and rebuilds content keys through a learned linear projection whose cost can be absorbed into the query at inference time. Structurally identical philosophy to [MLA](../architectures/mla.md), but reversed: cache *values* instead of a *latent*, and reconstruct *keys* instead of both.

### Main finding

At 350M params / 30B FineWeb-Edu tokens, the 16-dim positional variant reaches **44.18** average accuracy across 5 tasks vs **44.36** for GQA and **43.88** for MLA — essentially parity with GQA at ~45% less persistent cache. Custom decode kernels and open-source release are announced but not yet included.

### Why it matters

Adds a third orientation to the KV-compression family alongside GQA/MQA (share heads) and MLA (share latent). Cache-side wins compound with latent-side wins, and GVA suggests real headroom is still available beyond MLA. The absorption trick keeps decode fast.

## 8. LynnReal-Omni

**Authors:** Xiaofeng Mao, Peijia Lin, Shaohao Rui, Yibo Zhang, Haibin Wan, Weijie Ma — *(LynnReal team)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.15863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15863) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.15863) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.15863)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `multimodal`, `agents`

### TL;DR

A native multimodal video generation framework built on a **32B shared multimodal diffusion transformer**. One backbone unifies text-to-video, image-conditioned, reference-guided, structural-control, editing, degraded-video restoration, and long-video generation, all as conditioning modes of the same model. Aimed at *agentic* visual workflows that combine explicit references, editable 3D scenes, and executable game states with diffusion sampling.

### Key idea

Stop training a separate video model per conditioning mode. A single 32B DiT is trained to consume heterogeneous multimodal conditions (text, image, 3D scene, control map, degraded video, prior video) so an agent can compose them without switching models. This is the diffusion-side dual of the "one VLM for everything" thesis.

### Main finding

Handles all seven conditioning regimes (text-to-video, image-conditioned, reference-guided, structural control, editing, restoration, long-video) within one 32B model. Positioned as the multimodal diffusion analogue of "native multimodal" LLMs.

### Why it matters

For agents that generate video as an action (game-state visualization, scene editing, avatar rendering) the *composition* of conditions matters more than any single mode's quality. Native multimodal diffusion collapses the tool-chain into one call.

## 9. Orthrus precision

**Authors:** Ilya Koziev, Leonid Sinev, Ivan Oseledets — *AIRI / Skoltech*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.15504](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15504) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.15504) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.15504)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`, `eval`

### TL;DR

An adversarial reproduction of **Orthrus**, a hybrid autoregressive-diffusion speculative-decoding architecture that claims *lossless* speedups via an intra-model consensus mechanism. Under BF16 the reproduced Orthrus matches the autoregressive trajectory exactly in only **~45%** of 1,190 prompts across 12 domains; under FP32 it matches on **100%** — so "lossless" turns on numerical precision.

### Key idea

Distinguish two claims that speculative-decoding papers routinely conflate: **exact-trajectory equivalence** and **downstream-task equivalence**. The paper measures both, and demonstrates that they diverge under real BF16 serving even when downstream benchmarks look fine.

### Main finding

- Exact trajectory match under BF16: 45% (authors' checkpoint), 43% (independently trained). Under FP32: 100%.
- Divergence rate is strongly correlated with response-conditional perplexity of the reference model — easy prompts match, hard prompts don't.
- lm-eval-harness scores show no systematic downstream degradation despite the trajectory drift.

### Why it matters

Every "lossless" speculative-decoding paper now needs to specify *lossless under what precision*, and downstream-benchmark stability is no longer sufficient evidence for trajectory losslessness. This is a small paper with an outsized methodological upgrade.

### Main finding

More position than benchmark: introduces the DFM framework, then grounds it with GALILEO's Dry-Lab / Wet-Lab / evidence / revision loop as an existence proof for closed physical discovery.

### Why it matters

Reframes the "science-agent" literature under a single scaffold and forces the field to say what it means by open-ended discovery. Serves as a useful taxonomy handle for agentic-science papers that would otherwise fragment.

## 11. BVB

**Authors:** Yolo Y. Tang, Daiki Shimada, Chenliang Xu, et al. — *University of Rochester / Sony*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.15478](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15478) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.15478) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.15478)
**Topics:** `eval`, `multimodal`, `agents`

### TL;DR

**Blender-VideoBench** flips video understanding: instead of QA, agents must **programmatically reconstruct** real-world videos as animated Blender scenes through a shared coding harness (Mini-BVB) under a common sandbox and cost budget. Scoring: Dual VQA (spatiotemporal fact preservation) × Latent Similarity (perceptual similarity to source).

### Key idea

Programmatic reconstruction is a genuine capability test: if an agent understands a video, it can produce code that recreates it. The harness is uniform so different agents are compared on ability, not tooling. The dual-axis score keeps balanced performance from being gamed by either purely visual or purely factual solutions.

### Main finding

- Best system: **88.6** Latent Similarity but only **53.7%** spatiotemporal factual retention.
- More reasoning improves visual similarity but does not close the factual gap.
- Latent Similarity correlates strongly with human preference in a 15-rater blind study.
- 51 configurations from 10 model families evaluated.

### Why it matters

QA-style video benchmarks are running out of headroom and reward shortcut features. Programmatic reconstruction is a scalable, human-preference-correlated alternative that also exposes a specific weakness (semantic retention) current agents share.

## 12. AlayaVista

**Authors:** Jiaming Tan, Mingliang Zhai, Zhen Li, Yuwei Wu, Chuanhao Li, Kaipeng Zhang — *Shanghai AI Lab*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.14462](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14462) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.14462) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.14462)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `video`, `multimodal`

### TL;DR

A camera-controllable **streaming video world model** that decouples panoramic scene evolution from perspective observation synthesis. From a single perspective image it builds a 360° scene prior with a pretrained panorama expander, evolves the scene as a camera-conditioned panoramic latent state, and renders per-frame perspective views via a viewport renderer + perspective refiner.

### Key idea

Splitting the world into a **persistent panoramic state** plus a **local viewport renderer** solves the memory problem perspective-only video models have (off-screen content drifts) without paying the full cost of synthesizing 360° video at every step. Chunk-autoregressive generation with distilled few-step panorama and refinement makes the loop streamable.

### Main finding

Delivers streaming world modeling with camera control and off-screen-consistency, backed by a new large-scale panoramic video dataset **MUGEN** — 1,318 hours of ≥4K panoramic video with rich semantic and geometric annotations — which alone is a substantial community contribution.

### Why it matters

For interactive-agent world models and game-like generative environments, the panoramic-state / perspective-render split is a clean architectural pattern that lets agents "look around" cheaply. MUGEN also becomes a foundational dataset for future panoramic-video research.

## 13. Omni-Streaming Thinking

**Authors:** Enjun Du, Siyi Liu, Ziyu Zheng, Jingyu Li, Yiwen Guo, Yongqi Zhang, Difan Zou — *HKU / Nanjing / ByteDance*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.15128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15128) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.15128) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.15128)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `reasoning`, `eval`

### TL;DR

A streaming omni-modal reasoning method (**OST**) that tags claims as *pending* against a future verification interval, stores audio and visual evidence in separate registers, and rewrites state when the verified modality contradicts a pending claim. Built on a frozen Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct backbone with lightweight adaptation.

### Key idea

Streaming multimodal reasoning fails when a visually-supported claim locks in as a fact before audio arrives to contradict it (**premature cross-modal commitment**). OST fixes this with *structured deferred claims* — claims are linked to modality-specific evidence intervals and are refuted-and-updated rather than overwritten silently. An answer gate decides when the pending-claim state is safe to release.

### Main finding

- +10% relative average over strongest open baselines on 5 streaming/audio-visual benchmarks.
- On the new **OST-DiagBench** (fixed video, edited audio for agreement/absence/contradiction/coexistence/subtitle-speech conflict), OST reaches **d' = 2.95** vs ≤ 1.38 for open baselines, while reducing vision-induced auditory hallucinations.

### Why it matters

Names and solves a specific streaming-multimodal failure mode with a clean structural fix (pending claims + modality-scoped verification intervals). Also introduces a diagnostic benchmark that isolates cross-modal contradiction — a genuinely new eval axis.

## 14. HazardAuditor

**Authors:** Yunhao Feng, Ruixiao Lin, Ming Wen, Yanming Guo, Xingjun Ma, Yutao Wu, Xinhao Deng, Shouling Ji — *Fudan / Zhejiang U.*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.15134](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15134) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.15134) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.15134)
**Topics:** `safety`, `agents`, `RL`

### TL;DR

A safety framework for computer-use agents (CUAs) grounded in **executable threats** — real runtime behavior across browsers, terminals, filesystems, and services — rather than content classification alone. Normalizes heterogeneous agent interactions and adds **Guard Policy Optimization**, an RL loop that improves safety-model accuracy by up to **+16.5 pp** across multiple benchmarks.

### Key idea

Static content moderation misses the actual CUA threat surface, which lives in runtime side effects (file writes, network calls, credential handling, unbounded shell). HazardAuditor moves auditing into execution and trains a Guard model with an RL objective tied to intercepting real hazardous action sequences.

### Main finding

+16.5 pp safety accuracy over prior CUA guardrails across the paper's benchmarks; the framework normalizes browser/terminal/filesystem/service interactions into a common representation Guard Policy Optimization can consume.

### Why it matters

CUAs are being deployed faster than their safety story is being built. Grounding guardrails in *execution* rather than *content* is the correct shift, and coupling that with RL training rather than static rules gives a workable path to shipped moderation.

## 15. LLaDA-UI

**Authors:** Zhangxuan Gu, Haoxing Chen, Qi Qin, Yi Xin, Changhua Meng, Weiqiang Wang, Jianguo Li, Zhenzhong Lan, et al. — *Ant Group / Westlake*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.13287](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13287) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.13287) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.13287)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `multimodal`, `agents`

### TL;DR

A vision-language **GUI agent** built on a diffusion language backbone (**LLaDA**-style block-parallel, arbitrary-order generation). Targets latency-sensitive interface-interaction tasks where the agent must repeatedly perceive a screen and emit structured spatially-grounded actions.

### Key idea

Autoregressive LLMs pay a serial-decoding cost per action step; diffusion LLMs generate whole blocks in parallel, arbitrary-order, which fits GUI agent action emission (short, structured, grounded outputs) very well. LLaDA-UI adapts a block-wise diffusion LM to consume screen state and produce action programs.

### Main finding

Reports performance improvements over comparable baseline VLM GUI agents across the paper's benchmarks; exact numbers behind the abstract summary in the fetched extraction, but the paper is a clean pairing of a diffusion LM with a GUI agent workload.

### Why it matters

Diffusion LMs (LLaDA family) have been searching for the workload where their parallel generation actually beats autoregressive decoding. GUI agents — short, structured, latency-critical actions — are a strong candidate, and this paper is the first to lay it out.
