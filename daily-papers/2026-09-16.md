## 1. LimiX-2

**Authors:** Xingxuan Zhang, Gang Ren, … Peng Cui *(≈60 authors)* — *Tsinghua / LimiX*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.17488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17488) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.17488) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.17488)
**Topics:** `foundation-model`, `tabular`, `ICL`, `scaling`

### TL;DR

A next-generation tabular foundation model built on the "Contextual Mechanism Network" (CMN) paradigm. Instead of modeling `p(y | x, D_context)` like conventional tabular PFNs, LimiX-2 is pretrained on synthetic structural-causal-model (SCM) data to model the joint `p(x, y | D_context)`. It scales via a formal set of scaling laws and delivers SOTA on TabArena/TALENT/BCCO while its feature-attention pattern recovers causal skeletons.

### Key idea

Reframe tabular in-context learning as **mechanism-oriented joint modeling**: train the network to learn a context-conditional joint distribution over inputs *and* targets, using Context-Conditional Masked Modeling (CCMM) over SCM-sampled datasets. The synthetic-SCM curriculum spans different graph topologies, functional forms, and observation noise.

### Main finding

Outperforms both dataset-specific baselines and prior tabular foundation models on TabArena, TALENT, and BCCO; feature-attention weights recover true causal skeletons with high accuracy — an unusual byproduct for a predictor.

### Why it matters

Pushes the "one foundation model per modality" paradigm into tabular land while offering an unusual causality-aware side-effect. If it holds up under audit, the CMN paradigm gives a cleaner objective for pretraining on synthetic structured data than target-only PFN losses.

## 2. StepAudio 3 Realtime

**Authors:** Bin Lin, Bo Zhao, … Zixuan Wang *(≈90 authors)* — *StepFun*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.14005](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14005) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.14005) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.14005)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `audio`, `LLM`, `case-study`

### TL;DR

An audio-language foundation model built around a continuous **listen → converse → think → act** loop, designed for realtime spoken interaction. Introduces "Deep Perception" for acoustic understanding, "Seamless Duplex" for synchronized audio streams, and a novel "Think-While-Speaking" scheme that runs private reasoning in parallel with speech synthesis.

### Key idea

Rather than serialize reason-then-speak, StepAudio 3 decouples an inner "thinking" stream from the outer "speaking" stream, letting long-horizon reasoning proceed while audio output continues. Duplex support handles simultaneous listening + speaking, mimicking human turn-taking.

### Main finding

73.0 macro-average on StepAudioChat (reasoning mode); 90.6 on MMSU; 98.9 Overall on the Artificial Analysis Full-Duplex Bench; 56.0% task-success on τ-Voice.

### Why it matters

Realtime, full-duplex audio agents have been a systems bottleneck — most prior LLM-audio systems still block on generation. Think-While-Speaking suggests a new decomposition where reasoning latency no longer dominates conversational latency.

## 3. StepAudio 3 Music

**Authors:** Chengli Feng, Zhiyue Wu, … Chao Yan — *StepFun*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.16034](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16034) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.16034) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.16034)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `audio`, `diffusion`, `MoE`, `RL`

### TL;DR

Long-form (≥5:30) music generation model that combines a semantic 50-Hz single-codebook audio tokenizer, a MoE autoregressive planner that emits ABC-notation "arrangement plans" as CoT, and a flow-matching DiT that decodes continuous VAE latents into 48-kHz audio. DPO on preference data polishes the final model.

### Key idea

Two-stage generation: (1) a **MoE-AR** model produces an ABC-notation arrangement plan ("ABC-CoT") giving harmony/rhythm/melodic structure; (2) music tokens are then predicted conditional on that plan, and (3) a **flow-matching DiT** predicts continuous VAE latents that a decoder turns into audio. DPO on human preferences is the last polish.

### Main finding

Highest AudioBox Content Enjoyment / Content Usefulness / Production Quality among evaluated systems; highest MuQ-MuLan similarity; Quality Elo 1105 on the Artificial Analysis Music Arena Vocals (behind only Suno V5.5 and Mureka).

### Why it matters

Introduces "symbolic-plan-then-render" as an alternative to purely-continuous music DiTs. The ABC-CoT plan is human-editable and lets the model separate structure from timbre — a compositional angle rarely available in end-to-end music generators.

## 5. Last AI Built by Humans

**Authors:** Yi Duan, Ying Liu, … *(33 authors)* — *(multi-institution)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.11873](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11873) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.11873) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.11873)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RSI`, `survey`, `safety`

### TL;DR

Roadmap for recursive self-improvement (RSI). Introduces a **Headroom-Closed Index** to quantify what limits current LMs from bootstrapping themselves, and lays out a five-stage autonomy ladder — from execution through meta-improvement — with case-study areas in science, embodied agents, and software engineering.

### Key idea

RSI = converting experience and feedback into persistent changes that improve both the *system* and its *improvement process*. The Headroom-Closed Index scores how much of the improvement path a model can traverse without outside help; the five stages generalize instruction-following → meta-improvement.

### Main finding

Framework paper — no benchmark. Provides a scoring rubric, roadmap, and application audits.

### Why it matters

RSI is becoming the organizing question for "what comes after test-time compute". A shared vocabulary for autonomy stages and a concrete Headroom score are missing from the graph and would ground several recent tech reports (agents that improve their harness, self-evolving training loops).

## 6. Mind2Dialogue

**Authors:** Zixuan Wang, Yufan Zhou, … Zhen Wang — *(multi-institution: UCSD, Alibaba, Stanford ties per author list)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.15972](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15972) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.15972) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.15972)
**Topics:** `LLM`, `SFT`, `data`, `dialogue`

### TL;DR

Frames a "supervision gap" in current assistant SFT data: responses rarely reflect the user's *unspoken* beliefs and goals. Proposes a **psychology-guided simulation** pipeline that synthesizes user mental-state trajectories and uses **privileged distillation** to train assistant models on responses grounded in those states.

### Key idea

Two-stage synthetic-data pipeline: (1) simulate a user with an explicit mental-state model (beliefs/goals/intentions), (2) generate the "privileged" assistant reply that could see those states, then distill that behavior into a student assistant that cannot see the states. This trains "theory-of-mind"-like sensitivity into the model.

### Main finding

+26.6 to +40.9 percentage-point improvement in preference-following generation across Qwen, Llama, and OLMo baselines.

### Why it matters

Most SFT/RLHF data implicitly assumes the assistant can only see the surface text. Structuring training data around a *user model* is a lightweight route to more helpful assistants without expensive human collection.

## 7. ScienceBuddy

**Authors:** Shuhan Xue, Jianyuan Zhong, … Ling Yang — *(multi-institution)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.17523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17523) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.17523) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.17523)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RSI`, `RL`, `case-study`

### TL;DR

An interactive scientific-research workspace whose core is **recursive-in-recursive self-improvement (RiR-SI)**: an inner loop evolves the *agent harness* while the model is fixed, an outer loop then trains the *model* under the improved harness. Ships with four scientific-task-family benchmarks.

### Key idea

RiR-SI decouples harness evolution from model RL. The inner recursion optimizes prompts, tools, and orchestration around a frozen model; the outer recursion runs RL over the model with the improved harness as environment. Alternation lets each improve the ceiling of the other.

### Main finding

Product-report + case studies. Numeric wins are described qualitatively; the framework itself is the contribution.

### Why it matters

Most agent RL papers freeze the harness; most harness-evolution papers freeze the model. RiR-SI proposes an explicit alternation and packages it into a released product, giving the community a testbed for coupled evolution.

## 8. ModularRSI

**Authors:** Siwei Wu, Jincheng Ren, … Chenghua Lin — *(multi-institution)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.14857](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14857) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.14857) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.14857)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RSI`, `eval`, `case-study`

### TL;DR

A **benchmark-disjoint, contrastive, and modular** framework for evolving agent harnesses without overfitting to a specific benchmark. Decomposes an evolvable harness into five functional modules (Agent Loop, Tool Use, Observation Mgmt, Context Mgmt, Task Completion Detection) that each evolve independently, then an integration stage resolves conflicts.

### Key idea

Attribute recurring behavioral deficiencies by **contrasting** successful and failed trajectories on the same task and aggregating across tasks. Split the harness into five modules that can be optimized independently (limiting entanglement), then integrate. Evolution tasks are drawn from a 2,000-example set disjoint from downstream evaluation benchmarks.

### Main finding

Consistent gains on unseen in-domain and cross-domain tasks (TB2.0, SWE-Bench Verified), including transfer *across* foundation models — a rare property for evolved harnesses.

### Why it matters

Prior harness-evolution frequently degenerates into benchmark-specific hacks. Modular, benchmark-disjoint evolution offers a recipe for *generalizable* harness improvements that outlive the model they were tuned on.

## 9. Continual Learning Composes

**Authors:** Alvin Zhang, Daniel Khashabi, Tianmin Shu — *Johns Hopkins University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.06986](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06986) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.06986) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.06986)
**Topics:** `fine-tuning`, `LoRA`, `continual`, `LLM`

### TL;DR

Systematic study of continual SFT across 100 sequentially trained tasks with **no replay**. Organizes continual-learning tricks along two axes — *anchors* (data / function / weight) and *low-rank allocation* rules — and finds the compositions matter more than any single trick.

### Key idea

Frame continual-fine-tuning mechanisms as pairs `(anchor, allocation)`. The best recipe combines all three anchors (data replay proxies + function-space regularization + weight-space constraints) with **merged LoRA** as the allocation. Compositions transfer across datasets.

### Main finding

Naïve sequential fine-tuning: 1.2% final retention across 100 tasks. Best composed recipe: **34.9% retention — a 28× improvement**, ranking in the top-3 across all evaluated datasets.

### Why it matters

Long-horizon SFT is an obvious ingredient of any "always-learning" assistant. This paper isolates the compositional axes that matter and gives an unusually strong non-replay baseline for the continual-LM literature.

## 10. Register Tokens for Diffusion LMs

**Authors:** Albert Ge, Chandan Singh, Yufan Zhuang, Xiaodong Liu, Jianfeng Gao, Frederic Sala — *UW-Madison / Microsoft Research*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.16372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16372) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.16372) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.16372)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `reasoning`, `LLM`

### TL;DR

Masked-diffusion LMs (dLLMs) normally need to keep prior generated text in context to continue reasoning across chunks. This paper adds a small number of **register tokens** — fixed-position tokens whose continuous hidden states are trained to carry reasoning state — so the model can clear the emitted text and keep going from just the registers + prompt.

### Key idea

Post-train a dLLM to (1) decode a chunk, (2) clear the text but preserve the register hidden states, (3) continue decoding from the prompt + carried registers. Registers become a **bounded-size scratchpad** for long-horizon reasoning. Optional RL further sharpens their use.

### Main finding

On LLaDA and Dream: registers outperform discrete-text carry on every benchmark, with **up to +8.5 points on math and +19.5 on code**. Especially strong for bounded-code generation where correct programs span many chunks.

### Why it matters

dLLMs have been context-limited for multi-step reasoning because they must re-see everything they wrote. Registers give them a fixed-state working memory, closing a real gap vs. AR models on long-horizon tasks.

## 11. Directional Decomposition of Transformer Updates

**Authors:** Shwai He, Haichao Zhang, Shen Yan — *(unspecified)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.15975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15975) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.15975) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.15975)
**Topics:** `interp`, `pre-training`, `LLM`

### TL;DR

Decomposes each transformer additive update (attention, MLP, value-aggregation) into components **parallel** and **perpendicular** to the current residual direction, and shows that (i) the parallel component is unexpectedly large, (ii) exclude-self value-space parallel manipulation is uniquely robust, (iii) suppressing the aggregate parallel component during pretraining lowers loss and improves downstream averages.

### Key idea

Two decompositions: (a) attention+MLP updates vs. the hidden state, (b) attention value aggregation vs. the current token's own value. Under decomposition (b), scaling only the **non-self aggregate** in the parallel direction leaves reasoning largely intact — a sharp asymmetry with residual-space or perpendicular edits.

### Main finding

Full-aggregate parallel suppression during from-scratch pretraining **improves both validation loss and downstream averages**, with the value-space variant strongest. Perpendicular error separates compression methods more cleanly than parallel error.

### Why it matters

A rare mechanistic story that also delivers a pretraining recipe. Suggests a simple regularizer for training runs and a cleaner metric for evaluating compression / distillation methods.

## 12. Decoy Direction Optimization

**Authors:** Aashiq Muhamed, Mona T. Diab, Virginia Smith — *Carnegie Mellon University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.16204](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16204) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.16204) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.16204)
**Topics:** `safety`, `interp`, `defense`

### TL;DR

A fast, post-hoc, weight-editing defense against **Refusal Feature Ablation (RFA)** attacks on open-weight models. Rather than hiding the true refusal direction, DDO **injects a high-magnitude, nonlinear "decoy" signal** into MLP neurons so contrastive attacks lock onto a harmless orthogonal feature instead.

### Key idea

RFA finds the refusal direction with a contrastive estimator. DDO baits the estimator with a strong nonlinear decoy feature that the estimator prefers, while the real safety circuit stays intact. A spectral bound formalizes when the decoy dominates the estimator.

### Main finding

<10% ASR under standard RFA across six model families; on Llama-3-8B-Instruct, comparable to expensive trained defenses under adaptive multi-phase attacks (65% vs. 58% worst-case ASR); reduces Heretic weight-level attack ASR from **88.7% → 18%**, at 30–450× lower optimization cost than trained baselines.

### Why it matters

Weight-level safety attacks (RFA, abliteration) are cheap and effective against open-weight releases. A weight-editing defense that requires no fine-tuning is exactly what open-model releasers can bolt on before shipping.

## 13. Drift-Constrained Optimization

**Authors:** Fei Yuan, Changjiang Gao, Yilei Tu, Yifeng Liu, Shujian Huang, Yu Qiao — *Shanghai AI Lab / NJU*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.13680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13680) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.13680) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.13680)
**Topics:** `fine-tuning`, `LLM`, `multilingual`

### TL;DR

Reframes instruct-model fine-tuning as picking a *direction* inside a pre-declared **behavioral-drift budget** relative to the reference model. Argues "only direction matters": selective, layer-targeted modifications along a bounded drift direction retain reasoning / general capability while boosting the target task.

### Key idea

Specify a drift budget upfront, then treat fine-tuning as directional selection within it — layer-targeted edits chosen to advance the target task while staying inside the budget. Compared to full fine-tuning it's a controlled deformation, not a wholesale re-training.

### Main finding

Across Qwen3 sizes and 100+ languages, drift-constrained fine-tuning improves target-task performance while preserving reasoning and general-capability benchmarks better than unconstrained SFT.

### Why it matters

Uncontrolled drift is the reason "we just fine-tuned it for X" often silently kills other capabilities. A pre-declared budget + directional optimizer gives a knob for productionizing SFT on frontier models.

## 14. ImpossibleRubrics

**Authors:** Bowen Qin, Yi Xie, Yesheng Liu, Xi Yang — *(unspecified)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.16816](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16816) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.16816) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.16816)
**Topics:** `eval`, `rewards`, `safety`

### TL;DR

A stress-test for LM-generated rubrics used as reward signals. Introduces 169 **impossible tasks** — prompts that pressure the model toward an unsupported conclusion — paired with **oracle certificates** specifying what an honest answer may/may not claim, plus 48 answerable controls. Rubrics are then generated downstream and adversarially tested against certificate-violating answers.

### Key idea

Give the rubric generator only a *task environment + certificate*, not a fixed rubric. Then attack: does the generated rubric reward answers that violate the certificate? Impossible tasks are the hardest regime because honest answers must acknowledge the impossibility.

### Main finding

Benchmark contribution — headline number is that many LM-generated rubrics currently fail to withstand certificate-violating adversarial answers. Provides a shared reproducible stress-test.

### Why it matters

Rubric-based RL and LLM-as-judge pipelines are proliferating. This benchmark surfaces a specific failure mode (reward-hacking against generated rubrics on impossible tasks) that the community currently under-measures.

## 15. The Router Within

**Authors:** Ruishuo Chen, Xun Wang, Yu Chen, Zhuoran Li, Longbo Huang — *Tsinghua University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.15982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15982) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.15982) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.15982)
**Topics:** `agents`, `MoE`, `tool-use`

### TL;DR

Shows that a frozen agent LLM already carries the routing signal for **skill selection** in its forward passes; two small linear maps read it out with **no skill text in context**. The Gavel method routes over huge skill libraries at zero-shot without preloading skill metadata.

### Key idea

Two-step "glance and verdict": (1) *Glance* — project the task and each skill's mid-layer states through two trained linear maps and score the whole library against compact per-skill banks built at installation. (2) *Verdict* — resume the shortlisted skills' forward passes, read the model's own likelihood and yes/no judgment, fuse as product-of-experts.

### Main finding

On Qwen3-32B, outperforms progressive disclosure and retrieve-and-rerank pipelines that add 1.2B–16B external parameters by up to **13.4 pts** (written tasks) and **21.9 pts** (mid-rollout skill activation). In a bash-agent harness, triggers correct skill on Skill-Use more often than "far larger frontier models running in Codex". Zero-shot transfer to three public benchmarks and a new SkillTraj benchmark of 372 simulated trajectories.

### Why it matters

Contexts full of skill metadata cap library size and waste attention. If routing is already latent in the base model, cheap linear probes replace giant retrieval pipelines — a big deal for computer-use agents with hundreds of skills.
