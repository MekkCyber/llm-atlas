## 1. DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash

**Authors:** DeepSeek-AI (591-author consortium) — *DeepSeek-AI*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.19969](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19969) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.19969) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.19969)
**Topics:** `LLM`, `MoE`, `efficient-inference`, `quantization`, `multimodal`, `long-context`

### TL;DR

A frontier open-weight multimodal MoE (552B total, 16B activated at decode, 8B at prefill) built end-to-end for **agentic, input-heavy workloads at 1M context**. The centerpiece is a Causal Encoder-Decoder (CED) architecture combined with an aggressive KV-cache stack — cross-layer reuse via Compressed Sparse Attention 2 (CSA2), FP4 KV cache, and SSD-backed "SWA Bounded Replay" — that drops the global HBM-resident KV cache to ~890 bytes/token and shrinks persistent KV storage by ~8× versus DeepSeek-V4-Flash.

### Key idea

Attack the three long-context bottlenecks (compute, HBM, and bandwidth) simultaneously. The CED architecture asymmetrically activates fewer parameters during prefill than decode, so long prompts are cheap to ingest; CSA2 reuses compressed KV state across layers so each layer no longer keeps its own copy; FP4 storage crunches the bytes further; and a bounded-replay scheme spills persistent state to SSD without recompute.

### Main finding

At 45T-token multimodal pretraining, the model matches or beats the DeepSeek-V4-Flash baseline while cutting HBM KV cost ~4× and persistent KV ~8×. Prefill activates half the parameters of decode (8B vs 16B), directly reducing the dominant cost of long agentic prompts.

### Why it matters

This is the first widely released MoE positioned explicitly around **input-heavy agent workloads** rather than chat, and the FP4-KV + cross-layer-reuse combo is the most aggressive published open-weights attack on KV cost to date. The CED prefill/decode asymmetry is a new architectural axis that DeepSeek-MoE / MLA don't cover, and the whole system is a tech-report-scale release comparable to DeepSeek-V3.

## 2. MiniMax-H3 Physical World

**Authors:** Haoyu Zhao, Zihao Zhao, Tianyu Deng, Ziqin Xu et al. — *academic collaboration (affiliation not disclosed on HF page)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.18323](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18323) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.18323) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.18323)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `eval`, `physical-reasoning`

### TL;DR

An evaluation of MiniMax-H3, an omni-modal generative model, on physical-world reasoning across four multimodal input configurations (implicit prompts + frames, audio + image, prefix video, and audio + video). Introduces a four-dimensional physical reasoning framework and a 517-instance benchmark.

### Key idea

Design evaluations that *require* the model to fuse complementary signals across modalities rather than solve each in isolation — video decision reasoning, audio disambiguation, and cross-modal causal inference in single tasks.

### Main finding

Overall success is ~42% across 517 instances. Video-based decision reasoning is strongest (56%), audio-based disambiguation weakest (27.4%). Multi-frame reasoning is systematically weaker than single-frame — cross-modal fusion, not raw perception, is the bottleneck.

### Why it matters

Frontier "omni-modal" models are being marketed as universal reasoners, but this shows a large gap on tasks that need genuine cross-modal integration (especially audio ↔ vision). The benchmark is a durable stress test for the next generation of models like MiniMax-H3.

## 3. When EOS Tokens Disagree

**Authors:** Yuxiao Yang, Tianrun Yu, Shangzhe Li, Kaixiang Zhao, Xuchao Zhang, Chetan Bansal, Huaxiu Yao, Taylor W. Killian, Weitong Zhang — *UNC / Microsoft / Notre Dame*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.20511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20511) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.20511) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.20511)
**Topics:** `post-training`, `distillation`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

Diagnoses length inflation in on-policy distillation (OPD): student responses grow much longer than teacher references because the base student and post-trained teacher place stopping probability on *different* EOS-family tokens even when their declared stopping sets are identical.

### Key idea

Treat EOS as a *set of semantically equivalent stopping actions* rather than a token. When the distillation objective aligns the union-of-EOS mass instead of individual token probabilities, length inflation collapses.

### Main finding

Across Qwen3, Llama, and Gemma families, aligning stopping-set decoding is *not* enough — students still overshoot. Treating functionally equivalent EOS tokens as one action substantially reduces length inflation. K2-Horizon training-stage analysis shows termination preferences drift during post-training, and residual length inflation persists beyond termination alignment (a second, orthogonal channel to fix).

### Why it matters

OPD is the workhorse for distilling reasoning models (Qwen3, Llama, Gemma). Length inflation is a silent tax on serving cost and eval throughput; identifying the EOS-mismatch root cause is a small, deployable fix.

## 4. SoL-Pi

**Authors:** Haozhe Liu, Tian Ye, Sensen Gao, Qihang Cao, Yitong Li, Mingchen Zhuge, Duomin Wang, Ruihua Zhang, Ping Luo, Jiawang Bian, Lei Zhu, Ligeng Zhu, Enze Xie, Song Han — *MIT / KAUST*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.20519](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20519) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.20519) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.20519)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

An RSI-inspired auto-research pipeline that iterates on a **coding-agent harness** across many environments, distilling reusable improvements that survive out-of-distribution transfer. The winning combination — SoL-Pi — bundles four mechanisms (action execution, context compaction, observation handling, delegated reading).

### Key idea

Optimize the *harness*, not the model. Run RSI-style automated search where each iteration proposes changes to a coding-agent harness, evaluates across a diverse rollout population, and retains only harness edits whose gains transfer beyond the development setting.

### Main finding

On the 51-task EdgeBench evaluation, SoL-Pi matches Pi's performance across GPT-5.6 Sol and Opus 5 while cutting recorded token traffic by 44.7–49.0% and API cost by ~1/3.

### Why it matters

Harness design is the neglected axis of agent research — most reported "model wins" hide harness wins. A systematic, RSI-style search for reusable harness components generalizes beyond the tuner's model and matters for anyone deploying coding agents at scale.

## 5. Harness Design for Coding Agents

**Authors:** Run-Ze Fan, Zihao Zhang, Simin Ma, Yebowen Hu, Shouju Wang, Kaiqiang Song, Fei Liu, Hamed Zamani, Xiaoyang Wang — *UMass Amherst / Tencent*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.20804](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20804) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.20804) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.20804)
**Topics:** `agents`, `eval`

### TL;DR

A component-level ablation of coding-agent harnesses. Fixes the outer loop and varies three axes — planning, action space, and context management — across 4 models × 176 matched settings on SWE-Bench Verified and Terminal-Bench 2.1.

### Key idea

Stop treating harnesses as monoliths. Isolate context management (5 strategies × 4 window budgets), planning ablations, and action-space design (predefined tools vs bash-only), and measure each contribution against a fixed backbone.

### Main finding

Four clean rules of thumb: (1) context management gets more valuable as the window tightens, mostly by preventing overflow failures; (2) rule-based elision *before* LLM-based summarization is the strongest strategy — recoverable-elision machinery buys nothing; (3) planning is an accuracy scaffold for weak models but a cost saver for strong ones; (4) predefined tools help weak-bash models, bash-only wins for strong bash models on CLI tasks.

### Why it matters

The paper turns "which harness to use" from folklore into a decision tree indexed by model strength and budget. Ships as a modular framework so future harness components can be evaluated the same way.

## 6. JEPA-Anything

**Authors:** Taoyong Cui, Zhongyao Wang, Xinyue Xu, Weiyang Liu, Zhaochen Yu, Yuying Zhang, Qiang Gao, Mengyue Yang, Wanli Ouyang, Pheng Ann Heng, Yingcheng Wu, Zhenfei Yin, Ling Yang — *Shanghai AI Lab / CUHK*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.20800](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20800) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.20800) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.20800)
**Topics:** `world-models`, `foundation-model`, `representation-learning`

### TL;DR

A domain-agnostic JEPA extension called **orthogonal predictive factorization (OPF)**: decompose latent targets into complementary factors, learn each along its own pathway, then recombine in a shared predictor. Evaluated across seven radically different domains (vision, biology, clinical, control, molecular dynamics, physical fields, weather).

### Key idea

The standard JEPA target is one embedding; OPF factorizes it into orthogonal components learned by dedicated heads, recombined for prediction. The factorization gives a common substrate for radically different dynamics.

### Main finding

Beats matched JEPA baselines on all 10 dynamics tasks, cuts single-intervention error on Interventional Pong by 34.8%, and lands the lowest one-step and 100-step molecular error on 4 systems. A latent factor even recovers Keplerian orbital scaling (slope -1.4991, close to the ideal -3/2).

### Why it matters

Ties together JEPA-style predictive pretraining with the "one model across worlds" push. Suggests that factorized predictive representations transfer across radically different physical regimes — relevant to any team building foundation models beyond text.

## 7. Verifiable Social Reasoning

**Authors:** Amir Taubenfeld, Zorik Gekhman, Avigail Grinstein-Dabush, Itay Laish, Ariel Goldstein, Marian Croak, Avinatan Hassidim, Yossi Matias, Amir Feder — *Google Research*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.17496](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17496) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.17496) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.17496)
**Topics:** `eval`, `reasoning`, `agents`, `safety`

### TL;DR

Fuse — a multi-agent simulation framework where a hidden-motive agent interacts with others, then a user-representative queries an LLM to infer that motive. Because the ground-truth motive is a construct of the simulation, correctness is verifiable by design.

### Key idea

Bake verifiability into social-reasoning eval by *generating* the scenario together with its ground truth. Hidden intent → mediated conversation → LLM inference → check against the seed.

### Main finding

Across 12 models: user-mediated reasoning compounds inherent task difficulty; models are systematically biased toward the user representative's framing; they sometimes need more context than a human to hit the correct inference; and longer conversations don't monotonically help. Ship a 21K-example dataset.

### Why it matters

Most "theory of mind" or social-reasoning benchmarks are hand-crafted and fragile. Fuse's simulation-first design gives a scalable, contamination-resistant substrate for measuring genuine social inference in assistants — a load-bearing capability for agents that mediate between users and other people.

## 8. Self-Evolving Search Index

**Authors:** Sangam Lee, Wonjae Lee, Sunghwan Kim, Deogyong Kim, Jaehoon Kim, Daye Nam, SeongKu Kang, Dongha Lee — *Yonsei / Korea University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.19656](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19656) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.19656) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.19656)
**Topics:** `agents`, `retrieval`, `memory`

### TL;DR

SELF-INDEX: a retriever-agnostic system that lets an index evolve its own key representations. An Optimizer diagnoses retrieval gaps and rewrites index keys; a Query Simulator proactively generates additional demands to expand coverage.

### Key idea

Move index tuning from a one-shot build step to an online, self-diagnosed loop: retrieval failures are gradient signal for editing the index itself, not just the query encoder or retriever.

### Main finding

Consistent improvements over static index-optimization baselines across corpora and retrievers, with gains carrying over to search agents and agent-memory systems.

### Why it matters

Retrieval-augmented agents are only as good as their index — but existing systems assume that once built, the index is fixed. Self-evolving indices close a loop that most RAG stacks currently leave open, especially in long-lived agent memories.

## 9. RetireOPD

**Authors:** Yan Yu, Zhengxi Lu, Yizhou Liu, Yichen Pan, Aozhe Wang, Qipeng Chen, Hua Yang, Wenqi Zhang, Weiming Lu, Qianglong Chen, Yongliang Shen — *Zhejiang University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.20784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20784) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.20784) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.20784)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `distillation`

### TL;DR

RetireOPD couples on-policy distillation with **adaptive retirement**: the student mimics a specialized teacher while its per-skill performance is climbing, then automatically disengages once it plateaus, freeing capacity for other skills.

### Key idea

Distillation isn't a "always mimic" contract. Retire the teacher on a per-skill basis when the student stops improving — the retirement signal is a plateau detector on the student's own trajectory reward, not a fixed schedule.

### Main finding

On Qwen2.5 (1.5B–7B), gains of 14.1–18.8% on ALFWorld and 11.8–19.0% on WebShop over RL baselines. Exceeds teacher performance across all evaluated scenarios — retirement lets the student escape the teacher's ceiling.

### Why it matters

Multi-turn agents suffer from sparse reward; on-policy distillation from a teacher smooths early training. RetireOPD is a clean, autonomous way to combine distillation and RL without the usual "how long do we distill?" hyperparameter fight.

## 10. Reflect, Revise, Reuse

**Authors:** Bofan Chen, Boxuan Zhang, Fei Tang, Zhengxi Lu, Yong Du, Tongbo Chen, Weiming Lu, Jun Xiao, Yueting Zhuang, Yongliang Shen — *Zhejiang University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.17653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17653) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.17653) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.17653)
**Topics:** `agents`, `multimodal`, `GUI`

### TL;DR

EvoSkill-GUI — a **training-free** framework that lets a GUI agent evolve its skill library at deployment time via a reflect-revise-reuse loop: execution feedback triggers instant revisions, an isolated critic diagnoses failures, and a restricted tool interface edits skill files directly.

### Key idea

Treat the agent's skill library as a **live, editable artifact** rather than a frozen SFT/RL output. When plans invalidate (pop-ups, delayed loads, relocated widgets), the loop patches the skill in-place with no weight updates.

### Main finding

+16.2% on MobileWorld, +6.0% on AndroidWorld, +10.5% on OSWorld across multiple base models. Evolved skill libraries transfer to related tasks — the artifact learned is reusable, not just a per-task fix.

### Why it matters

Retraining GUI agents on every UI change is impractical. Training-free skill evolution is the pragmatic route to keep agents functional as the underlying software drifts, and this paper posts numbers that justify shipping it.

## 11. Video DeltaNet

**Authors:** Haocheng Xi, Yiming Xie, Hexu Zhao, Yiwen Zhang, Michael Liu, Thomas Creavin, Kurt Keutzer, Xiuyu Li, Zhaoyang Lv, Chenfeng Xu, Haiwen Feng — *UC Berkeley / Meta*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.20744](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20744) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.20744) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.20744)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `video`, `attention`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

VDN — a **hybrid attention** for video diffusion that pairs local softmax attention with bidirectional linear memory. The linear branch uses Video Delta Attention (VDA), a per-frame memory update over joint spatial tokens; softmax stays for interactions crossing text/audio.

### Key idea

Linear attention alone loses the fine-grained interactions video diffusion needs, and full softmax on long spatiotemporal token sequences is a bottleneck. VDN keeps softmax for text/audio ↔ video and swaps in linear memory only for video-to-video interactions, with gates and staged teacher-alignment folding it into a pretrained model.

### Main finding

Instantiated on MiniMax-H3: 14.3-second 768p video, 50-step DiT, in 6.70 seconds on 8× B200 — a **14.5× speedup** over dense H3, with 8-step distillation and an SGLang serving stack.

### Why it matters

Livestream video generation needs both quality and latency — pure linear-attention swaps lose quality, pure softmax is too slow. Hybrid attention is now a serious production route, and the VDN recipe (per-frame memory update, staged teacher alignment) is transferable to other video DiTs.

## 12. When2Think

**Authors:** Jaejun Shim, HyunJin Kim, Young Jin Kim, JinYeong Bak — *Sungkyunkwan University / Microsoft*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.19671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19671) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.19671) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.19671)
**Topics:** `reasoning`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

Instance-level Difficulty-Aware Control (IDAC) — a training and inference-time recipe that tells a hybrid reasoning model *when* to think. Pre-computed per-instance accuracy and token-usage stats regulate reasoning depth so the model overthinks less on easy problems and gives itself more room on hard ones.

### Key idea

Difficulty isn't a per-benchmark constant — it's per-instance. Reference statistics (accuracy, tokens) computed from a strong baseline become a lookup table that shapes reasoning length in supervised fine-tuning and at inference.

### Main finding

Better accuracy-vs-tokens trade-off than fixed-depth baselines, with clean gains on AIME24 and AIME25.

### Why it matters

Reasoning models overthink easy problems, wasting cost, and underthink hard ones, ceding accuracy. Instance-level control on both training and inference sidesteps global length-penalty tuning, which never fits all problems.

## 13. Privileged Information OPSD

**Authors:** XiuYu Zhang, Wei Chow, Junfeng Fang, Zhenkai Liang, Tat-Seng Chua — *National University of Singapore*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.20612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20612) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.20612) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.20612)
**Topics:** `post-training`, `distillation`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

Isolates the contribution of *privileged information* (a worked solution or answer given only to the teacher) in on-policy self-distillation. Introduces AMPLE-Math (5,319 problems × 6 reasoning views) to separate the two effects: (i) reference-free self-distillation and (ii) added information.

### Key idea

Most of Qwen3-1.7B's OPSD gains come from **cross-mode parameter transfer** — carrying strengths from direct-response into thinking-enabled inference — not from the privileged reference revealing the answer. The privileged signal adds value only in what it does for that cross-mode transfer.

### Main finding

Reference-free distillation drives most improvement on both in-domain and external benchmarks. Privileged references stop looking like an information leak and start looking like a transfer-mode enabler.

### Why it matters

Recasts the design of self-distillation pipelines: the point of a worked solution isn't "cheating" — it's aligning direct-response and reasoning modes. That's actionable for anyone building thinking/non-thinking hybrid models.

## 14. Candidate-Generation TTS

**Authors:** Mobina Kashaniyan, Ali Jannesari — *Iowa State University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.19499](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19499) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.19499) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.19499)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`, `eval`, `test-time-scaling`

### TL;DR

Shows that at fixed candidate count, **batching schedule** — one 8-wide call vs eight 1-wide calls — changes A100 energy and latency by 4–6× on Phi-3-mini and Qwen2.5-1.5B GSM8K, with no accuracy movement.

### Key idea

Test-time-scaling papers report sample counts and accuracy; the systems reality is that scheduling those samples (batch shape, KV reuse) dominates energy and latency. Argues for reporting batch schedule + GPU-level metrics alongside accuracy.

### Main finding

8 serial calls use 4.64–4.86× gross device energy and 5.77–6.12× P95 latency of one batched call — same accuracy. On A100 running Phi-3-mini and Qwen2.5-1.5B GSM8K.

### Why it matters

Fair TTS comparisons require reporting more than "N candidates". A leaderboard that only shows sample count is undercounting inference cost by up to 6×, which changes the tradeoff calculation for real deployments.

*Borderline: included because it changes how TTS results should be reported — a small but load-bearing methodological correction.*

## 15. Region-Level Policy Optimization

**Authors:** Yuheng Shi, Xiaohuan Pei, Minjing Dong, Chang Xu — *University of Sydney / City University of Hong Kong*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.19745](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19745) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.19745) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.19745)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `RL`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

Vision-RL2 — a region-level policy optimization for MLLMs. Treats image regions as RL actions and uses a frozen MLLM to score them; a mix of subtractive and additive objectives refines region selection without any region annotations.

### Key idea

Localization is more compressible than recognition (3–4× token headroom), so learn a policy over regions rather than compressing the whole visual token grid uniformly. Frozen MLLM serves as a self-supervised reward source.

### Main finding

Achieves baseline accuracy with ~75% fewer visual tokens across multiple benchmarks and MLLM backbones — a straight speed/cost win with matching quality.

### Why it matters

Multimodal inference cost is dominated by visual token count; region-level policies give a principled and annotation-free way to prune it. Complements KV-cache tricks with input-side compression.

## 16. Don't Mask the Environment

**Authors:** Juzheng Zhang, Disha Makhija, Manoj Ghuhan Arivazhagan, Vinayshekhar Bannihatti Kumar, Rashmi Gangadharaiah — *Amazon*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.20715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20715) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.20715) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.20715)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `SFT`

### TL;DR

ActObs — supervise both **action tokens** and **observation tokens** during agent SFT, instead of masking observations. Encourages the policy to model action consequences, at no extra data or compute.

### Key idea

Standard agent SFT masks environment tokens (they aren't the model's to predict). But letting the loss also cover observation modeling installs a world-model bias that persists into downstream RL.

### Main finding

On Qwen3-4B, GRPO started from ActObs-SFT beats action-only SFT at every sampled budget on pass@k, keeps higher entropy during RL, and needs less policy adjustment. Joint supervision prevents one-sided specialization.

### Why it matters

A one-line change to the loss mask meaningfully improves exploration under RL — the kind of small, free improvement that shifts the shape of agent post-training pipelines.
