## 1. DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash: Pushing the Limits of KV Cache Compression <a id="1-deepseek-v41-flash"></a>

**Authors:** DeepSeek-AI (591 contributors) — *DeepSeek-AI*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.19969](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19969) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.19969) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.19969)
**Topics:** `LLM`, `MoE`, `efficient-inference`, `quantization`, `multimodal`

### TL;DR

DeepSeek's next-generation flash model: a 552B-parameter multimodal MoE with a Causal Encoder-Decoder (CED) split, aggressively engineered to compress KV cache for long-horizon agentic workloads. Combines cross-layer KV reuse, FP4 KV caching, and a serving trick called SWA Bounded Replay to shrink the always-in-HBM KV footprint to ~890 bytes/token (¼ of V4-Flash) and the persistent SSD footprint to ~⅛.

### Key idea

Three stacked reductions on top of an MoE trunk. **CED** activates 16B parameters per decode token but only 8B during prefill — long inputs pay less. **Compressed Sparse Attention 2 (CSA2)** reuses compressed KV representations across layers, so the cache stops multiplying by depth. **FP4 KV caching** stores those compressed states in 4-bit floats. **SWA Bounded Replay** caps how much of the sliding-window history a serving stack must recompute on cache miss, so persistent state can live on SSD without blowing latency.

### Main finding

Pretrained on a 45T-token multimodal corpus. Global (HBM-resident) KV cache is ~¼ of DeepSeek-V4-Flash at 890 B/token; persistent (SSD/host) KV cache is ~⅛. Delivers better downstream scores than the V4-Flash baseline despite the smaller cache, across text and multimodal agentic benchmarks. Checkpoints released on Hugging Face.

### Why it matters

KV cache is the dominant scaling wall for agentic inference: long inputs are cheap to prefill in FLOPs but expensive to *store*. This paper composes four independent axes of compression (asymmetric activation, cross-layer reuse, low-bit storage, serving-aware replay) and shows they stack. It's the strongest signal so far that "the KV cache" as a monolithic HBM tensor is over — future long-context serving will look tiered, quantized, and layer-sparse.

## 2. Can MiniMax-H3 Reason About the Physical World? An Evaluation of Omni-Modal Generative Model <a id="2-minimax-h3-physical-reasoning"></a>

**Authors:** Haoyu Zhao, Zihao Zhao, Tianyu Deng, Ziqin Xu, Zihao Zhang, Xudong Wang, Jinxiang Guo, Chen Gao, Ziyi Ye, Yeying Jin, Jiaxi Gu, Zuxuan Wu, Shuicheng Yan — *Multi-institution (MiniMax + Fudan + NUS)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.18323](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18323) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.18323) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.18323)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `eval`

### TL;DR

An evaluation framework built specifically to test whether omni-modal generative models (unified text + image + video + audio) can reason about physical events when each modality only sees part of the story. 517 tasks across four setups: implicit-prompt+multi-frame, audio+image, prefix-video, and audio+video. MiniMax-H3 scores 41.97% overall.

### Key idea

Design tasks where no single modality contains enough evidence — the model must *fuse* streams to infer the latent event. This is a departure from standard video-generation evals where the prompt already describes the target.

### Main finding

Video-based decision reasoning: 56.00% success. Audio-based disambiguation: 27.40% (weakest). The gap between visual and audio grounding suggests omni-models still lean heavily on the visual channel even when audio carries the disambiguating signal.

### Why it matters

Signals what a "world model" eval looks like when the model is genuinely multimodal at input — not just image-captioning. Future frontier omni-models will need this shape of test to distinguish real cross-modal reasoning from a strong single-modality prior.

## 3. When EOS Tokens Disagree: Understanding Length Inflation in On-Policy Distillation <a id="3-eos-tokens-disagree"></a>

**Authors:** Yuxiao Yang, Tianrun Yu, Shangzhe Li, Kaixiang Zhao, Xuchao Zhang, Chetan Bansal, Huaxiu Yao, Taylor W. Killian, Weitong Zhang — *UNC + Microsoft Research*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.20511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20511) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.20511) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.20511)
**Topics:** `post-training`, `distillation`

### TL;DR

On-policy distillation (OPD) students often generate excessively long outputs that exhaust the budget. This paper traces one important cause: base student and post-trained teacher place their stopping probability on *different* EOS tokens, even when the declared stopping sets are identical. Treating semantically equivalent EOS tokens as one shared stopping action substantially reduces length inflation.

### Key idea

Termination is not a single token — modern tokenizers ship multiple EOS-like sentinels (`<|endoftext|>`, `<|im_end|>`, chat-template stops). If the teacher's mass sits on one and the student's on another, on-policy KL suppresses the student's natural stop without transferring the teacher's alternative. Fix: aggregate probability across a functional-EOS equivalence class before computing the loss.

### Main finding

Applied across Qwen3, Llama, and Gemma students, the shared-stopping-action treatment mitigates the inflation across the three families. Stage-wise analysis on K2-Horizon further shows a *second*, later-training source of inflation that persists after termination alignment — termination mismatch is important but not exhaustive.

### Why it matters

Distillation from post-trained teachers is a workhorse for building small, capable students. This paper identifies a subtle tokenizer/template pathology that any OPD pipeline can hit and gives a fix that costs nothing at inference. A useful diagnostic for anyone shipping distilled models.

## 4. SoL-Pi: Recursively Scaling Auto-Research Loops for Efficient Agent Harness <a id="4-sol-pi"></a>

**Authors:** Haozhe Liu, Tian Ye, Sensen Gao, Qihang Cao, Yitong Li, Mingchen Zhuge, Duomin Wang, Ruihua Zhang, Ping Luo, Jiawang Bian, Lei Zhu, Ligeng Zhu, Enze Xie, Song Han — *NVIDIA + HKUST + MIT*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.20519](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20519) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.20519) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.20519)
**Topics:** `agents`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

Coding agents burn tokens on long trajectories. SoL-Pi is a recursive-self-improvement approach applied at the *harness* layer: they scale auto-research loops across many rollout environments and keep only the harness modifications that transfer. Four survived — action execution, context compaction, observation handling, and delegated reading. Result: 44.7–49.0% token savings on EdgeBench with matched accuracy.

### Key idea

Instead of retraining models, evolve the *harness* around a fixed model. Diversity across rollout environments filters out overfit hacks; four mechanisms transfer robustly.

### Main finding

On 51-task EdgeBench: comparable accuracy to Pi baseline across GPT-5.6-Sol and Opus 5; -44.7% to -49% token traffic; ~⅓ cost reduction. Est. $8.75–$13.50/hr savings vs. Codex/Claude Code native harnesses.

### Why it matters

The frontier is agent economics as much as agent quality. This paper crystallizes "harness engineering" as a first-class research direction — token-per-task efficiency has orders of magnitude on the table before touching the model.

## 5. An Empirical Study of Harness Design for Coding Agents <a id="5-harness-design-coding-agents"></a>

**Authors:** Run-Ze Fan, Zihao Zhang, Simin Ma, Yebowen Hu, Shouju Wang, Kaiqiang Song, Fei Liu, Hamed Zamani, Xiaoyang Wang — *UMass Amherst + Tencent AI Lab*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.20804](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20804) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.20804) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.20804)
**Topics:** `agents`, `eval`

### TL;DR

A component-level ablation of coding-agent harnesses: fix the execution loop, vary planning, action space, and context management. 176 matched settings across four models on SWE-Bench Verified and Terminal-Bench 2.1. Distills four findings on when each component pays off.

### Key idea

Existing harness comparisons treat the whole harness as a monolith. This paper isolates individual axes to answer questions like "does planning still help strong models?" and "does recoverable context elision buy anything?"

### Main finding

(1) Context management matters more as budgets tighten and mostly prevents overflow failures. (2) Rule-based elision before LLM summarization dominates on efficiency; making elided content recoverable is dead weight. (3) Planning is an accuracy scaffold for weak models and a cost saver for strong ones. (4) Predefined tools help models with weak bash proficiency; bash-capable models are cheaper on a bash-only interface.

### Why it matters

Turns harness design from folklore into a matrix. The elision/recoverability finding in particular kills a common piece of complexity. This is the reference paper for anyone tuning an agent stack.

## 6. JEPA-Anything: Learning Predictive Models across Different Worlds <a id="6-jepa-anything"></a>

**Authors:** Taoyong Cui, Zhongyao Wang, Xinyue Xu, Weiyang Liu, Zhaochen Yu, Yuying Zhang, Qiang Gao, Mengyue Yang, Wanli Ouyang, Pheng Ann Heng, Yingcheng Wu, Zhenfei Yin, Ling Yang — *CUHK + Shanghai AI Lab + Bristol*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.20800](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20800) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.20800) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.20800)
**Topics:** `architectures`, `world-models`
*Borderline: included because the domain-agnostic predictive-factorization principle is directly relevant to representation learning for LLMs and world models.*

### TL;DR

A domain-agnostic JEPA extension. Orthogonal Predictive Factorization (OPF) decomposes the latent target into complementary factors, learns each through a dedicated pathway, then recombines. Evaluated on vision, biology, clinical trajectories, control, molecular dynamics, physical fields, and weather — beats matched JEPA baselines on all 10 dynamics tasks tested.

### Key idea

Rather than one big predictive head, split the latent target into orthogonal factors and predict each separately, then reunify. The claim is that this factorization is *the* shared pattern underlying all these worlds — a single learning principle across heterogeneous systems.

### Main finding

All 10 matched dynamics tasks improve; -34.8% single-intervention error on Interventional Pong; best 1-step and 100-step molecular rollouts across four systems. A biological intervention nominated by the factor recovery received experimental support in cell co-cultures, organoids, tumor fragments, and mice; a Keplerian latent-mode fit recovers the -3/2 exponent (measured -1.4991).

### Why it matters

If the OPF claim holds up, it's a strong argument for factorized-latent world models as the substrate for general prediction — bridging the gap between LLM latent prediction and physical-system rollouts.

## 7. Verifiable Social Reasoning for LLM Assistants <a id="7-verifiable-social-reasoning"></a>

**Authors:** Amir Taubenfeld, Zorik Gekhman, Avigail Grinstein-Dabush, Itay Laish, Ariel Goldstein, Marian Croak, Avinatan Hassidim, Yossi Matias, Amir Feder — *Google*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.17496](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17496) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.17496) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.17496)
**Topics:** `safety`, `eval`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

Fuse is a multi-agent simulation for evaluating social reasoning in assistants. A target agent has a hidden motive; other agents interact with it; a user-agent then consults the evaluated assistant to infer the motive. Because the motive is fixed by construction, ground truth is verifiable. 21k released examples; 24k human annotations.

### Key idea

Turn subjective social reasoning into a verifiable task by generating it from a known-motive simulation, then evaluating whether the assistant reconstructs that motive from *user-mediated* second-hand narrative.

### Main finding

Across 12 LLMs: (i) user mediation compounds difficulty; (ii) models are systematically sensitive to biased framing from the user-agent; (iii) LLMs need more detail than humans to converge on the right prediction; (iv) longer conversations don't monotonically help.

### Why it matters

Consultation-style social reasoning is a fast-growing use of LLM assistants and has been hard to benchmark because the ground truth is inherently subjective. Fuse gives an experimentally clean lever. The bias-to-user-framing finding is particularly concerning for alignment.

## 8. RetireOPD: Self-Retiring On-Policy Distillation for Agentic RL <a id="8-retireopd"></a>

**Authors:** Yan Yu, Zhengxi Lu, Yizhou Liu, Yichen Pan, Aozhe Wang, Qipeng Chen, Hua Yang, Wenqi Zhang, Weiming Lu, Qianglong Chen, Yongliang Shen — *Zhejiang University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.20784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20784) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.20784) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.20784)
**Topics:** `post-training`, `RL`, `agents`

### TL;DR

Multi-turn agents trained with RL get sparse (trajectory-level) rewards. RetireOPD adds a dense per-token supervision signal from a self-teacher that has privileged task skills, then *retires* that teacher automatically once the student saturates against it. Adaptive Retirement drops the teacher when the discrepancy between student and teacher stops shrinking and the student hits a target fraction of the teacher's success rate.

### Key idea

Two-stage: (1) optimize a decoupled, skill-conditioned teacher with environment rewards; (2) train a skill-free student jointly with RL and OPD. The retirement signal is behavioral (discrepancy plateau + success-rate target), not a fixed schedule.

### Main finding

Across Qwen2.5 (1.5B–7B): +14.1% to +18.8% ALFWorld success, +11.8% to +19.0% WebShop accuracy — and the student *surpasses* its own teacher in every setting.

### Why it matters

Dense supervision from privileged self-teachers is a common recipe, but knowing when to stop leaning on it has been folklore. Adaptive Retirement puts that on a principled behavioral trigger, and the "student > teacher" result is strong evidence that the schedule matters as much as the signal.

## 9. Video DeltaNet: Video-Native Hybrid Attention for Livestream Generation <a id="9-video-deltanet"></a>

**Authors:** Haocheng Xi, Yiming Xie, Hexu Zhao, Yiwen Zhang, Michael Liu, Thomas Creavin, Kurt Keutzer, Xiuyu Li, Zhaoyang Lv, Chenfeng Xu, Haiwen Feng — *UC Berkeley + Meta*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.20744](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20744) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.20744) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.20744)
**Topics:** `architectures`, `multimodal`, `diffusion`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

Video diffusion models are attention-bound on long spatiotemporal token sequences. Video DeltaNet (VDN) hybridizes local softmax attention with a bidirectional *linear* memory that updates once per frame — introducing Video Delta Attention (VDA). Applied to MiniMax H3 with staged teacher alignment and 8-step distillation, VDN-H3 does a 14.3-second 768p generation in 6.70s on 8× B200 — **14.5×** over the 50-step dense H3 baseline.

### Key idea

Linear attention alone can't match softmax quality on video, but you don't have to choose. Keep softmax where fine-grained spatial interactions matter (within a frame and to text/audio) and use a linear memory branch, one update per frame, for long-range video context. Learnable gates + separate output projections calibrate the two branches; staged teacher alignment introduces the linear pathway into a pretrained model without a from-scratch rewrite.

### Main finding

14.5× wall-clock speedup on 8× B200 vs. 50-step dense; retained generation quality after 8-step distillation.

### Why it matters

Livestream-quality (few-second) generation with high-res video has been out of reach at reasonable cost. Hybrid attention with a video-native linear branch pushes serving costs into a range where interactive video generation is plausible.

## 10. When2Think: Difficulty-Aware Length Control for Hybrid Reasoning <a id="10-when2think"></a>

**Authors:** Jaejun Shim, HyunJin Kim, Young Jin Kim, JinYeong Bak — *SKKU + Microsoft*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.19671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19671) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.19671) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.19671)
**Topics:** `reasoning`, `RL`

### TL;DR

Reasoning models overthink easy problems and underthink hard ones. When2Think allocates reasoning compute per-instance using a reward-shaping mechanism (IDAC — Instance-level Difficulty-Aware Control) that leverages pre-computed reference statistics on accuracy and token usage. Verifier-based reward + batch-standardized advantages give critic-free optimization.

### Key idea

Difficulty isn't observed at rollout time, but you can compute per-instance reference statistics offline and use them to shape rewards so easy instances get low target token usage and hard ones get long-CoT budget. No learned reward model, no online reference-model queries — just batch-wise standardized advantages.

### Main finding

On AIME24: +10.0% Pass@3 while token usage -27.9% vs. the base model. On AIME25: 40.0% Pass@3, beating compression and routing-only baselines.

### Why it matters

The overthink/underthink asymmetry is one of the sharpest inefficiencies in current reasoning models. Per-instance targets tuned by offline stats is a clean, verifier-friendly way to close it without a routing model or a learned reward.

## 11. What Does Privileged Information Add to On-Policy Self-Distillation? <a id="11-privileged-information-opsd"></a>

**Authors:** XiuYu Zhang, Wei Chow, Junfeng Fang, Xingyu Zhu, Zhenkai Liang, Tat-Seng Chua — *NUS*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.20612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20612) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.20612) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.20612)
**Topics:** `post-training`, `distillation`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

An ablation of what a *privileged* teacher (one that has seen the answer or a worked solution) adds over reference-free on-policy self-distillation. Introduces AMPLE-Math, a 5,319-problem suite with six matched reasoning views sharing an answer. Finding: most of the gain comes from reference-free distillation itself; adding a solution is a modest boost, and it disappears (or reverses) if rollouts are long thinking-enabled generations.

### Key idea

Match the reference and evaluate contribution of each view against reference-free distillation. If reference-free explains most of the improvement, privileged information isn't the main mechanism.

### Main finding

Reference-free distillation captures most of the Qwen3-1.7B improvement under thinking-enabled eval. Complete traces add ~2 pp for SmolLM3-3B at step 50; benefits collapse for long thinking-enabled rollouts even at the same checkpoint.

### Why it matters

Reframes what "privileged information" buys you in self-distillation: it's mostly about switching *access to* the model's existing capabilities across modes (direct-response ↔ thinking), not injecting new knowledge from the reference.

### Knowledge graph integration

## 12. UFO: Chain-of-Evaluation for Omni-Condition Alignment in Multi-Modal Image Generation <a id="12-ufo-alignment-eval"></a>

**Authors:** Danning Zhang, Yijing Lin, Shuhan Zhuang, Mengqi Huang, Shaojin Wu, Shancheng Fang, Zhendong Mao — *USTC*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.12397](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12397) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.12397) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.12397)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `diffusion`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Subject-driven image generation is evaluated per-modality (does the image match the text? the subject?), but real-world alignment is *simultaneous* across all conditions. UFO decomposes omni-condition alignment into fine-grained Atomic Evaluation Units (AEUs), routes each to a general or dedicated verifier, then chains the results.

### Key idea

Atomized Chain-of-Evaluation: (a) decompose into disentangled AEUs, (b) classify by modality-relevance, (c) use a general or specialist model to verify each unit, (d) chain them into a joint alignment score.

### Main finding

+15.25% average correlation improvement with human evaluation preferences over baseline evaluators. Introduces UFO-Bench for holistic customization eval.

### Why it matters

Image-gen model evaluation has been the weakest link in the loop — the simultaneity of the alignment objective was lost in per-modality metrics. UFO makes the eval match the objective.

## 13. Sample Count Is Not Enough: Candidate-Generation Strategy Shapes the Energy and Performance of LLM Test-Time Scaling <a id="13-candidate-generation-schedule"></a>

**Authors:** Mobina Kashaniyan, Ali Jannesari — *Iowa State*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.19499](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19499) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.19499) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.19499)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`, `reasoning`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Test-time scaling reports "we generated N candidates." But *how* those N candidates were scheduled — one batched call vs. several sequential calls — is invisible in the accuracy number and dominates the energy cost. At fixed N=8 on Phi-3-mini and Qwen2.5-1.5B, 8×1 serial takes **4.64–4.86× more energy** and **5.77–6.12× more p95 latency** than 1×8 batched.

### Key idea

Report the *generation schedule* (a × b: a calls with b candidates each) alongside N and accuracy. When candidates are independent and memory allows, prefer fewer larger-batch calls.

### Main finding

On A100 GPUs at N=8, the ratio between 1×8 batched and 8×1 serial: 4.64–4.86× on energy, 5.77–6.12× on p95 latency. Pattern replicates across three independently scheduled nodes per model and in short-output SciQ/V100 experiments.

### Why it matters

Test-time scaling is a growing share of production inference cost — and much of that cost is a scheduling choice, not a model choice. This paper is a benchmark-hygiene note that could re-order the leaderboards on efficiency.

## 14. Don't Mask the Environment: Observation Supervision Changes How Agents Explore Under RL <a id="14-actobs"></a>

**Authors:** Juzheng Zhang, Disha Makhija, Manoj Ghuhan Arivazhagan, Vinayshekhar Bannihatti Kumar, Rashmi Gangadharaiah — *Amazon*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.20715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20715) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.20715) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.20715)
**Topics:** `post-training`, `RL`, `agents`

### TL;DR

Standard agent SFT applies loss only to agent action tokens, using environment observations as context. ActObs also supervises the observation tokens. After SFT the two initializations perform similarly — but after GRPO, ActObs consistently reaches higher pass@k, retains more policy entropy during RL, and moves less from its SFT initialization.

### Key idea

Predicting what the environment will say next is free supervision — no new data, no new parameters, no extra forward passes. Learning to model action *consequences* prepares the policy for downstream RL exploration; action-only training leaves a large orthogonal observation gradient at SFT time that ends up degrading environment prediction below the base model.

### Main finding

On Qwen3-4B, ActObs beats action-only at every sampling budget on Terminal-Bench 2.0. On Qwen3-8B, +3.4 pp pass@16 (trading some pass@1). +4.2 pp pass@1 at 4B on aider-polyglot (out-of-domain). Retains higher entropy during RL, less policy movement from SFT.

### Why it matters

A "free" change to agent SFT that materially reshapes the RL that comes after. Suggests agent training regimes have been silently over-specializing to actions-only for years.

## 15. Region-Level Policy Optimization for Fine-grained MLLM Perception <a id="15-vision-rl2"></a>

**Authors:** Yuheng Shi, Xiaohuan Pei, Minjing Dong, Chang Xu — *University of Sydney*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.19745](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19745) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.19745) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.19745)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `RL`

### TL;DR

Fine-grained perception in MLLMs splits into (a) localizing the region of interest and (b) recognizing its content — and the two have very different resolution requirements. Vision-RL2 optimizes a lightweight region-proposal network with RL on region-level rewards, where a frozen MLLM reader scores each candidate by how removing it changes the answer likelihood.

### Key idea

Treat coherent image regions as actions and use answer-likelihood delta as the reward. Complementary subtractive and additive objectives suppress distracting proposals and recover missing evidence. Only the proposal network updates — no response sampling, no reasoning trajectories, no region annotations.

### Main finding

Across six fine-grained benchmarks and four MLLM backbones, Vision-RL2 improves accuracy at every token budget and matches or beats the base model's largest-budget accuracy with **~4× fewer visual tokens**.

### Why it matters

Vision-encoder resolution has been a scaling wall in MLLMs. Splitting the localization/recognition budgets asymmetrically, and using RL to optimize *only* the localizer, is a clean and label-free way to break it.
