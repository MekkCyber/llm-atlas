## 1. WorldCrafter: Consistent Video World Model with Implicit 3D-aware Memory {#1-worldcrafter}

**Authors:** Wangbo Yu, Kunhao Liu, Wenbo Hu, Shenghai Yuan, Chaoran Feng, Haiyang Zhou, Yukun Huang, Yiran Wang, Wang Zhao, Yingmin Luo, Ying Shan — *TencentARC (implied from author network)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.24984](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24984) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.24984) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.24984)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `multimodal`, `world-model`

### TL;DR

A video world model that grounds long-horizon scene exploration in an *implicit 3D-aware memory*: past frames are compressed into a fixed-budget set of tokens conditioned on the requested camera pose, letting a distilled few-step video generator revisit places consistently across minute-scale rollouts.

### Key idea

Instead of maintaining an explicit 3D scene reconstruction or a depth-based correspondence, WorldCrafter learns a memory encoder + pose-conditioned readout module *jointly with* the video generator. At each new viewpoint, the readout produces target-view-specific memory tokens that get concatenated with recent temporal context before denoising. The memory is queryable by camera pose but never surfaces geometry directly.

### Main finding

Substantial improvements over prior video world models on long-horizon consistency and camera-control accuracy, while sustaining minute-scale exploration in both static and dynamic scenes. Runs in a few-step distilled regime for streaming.

### Why it matters

Pushes video world models from "seconds of coherent generation" into the regime where they can serve as playable / navigable environments — a prerequisite for using them as simulators for RL agents or robotic planners.

## 2. RRSI: Regularized Recursive Self-Improvement of Agent Harnesses {#2-rrsi}

**Authors:** Peng Xia, Rujun Han, Zifeng Wang, Yanfei Chen, Yufan Zhang, Yoonho Lee, Chengsong Huang, Han Yu, Zhongying CuiZhu, Yifei Ming, Huaxiu Yao, Burak Gokturk, Tomas Pfister, Chen-Yu Lee — *Google (from the `google-research/rrsi` repo)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.24972](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24972) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.24972) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.24972)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Recursive self-improvement of an LLM agent's *harness* (prompts, tools, control flow, memory) overfits to the training benchmark. RRSI adds regularization at the *proposer* (bounded-budget edits, novelty-seeking) and *selector* (critic + pruner that drops small, expensive, or benchmark-specific edits) to preserve OOD gains.

### Key idea

Treat harness-evolution as a search problem where the objective silently drifts toward memorization. Regularize by (i) a temporally-annealed edit budget on the proposer, (ii) explicit exploration incentives from evolution history, (iii) a critic that filters benchmark-specific proposals, and (iv) a pruner that removes changes the harness can survive without.

### Main finding

Across eight benchmarks (coding, agentic workspace, engineering design): up to **+14.1 pts** on the evolved split and up to **+4.7 pts** on five OOD benchmarks, while the final harness uses **30% fewer policy tokens** than the unregularized baseline. Code at [github.com/google-research/rrsi](https://github.com/google-research/rrsi).

### Why it matters

Agent-harness auto-tuning is being reframed as a form of RSI. If naïvely applied it collapses to benchmark overfitting; RRSI is a concrete recipe for making the search transferable — relevant for anyone doing DSPy-style or AFlow-style harness optimization.

## 3. Transferring the Intelligence of VLMs to Robotic Control (RoboDawn) {#3-robodawn}

**Authors:** Meng-Hao Guo, Zhe-Han Mo, Jia-Jun Wang, Yi Zhang, Kejin Wang, Yi-Xuan Deng, Jia-Peng Zhang, Yongming Rao, Shi-Min Hu — *Tsinghua University (Shi-Min Hu group)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.22966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22966) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.22966) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.22966)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `agents`, `VLA`

### TL;DR

An agentic VLM controls a robot through a *discrete* translation / rotation / gripper interface in a perceive→reason→act closed loop, with a few in-context demonstrations for grounding. No robot-specific training is required and one demo takes it to SOTA on standard sim benchmarks.

### Key idea

Do not fine-tune a VLA on trajectories; instead, expose the physical world to an off-the-shelf VLM through a compact command alphabet so the VLM can plan by writing programs. In-context demos teach both the interface conventions and task strategy — the VLM's language and vision priors do the rest.

### Main finding

On **RoboTwin 2.0 C2R**, zero-shot success **53.2%**, one-shot **73.6%**, beating π0.5 baseline (46.0%). On **RoboDojo**, 35.67% → 47.17% with one ICL demo. Transfers to real Franka arms on block-in-basket and block-stacking without fine-tuning.

### Why it matters

Reopens the question of whether robot-specific policy training is necessary for a broad class of manipulation tasks — if VLMs already have the intelligence and only need a good interface, that changes what "training a VLA" means.

## 4. Realtime-Venus: A full-duplex interaction system with asynchronous delegation {#4-realtime-venus}

**Authors:** Venus Team — *Ant Group (with Tsinghua)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.13814](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13814) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.13814) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.13814)
**Topics:** `LLM`, `multimodal`, `agents`

### TL;DR

Two co-trained 9B models (audio and audio+video) run a *proactive full-duplex* conversational frontend on a shared causal timeline for user inputs, model outputs, and delegation events. Long-running tools run in a second "harness" loop and results are re-integrated into the ongoing conversation without breaking the interaction.

### Key idea

A **dual-loop runtime**: the foreground model owns perception, turn-taking and speech generation; delegated tasks (search, reasoning, tool calls) run asynchronously in a second harness model and stream results back into the foreground timeline. Post-training combines offline understanding, proactive full-duplex trajectories, and delegation workflows on both models.

### Main finding

On video benchmarks the omni model leads on 6/8 tasks (**StreamingBench 70.2**, **OVO-Bench 64.7**, **Daily-Omni 81.3**). On audio: **MMAU 78.0**, **MMAU-Pro 63.2**, VoiceBench AlpacaEval 4.81. On Full-Duplex-Bench v1.5: responds to **75%** of interruptions with **97 / 88 / 86%** continuation under backchannels / other-directed / background speech — above Gemini 3.1 Live and GPT-4o.

### Why it matters

A concrete production-scale answer to the "conversation while thinking" problem: how to keep a voice/video assistant conversational while it does slow work behind the scenes. The trajectory format and timeline abstraction are re-usable design patterns.

## 5. VideoGen-Agent: Reinforcing Video Generation Agents {#5-videogen-agent}

**Authors:** Binxu Li, Haoyi Duan, Yuhui Zhang, Yaohui Zhang, Zihao Lin, Kaituo Feng, Suozhi Huang, Xiangyi Li, Yu Li, Chunyuan Li, Shilong Liu, Mengdi Wang — *Stanford / Princeton (Mengdi Wang)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.24997](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24997) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.24997) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.24997)
**Topics:** `RL`, `agents`, `diffusion`

### TL;DR

Trains a multimodal agent with agentic RL to *use tools* around a base text-to-video model (augmentation, generation, verification), coordinating them across turns. Boosts the base generator by 19.1 pts on a new benchmark and — critically — improves further when the underlying generator is swapped for a better one, with no retraining.

### Key idea

SFT-warmup on teacher trajectories to instill tool-use behavior, then RL with a **category-aware hybrid reward** covering tool-call validity, task-appropriate tool selection, and generated video quality. A single policy handles six categories (procedural knowledge, identity preservation, physical consistency, scene composition, multi-shot temporal structure).

### Main finding

On the new **VABench** (600 held-out prompts): **56.5 → 75.6** (+19.1) over the base T2V generator; upgrading only the underlying tools further pushes to **86.1** with no additional agent training; human raters prefer the upgraded configuration 84.3% of the time.

### Why it matters

Argues that the "agent that wraps a generator" pattern is a durable capability layer — a model trained to *plan* with generation tools inherits future generator improvements for free.

## 6. onPanda: Efficient Annotation of On-Policy Alignment Data via Token-Level Correction {#6-onpanda}

**Authors:** Lei Yang, Mengyin Liu, Jia Wang, Hangyu Guo, Liang Zhao, Zheng Ge, Kang An, Binxing Jiao, Qi Han, Daxin Jiang, Siqi Shen, Xiangyu Zhang — *StepFun (Xiangyu Zhang / Daxin Jiang)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.24983](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24983) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.24983) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.24983)
**Topics:** `post-training`, `SFT`, `agents`

### TL;DR

An annotation UI where the human finds the first *wrong token*, picks a replacement from the model's candidate distribution (or types a small edit), then the model regenerates from that corrected prefix. Iterates until the response is good. Produces on-policy SFT and preference data with token-level supervision.

### Key idea

**Locate → correct → continue.** Because most tokens in the final answer are still model-generated, the finished response stays close to the model's own sampling distribution — the resulting SFT data is effectively "on-policy." Every correction also yields a token-level (position, wrong-token, right-token) supervision signal and paired positive/negative candidates for preference learning.

### Main finding

Median annotation time **-52%** vs. manual post-editing in a controlled study. Ships with **Panda-CVL** — a token-level-annotated agentic/vision dataset and benchmark.

### Why it matters

A practical answer to two chronic pains of alignment data: (a) off-policy human writes drift the model, and (b) global preference labels are cheap but low signal. Token-level on-policy correction is denser and cheaper at the same time.

## 7. Jev-Mem: System-One-Controlled Agentic Memory for Efficient AI Agents {#7-jev-mem}

**Authors:** Dongming Jiang, Yi Li, Bingzhe Li — *UT Dallas (Bingzhe Li)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.23986](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23986) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.23986) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.23986)
**Topics:** `agents`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

Splits agentic memory into a *fast* System-One controller (typing, routing, retrieval-budget allocation, graph traversal, adaptive stopping) that does *not* need a big LLM, and a slower System-Two path that only fires for real reasoning. Fewer LLM calls on the critical path, so memory ops get much cheaper.

### Key idea

Existing agentic-memory systems (MemGPT, Zep, LettaMem, etc.) autoregressively decide what to store, when to retrieve, and how to score candidates. Jev-Mem replaces that hot path with a compact learned controller, and only invokes the LLM for answer synthesis.

### Main finding

On **LoCoMo**: LLM-as-a-Judge **0.777** (+11.0% over the strongest baseline), memory construction **158 s** (6.6× faster than the fastest competitor), query latency **0.93 s** (-36.7%).

### Why it matters

Concrete engineering evidence that "an LLM everywhere" is over-provisioning for agentic memory — a small controller can carry most of the workload, which matters for long-lived agents at scale.

## 8. 1% of Tokens Can Be Enough: On Gradient Estimation in On-Policy Distillation {#8-1pct-tokens}

**Authors:** Huanxin Sheng, Zhiling Ye, Haonan Wang, Jian Wang, Jinjie Gu, Jian Kang — *Ant Group (Jinjie Gu) / U. Rochester (Jian Kang)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.24432](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24432) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.24432) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.24432)
**Topics:** `post-training`, `RL`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

Sparse on-policy distillation (teacher-guides-only-some-tokens) fails when the *estimated* gradient at the chosen tokens is noisy. This paper introduces an *information-efficiency ratio (IER)* — a signal-to-noise decomposition on the gradient — to pick tokens where the update is actually informative. Matches full OPD at 0.1–1% token budgets.

### Key idea

Frame token selection as a **gradient-estimation** problem, not a *usefulness* problem alone. Derive IER at a fixed prefix from an information-geometry view and use it (alone or combined with existing usefulness scores) to select which tokens receive teacher supervision. Objective stays sampled reverse-KL.

### Main finding

On mathematical and medical reasoning tasks, adding IER improves existing selectors in multiple settings; sparse configurations at **0.1–1%** token budgets match or beat *full* on-policy distillation. Code: [github.com/BruceSheng1202/IER-OPD](https://github.com/BruceSheng1202/IER-OPD).

### Why it matters

On-policy distillation is emerging as a scalable alternative to RL for reasoning models. Understanding *why* a small fraction of tokens carries most of the useful signal reframes the design of selectors — and drops teacher-inference cost by ~100×.

## 9. Why Do Video Diffusion Models Violate Physics? Unveiling the Flaws in Attention Mechanisms {#9-video-physics}

**Authors:** Yueyan Li, Haibo Wang, Caixia Yuan, Xiaojie Wang — *Beijing University of Posts & Telecommunications (Xiaojie Wang)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.23658](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23658) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.23658) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.23658)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `interp`, `architectures`

### TL;DR

Interpretability study of text-to-video diffusion: motion trajectories crystallize in *early denoising steps* via a small subset of attention heads. RoPE's spatial frequency causes excessive attention decay at those steps, locking motion into physically implausible paths. A per-step RoPE-frequency rescaling fixes it.

### Key idea

Probe the "motion planner" inside a video diffusion model by combining **cross-attention trajectory patterns** with **causal head contributions** to isolate a small set of motion-planning heads. Show that RoPE's decay is too aggressive at early steps and *scale RoPE frequencies per denoising step* to broaden the receptive field — a lightweight, training-free-and-training-based fix.

### Main finding

Improves physical commonsense of generated videos in both training-free and training-based regimes; identifies the specific attention heads causing violations.

### Why it matters

First interpretability lens on the "why do videos break physics" pathology — and localizes the fix to a well-understood component (RoPE), which is directly transferable to other video-DiT architectures.

## 10. Towards Full Pipeline FP8 Reinforcement Learning for LLMs {#10-fp8-rl}

**Authors:** Fanchao Chen, Ziheng Jiang, Ziyun Wei, Zheng Zhong, Du Li, Chi Zhang, Haibin Lin, Shivaram Venkataraman — *ByteDance Seed (Haibin Lin) / UW-Madison (Shivaram)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.22870](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22870) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.22870) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.22870)
**Topics:** `quantization`, `RL`, `systems`

### TL;DR

Full-pipeline FP8 RL (both actor and rollout in FP8) blows up at mid-training with entropy surges and garbled outputs. Root cause: FP8 quantization noise distorts the importance ratio so negative-advantage tokens fall outside the trust region and their gradients get clipped to zero. Fix: *calibrated clipping* that re-anchors the FP8 clipping bounds to the BF16 distribution.

### Key idea

Existing FP8 RL work (TIS-style corrections) fixes the *train-inference* mismatch but not the *compounded quantization noise*. This paper shows the failure mode is asymmetric: high-negative-advantage tokens leave the PPO/GRPO trust region under FP8 noise, so pathological outputs are never penalized. **Calibrated Clipping** rebalances the clipping quantiles between FP8 and BF16.

### Main finding

Across GRPO and DAPO, model sizes **8B → 32B**, and multiple FP8 scaling granularities: eliminates the entropy surges and matches BF16 baseline performance.

### Why it matters

FP8 RL was the last training regime where FP8 could not be safely swapped in. Making it work end-to-end is the difference between a 1.5–2× throughput win being theoretical and being production-ready.

## 11. Complex KDA: Understanding and Enhancing the Expressivity of Kimi Delta Attention {#11-complex-kda}

**Authors:** Julien Siems, Riccardo Grazzi, Korbinian Pöppel, Jaisidh Singh, Arber Zela, Timur Carstensen, Jenia Jitsev, Frank Hutter, Volkan Cevher, Antonio Orvieto, Aaron Klein — *ELLIS Institute Tübingen (Frank Hutter, Antonio Orvieto)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.24797](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24797) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.24797) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.24797)
**Topics:** `architectures`, `LLM`

### TL;DR

Shows that Kimi Delta Attention (KDA) can realize 2D rotations by *composing a delta-rule transition with the reflection provided by its channel-wise gate* — but only if the gate range extends to [-1, 1] and the delta coefficient β to [0, 2]. The resulting **CKDA** matches DeltaProduct₂'s state-tracking expressivity at KDA's single-step cost.

### Key idea

Prior work needed two chained delta-rule updates (DeltaProduct₂) to reach 2D-rotation expressivity, doubling cost. CKDA reaches the same class in a single update by re-using KDA's gate as a reflection. Every orthogonal diagonal-plus-rank-one matrix is provably a CKDA transition — a full characterization result.

### Main finding

CKDA tracks every finite group isomorphic to a subgroup of SO(3). Strongest length extrapolation among tested KDA range settings on S₃, S₄, and periodic-audio continuation. In LM: beats Transformers and other linear RNNs, matches KDA, promising scaling.

### Why it matters

A crisp expressivity-vs-cost result for the linear-RNN family that Kimi (and others) are pursuing as a Transformer alternative. Turns the parameter *ranges* of an existing block into an expressivity knob — no new operator required.

## 12. A Lie Detector Test for Language Models: Reading Knowledge a Model Won't Reveal {#12-lie-detector}

**Authors:** Hiskias Dingeto — *Independent (single author)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.21996](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21996) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.21996) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.21996)
**Topics:** `safety`, `interp`, `eval`

### TL;DR

A forensic-inspired **Probe of Internal Recognition (PIR)** presents a model with a question, a correct answer and plausible decoys, and reads *from the internal states* which candidate it recognizes as correct. Reference-free, works across five model families, and distinguishes "won't say" from "doesn't know."

### Key idea

Borrow the Concealed Information Test from forensic psychology: recognition is easier to hide behaviorally than internally. Train a light probe on hidden states to pick the recognized candidate. No honest reference model, no ground-truth corpus — just the model + the question + candidates.

### Main finding

Balanced accuracy **0.70–0.87** across Gemma / Qwen / Llama / Mistral / Phi, vs. **0.28–0.40** unknown-item baseline and 0.25 chance. Robust across sandbagging (prompted, trained, password-locked, circuit-broken): **0.85–0.93**. When knowledge is *unlearned*, recognition drops to the "never knew" level — cleanly separating unlearning verification from refusal.

### Why it matters

Directly relevant to sandbagging audits and unlearning verification — two safety-eval regimes where black-box behavioral cues aren't enough. Signal is causal and extends beyond MCQ to free-form generation.

## 13. The Functionalizer: Lossless Functional Decomposition for Subword Tokenization {#13-functionalizer}

**Authors:** Connor Makowski, Willem Guter — *Independent*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.15991](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15991) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.15991) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.15991)
**Topics:** `LLM`, `tokenization`

### TL;DR

A pre-tokenizer that factors orthographic variations (case, diacritics, repetition) into *reversible* opcode prefixes on a canonical base token, so `hello`, `Hello`, `HELLO`, `Héllo` share one vocabulary entry instead of four. Reduces vocabulary slot usage up to **19.7%** at equal coverage.

### Key idea

Encode "casing", "13 diacritic families", and "character repetition" as parametric transformation operators (opcodes) in the Unicode Private Use Area, applied to a canonical operand token. Fully reversible → lossless. Runs before BPE/Unigram.

### Main finding

Complete corpus coverage with 19.7% smaller vocabularies (unconstrained-exhaustion setting). On 98M-parameter GPT-2 runs: Python syntax validity **9.12%** vs. **7.70%** baseline; lower duplicate-n-gram repetition in prose.

### Why it matters

Tokenizer decisions ripple through everything (multilingual quality, code, embedding sharing). A lossless functional decomposition is a clean way to buy back vocabulary slots without sacrificing coverage — worth watching at production scale.

## 14. GameHorizon Suite: Multi-Horizon Data and Evaluation in Gameplay {#14-gamehorizon}

**Authors:** Yiran Wang, Xingyilang Yin, Junfu Pu, Guangzhi Wang, Kaifeng Li, Mingyu Ouyang, Huiqiang Sun, Lingen Li, Cheng Cheng, Wangbo Yu, Honghao Chen, Xiaodong Cun, Chi-Man Pun, Zhiguo Cao, Ying Shan — *TencentARC / U. Macau (Chi-Man Pun) / HUST (Zhiguo Cao)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.25001](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25001) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.25001) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.25001)
**Topics:** `eval`, `agents`, `multimodal`

### TL;DR

A unified dataset + benchmark for gameplay agents at multiple time horizons: 5,000 hours of expert human play across 21 AAA games with temporally-aligned videos, actions, and multi-horizon language instructions, plus an offline and stepwise-online test bench.

### Key idea

Split evaluation across horizons (short-term reflex, medium-term tactics, long-term strategy) with a scalable auto-annotator producing aligned instructions, then run *both* an offline reproducible test (thousands of standardized questions with diagnostic variants) and a stepwise-online rollout that localizes failures to steps.

### Main finding

47 models evaluated in >1M model invocations, revealing a difficulty hierarchy across horizons and pronounced capability differences between families. Dataset + annotator + benchmark will be released.

### Why it matters

AAA gameplay is one of the few "long-horizon multimodal control" regimes with high-quality expert traces. This yardstick lets you diagnose *which* horizon a model breaks at — much harder to fake than aggregate scores.

## 15. OmniEdu: Open Foundation Models for Learning and Teaching {#15-omniedu}

**Authors:** Hao Liang, Qihan Lin, Meiyi Qiang, Linzhuang Sun, Hengyi Feng, Mingrui Chen, Sizhe Qiu, Wentao Zhang — *PKU (Wentao Zhang)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.23088](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23088) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.23088) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.23088)
**Topics:** `data`, `SFT`, `LLM`

### TL;DR

An open family of K-12 tutoring/teaching foundation models (4B / 9B / 27B) trained on a **capability-balanced** instruction-tuning corpus, organized not by source but by four learned capabilities (subject competence, curriculum grounding, diagnostic reasoning, pedagogical scaffolding).

### Key idea

Reframe SFT-mixture construction as *balancing capabilities* rather than balancing sources. The pipeline layers deterministic cleaning, semantic auditing + rewriting, task-specific quality scoring, token-budgeted diversity selection, and pedagogical instruction assignment — producing 69,999 examples / 15.96M tokens (60,951 education-specific).

### Main finding

**OmniEdu-27B**: K12-Bench 63.12% EM / 76.69% F1; MathFish 85.89; EDUMATH 86.95; MathTutorBench Scaffold 78.74. Highest Teaching-average on LongTutor (3.02) among evaluated models. General capability preserved.

### Why it matters

A clean, reproducible instance of the "capability-balanced" data-mixture playbook that lands in production LLM training. The four-way capability decomposition is a re-usable frame for any domain-specific fine-tune.
