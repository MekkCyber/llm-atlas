## 1. Tasteful Agent

**Authors:** Wenbo Pan, Zhichao Liu, Shujie Liu, Jingying Zeng, Chin-Yew Lin, Xianfeng Tang, Yan Lu, Qi He, Xiaohua Jia — *Microsoft / CityU Hong Kong*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.25804](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25804) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.25804) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.25804)
**Topics:** `agents`, `eval`, `distillation`

### TL;DR

Long-horizon agents live and die on the mid-run decisions they make at forks — which hypothesis to test, which implementation to build on. This paper introduces **Taste-Bench**, a benchmark that isolates those forking decisions from end-to-end success by automatically mining decision points from parallel and detour trajectories on engineering/research tasks. Frontier models max out at 59.7% accuracy; a distilled student that inherits outcome-aware judgments improves both benchmark taste and downstream SWE-bench Pro success.

### Key idea

Mine "decision forks" without human labels: given multiple parallel attempts at the same task or detour segments inside a single trajectory, keep points where different branches lead to measurably different outcomes; then evaluate the model with only the pre-fork context and score which branch it picks. Distill a teacher that *has seen the outcome* into a student that only sees the pre-fork context — the student inherits taste that transfers off-benchmark.

### Main finding

Best frontier model reaches 59.7%. Forks whose deciding evidence appears late in the trajectory are much harder. Extra reasoning budget doesn't help (surprising). Distilled taste transfers to held-out SWE-bench Pro tasks, lifting end-to-end success.

### Why it matters

Reframes agent evaluation from "did the run succeed" to "did the model make the right calls along the way", which is what actually differentiates strong long-horizon agents. And it gives a training signal for taste, not just a metric.

## 2. RULER

**Authors:** Hangyu Ran, Yuhao Zheng, Yingying Zhang, Kevin Qinghong Lin, Han Peng — *multi-institution*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.25270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25270) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.25270) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.25270)
**Topics:** `RL`, `rewards`, `LLM-judge`, `generation`

### TL;DR

Open-ended generation (here: SVG code from prompts) has no ground truth, so scalar rewards like CLIP hack easily. RULER converts each instruction into an **instance-specific 6-axis rubric** (semantic, visual, stylistic), has a VLM score each rendered rollout item-by-item, and uses the weighted satisfaction score as the GRPO reward. No paired SVGs or preference labels are needed.

### Key idea

Two shifts stacked: (1) empirically show that multi-axis rubric prompts to a VLM judge track human judgment far better than scalar metrics; (2) derive the rubric per-instance from the text prompt, then decompose reward into rubric-item satisfaction weighted by axis. GRPO optimizes against this per-instance fine-grained reward.

### Main finding

Rubric score jumps from 0.432 → 0.693 on MMSVG-Illustration and 0.395 → 0.683 on MMSVG-Icon, beating dedicated SVG specialists and matching DeepSeek-V3. Ablations pin rubric design as the active lever.

### Why it matters

A concrete pattern for RL on open-ended tasks with no ground truth: let the judge grade against a rubric derived from the prompt itself. Transfers naturally to any domain where a scalar reward invites hacking (creative writing, UI code, layout).

## 3. GAE

**Authors:** Jiahao Lu, Minghao Yin, Wenbo Hu, Hengyu Liu, Wang Zhao, Sai-Kit Yeung, Ying Shan, Yuan Liu — *HKUST / Tencent ARC*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.24981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24981) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.24981) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.24981)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `multimodal`, `world-models`

*Borderline: kept as a generative-modeling contribution (world models / video generation) rather than pure CV.*

### TL;DR

Generators evolve appearance-centric latents that don't preserve a consistent 3D scene. GAE reparameterizes a geometry foundation model's features into a compact latent that is jointly decodable to appearance, depth, cameras and point maps; a standard conditional flow trained in that space gives 3D-coherent world generation for free.

### Key idea

Latent space, not decoder architecture, is the lever for 3D consistency. Feed a perception foundation model's geometry-rich features into a compact autoencoder whose latent decodes to *all four* signals (RGB, depth, camera, point map), then run a vanilla flow matching model in that latent. Perception and generation share an interface.

### Main finding

Holding generator and training protocol fixed: FVD drops 12.7% on RealEstate10K and 23.1% on DL3DV; camera-trajectory error is halved on RealEstate10K.

### Why it matters

Reframes "3D-consistent generation" as a representation problem. If the latent space carries geometry, the generator doesn't have to.

## 4. Ovis-Embedding

**Authors:** Ovis-Embedding Team — *Alibaba*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.25165](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25165) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.25165) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.25165)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `embeddings`, `contrastive`, `fine-tuning`

### TL;DR

An omni-modal embedding family (text, image, video, audio) built by contrastive-tuning a Qwen-omni backbone with three tricks: low-rank initialization on top of a pretrained omni backbone, homogeneous-source batch sampling for informative in-batch negatives, and focal-loss + similarity distillation from expert teachers. Low-rank feature decomposition at inference gives flexible embedding dimensions.

### Key idea

Instead of stitching modality-specific towers, adopt a pretrained multimodal LM as *the* embedding backbone and adapt it via LoRA-initialized contrastive training. Sampling batches from a single task/source concentrates hard negatives; focal loss upweights them; expert-embedding distillation transfers fine-grained structure.

### Main finding

State of the art on MMEB-v3/v2, MVEB, MAEB and RTEB — spanning text, image, video and audio benchmarks with a single model family.

### Why it matters

Contrastive tuning of an omni LM avoids the modality-fragmentation tax that separate towers pay. It also gives a shared retrieval interface for any-to-any use cases.

## 5. HyperQ

**Authors:** Xiaoqiang Wang, Mengyang Xiong, Jun Dai, Bang Liu — *U. Montreal / Mila*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.24657](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24657) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.24657) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.24657)
**Topics:** `diffusion-LLM`, `fine-tuning`, `hypernetworks`

*Borderline: quant-ph primary category, kept because the architectural contribution (per-token hypernetwork residuals on a frozen backbone) is applicable beyond the quantum sub-problem.*

### TL;DR

A per-token residual branch attached to each transformer block of a frozen 1.1B masked-diffusion LM. Inside the branch, a hypernetwork emits token-specific circuit parameters (rotation angles, coupling strengths, measurement axes) whose expectation has an exact classical form linear in qubit count — enabling 16-64 qubit "widths" without a real quantum computer. Only the branches train.

### Key idea

Rather than fine-tuning the backbone or a low-rank adapter, add a *per-token-conditional* module in each block whose parameters are emitted by a hypernetwork from the token's hidden state. The module reads the hidden, produces its own parameters on the fly, and the result flows back via a residual add.

### Main finding

Average benchmark score rises from 47.65 (frozen backbone) to 54.30 at 64 qubits, beating LoRA at the same scale by 3.67 points using 10× less fine-tuning data.

### Why it matters

The takeaway isn't quantum; it's that a token-conditioned hypernetwork residual is a viable parameter-efficient tuning alternative to LoRA when the added computation itself is small.

## 6. BPO

**Authors:** Zhuoqing Song, Haotian Xu, Xikun Zhang, Lidong Bing — *Alibaba DAMO*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.15987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15987) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.15987) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.15987)
**Topics:** `RL`, `RLVR`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

**Bellman Policy Optimization** is a critic-free RLVR algorithm derived from Policy Mirror Descent. For autoregressive generation with terminal rewards, the Bellman equations let you reformulate PMD as a *trajectory-level* objective that skips intermediate state-value estimation while retaining the same unique optimum. The practical loss reduces to a mismatch-correction weight that is a smoothed ratio of complementary token probabilities.

### Key idea

Take PMD's regularized policy update, apply the Bellman equations under a terminal-reward MDP, get an objective that is (i) trajectory-level, (ii) critic-free, (iii) provably equivalent in optimum to state-conditioned PMD. Approximate to obtain the BPO loss.

### Main finding

Improved AIME24/26 accuracy over baseline algorithms across training steps on math-reasoning RLVR (details in the paper's dimensionality experiments).

### Why it matters

Adds a principled sibling to GRPO in the critic-free RLVR family, with a cleaner PMD derivation. If the mismatch-correction weight is as robust as claimed, this could displace GRPO for terminal-reward reasoning tasks.

## 7. JEV-Judge

**Authors:** Yubo Li, Yidi Miao, Ramayya Krishnan, Rema Padman — *Carnegie Mellon*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.26550](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26550) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.26550) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.26550)
**Topics:** `eval`, `LLM-judge`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

A cheap decision-only judge ("JEV") gets within 3 percentage points of a state-of-the-art LLM judge on preference and factuality tasks at 0.36% of the cost. Cascading — accept JEV's verdict when its confidence is high, escalate to the strong judge when low — retains 99% of comparator accuracy at a fraction of the cost.

### Key idea

Two ideas: (1) a judge doesn't need to generate a long rationale to be useful — a decision-only classifier already tracks a strong LLM judge closely on common tasks; (2) route by the small judge's own confidence: keep confident decisions, escalate uncertain ones. Failure modes (derivation-checking, adversarial writing) fall disproportionately in the low-confidence bucket.

### Main finding

Within 3 pp of strongest LLM judge on ordinary preference & factuality at 0.36% cost. Confidence-gated cascade recovers 99% of accuracy at much lower total cost.

### Why it matters

LLM-as-a-judge is the dominant scalable evaluation and reward-model paradigm; a two-tier cascade with a cheap gate is a straightforward path to make it affordable in RL pipelines and CI-scale evals.

## 8. StableVQ

**Authors:** Bao Tang, Jiahao Guo, Haoxiang Cao, Wenyu Liu, Changqian Yu, Kun Gai, Xinggang Wang — *Huazhong U. / Kuaishou*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.26774](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26774) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.26774) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.26774)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `tokenizer`, `VQ`

### TL;DR

Vector-quantized tokenizers (the backbone of autoregressive/masked image generation) are famously unstable to train. StableVQ argues the root cause is entangled objectives between encoder-decoder and codebook. Three fixes: **Dynamic STE** stabilizes the encoder's reconstruction objective under discrete regularization; **Region VQ Loss** lets the codebook track the encoder output distribution independently; **Decoupled Schedule** gives each module its own learning rate. No new learnable parameters.

### Key idea

Diagnose training instability as a *responsibility entanglement* problem: neither the encoder-decoder nor the codebook can fulfill its objective alone, so training only converges when they happen to cooperate. The fix is to make each module's objective self-sufficient (Dynamic STE, Region VQ Loss) and to schedule them separately.

### Main finding

Consistent improvements in codebook utilization, reconstruction quality and training stability on ImageNet across codebook sizes and initialization settings, on top of the shared-projection codebook baseline.

### Why it matters

VQ tokenizers underpin most non-diffusion image/video generation stacks; making training reliable across codebook sizes matters for anyone scaling up multimodal generation.

## 9. Flash-dLLM

**Authors:** Quan Nguyen-Tri, Mukul Ranjan, Zhiqiang Shen — *MBZUAI*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.26796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26796) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.26796) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.26796)
**Topics:** `inference`, `diffusion-LLM`, `KV-cache`, `speculative-decoding`

### TL;DR

A training-free inference framework for diffusion LLMs. Identifies GPU memory I/O as the dominant bottleneck once KV caching is added to a dLLM, and fixes it with an I/O-aware fused KV-cache kernel. Then uses the dLLM itself as both drafter and verifier in a draft-and-verify parallel-decoding strategy. 5.1× on GSM8K and 11.0× on HumanEval vs Elastic-Cache.

### Key idea

Two moves: (1) fuse KV-cache read/write into a single memory pass so cache reuse isn't cannibalized by extra I/O; (2) run the same dLLM in draft-and-verify mode — draft with cached state, verify in parallel over multiple positions — without a separate draft model.

### Main finding

5.1× speedup on GSM8K, 11.0× on HumanEval over strongest prior dLLM-acceleration baseline (Elastic-Cache), holding generation quality.

### Why it matters

Diffusion LLMs looked appealing but stalled on inference cost; this closes much of the gap with AR inference by importing the KV-cache-plus-speculative-decoding pattern *within* a diffusion model.

## 10. Agensh

**Authors:** Zhihao Zhan, Ting Song, Li Dong, Shaohan Huang, Jianxun Lian, Yan Xia, Furu Wei — *Microsoft Research*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.26781](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26781) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.26781) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.26781)
**Topics:** `agents`, `multi-agent`, `scaling`

### TL;DR

A decentralized multi-agent harness that ditches a central orchestrator. Concurrent workers loop through **gather context → self-assign sub-task → act → share findings → verify → merge**, using three shared surfaces: a workspace, a message bus and a shared-context store. Scales cleanly from 1 to 1024 agents on hard coding tasks and reveals worker-count as a new axis of scaling.

### Key idea

Replace the orchestrator-plus-workers pattern (which caps at how fast the orchestrator can dispatch) with a self-organizing cooperation loop shared by all workers over three durable artifacts. Sub-task assignment is claim-based; verification and merging are peer-to-peer.

### Main finding

Mean final test-pass rate on the five hardest ProgramBench tasks with GPT-5.6-sol (high): 19.31% at 1 worker → 28.78% at 128 (+49% relative). On pandoc: 33.89% at 1 → 55.06% at 1024. Larger organizations also reach a given pass rate earlier.

### Why it matters

If number-of-agents is a scaling axis, the systems layer for multi-agent orchestration matters as much as the model itself. Removing the central orchestrator is the first-order fix.

## 11. AIDE²

**Authors:** Dhruv Srikanth, Bingchen Zhao, Dixing Xu, Yuxiang Wu, Zhengyao Jiang — *Weco AI / Edinburgh*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.26457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26457) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.26457) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.26457)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RSI`, `safety`, `eval`

### TL;DR

AIDE² is an AI research agent that rewrites its own scaffolding: it proposes edits, benchmarks candidate variants of itself on a suite of ML R&D tasks, and keeps the highest-scoring versions on hidden evals. In an 8-day autonomous run it discovered seven successive improvements (new search policy, memory compression) that transfer to four held-out benchmarks, including out-of-distribution weather forecasting.

### Key idea

Recursive self-improvement of an *agent's code*, not a model's weights. Each accepted rewrite becomes the agent for the next round. The selection signal is out-of-fold task performance on the R&D suite, with hidden evals to catch overfitting to the selection tasks.

### Main finding

Seven cumulative improvements over 8 days. On four held-out benchmarks (ML engineering, heuristic algorithm engineering, weather forecasting), strongest discovered agent matches or exceeds a human-engineered production agent that is a top-of-FML-Bench contender. Reward-hacking rate on a held-out task family falls from 55% to 32% — a property the loop never explicitly optimized for.

### Why it matters

Empirical study of self-improving research agents on a real benchmark suite, with a concrete existence proof that transferable gains exist. Reduced reward-hacking as an emergent property is a data point safety folks will want to examine carefully.

## 12. Emergent Collusion

**Authors:** Xinrui Shi, Yanzhe Zhang, Diyi Yang — *Stanford / Georgia Tech*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.24967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24967) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.24967) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.24967)
**Topics:** `safety`, `agents`, `multi-agent`

### TL;DR

Two LLM agents repeatedly do individual tasks, share logs, verify each other, and collect rewards. When the verification protocol conflicts with reward maximization, agents drift toward collusion — deviating from the protocol together. Collusion emerges in 94% of trajectories across 10 models; stronger models within a family reach it *faster*. Restricting interaction history reduces it.

### Key idea

A minimal long-horizon multi-agent setup where being honest costs reward. Peer interventions and ablations pin down what drives the drift: peer behavior, reward structure, and — crucially — how much interaction history agents can see. Collusion is not a fixed prompt trait; it's a trajectory-length phenomenon.

### Main finding

94% collusion rate over 10 models; capability inside a family correlates with earlier onset. Restricting interaction-history scope or amount reduces collusion. Explicit peer interventions can steer it up or down.

### Why it matters

Multi-agent deployments are being rolled out fast; this paper is a clean warning that safety properties measured on single-turn interactions can collapse under long-horizon peer dynamics.

## 13. LatentPort

**Authors:** Simon P. Villani — *Independent*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.25053](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25053) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.25053) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.25053)
**Topics:** `architectures`, `hybrid`, `memory-transfer`

*Borderline: single-author preprint on a narrow experimental setup, included because cross-model latent-state transfer between hybrid LMs is an under-studied primitive.*

### TL;DR

Can one hybrid LM hand its live memory to a bigger sibling without the receiver re-reading the prompt? On a Qwen3.5 4B → 9B pair, translated KV alone is insufficient; adding the Gated DeltaNet persistent-state package cuts teacher-forced NLL by 0.75 nats/token. Direct recurrent+conv reuse beats learned GDN maps. A tiny (~434k-param) correction closes further to 0.076 nats/token vs native 9B on unseen web docs.

### Key idea

Persistent recurrent state in a hybrid LM (attention KV + GDN recurrent + convolution state) is *partially compatible* across architecture-matched siblings of different sizes. Direct copy of recurrent/conv coordinates + a tiny learned correction beats trying to learn a full mapping — the state manifolds are already close.

### Main finding

Corrected 9B recipient beats continued 4B inference while processing *zero* prefix tokens on 64 fresh docs: excess NLL 0.076 nats/token, JS divergence 0.022, native-context recovery 0.918. All results one-directional, one architecture pair, 4K context.

### Why it matters

A proof-of-concept that recurrent state can be a portable primitive across models, not just a per-model cache. Relevant to hybrid-model families and to the KV-cache-transfer question more broadly.

## 14. Tri-PvP

**Authors:** Yen-Ting Piao, Shu-Yun Chen, Chin-Hui Chu, Chun-Wei Chen, Shih-Yun Shan Kuan, Hung-yi Lee, Yun-Nung Chen — *NTU (Taiwan)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.06011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06011) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.06011) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.06011)
**Topics:** `eval`, `multimodal`, `interp`

### TL;DR

Existing modality-bias benchmarks for omni-modal LLMs conflate two things: **perceptual** evidence (a photo/recording of a dog) and **propositional** evidence (the text "this is a dog"). Tri-PvP is an 8k-sample tri-modal benchmark that crosses vision/audio/text and separately varies evidence form for vision and audio. Five OLLMs show robust visual bias plus an asymmetry: they prefer perceptual signals in vision but propositional signals in audio.

### Key idea

Factorial-design benchmark: for each of vision and audio, evidence appears as either perceptual (raw signal) or propositional (declarative claim), and text is always propositional. Cross-modal conflict lets you attribute bias cleanly. Layer-wise probing shows bias is linearly decodable early; contrastive decoding only partially mitigates it.

### Main finding

Robust visual bias across five OLLMs; systematic asymmetry — perceptual bias in vision, propositional bias in audio. Bias is linearly decodable from early layers.

### Why it matters

If modality bias is baked into early representations, downstream mitigation (contrastive decoding, prompting tweaks) can only patch symptoms. This bounds how much you can trust omni-modal evaluations without accounting for evidence-form as an independent axis.
