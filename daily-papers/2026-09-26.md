## 1. Rufus-Air: An Open LLM Post-Training Recipe {#1-rufus-air}

**Authors:** Chia-Yuan Chang, Renyuan Cheng, Rui Feng, Xiaotian Han, Yuan He, Hongye Jin, Linwei Li, Shiyang Li, Fenglin Liu, Xin Liu, Priyanka Nigam, Haoyang Wen, Zhenghao Xu, Zhuocheng Xu, Bing Yin, Qingyu Yin, Chao Zhang, Rongzhi Zhang, Zhihan Zhang, Zixuan Zhang, Tuo Zhao — *Amazon*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.29421](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29421) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.29421) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.29421)
**Topics:** `LLM`, `post-training`, `RL`, `agents`

### TL;DR

Amazon's open, end-to-end post-training pipeline that turns the open-weight GLM-4.5-Air-Base (106B total / 12B active MoE) into an aligned assistant with agentic skills, using only public data and open components — no proprietary teacher distillation, no fresh human annotation.

### Key idea

A strictly serial eight-stage pipeline that layers capabilities in a fixed order: SFT → Reasoning RL → Coding RL → Instruction-Following RL → General Agent → Coding Agent → Search Agent → RLHF. Each stage's checkpoint feeds the next, letting the authors ablate every stage's contribution and package the full recipe (data, prompts, rewards) as reproducible open source.

### Main finding

The full pipeline lifts the GLM-4.5-Air-Base checkpoint to competitive open-model scores across reasoning, coding, IF, and agentic search benchmarks without proprietary distillation. Each stage delivers a measurable additive gain (per-stage ablations in the paper); the search-agent stage is the biggest contributor on multi-turn browsing tasks.

### Why it matters

Reproducibility is the story: unlike DeepSeek-R1 or Qwen recipes that quietly rely on strong closed teachers or unreleased data, Rufus-Air commits to *open ingredients only*, giving the community a bit-for-bit runnable baseline for LLM post-training at 100B-A12B scale. It also codifies the "serial capability stack" pattern that Kimi and DeepSeek use implicitly.

## 2. Your Transformer Can Hold Two Thoughts at Once: Evidence of Linear Superposition in LLMs {#2-superposition-linearity}

**Authors:** Pavel Tikhonov, Anton Korznikov, Matvey Mikhalchuk, Nikita Dragunov, Temurbek Rahmatullaev, Polina Druzhinina, Anton Razzhigaev, Ivan Oseledets, Elena Tutubalina — *AIRI / Skoltech (unstated on HF)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.29845](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29845) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.29845) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.29845)
**Topics:** `interp`, `architectures`, `LLM`

### TL;DR

Linear combinations of two prompts, when fed to a Transformer, produce next-token distributions that are near-averages of the individual prompts' distributions. The authors call this the **Superposition Linearity Hypothesis** and use it to decode two coherent streams from one forward pass.

### Key idea

Feed the model `α·h(x₁) + (1-α)·h(x₂)` in embedding space and compare the resulting logits to `α·p(x₁) + (1-α)·p(x₂)`. Linearity is *higher at initialization* and *decays during pretraining* — but a light fine-tune restores it. The trick then: **guided decoding** disentangles the two streams by projecting each token step against one of the source distributions, generating both continuations in parallel from a single forward.

### Main finding

Pretraining reduces cross-prompt linearity (measured as KL between actual and averaged distributions); short fine-tuning recovers it. On the recovered model, guided decoding produces two coherent, semantically distinct continuations from one forward pass — effectively a 2× throughput trick, though quality trails standard sampling on hard prompts.

### Why it matters

Reframes a decade-old interpretability result (linear features in embedding space) as an *architectural* property, not just an emergent one. Practically, it hints at a new axis for efficient inference — packing independent requests into a single forward — and gives interp researchers a training-dynamics signal to track.

## 3. WanPE: Cinematic Prompt Enhancement for Modern Text-to-Video Generation {#3-wanpe}

**Authors:** Yubo Zhu et al. (30 authors) — *Alibaba Wan team*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.30221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30221) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.30221) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.30221)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `video`, `prompting`, `RL`

### TL;DR

A 397B-parameter *prompt enhancer* that rewrites user prompts into director-level cinematic screenplays before they hit Alibaba's Wan3.0 video generator, trained on 1.05M real videos with a new GRPO variant that keeps rewrites faithful to the original intent.

### Key idea

Two moves: (a) **video-grounded reverse construction** — start from a real video, extract per-shot action/camera/lighting/sound plans, and train the enhancer to *reconstruct* those plans from a short user prompt (reverse rather than forward rewriting); (b) **Semantic-Consistency GRPO (SC-GRPO)** — a GRPO variant whose reward penalises drift from the user's stated intent while rewarding cinematic richness across shots.

### Main finding

Powering Wan3.0, WanPE-397B raises human preference over raw user prompts by **10.66–18.84 points at 5–15 s** and by **50.86 points at 30 s**; reverse construction beats forward rewriting in ablations; the enhancer leads all evaluated commercial offerings at 5–15 s and is competitive with Seedance 2.5 at 30 s.

### Why it matters

Confirms the emerging pattern that in long-horizon generative pipelines, most of the "prompt engineering" burden shifts to a *dedicated* LLM sitting upstream of the generator, and shows that faithfulness — not just richness — has to be an explicit reward signal.

## 4. Agent-Editing World Model: Rethinking World Modeling for LLM Agents (AEWM) {#4-aewm}

**Authors:** Shuang Sun, Guoxin Chen, Fanzhe Meng, Jia Deng, Huatong Song, Jinhao Jiang, Wayne Xin Zhao, Hongteng Xu, Ji-Rong Wen — *Renmin University of China*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.28416](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28416) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.28416) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.28416)
**Topics:** `agents`, `LLM`, `world-model`

### TL;DR

Instead of predicting future *observations* (as classical world models do), AEWM keeps a lightweight editable summary of the agent's state, adds an **Action Judge** to filter unsafe/unsupported actions, and a **State Revision** module to scrub outdated assumptions from the history — attacking the "task-state contamination" that degrades long-horizon LLM agents.

### Key idea

Reframe world modeling as *state hygiene*: don't reconstruct high-entropy tool responses that will be observed anyway; instead, actively curate the running belief. Action Judge scores candidate actions against the current state; State Revision rewrites the history to drop assumptions that later observations contradicted.

### Main finding

Notable improvements over ReAct-style baselines across several long-horizon benchmarks (exact numbers in the paper), driven mostly by fewer looping / stale-assumption failures.

### Why it matters

A concrete alternative to observation-predicting world models for LLM agents that clarifies *what* an LLM agent's world model should actually store — belief state, not sensory prediction.

## 5. Parts-of-Speech as Emergent Categories in SAE Latent Space {#5-sae-pos}

**Authors:** Alessandro Bondielli, Lucia Passaro, Serena Auriemma, Alessandro Lenci — *University of Pisa (CoLingLab)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.29362](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29362) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.29362) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.29362)
**Topics:** `interp`, `SAE`, `LLM`

### TL;DR

Uses part-of-speech tagging as a controlled probe of Sparse Autoencoder (SAE) latents on an LLM: POS is highly recoverable, but never sits on a single latent — it's carried by small, stable *groups* of features, with open- and closed-class words behaving very differently.

### Key idea

Train linear probes on top of SAE activations to classify POS, then measure which latents drive each category. Add held-out and cross-category checks to distinguish "SAE feature encodes POS" from "SAE feature memorises lexemes".

### Main finding

POS is decoded with high accuracy from SAE latents but the mapping is **distributed and category-specific** — 5–20 latent group members per POS, with overlap between related tags (e.g. Determiner ↔ Article). Open classes (Noun, Verb) recruit more latents than closed classes (Determiner, Preposition).

### Why it matters

Undercuts a common informal claim that SAEs give "one feature per concept": for a canonical linguistic category, the mapping is polyfeatural. Sets a concrete probe methodology other researchers can reuse for morphology, syntax, or semantics.

## 6. Qwen-Planner-Agent: Closed-Loop AI-for-AI Framework for Mobile Planners {#6-qwen-planner-agent}

**Authors:** Tingyu Qu, Weigao Sun, Yuecheng Liu et al. (26 authors) — *Alibaba Qwen team*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.29892](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29892) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.29892) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.29892)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `LLM`

### TL;DR

An end-to-end recipe for training mobile-planner agents: an "agentic flywheel" that self-generates trajectory data, an SFT+RL training loop dubbed **CARE**, and a runtime that keeps model and tool inventory co-evolving.

### Key idea

Three layers as one loop: (1) data — a flywheel where deployed agents keep producing new trajectories and self-critiques; (2) training — CARE = *Cascaded Advantage-Reweighted Estimation* (SFT then RL, RL advantages reweighted by whether the SFT model would already have solved the case); (3) runtime — tool schemas and the underlying planner update together, so tool additions don't need a full retrain.

### Main finding

Best-in-class results on mobile-planning benchmarks among evaluated models without regressing general capability — the paper emphasises stability of the co-evolution loop rather than a single new algorithm.

### Why it matters

A concrete blueprint for the "keep the agent shipping" problem: not one training run, but a continuous pipeline. Also the first Qwen-family paper to open-source a full agent-planner training loop as a system.

## 7. IterSynth: Role-Decoupled Iterative Synthesis (RDPO) {#7-itersynth}

**Authors:** Xingyu Wu, Yuchen Yan, Zhengxi Lu, Siqi Chen, Xin Zhang, Aiting Liu, Chao Deng, Jie Liu, Jin Ma, Jian Shao, Jun Xiao, Yongliang Shen — *Zhejiang U.*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.29444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29444) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.29444) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.29444)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

For deep-search agents, splits the classical ReAct loop into two roles — a **Planner** that decides what to search next and a **Synthesizer** that folds new evidence into an evolving summary — and trains them with **Role-Decoupled Policy Optimization (RDPO)**, an RL objective that gives each role its own advantage signal.

### Key idea

Two changes at once: (1) architecturally, replace "one policy for planning + evidence use + synthesis" with two specialised policies and keep the running summary as the sole persistent state (no growing scratchpad); (2) training-side, RDPO computes role-specific advantages by combining a terminal outcome reward with turn-level rubric scores, so the Planner is credited for information gain and the Synthesizer for evidence integration.

### Main finding

IterSynth-8B scores **50.7 avg** across five long-horizon deep-search benchmarks (BrowseComp, Xbench-DS, …), beating the strongest prior ≤8B agent by **+4.2**. It also works zero-shot as a *prompting paradigm* on frontier proprietary models, outperforming ReAct.

### Why it matters

Elevates the "roles vs. one megalithic policy" question from prompting folklore to a full RL objective, and shows that role-specific credit assignment is worth the extra machinery on long-horizon tasks.

## 8. ExplorationBench: Verifiable Alien Worlds {#8-explorationbench}

**Authors:** Ming Zhang, Zhenghao Xiang et al. (20 authors) — *Fudan-led consortium*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.30199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30199) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.30199) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.30199)
**Topics:** `eval`, `agents`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

A benchmark that drops LLM agents into small, executable "alien world" sandboxes with unfamiliar rules, and measures whether they can *frame hypotheses, design experiments, and iterate* — not just recall known solutions.

### Key idea

Every world has fully executable ground-truth rules, so any answer can be checked exactly. Two sandbox families and 141 total tasks tile the space between "guess the rule from observations" and "apply an inferred rule to solve a downstream problem". Metrics separate *exploration* (finding the rule) from *exploitation* (using it).

### Main finding

Frontier models are much closer on exploitation than on exploration; even top models fail to acquire novel rules from limited observations in a meaningful fraction of tasks. Exploration performance correlates weakly with standard reasoning benchmarks — this is measuring something else.

### Why it matters

Fills a real gap in agent eval: most benchmarks assume the rules of the environment are already knowable from training data. ExplorationBench isolates the *scientific* mode agents need for open-ended discovery.

## 9. Learning to Discover Interesting Mathematics {#9-interesting-math}

**Authors:** Niket Patel, Ahmad Rammal, Amaury Hayat, Rémi Munos, Julia Kempe — *Meta / NYU / École des Ponts*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.28603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28603) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.28603) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.28603)
**Topics:** `reasoning`, `eval`, `math`

### TL;DR

Defines a theorem's **intrinsic interestingness** as `proof-length / statement-length`, shows it correlates with downstream usefulness in Mathlib, and trains a 27B model that predicts proof difficulty better than frontier general models — enough to steer conjecture generation towards genuinely novel math.

### Key idea

The core primitive is a *proof-difficulty predictor* conditioned on a set of premises. From it you can compute the interestingness ratio, rank candidate theorems, and use the ranking as an inner-loop reward for a conjecture-and-prove system that iteratively grows a machine-verified library.

### Main finding

Optimising for the metric reduces Mathlib-overlap from **91.9% → 30.6%**, i.e. produces theorems substantially outside the training distribution while keeping formal verifiability. The 27B difficulty predictor beats frontier general models on proof-difficulty ranking.

### Why it matters

Practical answer to "so LLMs can prove theorems — what should they prove?". Frames automated math as a search problem where the *selection* signal is the bottleneck, not the prover.

## 10. AgentKernel: The Trust-Native Agentic OS {#10-agentkernel}

**Authors:** Zhenhua Zou, Sheng Guo, Qiuyang Zhan, Lepeng Zhao, Shuo Li, Zhuotao Liu — *(affiliations unstated)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.29647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29647) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.29647) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.29647)
**Topics:** `safety`, `agents`

### TL;DR

A proposal to move agent security from the app layer to an OS-like substrate that enforces separation across four pillars — *Identity, Perception, Cognition, Execution* — so prompt injection, memory poisoning, and tool misuse become kernel-level violations rather than model-level surprises.

### Key idea

Treat every agent action as an OS syscall: caller identity, information-flow labels, cognition-time policy, and execution-time capability tokens are all mediated by a kernel that sits *beneath* orchestration frameworks. The four-pillar decomposition mirrors classical OS security (subjects/objects/policies/enforcement) but adapted to agentic pipelines.

### Main finding

Position paper more than benchmark: the contribution is a coherent architecture and a mapping from known agent vulnerabilities (prompt injection, memory poisoning, tool misuse) to which pillar catches them. Implementation is prototyped over popular orchestration frameworks.

### Why it matters

Formalises the emerging consensus that "agent safety" cannot live inside a single model or a single prompt — the trust boundaries are systemic. A useful vocabulary for the safety-of-agents subfield.

## 11. Just Ask Jev: RL for Calibrated Decisions as a Zero-Shot Alignment-Failure Detector {#11-jev-rlcd}

**Authors:** Ruoqi Guo, Yi Liu, Gelei Deng, Yuekang Li, Lida Zhao, Yutao Wu, Simin Chen, Ying Zhang, Leo Yu Zhang — *UNSW / NTU / Griffith*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.29429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29429) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.29429) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.29429)
**Topics:** `safety`, `eval`, `RL`

### TL;DR

Jev is a small guard model trained with **Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions (RLCD)** that answers *many* typed yes/no questions about a single input in one forward pass with calibrated probabilities — replacing the "one criterion, one generative-judge call" pattern.

### Key idea

Cast alignment-failure detection as multi-question calibration: the model is trained to output well-calibrated probabilities on a typed schema of failure questions (deception, refusal-avoidance, tool-misuse, etc.). RLCD's reward signal is the calibration/Brier loss on ground-truth labels rather than a preference-pair signal.

### Main finding

Jev matches generative-judge accuracy on standard safety benchmarks while running at classifier-like cost (a fraction of a decoded token per criterion), and beats fixed-label classifiers like Llama Guard because it can answer many questions per call.

### Why it matters

Generative judges dominate current alignment eval infra but are expensive. RLCD gives a plausibly reliable, cheap alternative and reframes the classifier-vs-judge dichotomy as a *training-signal* question rather than an architectural one.

## 12. AV-GRPO: Modality-Anchored Decoupling Diffusion RL for Audio-Video Generation {#12-av-grpo}

**Authors:** Zhiyu Xu, Weilong Yan, Yufei Shi, Shiyang Li, Yihao Liu, Kin-Man Lam, Yuewen Cao — *PolyU / consortium*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.29816](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29816) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.29816) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.29816)
**Topics:** `RL`, `diffusion`, `multimodal`

### TL;DR

Applies GRPO-style RL to a joint audio-video diffusion model by **decoupling** the multimodal preference signal into modality-anchored unimodal subproblems, plus a small dataset (5DAV) to make the training tractable.

### Key idea

Multimodal RL post-training with a single joint reward is unstable because gradients mix cross-modal and within-modal quality. AV-GRPO factors the preference into (a) audio-anchored, (b) video-anchored, and (c) sync sub-rewards, and runs GRPO independently on each while sharing the policy. 5DAV is a dedicated preference set with disentangled labels.

### Main finding

Beats LTX-2.3 on per-modality fidelity, text alignment, and cross-modal sync; ablations show the modality decoupling is what stabilises the RL run — a joint objective diverges.

### Why it matters

Confirms that RL post-training will need *modality-aware* reward shaping to scale beyond text; a template for how future omni-modal generators (audio+video+text) will incorporate preferences.

## 13. ViRDM: Representation Distribution Matching for Few-Step Causal Video Generation {#13-virdm}

**Authors:** Zichong Meng, Chongjian Ge, Chun-Hao P. Huang, Yang Zhou, Huaizu Jiang — *Northeastern / Adobe*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.28923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28923) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.28923) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.28923)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `video`, `distillation`

### TL;DR

A few-step distillation method for causal (autoregressive) video diffusion that drops the classic Distribution Matching Distillation setup (large teacher + online critic) and matches student and reference distributions in **representation space** directly.

### Key idea

Reformulate the matching signal from diffusion scores to *feature-space* moments of a frozen encoder, letting a small student learn without an online critic. Three tricks make it work for video: memory-efficient windowed matching (fits in GPU RAM), a time-adjusted moment estimator (video training curves differ from images), and a temporal-consistency regulariser to constrain motion.

### Main finding

Reaches **VBench 84.87** with only **20 generator updates** — competitive with prior few-step video baselines while removing the teacher-and-critic overhead.

### Why it matters

Cheaper path to low-latency streaming video generation; the "representation distribution matching without a critic" idea might generalise to other few-step distillation settings (audio, 3D).

## 14. OmniEcho: Audio-Visual Spatial Understanding for Omni-Modal Embodied Agents {#14-omniecho}

**Authors:** Ruixun Liu, Yuxuan Wang, Jiacheng Xie, Yuhuan You, Donghua Cai, Junming Lin, Xiong-Hui Chen, Zhifang Guo, Yunfei Chu, Qize Yang, Xize Cheng, Jin Xu, Yiwu Zhong — *Alibaba Qwen team / consortium*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.23407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23407) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.23407) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.23407)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `agents`, `eval`

### TL;DR

An FOA (first-order ambisonics) spatial audio encoder wired alongside a pretrained semantic audio pathway lets an omni-modal LLM localise sound sources and reason about them jointly with vision. Comes with OmniEchoBench, 197-scene / 6-task benchmark.

### Key idea

Two-path audio front-end: (1) a *semantic* branch reusing an existing audio-LM encoder for content, (2) a *spatial* branch trained specifically on FOA signals for direction-of-arrival. The two are fused before the LLM sees them, so the model can answer both "what sound?" and "where did it come from?" in one context.

### Main finding

Strong results on the new spatial audio-visual perception benchmark and on sound-guided navigation; exact numbers depend on task, but the two-path front-end consistently beats single-encoder baselines.

### Why it matters

Adds a spatial modality that most audio-LMs ignore, giving embodied agents a plausible way to use ears the way humans do. Also a rare non-vision push in multimodal LLMs.

## 15. Training Object Permanence in World Models (WROP) {#15-wrop}

**Authors:** Haotian Zhang, Fengyuan Yu, Dezhi Luo, ... (31 authors incl. Yilun Du, Hokin Deng, Alan Yuille) — *consortium*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.28654](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28654) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.28654) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.28654)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `world-model`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Asks whether video generation models — increasingly used as world models — encode **object permanence**. Answer: not by default. Introduces WROP, a suite of 150 cognitive-science-inspired probing tasks in six categories, and shows targeted training closes much of the gap.

### Key idea

Borrow paradigms from developmental psychology (violation-of-expectation, occlusion, containment) as *tasks* rather than probes: the world model is asked to complete videos in ways that require tracking hidden objects. Score consistency of the completion against the physically correct answer.

### Main finding

Off-the-shelf video generators fail the majority of WROP tasks (in particular any involving occlusion). Fine-tuning on WROP data recovers much of the deficit, indicating the failure is a data/training-signal problem, not an architectural one. *Borderline: included because "world model" here explicitly means a video-generation foundation model.*

### Why it matters

Turns "does the model understand physics?" from a rhetorical question into a benchmark. Also relevant to safety/interp — a video model that violates object permanence is one an agent shouldn't rely on for planning.
