## 1. YuE2

**Authors:** Ruibin Yuan, Jiahao Pan, Junyan Jiang, Zhiyue Wu, Ziya Zhou, Jiankai Sun, Yizhi Li, Ge Zhang, Yicheng Gu, … Wei Xue, Yike Guo — *Multi-org open-source consortium (HKUST / M-A-P / Skywork adjacent)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.33757](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33757) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33757) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.33757)
**Topics:** `audio-LLM`, `MoE`, `generative`

### TL;DR

Successor to the YuE full-song music generator. YuE2 folds symbolic score composition and audio waveform synthesis into a single AR-NAR **Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)** stack: the model first writes a human-readable score, expands it into semantic music tokens, and finally realizes it as full-song audio, all inside one policy. Positioned as the first open frontier system to match closed commercial music generators end-to-end.

### Key idea

An AR-NAR MoT where different expert transformers own different modalities (symbolic score, semantic music tokens, acoustic tokens) but share attention across a single sequence. Symbolic planning is treated as a first-class hidden CoT for audio generation — the score is not a preprocessing step but a conditioning stage inside the same forward path.

### Main finding

Trained across the full symbolic→semantic→acoustic pipeline; the authors report frontier-quality full-song outputs (their HF community upvote count is ~10k, an outlier for the daily list). Numeric benchmark deltas against the closed commercial baselines are in the paper body.

### Why it matters

Music has been the last major generative modality where open-source severely trailed closed systems. Unifying symbolic composition with audio synthesis in one policy is the same "explicit plan, then render" pattern that pushed image generators past pure-diffusion baselines — expect this to become the recipe for open frontier audio.

## 2. MassAlloc Attention

**Authors:** Jingze Shi, Zhangyang Peng, Xianduo Li, Yanlin Qi, Xiaotian Lin, Haoxian Chen, Liangdong Wang, Guang Liu, Yuyu Luo — *(institutional affiliation not disclosed on the arXiv page)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.32712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32712) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.32712) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.32712)
**Topics:** `attention`, `efficient-inference`, `long-context`

### TL;DR

MALA (Mass Allocation Attention) is a fused attention primitive that keeps the full causal score matrix but only performs the *post-score* work on the fraction of interactions carrying non-negligible normalized mass. The online-softmax normalizer built during the forward pass drives a tolerance-based retention decision, and the same finalized normalizer is reused in the backward pass — no auxiliary index, sketch, or trainable router.

### Key idea

Recognize that FullAttn already computes the normalizer that says which QK interactions matter; use it in-flight to decide which value projections and dV contributions to compute. Retention is governed by a single scalar tolerance shared across training and inference, giving distribution-adaptive sparsity without changing model semantics.

### Main finding

At 128K tokens with tensor parallelism, MALA cuts training forward/backward attention latency by **2.2×/3.0×** and decoding latency by **1.6×** vs FullAttn. In matched-work 8K experiments the omitted-mass fraction is 0.0188% (vs a 0.0182% oracle). Scaling-law runs from 0.6B → 14B parameters track FullAttn perplexity while burning fewer training FLOPs.

### Why it matters

Existing sparse-attention families (sliding window, top-k, block-sparse) trade correctness for speed via a *fixed* rule chosen before you know the distribution. MALA turns that rule into a *learned-at-inference* quantity read off the softmax itself, which is exactly the missing link between "correct dense attention" and "cheap sparse attention."

## 3. TaH2

**Authors:** Yichen You, Tianyu Fu, Aosong Feng, Xingtai Lv, Xuefei Ning, Ning Ding, Yu Wang — *THU-NICS (Tsinghua) / Yale*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.35748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35748) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35748) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.35748)
**Topics:** `reasoning`, `test-time-compute`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

Looped transformers reuse the same layer stack for several latent iterations per token, but existing recipes waste iterations on tokens that would not benefit. TaH2 post-trains the backbone jointly with a lightweight per-token **iteration decider**, supervised by *lookahead* labels indicating whether one more loop would change the prediction. Extra depth is spent only where it earns a better answer.

### Key idea

Per-token adaptive loop depth trained by lookahead supervision. During training, a probe runs one extra iteration and compares predictions; the resulting binary label teaches the decider when looping helps. At inference, this becomes a cheap head that gates further iterations without any external verifier.

### Main finding

On AIME, TaH2 improves the accuracy-vs-decoding-FLOP slope from **1.79 → 2.74 (+53%)** over a non-looped baseline, and beats the baseline's peak accuracy by **~3.4 points** at matched compute. The gap over the non-looped baseline grows monotonically with the maximum loop depth (from +2.8 at depth 2 to +3.9 at depth 8), while fixed-depth looped models plateau.

### Why it matters

Test-time compute scaling has largely meant "sample more" (best-of-N, tree search) or "think longer" (long CoT). TaH2 shows a third axis — *depth per token* — that's orthogonal to both and doesn't require sampling budget or an external verifier. Adaptive looping also gives a cleaner accuracy-compute curve than fixed loops, which matters for anytime inference.

## 4. HexaAnything

**Authors:** Hongcheng Gao, Jingjing Zhou, Zelin Zheng, Shijia Ge, Jay Zhu, Yazhe Wang, Jianshu Zeng, Xuan Shangguan, Di Wu, Lingyu He, Zhiqi Jia, Sihang Wu, Xiao He — *hexafuture.ai*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.35432](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35432) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35432) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.35432)
**Topics:** `agents`, `VLA`, `tool-use`

*Borderline: kept because the paper reframes physical agents as an LLM-style coding-agent loop, which is directly relevant to how agent policies are trained.*

### TL;DR

Instead of a monolithic vision-language-action (VLA) policy that maps pixels to joint torques, HexaAnything treats a physical task as a code trace: an LLM-style coding agent maintains explicit state ("code as world"), plans and revises procedures ("code as policy"), and calls VLA/WAM sub-policies as tools. Verified traces feed back as training data for a distilled inner model (HexaModel).

### Key idea

**Physical Coding.** Represent object state, relations, constraints, and progress in code; have an LLM policy call perception, planning, and control tools (VLAs included) as functions; keep every successful trace as reusable data. Errors show up as ordinary exceptions and get patched, and the outer coding policy periodically distills its behavior into a smaller inner model.

### Main finding

On RoboCasa365, HexaAnything beats the XR-1 VLA baseline on Composite-Unseen and overall success; the distilled HexaModel then beats the base VLA on every split. On PhyBench and a dual-arm AgileX robot, the agent autonomously completes physics experiments and most tabletop tasks, often faster than the published prior results.

### Why it matters

VLAs generalize badly because their policy is implicit in action sequences. Wrapping them in a coding-agent loop makes state and progress inspectable, which turns "gather more trajectories" into "collect verified programs" — the same shift that made code-agent RL productive for LLMs. Also validates the tool-use → self-distillation loop in a physical setting.

## 5. DRM

**Authors:** Xiangyang Wang, Bingxiang He, Zeyuan Liu, Jiaze Wang, Ziqing Qiao, Yuxin Zuo, Huan-ang Gao, Cheng Qian, Wenbin Zhang, Ran Li, Youbang Sun, Ning Ding, Yuanchun Shi, Zhiyuan Liu, Chaojun Xiao, Chun Yu — *Tsinghua (THUNLP-adjacent)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.33803](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33803) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33803) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.33803)
**Topics:** `RL`, `reward-modeling`, `diffusion`

### TL;DR

Standard reward models emit either a scalar or a fixed parametric distribution over rewards. DRM (Diffusion Reward Model) recasts reward modeling as **conditional density estimation** over `p(r | x, y)` using a lightweight Diffusion Transformer that denoises Gaussian noise into a reward vector, conditioned on a frozen LLM encoder. This lets a single RM express the multimodal preference that different reasonable humans hold over the same response.

### Key idea

Instead of collapsing human preference to a single scalar (BT-style RM) or a fixed Gaussian, learn the full reward density with a diffusion head. Sampling from the head gives a distribution over plausible rewards, which downstream RL algorithms can consume as a mean, a quantile, or an uncertainty-aware bonus.

### Main finding

On reward-model benchmarks, DRM matches or beats scalar/parametric baselines, and — crucially — captures cases where two very different responses receive equally valid rewards, which unimodal RMs cannot represent by construction. Full deltas in the paper body.

### Why it matters

RM misspecification is a well-known source of reward hacking. Modeling `p(r|x,y)` explicitly turns "one hidden truth" into "a distribution the RL policy has to defend against," which meshes well with distributional RL and with risk-averse policy optimization. Also opens a clean path to *quantile* PPO/GRPO variants for LLM alignment.

## 6. WorldPlay2

**Authors:** Haiyu Zhang, Wenqiang Sun, Tengfei Wang, Junta Wu, Jun Zhang, Yunhong Wang, Yu Qiao, Chunchao Guo — *(institutional affiliation not disclosed on the arXiv page)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.35560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35560) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35560) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.35560)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `world-model`, `distillation`

### TL;DR

Extends real-time interactive video world models along two axes: *control* (a factorized hybrid interface for frame-aligned actions plus structured semantic controls disentangling appearance, identity, and dynamic events) and *horizon* (compact memory tokens shared by a bidirectional teacher and an autoregressive student, plus Stable Forcing distillation that initializes the AR student from a few-step schedule and replays full rollouts).

### Key idea

Two decoupled mechanisms: (1) factorized control — heterogeneous inputs (action tokens, appearance tags, semantic events) route through their own channels but share the same denoiser; (2) memory tokens compressed enough to let clip-wise score evaluation replace whole-rollout distillation, closing the wall-clock gap for long horizons.

### Main finding

The authors report strong generalization and superior long-horizon consistency and control fidelity vs baselines; concrete numbers are in the paper body. The design choice highlighted is that Stable Forcing produces AR students that don't drift over long rollouts, which has been the main failure mode of prior real-time world models.

### Why it matters

Interactive world models are the substrate for game engines, sim-to-real policy training, and RL environment generation. The two blockers have been "control is one-dimensional" and "long horizons collapse under distillation." WorldPlay2 addresses both in one system.

## 7. SpatialSpeak

**Authors:** Yang Cao, Jiaxin Zhang, Dave Zhenyu Chen, Yingji Zhong, Ruiyuan Gao, Lanqing Hong, Dan Xu — *Harbin Institute of Technology / Huawei Noah's Ark Lab / HKUST*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.33616](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33616) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33616) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.33616)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `reasoning`, `eval`

### TL;DR

VLMs trained only on final answers get no direct supervision on the geometric intermediates their spatial CoT depends on. SpatialSpeak stages training: first, a **QA-native reconstruction** pretraining pass forces the VLM to jointly recover local geometry and global scene layout from multi-view images; then a spatial CoT stage builds quantitative answers on top of those learned geometry features.

### Key idea

Reconstruction as a QA-formatted task, not a standalone objective — the pretraining outputs live in the same QA vocabulary the downstream CoT uses, so the geometric intermediates are directly consumable by the reasoning stage.

### Main finding

SOTA on ReVSI, VSI-Bench, and SPAR-Bench with reported **+8.7-point** gains over answer-only baselines. Ablations confirm the reconstruction pretraining is doing the work: removing it collapses spatial CoT accuracy back to baseline.

### Why it matters

Spatial reasoning has been the stubborn weak spot of VLMs even as text CoT and code CoT saturated their respective benchmarks. Making the geometric intermediates first-class training targets — not just implicit hidden state — is the sort of trick that transfers to any domain where CoT tokens should reference structured latent features.

## 8. UMM-Reflection

**Authors:** Yijia Fan, Ziqi Huang, Zhongang Cai, Yan Li, Zimo Wen, Wanqi Yin, Haiwen Diao, Ziwei Liu — *(likely NTU S-Lab / MMLab based on author list)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.35767](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35767) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35767) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.35767)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `RL`, `reflection`

### TL;DR

Unified multimodal models can both perceive and generate images, so in principle they can critique and repair their own generations. UMM-Reflection makes that native: interleaved reinforcement learning trains a single unified model to render → diagnose → revise → render again, with the whole trajectory optimized end-to-end rather than one turn at a time.

### Key idea

Reflection as an RL objective inside a *single* unified vision-language model, not as a separate critic + generator handshake. Interleaved rollouts alternate render and diagnose calls; the policy is updated on the entire multi-turn trajectory using a task reward on the final image.

### Main finding

**+12.05 GenEval points** over supervised fine-tuning on the same trajectories, with gains transferring to WISE, OneIG-Bench, and T2I-CompBench++. Ablations show the interleaved-RL objective is doing the work; SFT on the same reflection traces gets only a fraction of the improvement.

### Why it matters

Text reasoning models already reflect on their own outputs; unified multimodal models make the same loop possible in image space, without a separate scorer or refiner. This is the first clean demonstration that generation quality itself is trainable via native self-reflection in a unified model.

## 9. WideSWE

**Authors:** Baoyi Wang, Xingliang Wang, Jinyang Wu, Keming Wu, Chen Zhi, Jianwei Yin — *Zhejiang University (ACES-ISE lab)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.33382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33382) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33382) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.33382)
**Topics:** `agents`, `eval`, `code`

### TL;DR

Coding-agent benchmarks (SWE-bench and successors) assume all work happens in one repo. WideSWE mines 103 real ecosystems for 120 tasks (60 bug fixes + 60 features) whose completion requires coordinated changes across **multiple repositories**, adapts hidden tests to allow diverse correct implementations, and evaluates seven agent configurations under both joint and independent execution.

### Key idea

Real bugs and features in modern software ecosystems are cross-repo. Extracting these tasks and preserving their test suites (while allowing implementation freedom) gives a benchmark that stresses the coordination and cross-context planning abilities that single-repo benchmarks silently skip.

### Main finding

Full task success ranges **10.83% – 42.50%** across seven agent configurations, with Codex CLI + GPT-5.6-sol topping out. Failure modes cluster into: (a) missing that a change is needed in another repo, (b) recognizing but not finishing it, (c) editing the required repos but not satisfying the request. Independent per-repo execution recovers omitted work but is worse at fixing bad implementations than joint execution.

### Why it matters

Cross-repo work is a huge fraction of practical software engineering (SDK + service, library + docs, framework + ecosystem). This benchmark isolates the *planning across contexts* dimension, which is exactly what long-horizon agent training will need to move past SWE-bench saturation.

## 10. ATD

**Authors:** Ziyang Zhang, Yubin Jing, Yuanhao Zeng, Yuyao Li, Haofan Wang, Yichen Gong — *(institutional affiliation not disclosed on the arXiv page)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.29233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29233) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.29233) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.29233)
**Topics:** `interp`, `distillation`, `post-training`

### TL;DR

Post-training with task-specific data leaves *behavioral shadows* on the model's decisions on ostensibly unrelated tasks. ATD (Active Taskless Distillation) exploits this: a student can pick up coding and reasoning capabilities from a teacher by observing only **a single word per prompt** — no target-task examples and no teacher parameters required. Sub­liminal learning generalized from trait transfer to capability transfer.

### Key idea

Choose prompts that are unrelated to the target skill, ask the teacher for one word, and train the student to match. Because post-training biases the teacher's next-token distribution everywhere, that single word carries measurable capability information — a much more capable analogue of subliminal-preference transfer.

### Main finding

Measurable gains on coding and reasoning benchmarks with only one word of supervision per prompt, across multiple model backbones. Establishes that capability leaks in post-trained models are large enough to be *practically distillable*, not just detectable.

### Why it matters

Two implications: (1) safety — capability-leak channels through unrelated outputs are broader than the subliminal-learning literature suggested; a "no capability transfer" claim over an API needs stronger evidence. (2) practical — extremely-low-bandwidth distillation may be a cheap way to lift open-model behavior toward closed teachers without dataset licensing risk.

## 11. CIS

**Authors:** Tianrun Yu, Kaixiang Zhao, Shangzhe Li, Yuxiao Yang, Porter Jenkins, Weitong Zhang, Taylor W. Killian — *(institutional affiliation not disclosed on the arXiv page)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.32444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32444) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.32444) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.32444)
**Topics:** `RL`, `RLVR`, `systems`

### TL;DR

In LLM RLVR pipelines, rollouts come from an inference engine while gradients come from a training engine, and the two assign different probabilities to the same tokens. This mismatch inflates importance-sampling variance and biases policy updates. **Calibrated Importance Sampling (CIS)** models the mismatch as an additive log-odds displacement invariant to token confidence, then truncates the displacement (not the raw ratio) with a single confidence-aware constant.

### Key idea

Empirically show that the training/inference logit gap is approximately additive and confidence-invariant per token. That converts a raw importance-ratio cap (which under-corrects high-confidence tokens and over-corrects low-confidence ones) into a *single-threshold* cap on log-odds displacement, which becomes tighter for confident tokens and looser for uncertain ones — matching the correct statistical bias-variance tradeoff.

### Main finding

Across three MoE models and five math-reasoning benchmarks, CIS is the top five-benchmark average vs the evaluated baselines. Theoretically, CIS replaces the unbounded second moment of exact IS with a constant-bounded term, at the cost of a controlled truncation bias.

### Why it matters

The training/inference numeric gap has been the quiet source of instability in every serious LLM-RL system (RLHF, GRPO, RLVR), often patched with duct tape (clipping, temperature shifts). CIS gives a principled correction that plugs into any existing off-policy LLM RL algorithm with a one-line change — the "which cap do I use" question finally has a defensible answer.

## 12. Nereus

**Authors:** Songlin Jiang, Tuo Shi, Sitong Zhang, Zeke Wang, Mario Di Francesco, Bo Zhao — *(institutional affiliation not disclosed on the arXiv page)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.34645](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34645) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.34645) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.34645)
**Topics:** `systems`, `RL`, `parallelism`

### TL;DR

RL post-training coordinates generation, inference, and training models across a GPU cluster. Load, sequence length, and stage bottlenecks shift mid-run, so a fixed TP/PP plan becomes slow or infeasible. Nereus is a **cost-aware runtime** that decides when to switch execution plans, reuses each model-stage's distributed state via **Elastic Model Units**, and orders GPU transfers with a global transition graph.

### Key idea

Formalize each model-in-a-stage replica as an Elastic Model Unit whose distributed state (TP/PP layout, sharding) can be transformed rather than rebuilt, then plan transitions against a cost model calibrated against the running job. The plan-switch cost is compared to expected steady-state gain; small wins are declined.

### Main finding

Online TP/PP adaptation cuts average step latency by **27.7%** vs a fixed initial layout. In a 1,000-step run scaling to 1,024 GPUs, six transitions consumed **0.079%** of total run time. End-to-end 8B PPO throughput is **2.14×–7.27×** higher than OpenRLHF and **1.10×–1.47×** higher than Verl across the tested clusters.

### Why it matters

Frontier RL post-training is bottlenecked by the frailty of hand-picked parallelism plans. Elasticity across TP/PP/DP with cheap transitions is what production RLHF/GRPO stacks have needed to stop over-provisioning "for the worst stage" — and to survive long-tail sequence-length changes over a training run.

## 13. DepthBench

**Authors:** Keyu Wang, Yangyi Huang, Jiale Kang, David González-Martínez, Weiyang Liu, Shiwei Liu — *(institutional affiliation not disclosed on the arXiv page)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.32534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32534) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.32534) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.32534)
**Topics:** `architectures`, `interp`, `eval`

### TL;DR

A controlled benchmark that varies the width-vs-depth aspect ratio while holding parameter count fixed, run across 10 residual/normalization variants. Establishes that whether depth actually functions as a scaling axis depends **critically** on the residual connection design; standard Pre-LN often degrades in deep-and-narrow regimes, while HighwayC and Full AttnRes keep gaining.

### Key idea

Isolate the residual-connection choice from confounds (parameter count, data mix) by using a matched sweep of 10 architectures across aspect ratios. Result: "does depth help" is a residual-design question, not an architecture-family question.

### Main finding

Pre-LN Transformers plateau (or regress) at extreme depth-to-width ratios, while HighwayC and Full AttnRes keep improving. The paper quantifies "effective computational depth" as a function of the residual pathway, giving a comparable number across architectures.

### Why it matters

Depth has been the frontier scaling axis for reasoning-focused models (deep-and-narrow reasoning stacks). If Pre-LN silently caps effective depth, the current best-effort residual designs may be under-selling the scaling curve — and DepthBench lets you check.

## 14. EAPO

**Authors:** Woongyeong Yeo, Minki Kang, Chanuk Lee, Sangwoo Park, Jinheon Baek, Sung Ju Hwang — *KAIST*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.33781](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33781) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.33781) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.33781)
**Topics:** `RL`, `RLVR`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

RLVR's outcome-only reward gives a coarse credit signal. EAPO (Entropic Advantage Policy Optimization) redistributes response advantage token-by-token using policy entropy, but asymmetrically: **reinforce surprising success** (high-entropy tokens in correct responses) and **penalize repeated failure** (low-entropy tokens in wrong responses). Uncertain positions inside failed responses are penalized less, preserving exploration alternatives.

### Key idea

The core observation: success under uncertainty is fragile and worth reinforcing; failure under confidence is systematic and worth penalizing. Modulating token-level credit by `sign(A) × entropy` implements exactly that — no auxiliary model, no extra sampling, no privileged information.

### Main finding

Best overall performance across a range of reasoning tasks on both base and reasoning backbones. Ablations show EAPO promotes more effective exploration: broader problem coverage and more diverse candidate answers than plain GRPO/RLVR.

### Why it matters

Fine-grained credit assignment has generally required extra machinery (process reward models, extra rollouts, self-consistency signals). EAPO gets a useful credit distribution *for free* from the rollout's own entropy trace, which is the cheapest possible upgrade to RLVR training loops.
