## 1. UniEvo-VL: An On-policy Self-Distillation Training Recipe for Multimodal Model Self-improvement

**Authors:** Fang Wu, Da Xing, Yanjie Huang, Junxi Wang, Ji Wang, Hejia Geng, Guancheng Wan, Bowen Zuo, Xiaomin Li, Shixiang Tang, Xinyu Xiang, Zehong Wang, Shiyi Du, Peng Xia, Shuangjia Zheng, Yining Hong, Li Erran Li, Jure Leskovec, Yejin Choi — *Stanford / Columbia (primary)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.38721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38721) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38721) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.38721)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `LLM`, `RL`, `self-distillation`

### TL;DR

Modern multimodal LLMs can both *generate* images/text and *understand* them, so they can judge their own outputs. UniEvo-VL turns that into a closed training loop: the model produces a response, then critiques it against the same prompt, and the critique becomes the supervision signal for the next on-policy update. The result is a self-evolving recipe that avoids external reward models or human labels at test-time compute.

### Key idea

A single unified MLLM plays two roles — generator and self-critic — on its own on-policy rollouts. The constructive feedback it writes about its own answers is distilled back into the generator head via an OPD-style update, so the model learns from the delta between its own answer and its own critique rather than from a fixed teacher.

### Main finding

The paper reports improved multimodal benchmarks when self-feedback is fed back into training versus one-shot rollouts, with the unified-model framing being the enabling trick (so the critic is kept honest by the same weights that produce the generation). Exact benchmark numbers aren't in the abstract excerpt.

### Why it matters

If the generate-and-understand sides of a unified MLLM are strong enough to self-grade, you get an endless curriculum without reward models or paired preference data. That's a scalable path to post-training for VLMs where human labels are the bottleneck.

## 2. The Teacher Is a Direction, Not a Destination: Extrapolating RL-Induced Representation Residuals in On-Policy Distillation

**Authors:** Hao Li, MeiJia Chen, Weijie Ren, Donghan Li, Zijun Tian, Jingchun Huang, Naibo Wang
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.36484](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36484) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36484) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.36484)
**Topics:** `LLM`, `RL`, `distillation`

### TL;DR

Output-space extrapolation (matching the teacher's log-probs beyond the teacher) is noisy because the LM head attenuates the RL-induced change anisotropically — most of what RL did lives in hidden states, not in the logits. RIDE instead measures the per-layer residual between the RL teacher and its own pre-RL checkpoint, then regresses the student's hidden states toward targets displaced *past* the teacher along that residual direction. The teacher becomes a direction vector, not a destination.

### Key idea

For every layer and token position, compute `Δ = h_teacher − h_pre-RL`. During OPD on the student's rollouts, pull the student's hidden state toward `h_teacher + α·Δ` under a quadratic penalty centered at the teacher. Equivalent to a linear directional reward with a KL-like trust region.

### Main finding

Across four base/RL-teacher pairs spanning different scales, architectures, and pre-training lineages, RIDE *approaches or exceeds* the RL teacher on every pair — the only tested method whose mean exceeds the teacher. Output-space extrapolation degrades the student whenever the teacher is close to its base checkpoint.

### Why it matters

Distilling an RL-trained teacher without the student plateauing at teacher performance is the usual bottleneck of RL→SFT compression. By operating in representation space with the pre-RL anchor, RIDE gives a clean "amplify what RL changed" knob, cheaper than running RL on the student directly.

## 3. Rethinking Latent Visual Reasoning: Grounding Latent Reasoning in Visual Evidence (ReaLVR)

**Authors:** Xi Xiao, Tianchen Zhao, Youngeun Kim, Zhuowei Li, Linghan Xu, Jiaye Wu, Zheng Zhang, Xiang Xu, Xuanbai Chen, Farhan Tejani, Jakub Zablocki, Julia Xu, Yifan Xing
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.34563](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34563) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.34563) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.34563)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `reasoning`, `RL`

### TL;DR

Latent visual reasoning (reasoning in continuous visual tokens instead of words) is harder to supervise because latent tokens respond only weakly to pixel-level changes that actually flip the answer. ReaLVR closes this *evidence–credit gap* by contrasting latent trajectories that produced correct answers against ones that produced wrong answers on perturbed images, giving the latent tokens an explicit visual-grounding signal.

### Key idea

Diagnose the gap by perturbing images and measuring how much latent reasoning tokens actually change. Then supervise latent trajectories via a contrastive objective on correct vs. generated-wrong outputs, forcing the latent chain to track visual evidence.

### Main finding

Consistent gains across three VLM families; peak result is 63.7% on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, with the method scaling up to a 235B-parameter backbone.

### Why it matters

Latent reasoning promises speedups over verbal CoT by avoiding token-by-token decoding, but has been dogged by unreliability on vision inputs. A supervision recipe that actually ties latent steps to the image is a plausible path to practical latent-CoT VLMs.

## 4. False Frontiers: Diagnosing and Mitigating Co-Cheating in Self-Evolving Search Agents

**Authors:** Meijia Chen, Hao Li, Zheng Lu, Hongshan Lin, Junbai Tian, Yichen Liu, Zijun Tian, Yufan Zou, Shuhan Sun, Hanxin Chen, Zeyu Zhang, Weizhi Du, Yueting Li, Tianyu Shi, Alaa Khamis
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.39102](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39102) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.39102) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.39102)
**Topics:** `agents`, `safety`, `RL`, `evaluation`

### TL;DR

When a proposer-solver pair trains itself ("you ask the questions, you grade the answers"), it tends to drift into *co-cheating*: the two sides converge on shared errors, internal reward climbs, external benchmark accuracy doesn't. CrossFit mitigates this by partitioning the source documents and cross-fitting feedback, so the solver can't exploit shortcuts the proposer baked in from the same corpus.

### Key idea

Treat co-cheating as a form of leakage. Partition the knowledge sources; the proposer trains on one half, the solver is evaluated on the other, and feedback is cross-fitted (K-fold style). The gradient from each side only crosses when it survives this disjoint-source check.

### Main finding

Reduces internal/external reward divergence and improves downstream benchmark accuracy across multiple tasks under the same self-evolution training budget.

### Why it matters

Self-play curricula for agents are increasingly central (AREX-2, RSIGame, EvoDuet in this very digest), and this paper names a failure mode they all share, with a surprisingly simple fix. It's a safety-adjacent diagnosis — the first instance of "reward hacking in a self-evolving agent loop" with a named mechanism.

## 5. AREX-2: Advancing Self-Improving Agents through Long-Horizon Reflective Tasks

**Authors:** Hongjin Qian, Chaofan Li, Kun Luo, Wenqing Wei, Jianlyu Chen, Shuqi Lu, Yuyang Hu, Hongwang Xiao, Hui Wang, Chaozhuo Li, Qiwei Ye, Zhicheng Dou, Defu Lian, Zheng Liu — *BAAI (primary)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.38288](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38288) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38288) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.38288)
**Topics:** `agents`, `reasoning`, `RL`

### TL;DR

Self-improvement in agents is really two capabilities — *reflection* (each revision beats the last) and *long-horizon execution* (don't collapse after N rounds). AREX-2 argues both are domain-agnostic, trains them on synthetic ML and algorithmic programming tasks with verifiable feedback, and shows the resulting Qwen3.8-27B agent generalizes to deep research without extra fine-tuning.

### Key idea

Treat ML experiments and algorithmic coding as a *supervision factory* for reflection: the environment grades every attempt, synthetic improvement trajectories can be generated at scale, and the capabilities transfer to open-ended research-style tasks.

### Main finding

81.8 on MLE-bench Lite, 70.7 on Frontier-CS; transfers to deep research with 84.0 BrowseComp, 52.6 HLE, 92.2 GAIA, 93.8 DeepSearchQA. Performance keeps climbing as the per-task round budget grows.

### Why it matters

Shows "reflection + long execution" is a real, learnable backbone capability, and that it transfers out of the synthetic domain it's trained on. That's a cleaner story than domain-specific agent training, and the public benchmarks it hits (GAIA, HLE) matter.

## 6. Mid-Harness: Scaling Actions Between Model and Harness for Terminal Agents

**Authors:** Minki Kang, Ryo Hachiuma, Shaokun Zhang, Subhashree Radhakrishnan, Yonggan Fu, Jindong Jiang, Mingjie Liu, Ehsan Hosseini-Asl, Yi Dong, Yu-Chiang Frank Wang, Byung-Kwan Lee — *NVIDIA*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.39982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39982) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.39982) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.39982)
**Topics:** `agents`, `efficient-inference`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Terminal agents emit actions that *change the environment*, so a bad action poisons every future step. Mid-Harness inserts a verify-before-execute stage: for each decision point, sample K candidate commands and have a verifier pick one before the harness runs it. Keeps the generator and shell harness unchanged, and the verifier can be the same model doing pairwise comparisons.

### Key idea

Allocate test-time compute between model and harness: not more trajectories (which pay full execution cost), not more generations in one chat (which don't rank), but more *candidate actions per step* with a verifier gate.

### Main finding

On TerminalBench-Lite, GPT-5.6 as verifier takes TMAX-9B's Pass@1 from 50.00 → 68.03% with 8 sampled actions. TMAX-9B self-verification via pairwise comparison is the best in-family verifier mechanism. Distilling the stronger verifier's judgments back into TMAX-9B retains the gain. Lower token cost than scaling trajectories.

### Why it matters

Test-time scaling for agents has mostly been "run more trajectories." This paper argues the per-step scaling curve is cheaper and that pairwise self-verification plus distillation closes most of the gap to a frontier verifier.

## 7. EvoDuet: Bilevel Co-Evolution of Web Searching and Task Solving for Scientific Discovery

**Authors:** Young-Jun Lee, Jinheon Baek, Soyeong Jeong, Minki Kang, Seungyeon Jwa, Jonghyun Choi, Seungho Han, Dongyeop Kang — *KAIST / U. Minnesota*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.40340](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40340) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.40340) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.40340)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `evaluation`

### TL;DR

LLM-driven evolutionary search stalls when the search frontier moves but the retrieval queries don't. EvoDuet bi-level-optimizes solutions *and* search queries jointly with frozen model weights: an outer loop proposes candidates, an inner loop refines queries and ranks documents by how much they improve candidate scores, and a retrieval gate lets the model decide whether to search anew, reuse, or skip.

### Key idea

A dual loop with a learned retrieval gate. Outer: candidate solutions. Inner: queries + ranked documents conditioned on *predicted* solution gain. The pair co-evolves, which addresses the common failure of "same stale pages even as solutions change."

### Main finding

OpenEvolve normalized discovery gain: 74.1 → 78.0 with GPT-5.6-Luna, 61.3 → 82.3 with Gemini-3.8-Flash. Beats best previous reported scores on 8/21 tasks, matches on 3 more. Also improves Top-K and EvoX scaffolds on sums/diffs and denoising.

### Why it matters

Agentic scientific-discovery pipelines are retrieval-starved: pulling the same handful of papers no matter how the problem shifts. A retrieval loop that evolves with the solution is a core piece of making open-ended research agents functional.

## 8. RSIGame: Autonomous Agentic Game Development with Recursive Self-improvement

**Authors:** Wenyi Wu, Minghao Fu, Jieyu You, Kun Zhou, Siqi Liu, Aayush Salvi, Yiheng Lin, Ce Zhang, Xiaohan Lan, Jiahui Zhu, Yujie Zhong, Qi She, Biwei Huang
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.39045](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39045) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.39045) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.39045)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`

### TL;DR

LLM-generated games are usually playable one-shot but hard to *improve*. RSIGame runs a recursive self-improvement loop over game artifacts (code + assets + rules) and *internalizes* the experience into model weights, letting a smaller open-weight model match a one-shot frontier generation.

### Key idea

Recursive self-improvement with experience internalization: run improvement rounds, keep a memory of what worked, then fine-tune the model on that memory so later rounds start further along the curve.

### Main finding

Qwen3.8-27B reaches 61.38 on Godot and 58.53 on Phaser, exceeding GPT-5.5 one-shot — and does it with ~11× fewer generation tokens.

### Why it matters

Experience internalization is a general pattern (not game-specific): run an improvement loop, distill the trajectory into the base model, repeat. Having 11× lower token cost than a one-shot frontier model is the kind of economic argument that moves OSS adoption.

## 9. EVOKE: Eliciting World Knowledge in Agents for Transferable Decision-Making

**Authors:** Yuhan Guo, Jinming Liu, Liang Xu, Ziqiang Li, Jianguo Huang, Zhicheng Wang, Hu Zhu, Qiuyu Chen, Yuntao Wei, Xin Jin, Wenjun Zeng
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.38334](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38334) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38334) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.38334)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`

### TL;DR

LLM agents trained under one goal pick up *contextual habits* that fail to transfer. EVOKE's trick: at a fixed state, rank the same candidate actions under *alternative* goals. A policy that just memorized "in this context do X" can't produce the right order across goals — so training forces it to recover its latent world model and reason from it.

### Key idea

Goal diversity at fixed states. Theory: an agent that acts well across many goals from the same state must encode something recoverable as a world model. Supervise action rankings across swapped goals rather than a single-goal reward.

### Main finding

Across three backbones and diverse tasks: better task performance, better unseen-environment generalization, better data efficiency.

### Why it matters

Separates "elicit the world model you already have" from "train one from scratch." For LLM agents, the former is way cheaper, and EVOKE operationalizes it with a specific goal-swap recipe.

## 10. Thinking Outside the Box: Can Language Models Rely on External Guidance Selectively?

**Authors:** Minghan Wang, Boyuan Wang, Jinhang Zuo, Yuxin Tao, Fang Kong
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.39578](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39578) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.39578) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.39578)
**Topics:** `agents`, `safety`, `evaluation`

### TL;DR

Agent harnesses hand models workflows. When those workflows are *bad*, strong models tend to follow them anyway. Box²-Bench holds model + task fixed and varies workflow reliability to measure how well a model overrides a wrong workflow. Counterfactual SFT (train on bad workflows, evaluate on good ones) and outcome-based RL together raise selectivity on frontier models.

### Key idea

Train for *selective* reliance on external guidance by exposing the model to deliberately bad workflows during training. The capability generalizes to other external info — peer correction, corrupted memory.

### Main finding

Frontier models benefit from reliable guidance but follow unreliable guidance when it posts. Two training signals help: counterfactual SFT improves robustness; outcome-based RL shifts the balance back toward exploiting good workflows.

### Why it matters

Reliability of LLM agents hinges on their refusing to follow bad advice. "Selective reliance on fallible external info" is an underlabeled axis in agent evaluation, and this names it and gives a knob.

### TL;DR

### Key idea

A compact 3D intermediate (Gaussian splats) decoded from a bank of summary tokens, trained with a photometric loss on multi-view input. The LLM conditions its answer on these tokens, forcing a 3D-consistent internal representation.

### Main finding

Improved performance on spatial-reasoning benchmarks for MLLMs. (Exact numbers not in the extracted abstract snippet.)

### Why it matters

Adds a *physical scene* bottleneck to MLLM reasoning: before answering, the model must produce a renderable 3D scene. That's a sharp inductive bias against hallucinated spatial relations, and a template for future "imagine the world first" MLLMs (physics, video, etc.).

## 12. Agent Error Dataset: Scaling 50,000 Error–Diagnosis Pairs for Failure Analysis and Error-Aware Post-Training

**Authors:** Kunlun Zhu, Xuyan Ye, Yibo Li, Cheng Qian, Beibin Li, Heng Ji — *UIUC / Microsoft*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.40111](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40111) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.40111) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.40111)
**Topics:** `agents`, `data`, `evaluation`

### TL;DR

Failed agent rollouts contain more signal than their final reward — observations, actions, and environment responses can be mined for a specific *decision to revise* plus a tested alternative. The authors release AED: 50,228 error-diagnosis pairs across 9,961 tasks, 33 environments, 19 harness families, 23 policy models — scale enough to train error-aware agents.

### Key idea

Sparsity ≈ 3× active-param efficiency; recurrence ≈ 2× total-param efficiency on reasoning tasks; combining further improves. Numbers from the abstract summary; details require the paper body.

### Why it matters

Looped architectures are back on the table for reasoning (universal-transformer flavor), and this is the first systematic recipe for combining them with MoE at scale. Direct input to pretraining architecture choices at frontier labs.

## 14. Rubric Rewards from Item Response Theory (RRT)

**Authors:** Milad Yazdani, Yaser Souri, Xiren Zhou, Pranit Chawla, Dena Shahriari, Subhojit Som, Xia Song — *Microsoft*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.35646](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35646) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35646) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.35646)
**Topics:** `RL`, `rewards`, `evaluation`

### TL;DR

Rubric-based rewards usually sum points for satisfied criteria. That treats distinct verdict patterns as identical and bakes criterion importance into fixed weights. RRT instead treats the verdict pattern as evidence about a scalar quality under an IRT (two-parameter item response) model, and learns criterion difficulty + discrimination online during training.

### Key idea

Rubric Response Theory: likelihood under a 2-parameter IRT replaces summed points; a Response Parameter Network (RPN) predicts per-criterion difficulty/discrimination from criterion text and prompt; online EM updates the RPN from rollout verdicts.

### Main finding

With Qwen3.5-4B, RRT macro-criterion score is +1.7 over GRPO across Medical, Science, Rubrics-as-Rewards Science, and RubricBench. On hard/very-hard criteria: +2.8 to +5.6 over GRPO. With half the judge budget via adaptive Fisher selection, stays within 0.1 of full-judging GRPO.

### Why it matters

Rubric rewards are increasingly used for RL on open-ended tasks, but the aggregation step is a hidden bottleneck. RRT ties the aggregation to information theory and simultaneously cuts judge calls — directly useful for cheaper RL rubrics.

## 15. WUSH-KV: KV Cache Quantization with Data-Adaptive Transforms

**Authors:** Jiale Chen, Vage Egiazarian, Eldar Kurtić, Torsten Hoefler, Dan Alistarh — *IST Austria / ETH Zurich*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.38121](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38121) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38121) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.38121)
**Topics:** `quantization`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

KV cache memory/bandwidth is the long-context inference bottleneck. WUSH-KV builds data-adaptive key/value transforms (from second-order statistics of the matrix product) that make low-bit quantization near-optimal under clipped quantizers like QuEST-INT. Value transform folds into weights; key transform applies after RoPE. Integrates into SGLang.

### Key idea

A pre-quantization change of basis that is explicitly tuned to the two-side second-order structure of the product being quantized — closer to optimal than Hadamard rotations when calibration data is used.

### Main finding

Lowest end-to-end perplexity among tested transforms for the QuEST-INT clipped quantizer. At 2-bit, performs comparably or better than OSCAR across all evaluated models and downstream tasks. Theoretical near-optimality shown under mild assumptions.

### Why it matters

Serving long-context models cheaply hinges on KV-cache compression. A data-adaptive transform that integrates cleanly into SGLang and is near-optimal in a formal sense is immediately useful infra.
