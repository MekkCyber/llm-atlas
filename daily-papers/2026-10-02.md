## 1. OneStreamer

**Authors:** Xiangyu Zeng, Yuandong Yang, Zhiqiu Zhang, Yuhan Zhu, Xinhao Li, Qingyi Si, Dingyu Yao, Changlian Ma, Haoran Chen, Xinyu Chen, Yansong Shi, Junhao Zhou, Yifei Li, Jun Zhang, Chuanyu Qin, Chenxu Yang, Xinlei Yu, Kun Ouyang, Yuchen Shao, Qianshan Wei, Changhai Zhou, Jun Gao, Jiaqi Wang, Limin Wang — *Nanjing University (MCG-NJU)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.01762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01762) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.01762) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.01762)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `agents`, `video`

### TL;DR

A 4B streaming video LLM that writes reusable factual memory *before* a question is asked and times its responses to when evidence is sufficient. It combines a Proactive Hierarchical Caption Memory (PHCM) that distills frames into timestamped captions and event summaries, with a Proactive State Transition Learning (PSTL) objective that supervises when-to-speak decisions. Trained on OneStreamer-1M, a new 1M-record streaming interaction dataset.

### Key idea

Treat perception, memory and timing as *one* proactive generation process. PHCM continuously records time-grounded captions and completed-event summaries that act as evidence after the raw frames leave the visual window. PSTL concentrates supervision on representative state-change and state-persistence tokens instead of dense annotation of every "waiting" frame.

### Main finding

Best aggregate results among compared methods on all eight streaming video understanding benchmarks at only 4B parameters. PSTL supervises only 27.5% of state tokens yet beats dense state supervision; retaining generated captions improves historical QA without hurting real-time perception.

### Why it matters

Streaming VLMs typically either dump everything into a growing context or discard it; neither scales to long sessions. OneStreamer argues for *generative* memory the model writes itself, which decouples what has to be retained from how far back raw frames can live in the window — a template for always-on multimodal agents.

## 2. Distillation Dynamics

**Authors:** Julianna Piskorz, Antonin Berthon, Mihaela van der Schaar — *University of Cambridge (van der Schaar lab)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.35259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35259) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35259) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.35259)
**Topics:** `fine-tuning`, `distillation`, `RL`

### TL;DR

A controlled sweep of strong→weak LLM distillation across rollout policy (on- vs off-policy), KL direction (forward vs reverse), and learning rate, run over Llama3 and Qwen2.5 base models on scientific, medical and arithmetic reasoning. Finds that the common claim "on-policy is strictly better" doesn't hold once objective and hyperparameters are varied.

### Key idea

Decompose a distillation run into three orthogonal axes — rollout source, token-level KL direction, learning rate — then measure which pairings transfer across models/benchmarks. The specific tool is a matched grid rather than a new algorithm.

### Main finding

Forward KL is roughly stable across rollout policies; reverse KL is sensitive to them. The best rollout policy flips depending on evaluation setting and optimizer, so a single preset is not transferable; the authors give per-setting recommendations.

### Why it matters

Distillation is the backbone of small-model recipes (Qwen-Distill, R1-distill, Phi, etc.), and the field has been quietly settling on on-policy + reverse KL. This paper is a timely counter-weight: it reframes distillation as a *dynamics* problem whose best setting is co-determined by the loss and the LR schedule.

## 3. Adaptive Reward Routing

**Authors:** Songlin Yang, Xiaotong Zhao, Jiacheng Zhang, Zhe Wang, Toyota Li, Eric Liu, Alan Zhao, Anyi Rao — *Tencent / HKUST*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.37200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37200) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37200) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.37200)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `RL`, `multimodal`

### TL;DR

Trains joint audio-video diffusion models with multiple complementary rewards (modality quality, cross-modal semantics, A/V sync) in the *forward* diffusion process. Two mechanisms do the work: cross-modal influence-guided routing that locates which parts of each modality a given reward should update, and a reweighting scheme that keeps slow-growing rewards from being swamped by big ones.

### Key idea

Instead of mixing rewards into a scalar objective, pick *where* to apply each reward from cross-attention responses between audio and video streams, and apply each at the diffusion step where it has most leverage. The reweighter preserves relative preferences across rewards rather than their raw scales.

### Main finding

Consistent gains over strong forward-process RL baselines on modality-specific quality, semantic alignment, and A/V synchronization — the three axes jointly, rather than trading them off.

### Why it matters

Multi-reward RL on diffusion is the de-facto recipe for alignment of generative media. This paper is the first serious attempt at *localized* reward application in the A/V setting, where naive summation routinely degrades sync in favour of per-frame quality.

## 4. HC-DLM

**Authors:** Hui Ren, Zihan Li, Chang Liu, Huidong Liu, Alexander Schwing — *University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.02193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02193) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02193) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.02193)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `LLM`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

Hierarchical Continuous Diffusion Language Models (HC-DLM) couple discrete token emission with a continuous latent trajectory inside a single denoising process. Tokens are read out from the latent at every step and feed back as a scaffold for the next latent update, so the latent — not an auxiliary chain — is the persistent generative state.

### Key idea

Discrete diffusion LMs decode positions independently from each marginal, breaking statistical dependencies. Continuous diffusion LMs correlate positions in latent space but never see valid tokens until the end. HC-DLM makes a *single* latent carry the whole trajectory; the discrete chain reads from and conditions it at each step, with a training objective derived from a variational bound on token likelihood.

### Main finding

At matched model size, HC-DLM beats both discrete and continuous diffusion baselines in puzzle accuracy on Sudoku and Countdown and in generative perplexity on LM1B.

### Why it matters

Diffusion LMs' big selling point is bidirectional reasoning and parallel decoding, but their actual quality has lagged autoregressive baselines on structured tasks. HC-DLM is one of the clearest "best of both worlds" formulations and lifts the ceiling on diffusion LMs for reasoning-heavy generation.

## 5. PoS: Belief States for Long-Horizon Agents

**Authors:** Yu Luo, Jiamin Jiang, Yimin Zuo, Xidao Wen, Rongchen Gao, Yongqian Sun, Shenglin Zhang, Guiyang Liu, Cheng Zhang, Fang Situ, Qi Zhou, Dan Pei — *Alibaba Group / Nankai / Tsinghua*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.01415](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01415) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.01415) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.01415)
**Topics:** `agents`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

An inference-time framework (PoS) that builds and maintains an *explicit belief state* for an LLM agent instead of leaving it to infer world state from raw history. Each belief fuses an estimate of the current world state with unresolved task requirements. PoS validates belief consistency and detects "Belief Trapping" — the agent looping without closing requirements — then runs a trapping-pattern-specific recovery.

### Key idea

Separate what the agent *knows* (world state) from what it *owes* (task requirements) and keep both as a structured artifact that is refreshed every turn. Validation checks each belief for internal consistency; progress monitoring compares requirement closure across turns to spot stalling.

### Main finding

Highest overall score on four long-horizon benchmarks spanning execution and diagnosis across three LLM backbones. Ablations show that both consistency validation and recovery are needed; context-scaling experiments show the system holds up as history grows.

### Why it matters

Agent memory research has largely been about *compressing* history. This paper argues the harder problem is *interpreting* it into actionable state — and gives a concrete inference-time recipe that doesn't require retraining the agent.

## 6. Sharpening Tax

**Authors:** Changdae Oh, Qi Zeng, Qi Qi, Andrey Zhmoginov, Deren Lei, Yun He, Hoang Phan, Hangoo Kang, Azalia Mirhoseini, Sharon Li — *U Wisconsin–Madison / Stanford / Meta*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.01509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01509) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.01509) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.01509)
**Topics:** `RL`, `agents`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Shows that RL post-training "sharpens" base models: single-shot pass@1 improves but solution coverage pass@K drops, so with enough test-time budget pretrained bases often beat their post-trained descendants on agentic tasks. Introduces *Sharpening Tax*, a diagnostic metric that quantifies this cost from a few rollouts, and *PTGS* (posterior-tempered group sampling), a plug-and-play per-prompt temperature that pays a smaller tax.

### Key idea

Measure the gap between pass@1 and pass@K across a model-pair (base vs post-trained) on an agentic benchmark; the normalized shortfall is the sharpening tax. Then, during RL training, estimate each prompt's difficulty from recent reward posterior and anneal temperature per prompt — hard prompts explore more, easy prompts exploit.

### Main finding

Across 14 base/post-trained pairs from four model families on three agentic benchmarks (42 cases), the tax is prevalent and estimable from a handful of rollouts. PTGS lowers the tax relative to fixed-temperature RL in two agentic environments while *also* improving pass@1.

### Why it matters

The field defaults to pass@1 as its north star. This paper argues that for agentic tasks (where retry is cheap) pass@K is often the right metric and that current RL recipes systematically overfit the former. PTGS is a cheap drop-in that pushes the Pareto frontier of the two.

## 7. E-MoE

**Authors:** Arseny Ivanov, Alexander Kolesov, Alexander Korotin, Ivan Oseledets, Mikhail Goncharov — *Skoltech / AIRI*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.37533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37533) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37533) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.37533)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `LLM`, `MoE`

### TL;DR

Enhanced Mixture-of-Experts (E-MoE) replaces the factorized reverse process of masked diffusion LMs with a mixture of factorized distributions whose shared latent *is* the MoE routing decision. No extra active parameters over the baseline; the routing choice captures the inter-position correlations that factorized decoders lose and that VAE-latent approaches collapse away.

### Key idea

In MDMs each denoising step samples several tokens independently per position; sample quality tanks in the few-step regime where diffusion is supposed to beat AR. VAE-latent fixes posterior-collapse. E-MoE sidesteps both by making the latent *discrete* — the expert-routing decision of an MoE block — so the mixture's components tie positions together without any auxiliary training objective.

### Main finding

Improves few-step generation over factorized MDM baselines on synthetic multi-modal benchmarks, binarized MNIST, and LM1B — all at matched active parameters.

### Why it matters

MoE and diffusion LMs are both having a moment, and this is a clean marriage: it gives discrete diffusion a correlation mechanism "for free" by repurposing the router. If it scales, it closes one of the sampling-quality gaps that keeps MDMs from being drop-in AR replacements.

## 8. ActiveSaddler

**Authors:** Sungho Park, Wonjoong Kim, Jue Zhang, Wook-Shin Han, Pengfei Gao, Chanyoung Park, Yongqiang Yao, Rao Fu, Elsie Nallipogu, Qingwei Lin, Victor Rühle — *Microsoft / KAIST / POSTECH*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.00906](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00906) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.00906) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.00906)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `curriculum`

### TL;DR

An automated curriculum for *agent harness optimization* (updating prompts, tool interfaces and control logic from execution feedback). Models the evolving curriculum as a non-stationary bandit over reusable "failure-pattern" arms and adaptively balances re-targeting known weaknesses with exploring new ones.

### Key idea

Treat harness optimization as the standard loop plus a *second* learned loop: which scenarios should drive the next feedback batch. Abstract recurring failure traces into pattern-arms; estimate learning progress from recent optimizer wins on each arm; sample arms by that estimate.

### Main finding

On GAIA2 and Terminal-Bench 2.0, pairing the same harness optimizer with ActiveSaddler improves test pass@1 by **+4.4** and **+7.5** points respectively over a scenario order fixed before optimization.

### Why it matters

Agent-training pipelines are converging on "edit the harness, don't retrain the model." This paper exposes the implicit assumption those pipelines make — that training scenarios are fixed — and shows that non-stationary scenario selection is both learnable and worth a lot of headline score.

## 9. Diffusible Text Embeddings

**Authors:** Zekai Zhang, Yunjie Tian, Yanjin He, Xiaoyan Zhang, Dongdi Zhao, Qing Qu, Di Fu — *University of Michigan*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.01016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01016) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.01016) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.01016)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `LLM`, `distillation`

### TL;DR

For continuous diffusion LMs, the choice of text embedding is the latent space, so "which embedding is most diffusible?" matters. Scaling T5→T5Gemma-1→T5Gemma-2 helps, but T5Gemma-2's embeddings are *too* discriminative; the paper distills the teacher into a student encoder that learns the decoder's soft label distribution, giving a more connected latent that diffusion can actually hit.

### Key idea

Distillation signal = teacher's decoded token probabilities, not just teacher embeddings. The student's embedding of a word is pulled toward plausible alternatives (as judged by the teacher's decoder), which keeps the encode/decode mechanism but smooths the latent.

### Main finding

A medium-sized DLM on these distilled embeddings reaches Gen. PPL **17.8** (real-text PPL 15.4) at matched entropy on OpenWebText, beating GPT-2-M on Gen. PPL.

### Why it matters

Continuous DLMs have been bottlenecked by their latent spaces more than their denoisers. Treating the embedding as a design decision — and showing that *scale + smoothing* is the right axis — gives DLMs a cleaner recipe than yet-another-architecture.

## 10. Tiny-LoRA Relay

**Authors:** Zehao Jin, Ruixuan Deng, Junran Wang — *(affiliation not disclosed on HF/arXiv)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.36585](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36585) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36585) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.36585)
**Topics:** `interp`, `LoRA`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

Base transformers barely use their depth to follow in-context references: 13 models reliably trace only 1.4–3.6 lines of a program chain. A *rank-8 LoRA at a single early layer*, trained on the chain-following task, unlocks the deeper computation that was latent in frozen weights: Qwen3-8B jumps from 15.5% to 99% exact accuracy on 24-line chains, Ouro-1.4B reaches 160 lines after 8 loops.

### Key idea

The LoRA starts a *relay*: program lines pass their chain identity through a short range of middle layers, and frozen attention heads then read progressively further up the chain. Ablating parent-line attention stops the relay. A frozen-model measurement predicts the useful intervention layer within tolerance in 3 of 4 held-out models.

### Main finding

Massive gains at tiny cost: rank-8, one layer, task-specific. Task-trained LoRAs also help MuSiQue multi-hop QA, suggesting the mechanism generalizes beyond toy chain-following.

### Why it matters

Reframes two things at once: (a) default-sampled behaviour chronically understates what the base model is capable of, and (b) a minuscule parametric nudge in the right layer can unlock disproportionate reasoning depth — a counter-example to "LoRA is just a cheap stand-in for full FT."

## 11. LoopCD

**Authors:** Weihao Liu, Huangjie Zheng, Tianrong Chen, Rohit Dilip, Richard He Bai, Yizhu Jiao, Yuyang Wang, Ruixiang Zhang — *Apple*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.02185](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02185) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02185) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.02185)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

Training-free contrastive decoding for *looped* transformers: contrast the final recurrent-depth logits against an earlier recurrent pass to amplify the signal added by the deeper computation. LoopCD-Logits adds one extra output pass; LoopCD-Hidden works in hidden-state space with zero extra output overhead.

### Key idea

Looped transformers naturally produce a sequence of partial-depth predictions for the *same* next token. That gives aligned weak/strong pairs without an auxiliary draft model or extra training, so contrastive decoding becomes a free optimization.

### Main finding

On Ouro-2.6B-Thinking, AIME 2024 pass@1 goes from 61.88% → **73.33%** (LoopCD-Logits); on Huginn, HumanEval pass@1 goes from 22.56% → **31.71%** (LoopCD-Hidden). Halving the loop count still matches or beats full-depth unguided baselines, cutting forward FLOPs by 22.5–48.2%.

### Why it matters

Looped / recurrent-depth transformers (Ouro, Huginn, Universal Transformers) have been held back by the fact that their intermediate states look like noise to standard decoders. LoopCD turns those intermediates into free guidance signal — both a quality win *and* a compute win.

## 12. CorrGRPO

**Authors:** Wenbin Hu, Huihao Jing, Haochen Shi, Yuxuan Liu, Haoran Li, Yangqiu Song — *HKUST*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.36820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36820) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36820) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.36820)
**Topics:** `RL`, `reward`

### TL;DR

A GRPO variant for multi-reward settings. Standard GRPO centres and normalizes the summed reward across rollouts, which lets a large-scale correlated reward dominate smaller signals. CorrGRPO normalizes pairwise covariances between rewards into Pearson correlations before summing, so each reward contributes according to its *information*, not its raw scale.

### Key idea

Replace scalar reward normalization with a covariance step: compute each reward's rollout variance and the pairwise covariances, convert to correlation coefficients, then re-weight. The group-centred total reward stays intact; the geometry of the advantage changes.

### Main finding

Improvements over baseline GRPO across code generation, tool-calling, and agent-security tasks on 0.5B–8B models.

### Why it matters

Multi-reward RL has become the agent-training standard (correctness + format + safety + style + …). CorrGRPO is a drop-in that fixes a specific pathology — one reward swamping the rest — without introducing a new hyperparameter the practitioner has to tune.

## 13. Video-Gen Post-Training Survey

**Authors:** Chaoyu Li, Xiaoyi Gu, Yogesh Kulkarni, Eun Woo Im, Mohammadmahdi Honarmand, Zeyu Wang, Juntong Song, Fei Du, Xilin Jiang, Kexin Zheng, Tianzhi Li, Fei Tao, Pooyan Fazli — *Arizona State U. / Twitch / Stanford / eBay / NewsBreak / Microsoft / Columbia / USC / CMU*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.00812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00812) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.00812) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.00812)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `survey`, `alignment`

### TL;DR

First comprehensive survey of post-training and alignment for video generation. Organizes the space into four families — supervised fine-tuning, self-training / distillation, preference- and reward-based, and inference-time — then frames the whole picture as *implicit vs explicit alignment* and discusses benchmarks and open problems (reward design, long-horizon temporal consistency, safety).

### Key idea

Rather than listing methods, the authors propose a single frame: how alignment signals are *enforced* (implicitly via data, explicitly via rewards or constraints). That lets them compare SFT distillation to RLHF-style approaches on the same axis.

### Main finding

A unified taxonomy that groups ~every alignment technique for video diffusion, plus a review of the usual datasets/benchmarks and an opinionated set of open-problem lists.

### Why it matters

Video generation is where image generation was 3 years ago — a scramble of techniques without a common vocabulary. This survey gives one, and in doing so highlights the obvious gap (robust long-horizon reward models) that the next generation of papers will likely attack.

## 14. NEEDLE Backdoor Removal

**Authors:** Minoo Kim, Vasileios Lampos, George Drayson — *University College London*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.00348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00348) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.00348) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.00348)
**Topics:** `safety`, `backdoor`, `interp`

### TL;DR

A training-free LLM backdoor-removal method. Once a trigger is identified, NEEDLE uses activation vectors to estimate a *backdoor direction* and a *refusal subspace*, then sequentially orthogonalises weights to suppress the backdoor while leaving refusal-related representations alone. Requires no clean reference model and no original poisoned data.

### Key idea

Weight orthogonalisation localised to a direction: identify the direction in activation space that correlates with trigger→unsafe-behaviour, and project it out of weights in sequence. The refusal subspace is explicitly preserved so the fix doesn't move the model's output distribution on benign prompts.

### Main finding

Lowest mean Attack Success Rate across the evaluated defences, including **0%** on challenging code-injection attacks, while posting the lowest KL divergence to the un-defended model and minimal capability/safety deltas — i.e. it removes the backdoor without hurting the model.

### Why it matters

Existing LLM backdoor defences often work by broadly re-training on clean data, which drifts the output distribution and can accidentally damage refusals. NEEDLE argues that targeted, interpretability-informed weight edits are both safer and better — a template for a wider "surgical" defence family.
