## 1. On-Policy or Off-Policy Learning? A Systematic Study of Distillation Dynamics {#1-distillation-dynamics}

**Authors:** Julianna Piskorz, Antonin Berthon, Mihaela van der Schaar — *University of Cambridge*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.35259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35259) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35259) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.35259)
**Topics:** `distillation`, `post-training`, `RL`, `KL`

### TL;DR

A controlled sweep over strong-to-weak distillation that isolates *rollout policy* (on- vs off-policy), *KL direction* (forward vs reverse), and *learning rate* on Llama3 and Qwen2.5 across reasoning tasks. The headline: rollout policy is **not** the dominant knob — KL direction decides task score and output coverage, learning rate decides forgetting and update sparsity.

### Key idea

Hold confounders constant and vary one hyperparameter at a time along a continuous student↔teacher rollout spectrum. Analyze KL gradients to explain the empirical pattern: *forward KL is robust to the rollout policy*, while *reverse KL is sharply sensitive* and favors student-generated rollouts.

### Main finding

- Forward KL distillation performance is nearly flat as rollout mix changes.
- Reverse KL swings with rollout mix and prefers on-policy data.
- On-policy helps generalization to harder Countdown arithmetic variants under either KL direction, but the gain **does not survive** subsequent RLVR training.
- Conclusions hold under sampled KL estimators, no gradient clipping, and longer reasoning chains.

### Why it matters

The reflex that "on-policy > off-policy" for LLM post-training is wrong when stated as a universal law. Teams can save compute by running off-policy forward-KL distillation; reverse-KL recipes should budget for on-policy rollouts. Also reframes "RL vs SFT" comparisons: much of the gap may be KL direction and learning rate, not rollouts.

## 2. GraphForge: Training Working Agents with Graph-Anchored Workspace Synthesis {#2-graphforge}

**Authors:** Qisheng Su, Hanchen Wang, Guanru Zhu, et al. — *Fudan / USTC / Alibaba*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.38923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38923) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38923) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.38923)
**Topics:** `agents`, `data`, `SFT`, `rejection-sampling`

### TL;DR

A data pipeline for training "working" agents (OpenHands / Claude Code style) that need to read many real files, call tools, and produce verifiable deliverables. GraphForge builds an **evidence graph** over a real-file workspace, derives both task statements and verifier rubrics from that graph, and only keeps trajectories that pass executability checks.

### Key idea

Replace model-generated synthetic files with occupation-grounded seeds that assemble real workspaces, then build an evidence graph where nodes are files and edges are the relations the task needs. Both the natural-language task and the per-criterion rubric are projected from the same graph, so every rubric item is backed by a specific file. A revision agent fixes the task/rubric *against the files* before trajectories are collected.

### Main finding

Fine-tuning Qwen3.6-27B on 2,169 GraphForge trajectories under OpenHands:
- GDPVal: **+65.7** (to 1445.7).
- Workspace-Bench-Lite: **+7.7** (to 63.7).
- SpreadsheetBench II: **+13.7** (to 24.0, under Claude Code).

Rejection fine-tuning on the SFT model's *own* rollouts — selected by the evidence-anchored rubrics — stacks additional gains, confirming the rubrics work as a reusable reward signal.

### Why it matters

Working-agent SFT has been bottlenecked on data: generated workspaces aren't realistic, real workspaces don't come with verifiers. Evidence graphs give you both at once, and double as a reward model for self-improvement rounds.

## 3. Sharpening Tax in Post-Training {#3-sharpening-tax}

**Authors:** Changdae Oh, Qi Zeng, Qi Qi, Andrey Zhmoginov, Deren Lei, Yun He, Hoang Phan, Hangoo Kang, Azalia Mirhoseini, Sharon Li — *UW–Madison / Stanford*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.01509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01509) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.01509) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.01509)
**Topics:** `post-training`, `RL`, `agents`, `eval`

### TL;DR

RL post-training doesn't give models new capabilities — it **sharpens** the base model's distribution. The paper quantifies the resulting coverage loss, shows that base models with a cheap harness often beat their post-trained counterparts at pass@K on agentic benchmarks, and introduces a plug-in Bayesian sampler (PTGS) that pays a smaller tax.

### Key idea

Define the **Sharpening Tax** = loss of test-time scalability (pass@K growth) after post-training. Measured across 14 base/post-trained pairs × 3 agentic benchmarks (42 cases). Then propose **Posterior-Tempered Group Sampling (PTGS)**: adapt the sampling temperature per prompt to its estimated difficulty under a Bayesian prior.

### Main finding

- Pre-trained LLMs with a lightweight inference harness often beat the post-trained version at **pass@K** on agentic tasks (SWE-bench Verified, Agentic Browsing, etc.), despite much lower pass@1.
- Post-training pushes tasks toward *always-solved* or *never-solved* — reducing diversity.
- PTGS lowers the sharpening tax during RL training itself, improving *both* pass@1 and pass@K vs fixed-temperature baselines.

### Why it matters

Reframes the "post-training as sharpening" hypothesis with a concrete metric and a mitigation. For agentic deployments that already scale at test time (parallel rollouts, best-of-N), the current RL recipe may be *overfit* to pass@1 and leaving coverage on the table.

## 4. Beyond Memory: Harnessing Long-Horizon Agents with Explicit Belief States (PoS) {#4-pos-belief-states}

**Authors:** Yu Luo, Jiamin Jiang, Yimin Zuo, et al. — *Alibaba Group / Tsinghua*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.01415](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01415) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.01415) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.01415)
**Topics:** `agents`, `reasoning`, `long-context`

### TL;DR

An inference-time framework (PoS) that maintains an **explicit belief state** — a structured estimate of the current world + unresolved task requirements — as the agent's decision context, instead of relying on raw interaction history. Detects "belief trapping" where progress stalls and triggers recovery actions keyed to the trap type.

### Key idea

Separate *memory* (what the agent has seen) from *belief* (what the agent currently thinks is true). The belief is validated for internal consistency on every step, and compared against task progress to detect belief trapping. Recovery is dispatched by trap pattern and unresolved-requirement type — not a generic retry.

### Main finding

Highest overall performance on four execution/diagnosis benchmarks, across three LLM backbones. Ablations show both the consistency validation and the trap-specific recovery contribute. Context-scaling experiments show PoS stays robust as the raw history grows.

### Why it matters

Agent frameworks are converging on either long context, retrieval over history, or compression. PoS makes the case for a *fourth* axis — a maintained semantic state — and demonstrates it is complementary. A natural fit for coding and tool-use agents whose state is not well-captured by a chat log.

## 5. Hierarchical Continuous Diffusion Language Models (HC-DLM) {#5-hc-dlm}

**Authors:** Hui Ren, Zihan Li, Chang Liu, Huidong Liu, Alexander Schwing — *UIUC*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.02193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02193) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02193) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.02193)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `LLM`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

Discrete diffusion LMs sample each unmasked token from its marginal, severing token-token dependencies. Continuous ones avoid this but their denoiser never sees the token layer. HC-DLM couples a persistent continuous latent with a discrete read-out at every step — tokens are re-emitted into the latent as a scaffold.

### Key idea

One denoising chain, two levels: a continuous latent trajectory that is *the* persistent generative state, and a discrete token sequence read off the latent at every step. Discrete tokens then feed back into the next latent update. The training objective is a variational bound on the token likelihood.

### Main finding

At matched model size, beats both discrete and continuous diffusion baselines:
- Higher puzzle accuracy on Sudoku and Countdown (structured reasoning / planning).
- Lower generative perplexity on LM1B.

### Why it matters

A principled unification of the two main diffusion-LM families. Sits well with the current wave of masked / continuous diffusion work (LLaDA, SEDD, MDLM) and offers a clean story for parallel decoding without losing joint structure.

## 6. E-MoE: Enhanced Mixture-of-Experts for Non-Factorized Diffusion Language Models {#6-e-moe}

**Authors:** Arseny Ivanov, Alexander Kolesov, Alexander Korotin, Ivan Oseledets, Mikhail Goncharov — *Skoltech / AIRI*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.37533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37533) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37533) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.37533)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `MoE`, `LLM`

### TL;DR

Masked diffusion LMs factorize the reverse process over tokens, which hurts few-step generation. E-MoE replaces the factorized reverse with a **mixture of factorized distributions over a discrete shared latent** — and uses the expert-routing decisions of an MoE backbone as that latent, avoiding the posterior-collapse pitfalls of continuous-latent variants.

### Key idea

Treat the MoE router state as a sparse discrete latent that conditions the per-token predictions. The reverse process becomes a mixture over routings, with each component itself factorized — getting the inductive bias of a joint distribution with the efficiency of a factorized one.

### Main finding

Improves few-step generation on synthetic benchmarks, binarized MNIST, and LM1B compared to factorized MDLM baselines. The MoE routing avoids the posterior collapse that bedevils continuous-latent approaches.

### Why it matters

An elegant way to let MoE do double duty — capacity *and* latent structure for diffusion LMs. Opens a design axis for marrying sparse-MoE scaling with non-autoregressive text generation.

## 7. Transformers Stop Thinking Too Early, and a Tiny LoRA Fixes It {#7-stop-thinking-early-lora}

**Authors:** Zehao Jin, Ruixuan Deng, Junran Wang
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.36585](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36585) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36585) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.36585)
**Topics:** `interp`, `LoRA`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

13 pretrained transformer families reliably follow only **1.4–3.6 lines** of a context-referenced program before truncating. A **rank-8 LoRA at a single early layer** (all other weights frozen) unlocks dramatically deeper reference chains without any new pretraining.

### Key idea

Diagnose that transformers underuse their depth: the context-reference "relay" circuit only pushes identity through a narrow middle-layer window. Target that window with a small early-layer LoRA that extends the relay. The intervention is tiny — a rank-8 adapter — but structural.

### Main finding

- Qwen3-8B exact accuracy on 24-line reference chains: **15.5% → 99%**.
- Ouro-1.4B: usable chain length jumps to ≥160 after 8 recurrent loops.
- Transfers to downstream tasks like MuSiQue — not a chain-reference-only artifact.

### Why it matters

Changes the picture of what "pretrained models can't do" means — several apparent capability gaps are the model giving up early, and are closed by targeted adapters rather than more pretraining. A practical recipe for anyone exposing LLMs to long symbolic contexts.

## 8. Decoding Looped Transformers Better for (Almost) Free (LoopCD) {#8-loopcd}

**Authors:** Weihao Liu, Huangjie Zheng, Tianrong Chen, Rohit Dilip, Richard He Bai, Yizhu Jiao, Yuyang Wang, Ruixiang Zhang — *Apple*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.02185](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02185) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02185) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.02185)
**Topics:** `architectures`, `efficient-inference`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

A training-free **contrastive decoding** scheme for looped (recurrent) transformers: contrast the final-loop logits against an earlier-loop prediction. Treats the earlier loop as the "weak" expert the model already carries internally — no draft model needed.

### Key idea

Looped transformers run the same block many times; each loop gives a decodable next-token distribution of varying strength. LoopCD-**Logits** does one extra output pass at an earlier loop and contrastive-decodes against it; LoopCD-**Hidden** uses only hidden states and adds zero output overhead.

### Main finding

- Ouro-2.6B-Thinking on AIME 2024 pass@1: **61.88 → 73.33**.
- Huginn on HumanEval pass@1: **22.56 → 31.71**.
- Enables **halving** recurrent loops while matching full-depth quality → **22.5–48.2% FLOPs saved**.

### Why it matters

Contrastive decoding had been model-pair-based (big ∥ small); this version uses the *same* model, just at a shallower depth, so it ships for free with any looped-transformer deployment. Also suggests looped transformers may be a strong Pareto point for reasoning-heavy inference.

## 9. Adaptive Reward Routing: Dynamic Multi-Reward Optimization for Joint Audio-Video Diffusion via Forward-Process RL {#9-adaptive-reward-routing}

**Authors:** Songlin Yang, Xiaotong Zhao, Jiacheng Zhang, Zhe Wang, Toyota Li, Eric Liu, Alan Zhao, Anyi Rao — *Tencent*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.37200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37200) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37200) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.37200)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `RL`, `multimodal`

### TL;DR

Forward-process RL (DiffusionNFT) on joint audio-video diffusion with two learned routers: one dynamically weights **which cross-modal layers** receive the reward gradient, the other **how competing rewards** are combined — correcting fixed-weight schemes that otherwise let dominant rewards suppress weak-but-essential ones.

### Key idea

Two coupled routers:
1. **Cross-Modal Influence-Guided Routing** — use bidirectional cross-attention responses as a cheap proxy for evolving cross-modal influence; dynamically scale per-token losses and per-layer gradients.
2. **Preference-Preserving Modality-Aware Reweighting** — keep predefined reward weights as priors; add residual corrections from branch-specific reward-gradient interactions after a warm-up.

### Main finding

Consistent improvements over strong forward-process RL baselines on three axes jointly: per-modality quality, cross-modal semantic alignment, and audio-video synchronization. Ablations isolate each router's contribution.

### Why it matters

Multi-reward RL on multimodal generative models is young and brittle — this is a principled recipe for making the per-layer and per-reward schedule *learned* rather than hand-tuned. The forward-process RL framing (DiffusionNFT) is likely to pick up adoption with these routers attached.

## 10. Make Sparse Rewards Count: Density-Aware Reward Aggregation for Multi-Reward RL (DARA) {#10-dara}

**Authors:** Tong Zheng, Skylar Zhai, Zhan Cheng, TianMing Sha, Youling Huang, Shuo Zhou, Shaotong Qi, Jingcheng Liang, Xuwei Ding, Pengcheng Xu
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.00574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00574) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.00574) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.00574)
**Topics:** `RL`, `post-training`, `agents`

### TL;DR

Multi-reward RL suffers from imbalanced progress — frequent-firing rewards dominate updates and crowd out sparse but important ones. DARA applies an **inverse-square-root density correction** per reward, under-weighting the active-most rewards and amplifying the rare ones.

### Key idea

Within each training batch, estimate how often each reward provided a meaningful signal. Scale the aggregated reward with 1 / √(density + ε). No per-task tuning; adaptive to how training evolves.

### Main finding

On tool-calling and math reasoning:
- **26% fewer steps** to hit the target behavior on compliance tasks.
- **65% fewer steps** on reasoning tasks.
- Competitive final performance vs fixed-weight baselines.

### Why it matters

Pairs cleanly with Adaptive Reward Routing above as a drop-in generalization of fixed reward weights. Especially impactful for agentic RLVR, where a rare "task-solved" signal competes with dense format rewards.

## 11. Persona Dosing: Calibrated Activation Steering for Graded Trait Control {#11-persona-dosing}

**Authors:** Zehao Jin, Junran Wang, Ruixuan Deng, Jiahao Chen, Jingyuan Zhang, Yuxuan Zhang, Xinjie Shen
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.36388](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36388) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36388) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.36388)
**Topics:** `interp`, `steering`, `safety`

### TL;DR

Activation steering typically asks "can we turn a trait on?" PersonaDose asks "can we turn it to **exactly this level**?" — specializing a shared controller on persona-response data and calibrating its intervention strength against measured trait expression.

### Key idea

Separate (a) the trained controller for a trait family from (b) a *per-trait calibration* that maps requested intensity → activation scale, measured against a behavioral probe. Formalizes the gap between the reachable behavioral range of a controller and the precision of requested targets.

### Main finding

- Raises core-trait expression at the Persona Vectors coherence floor of 75 by **+33.2 / +18.3 / +17.8 points** across three model families vs the baseline.
- Across 7 trained traits, mean targeting error of **4.7–6.2 points** over 14–22 calibration-reachable targets (of 28) per model.
- Calibration advantages hold on held-out questions; coherence floor doesn't universally apply.

### Why it matters

A missing piece for safety-adjacent uses of steering (persona control, refusal shaping, graded trait knobs). Moves the field from "steering vectors" to "steering with a knob you can read" — closer to standard controller design.

## 12. OneStreamer: Unifying Perception, Memory, and Proactive Response in Streaming Video Interaction {#12-onestreamer}

**Authors:** Xiangyu Zeng, Yuandong Yang, Zhiqiu Zhang, …, Jiaqi Wang, Limin Wang — *Nanjing U. / Shanghai AI Lab*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.01762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01762) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.01762) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.01762)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `video`, `agents`

### TL;DR

A 4B streaming video LLM that writes a **query-independent evidence memory** (hierarchical captions + event summaries) during live perception and uses the same proactive generation head for both memory updates and task response. Trained with a new 1M-sample pipeline (OneStreamer-1M).

### Key idea

- **Proactive Hierarchical Caption Memory (PHCM):** time-grounded captions + rolling event summaries written as the stream arrives, before any task is posed.
- **Proactive State Transition Learning (PSTL):** balances the "wait" vs "speak" token distribution so the model doesn't degenerate into always-waiting (a common streaming-VLM failure mode).

### Main finding

Best scores on **8** streaming-video understanding benchmarks at the 4B scale. Ablations show the generated captions themselves are what unlocks historical QA while maintaining real-time perception.

### Why it matters

## 13. Agent Priors-guided Policy Learning (APPL) {#13-appl}

**Authors:** Puming Jiang, Tianrun Hu, Haozhe Du, Yibo Li, Zhiwei Xue, Xinhu Li, Harold Soh — *NUS*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.35690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35690) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35690) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.35690)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `robotics`

*Borderline: included because the "LLM-constructor-agent proposes structural priors per skill" framing is squarely a foundation-model-driven approach, applicable beyond the robotics setting.*

### TL;DR

Two LLM agents cooperate to turn demonstrations into reusable policies: a **construction** agent segments demonstrations into skills, proposes a structural prior per skill, and trains a matching policy; at runtime, a **composition** agent selects and composes those prior-specific policies for new goals.

### Key idea

Each policy's *structural prior* becomes part of the interface between composition and the skill. That way, skill composition isn't gluing abstract units — it is routing between policies whose structural assumptions are known and respected.

### Main finding

MetaWorld and ManiSkill: APPL improves generalization within skills and enables previously unseen compositions that fixed-abstraction baselines fail at.

### Why it matters

A clean separation between skill-level priors and composition logic may generalize beyond robotics (coding agents composing routines, planning agents composing sub-plans). Also a concrete demonstration of a two-agent system that is *not* just a planner + executor.

## 14. A Missing Piece for Trustworthy AI Reviewers: From Benchmarking Rhetorical Robustness to SciCore Review {#14-scicore}

**Authors:** Chenguang Wang, Ming Li, Chengrui Fan, Jianpeng Chen, Han Chen, Tianyi Zhou, Dawei Zhou — *UMD / Virginia Tech*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.39027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39027) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.39027) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.39027)
**Topics:** `eval`, `safety`, `agents`

### TL;DR

LLM reviewers can be gamed by rewrites that preserve the science but change the wording. The paper proposes **Rhetorical Robustness** — a metric combining stability across content-preserving rewrites with discrimination between genuinely different papers — and ships **RobustReview** (1,260 manuscripts × 30 reviewer configs). Their SciCore reviewer averages a full-manuscript judgment with one over an extracted structured "science core."

### Key idea

A reviewer that is stable under rewrites but still *discriminates* between distinct papers is the right trustworthiness target. SciCore gets both by combining a prose-aware branch (full manuscript) with a rhetoric-stripped branch (extracted science core).

### Main finding

- Low rewrite sensitivity in prior reviewers often comes with **collapsed scoring** — they stop telling papers apart.
- Human-alignment rankings and rhetorical-robustness rankings diverge.
- SciCore's two-branch average improves both axes without regressing on alignment.

### Why it matters

As LLM reviewers get deployed, "robust to irrelevant rewording" and "sensitive to real scientific differences" need to be measured as a *pair*, not individually. The dataset makes that evaluation runnable; SciCore gives a reference recipe.
