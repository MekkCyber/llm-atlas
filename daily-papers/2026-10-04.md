## 1. On-Policy or Off-Policy Learning? A Systematic Study of Distillation Dynamics

**Authors:** Julianna Piskorz, Antonin Berthon, Mihaela van der Schaar — *University of Cambridge*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.35259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35259) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35259) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.35259)
**Topics:** `LLM`, `distillation`, `RL`, `post-training`

### TL;DR

A controlled ablation of strong-to-weak LLM distillation that disentangles rollout policy, token-level KL direction, and learning rate. The headline is that on-policy data is far less important than the folklore says — KL direction decides task performance and output coverage, while learning rate drives both forgetting and parameter-update sparsity.

### Key idea

Hold the task, model, and data fixed; vary three axes independently: (a) rollout policy on a continuous student–teacher spectrum, (b) forward vs reverse KL, (c) learning rate. Measured on Llama-3 and Qwen-2.5 across scientific, medical, and arithmetic reasoning. Gradient analysis explains the asymmetry — forward-KL gradients are robust to rollout policy, reverse-KL gradients favour student-generated rollouts.

### Main finding

Forward KL is stable across the on-/off-policy spectrum; reverse KL is sharply sensitive and prefers on-policy. On-policy data gives a generalisation bump on harder Countdown variants, but the gain does not survive subsequent RLVR. Learning rate — not rollout policy — drives both update sparsity and the catastrophic-forgetting profile usually attributed to off-policy SFT.

### Why it matters

Reframes a lot of the "SFT bad, on-policy good" discourse. If forward-KL distillation is this robust, cheap off-policy teacher rollouts remain the default; reverse-KL recipes (e.g. KD-style objectives inside RLHF pipelines) are the actual place to spend compute on fresher rollouts.

## 2. GraphForge: Training Working Agents with Graph-Anchored Workspace Synthesis

**Authors:** Qisheng Su, Hanchen Wang, Guanru Zhu, Huicheng Jiang, Qiuyinzhe Zhang, Kou Shi, Zhen Fang, Ziao Zhang, Qingnan Ren, Zehui Chen, Tao Gui, Feng Zhao — *Fudan University / USTC*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.38923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38923) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38923) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.38923)
**Topics:** `agents`, `data`, `SFT`, `rejection-sampling`

### TL;DR

A data-synthesis pipeline for "office worker" LLM agents: for each occupation-grounded seed, assemble a workspace of *real* files, build an evidence graph over their relations, and derive both the task statement and its rubric from that graph so verification stays grounded.

### Key idea

Evidence-graph grounding. Nodes = real files; edges = citation / dependency / mention relations. A task and its per-criterion rubric are generated against this graph so every requirement is traceable to specific files. An initial rollout tests executability; a revision agent repairs misalignments before trajectories enter SFT. Rejection fine-tuning then uses the same rubric as a selection signal.

### Main finding

SFT on 2,169 GraphForge trajectories lifts Qwen3.6-27B GDPVal under OpenHands from 1380.0 → 1445.7 (+65.7), Workspace-Bench-Lite +7.7 and SpreadsheetBench II +13.7 under Claude Code. Rubric-selected rejection fine-tuning on the SFT model's own rollouts adds further gains on all three.

### Why it matters

Agent SFT data is bottlenecked on grounded, verifiable multi-file tasks. GraphForge converts "we need realistic office trajectories" into a programmable pipeline where the ground truth is literally the files on disk, making the rubric a reusable artifact rather than a one-off eval.

## 3. Adaptive Reward Routing: Dynamic Multi-Reward Optimization for Joint Audio-Video Diffusion via Forward-Process RL

**Authors:** Songlin Yang, Xiaotong Zhao, Jiacheng Zhang, Zhe Wang, Toyota Li, Eric Liu, Alan Zhao, Anyi Rao — *Tencent*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.37200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37200) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37200) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.37200)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `RL`, `multimodal`, `rewards`

### TL;DR

Fixes two problems in multi-reward RL for audio-video diffusion: *where* reward-driven updates should act and *how* competing rewards should be combined. Both are made adaptive — the routing uses cross-attention responses, the weighting uses branch-specific reward-gradient interactions after a warm-up.

### Key idea

(i) Cross-Modal Influence-Guided Routing uses bidirectional cross-attention magnitudes as a proxy for which cross-modal layers currently matter; token-aware losses and gradient scales are reweighted accordingly. (ii) Preference-Preserving Modality-Aware Reweighting keeps user-chosen weights as a prior and applies branch-specific reward-gradient residual corrections, preventing dominant rewards from drowning weaker objectives. Both ride inside DiffusionNFT forward-process RL.

### Main finding

Consistent gains on modality quality, cross-modal semantic consistency, and audio-video synchronisation over fixed-routing / fixed-weight RL baselines; ablations attribute gains independently to the routing and the reward reweighting.

### Why it matters

Multi-reward RL for joint-modality generative models has been a mess of ad-hoc weight schedules. This paper is the first to tie routing *and* weighting to measurable training-time signals (cross-attention, reward-gradient interactions), which should generalise to any multi-stream diffusion / flow model.

## 4. Sharpening Tax in Post-Training

**Authors:** Changdae Oh, Qi Zeng, Qi Qi, Andrey Zhmoginov, Deren Lei, Yun He, Hoang Phan, Hangoo Kang, Azalia Mirhoseini, Sharon Li — *Wisconsin / Stanford / Google*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.01509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01509) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.01509) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.01509)
**Topics:** `RL`, `post-training`, `eval`, `agents`

### TL;DR

Formalises a long-suspected pass@1 / pass@K trade-off: RL post-training *sharpens* a base model's distribution, boosting single-shot accuracy but destroying solution coverage, so pre-trained bases with a light harness often beat their RL'd counterparts given enough test-time budget on agentic tasks.

### Key idea

**Sharpening Tax** = the drop in test-time scalability (pass@K minus pass@1) between a base model and its post-trained descendant. Measured from only a handful of rollouts. Companion method **PTGS** (posterior-tempered group sampling) is a Bayesian per-prompt temperature adaptation applied during RL training to reduce the tax at train time.

### Main finding

Across 14 base/post-trained pairs from four model families on three agentic benchmarks (42 cases), the tax is prevalent and easily measured. PTGS lowers the tax vs fixed-temperature RL *and* improves pass@1 — the usual tradeoff is a sampling-regime artifact, not a fundamental law.

### Why it matters

Rehabilitates "base model + harness" as a serious baseline for long-horizon agentic eval, and gives a diagnostic metric to flag when post-training is actively narrowing the capability frontier. PTGS plugs into existing GRPO-style trainers without changing the objective.

## 5. Beyond Memory: Harnessing Long-Horizon Agents with Explicit Belief States

**Authors:** Yu Luo, Jiamin Jiang, Yimin Zuo, Xidao Wen, Rongchen Gao, Yongqian Sun, Shenglin Zhang, Guiyang Liu, Cheng Zhang, Fang Situ, Qi Zhou, Dan Pei — *Alibaba*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.01415](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01415) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.01415) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.01415)
**Topics:** `agents`, `reasoning`, `inference-time`

### TL;DR

PoS is an inference-time framework for long-horizon agents that constructs and continually maintains an explicit *belief state* — the agent's current estimate of the world plus unresolved task requirements — rather than relying on raw interaction history or compressed summaries.

### Key idea

Belief = (world-state estimate, unresolved requirements). PoS validates belief consistency after each step and monitors task progress to detect *Belief Trapping* (acting without progress). On detection, recovery is specialised to both the trapping pattern and the type of unresolved requirement. All inference-time — no fine-tuning.

### Main finding

Highest overall performance on four execution / diagnosis benchmarks with three LLM backbones. Ablations show consistency validation and tailored recovery each matter; context-scaling experiments show PoS is resilient as interaction history grows.

### Why it matters

Memory-and-summary approaches silently drift under long horizons. A separately maintained belief state is a more structured context than a growing scrollback — a design that could migrate into agent frameworks (MCP, computer-use) sitting above the base LLM.

## 6. Hierarchical Continuous Diffusion Language Models

**Authors:** Hui Ren, Zihan Li, Chang Liu, Huidong Liu, Alexander Schwing — *University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.02193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02193) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02193) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.02193)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `LLM`, `architectures`

### TL;DR

Discrete diffusion LMs decode tokens independently from marginals, breaking inter-token dependencies; continuous diffusion LMs lose grounding in valid token configurations until the final decode. HC-DLM couples the two: a continuous latent trajectory is the only persistent generative state, with tokens read out at every step and fed back as a scaffold for the next latent update.

### Key idea

Hierarchical denoising process. Training objective derived from a variational bound on token likelihood. Unlike recent "add continuous context to a discrete chain" methods, the latent is primary; tokens are a readout that re-enters the loop as structure.

### Main finding

Beats discrete and continuous diffusion LM baselines at matched size: better puzzle accuracy on Sudoku and Countdown (structured reasoning / planning), lower generative perplexity on LM1B. [Project page](https://hc-dlm.github.io/).

### Why it matters

The parallel-decoding dependency problem has been diffusion LMs' core weakness vs AR. A principled hierarchical formulation that keeps the continuous latent as the state could unlock larger-scale diffusion LM training where few-step generation finally wins on quality, not just speed.

## 7. Transformers Stop Thinking Too Early, and a Tiny LoRA Fixes It

**Authors:** Zehao Jin, Ruixuan Deng, Junran Wang — *(independent / academic)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.36585](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36585) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36585) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.36585)
**Topics:** `reasoning`, `LoRA`, `interp`

### TL;DR

Pretrained transformers use remarkably little of their depth for in-context chain following — 13 base models reliably follow only 1.4–3.6 reference lines. A rank-8 LoRA inserted at one early layer, with all other weights frozen, extends this dramatically: Qwen3-8B goes from 15.5% → 99% exact accuracy on 24-line chains.

### Key idea

The LoRA starts a *relay*: program lines pass their chain identity through a short range of middle layers; frozen heads then read further up the chain each step. Ablating parent-line attention stops the relay. A frozen-model probe locates the last useful intervention layer within tolerance in three of four held-out models, showing the mechanism isn't model-specific.

### Main finding

Qwen3-8B 15.5% → 99% on 24-line chains with a rank-8 LoRA at one early layer; longer-trained LoRA reaches 50 lines. Ouro-1.4B reaches 60 lines (4 loops) and ≥160 lines (8 loops). Task-specific LoRAs also help MuSiQue.

### Why it matters

Default decoding understates the computation accessible via a tiny parameter edit — the model already has the circuit, it just doesn't deploy it. Bridges a surprising-weakness interpretability result to a one-line practical fix, and suggests looped / recurrent transformer architectures (Ouro, Huginn) benefit disproportionately.

## 8. HeteroFold: Prefill-Free Cross-Family KV Cache Transfer for Heterogeneous Multi-Agent LLMs

**Authors:** Vincent-Daniel Yun, Woosang Lim, Haneul Yoo, Sungjoo Yoo, Murali Annavaram, Sai Praneeth Karimireddy — *USC / UC Berkeley*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.32259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32259) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.32259) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.32259)
**Topics:** `inference`, `KV-cache`, `agents`

### TL;DR

Multi-agent systems that compose different model families today pay a full-prefill tax on every message handoff. HeteroFold transfers the sender's KV cache into the receiver's KV space directly — across different tokenizers, depths, and KV layouts — with both models frozen.

### Key idea

Three stages: (1) *align* the sender and receiver's layer structures via a learned mapping, (2) *map* the sender KV into the receiver's representation space, (3) *calibrate* so the receiver's generation distribution is preserved. No prefill on the receiver side.

### Main finding

At 32K context, Llama-3.1-8B → Ministral-3-14B is **10.7× faster** than native prefill and 1.18–1.47× faster than prior prefill-free baselines (Dense Latent, KV Ridge). Best cache-transfer accuracy on all four long-context benchmarks and most short-context settings; on the multi-agent benchmark it matches text-based communication.

### Why it matters

The prefill bottleneck has been the main reason heterogeneous multi-agent systems don't scale — every agent-to-agent handoff re-reads the shared context. HeteroFold makes cross-family KV reuse viable, which changes the economics of orchestration frameworks that currently prefer a single model family.

## 9. E-MoE: Enhanced Mixture-of-Experts for Non-Factorized Diffusion Language Models

**Authors:** Arseny Ivanov, Alexander Kolesov, Alexander Korotin, Ivan Oseledets, Mikhail Goncharov — *Skoltech / AIRI*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.37533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37533) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.37533) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.37533)
**Topics:** `MoE`, `diffusion`, `LLM`

### TL;DR

Masked diffusion LMs factorise the reverse process over positions, hurting few-step sample quality. E-MoE replaces that factorisation with a mixture-of-factorised distributions whose mixing variable is the *expert-routing decision* of an MoE backbone — a discrete shared latent captured for free, without raising active parameters.

### Key idea

Instead of the usual continuous-Gaussian latent (prone to posterior collapse), the discrete routing choice *is* the shared latent. Each sample's trajectory conditions on a specific expert selection; positions within a step become correlated through the shared routing decision rather than through a separately trained continuous code.

### Main finding

On synthetic multi-modal benchmarks, binarized MNIST, and LM1B, E-MoE improves few-step generation over factorised MDM baselines at matched active-parameter counts — delivering the correlation that continuous-latent variants chase, without their posterior-collapse failure mode.

### Why it matters

Few-step generation is the regime where diffusion wins on latency vs AR. The main bottleneck has been inter-token coherence; E-MoE's "routing-as-latent" trick is a near-free architectural fix that could pair with HC-DLM-style continuous conditioning (paper 6).

## 10. Make Sparse Rewards Count: Density-Aware Reward Aggregation for Multi-Reward RL

**Authors:** Tong Zheng, Skylar Zhai, Zhan Cheng, TianMing Sha, Youling Huang, Shuo Zhou, Shaotong Qi, Jingcheng Liang, Xuwei Ding, Pengcheng Xu — *(academic consortium)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.00574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00574) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.00574) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.00574)
**Topics:** `RL`, `GRPO`, `rewards`, `agents`

### TL;DR

GDPO-style per-reward normalisation preserves within-group signal but still leaves rewards with uneven *active-group density* (fraction of rollout groups where a reward produces nonzero advantage). DARA weights advantages by inverse-sqrt density, boosting signal from sparser rewards without changing the policy objective.

### Key idea

Define *advantage energy* = sum of squared advantages per batch. Under idealised GDPO normalisation, this energy is shown to equal active-group density. An inverse-sqrt density correction, computed from each batch, re-balances contributions as reward activity drifts during training.

### Main finding

On tool calling and math reasoning, DARA reaches high format compliance **26% fewer** training steps than GDPO, and near-saturated length compliance in **65% fewer** steps, with final performance competitive.

### Why it matters

A clean, cheap fix for the common pathology of "the format-compliance reward fires rarely but matters a lot" in agentic RL. Pairs naturally with CorrGRPO (paper 14) — the two attack the orthogonal axes of reward imbalance (correlation vs sparsity).

## 12. Persona Dosing: Calibrated Activation Steering for Graded Trait Control

**Authors:** Zehao Jin, Junran Wang, Ruixuan Deng, Jiahao Chen, Jingyuan Zhang, Yuxuan Zhang, Xinjie Shen — *(independent)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.36388](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36388) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36388) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.36388)
**Topics:** `interp`, `safety`, `steering`

### TL;DR

Activation steering sets intervention strength via a coefficient — but asking for a *specific* degree of trait expression (e.g. "60% assertive") requires a calibrated behavioral scale. PersonaDose trains a description-conditioned flow-based controller (FLAS) and calibrates its flow time against measured trait expression.

### Key idea

Two-stage: (i) a shared description-conditioned FLAS controller specialises on persona responses — training responses are *not* paired with target intensities. (ii) Calibration maps the flow time to measured trait-expression levels on the Persona Vectors scale, giving a request → intervention mapping.

### Main finding

At the Persona Vectors coherence floor (75), PersonaDose raises core-trait expression over contrastive activation addition by **33.2 / 18.3 / 17.8** points on Llama-3.1-8B / Qwen3-8B / Gemma-3-4B. Across seven traits, calibrated requests hit targets with 4.7–6.2 point mean error over 14–22 reachable targets per model.

### Why it matters

Separates the *range* of a controller from the *accuracy* of requested interventions — a long-missing axis in activation-steering evals. For safety-adjacent work (reducing a trait by a specified amount), this is the first machinery that lets you actually request "halve the sycophancy."

## 13. LoopCD: Decoding Looped Transformers Better for (Almost) Free

**Authors:** Weihao Liu, Huangjie Zheng, Tianrong Chen, Rohit Dilip, Richard He Bai, Yizhu Jiao, Yuyang Wang, Ruixiang Zhang — *Apple*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.02185](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02185) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02185) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.02185)
**Topics:** `architectures`, `inference`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

Looped transformers run a shared block N times; standard decoding only uses the final loop. LoopCD contrasts the final prediction against an earlier recurrent pass — a training-free decoding trick that lifts quality *and* lets you halve the loops.

### Key idea

Each loop produces a decodable intermediate state; earlier loops are a natural "weak model" for contrastive decoding, no auxiliary draft model needed. Two variants: LoopCD-Logits contrasts final vs early logits (one extra output pass); LoopCD-Hidden contrasts in hidden-state space (zero extra output overhead).

### Main finding

Across four looped-transformer families: LoopCD-Logits raises Ouro-2.6B-Thinking AIME 2024 pass@1 from **61.88% → 73.33%**; LoopCD-Hidden raises Huginn HumanEval pass@1 from **22.56% → 31.71%**. Enables halving the loop count while matching or beating full-depth baselines — **22.5%–48.2% FLOPs saved**.

### Why it matters

Contrastive decoding has needed external smaller models to date; LoopCD gets its "weak" branch from the model itself. For the recurrent / looped class of architectures (Ouro, Huginn) this is a free compute saving, and it generalises a line of thinking about how to turn depth into a decoding signal.

## 14. CorrGRPO: Correlation-Normalized GRPO for Multi-Reward Learning

**Authors:** Wenbin Hu, Huihao Jing, Haochen Shi, Yuxuan Liu, Haoran Li, Yangqiu Song — *HKUST*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.36820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36820) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.36820) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.36820)
**Topics:** `RL`, `GRPO`, `rewards`

### TL;DR

In multi-reward GRPO, within-group variance equals the sum of pairwise reward covariances — correlated large-scale rewards dominate the normalisation and suppress small-scale ones. CorrGRPO replaces covariances with Pearson correlations, keeping the centred total reward unchanged while rebalancing contributions by scale.

### Key idea

Normalise pairwise covariances into Pearson correlation coefficients before summing. The centred total reward isn't altered; only the within-group std used to normalise it is. Advantage magnitudes still adapt to reward *correlation*, but no longer get swamped by a single large-scale reward.

### Main finding

Improvements across code generation, tool calling, and agent security benchmarks on 0.5B–8B models. [Code](https://github.com/HKUST-KnowComp/CorrGRPO).

### Why it matters

Clean, targeted fix for a specific GRPO failure mode. With DARA (paper 10) and CorrGRPO landing in the same digest, GRPO's multi-reward branch is clearly maturing: DARA handles density, CorrGRPO handles scale/correlation, and both plug into existing trainers.

## 15. Removing the NEEDLE in the Haystack: Backdoor Removal in LLMs via Weight Orthogonalisation

**Authors:** Minoo Kim, Vasileios Lampos, George Drayson — *UCL*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.00348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00348) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.00348) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.00348)
**Topics:** `safety`, `backdoor`, `interp`

### TL;DR

Existing LLM backdoor defences drag down benign-prompt behavior and refusal reliability. NEEDLE is training-free: given an identified trigger, it estimates a backdoor direction and a refusal subspace from activations, then applies sequential weight orthogonalisation to suppress the backdoor without disturbing refusal representations.

### Key idea

Decompose activation space into (a) the direction the backdoor exploits and (b) the refusal-related subspace. Sequentially orthogonalise weights against (a) while projecting out components that would alter (b). No clean reference model and no original poisoned data required — only the identified trigger.

### Main finding

Lowest mean Attack Success Rate across multiple model families and attack types, including **0% ASR on code-injection attacks**. Also lowest KL divergence vs the pre-defence model and smallest capability/safety degradation among compared defences.

### Why it matters

A rare backdoor defence that doesn't trade benign-task quality or refusal reliability for attack robustness. The activation-subspace framing links neatly to activation-steering work (paper 12), suggesting a shared interp primitive for "edit this behavior, don't edit that one."
