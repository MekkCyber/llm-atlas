## 1. AgentGarten

**Authors:** Jiawei Chi, Shangchen Miao, Zhiyuan Shi, Kailu Wu, Hanyang Wang, Weiliang Chen, Qiyu Dai, Jinshan Ren, Jun Gao, Mingsheng Long, Yueqi Duan, Jiangran Lyu, Jialong Wu, Fangfu Liu — *MirroS, Tsinghua University, Peking University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.12374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12374) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12374) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.12374)
**Topics:** `agents`, `world-model`, `RL`, `eval`

![Hero figure](assets/2026-10-09/2610.12374.png)

### TL;DR

AgentGarten is a framework for growing agent capabilities by *procedurally generating* interactive worlds. Program-driven simulators or game engines produce layouts and dynamics; a shared neural renderer — trained with an *Adversarial Forcing* distillation objective — turns them into real-time visual observations. Agents learn by exploring the generated worlds and write playbooks that later agents extend.

### Key idea

Decouple **mechanism** (procedural game/sim engines) from **appearance** (a neural renderer distilled across many engines). Agents never touch the engine source — they interact through the rendered visual stream — but the engines provide ground-truth dynamics, physics, and task structure for free. "Adversarial Forcing" is the distillation trick that keeps the renderer temporally consistent under long autoregressive rollouts.

### Main finding

Across a sweep of procedurally generated worlds the agent population improves on open-ended exploration and task completion as more worlds and more playbook entries accumulate, with the shared renderer generalising to new engines without per-world fine-tuning.

### Why it matters

Agent benchmarks keep running out of problems. AgentGarten points at a pipeline where the environment supply grows faster than any curated benchmark can — procedural engines on one side, a single shared visual world model on the other, with agents and playbooks compounding in between. The same recipe could seed VLA, computer-use, and web-agent training without hand-authoring environments.

## 2. Learn2Play Bench

**Authors:** Yibo Li, Jinhang Qiu, Zhi Zheng, Qianyun Guo, Jiaying Wu, Shuo Ji, Bryan Hooi — *National University of Singapore*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.08215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08215) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.08215) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.08215)
**Topics:** `agents`, `eval`, `in-context`

### TL;DR

A text-game suite with deliberately *novel or counterintuitive* rules, built so LLM agents cannot win from pretraining alone — they have to learn from the game's own feedback. The authors measure how well agents accumulate experience across episodes and compare harness variants that keep full action/feedback transcripts against ones that compress them into summarised rules.

### Key idea

Design games whose dynamics are rare or inverted (e.g. unusual physics, reversed causality) so that pretrained priors mislead rather than help. Then test two memory shapes: a verbatim action+feedback log vs. an LLM-summarised rulebook. The verbatim log wins — compressing experience into rules loses information agents still need.

### Main finding

Keeping complete action/feedback records beats summarised rulebooks for cross-episode learning. Top human players still exceed the strongest agents at peak score. Harness changes (prompting layout, retrieval over history) can lift performance *and* cut inference cost at the same time.

### Why it matters

Most agent benchmarks are solvable from pretrained priors, which conflates "knowing the game" with "learning the game." Learn2Play isolates the second. The verbatim-vs-rulebook result also pushes back on the current fashion of compressing agent memory into rules — a reminder that lossy summarisation can erase the exact signal RL or in-context learning needs.

## 3. TokenRouter

**Authors:** Tianyu Fu, Tengxuan Liu, Ruoxi Wang, Yixin Dong, Yi Ge, Yichen You, Yu Wang — *Tsinghua NICS-EFC*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.12242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12242) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12242) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.12242)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`, `serving`

### TL;DR

Token-level LLM routing (choose a different model to produce the next token, inside the same decode) can trade cost for quality, but existing serving systems fight it: they synchronise generation on a single model and stall at every router switch. TokenRouter is a request-centric serving stack with per-model subservers, asynchronous dispatch, and a delayed-batching scheduler tuned from an explicit throughput model.

### Key idea

Flip the serving model: "request-centric programming, model-centric execution." Each request owns a dataflow across models; each model owns its own batched subserver. A delayed-batching policy, parameterised by a throughput-latency model, hides step-desynchronisation across models and keeps batches full even when routing decisions arrive asynchronously.

### Main finding

**2.01× to 64.15× higher decoding throughput** than existing serving systems on token-routed workloads, with the gap growing with router switching rate and model-pool size.

### Why it matters

Model-routing papers routinely report quality gains but rarely survive the move from offline simulation to real serving. TokenRouter removes that cliff. It also generalises: the delayed-batching scheduler maps onto any workload where different requests touch different model stages (speculative decoding draft/verify, MoE expert parallelism, prefill/decode disaggregation).

## 4. Trace2Env

**Authors:** (not fully listed on HF page) — *(affiliation unspecified)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.06100](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06100) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.06100) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.06100)
**Topics:** `agents`, `world-model`

### TL;DR

A learning-free framework that turns archived interaction traces into a reusable *environment worldbook*: action/state schemas, grounded transitions, and induced behavioural rules. At inference, an LLM agent consults the worldbook plus episode state to predict the next observation and persistent state change, with a shared harness committing or rejecting each step.

### Key idea

Treat traces as *programmable evidence* rather than imitation data. Decompose a worldbook into (i) schemas (what the actions and state look like), (ii) grounded evidence (observed transitions, demonstrations), and (iii) induced rules/constraints (abstract behavioural patterns). A world-model agent then reasons over this bundle at every step — no training needed.

### Main finding

Beats prompt-based language world models on next-observation fidelity across nine environments; in ALFWorld and SciWorld, actions chosen inside the simulator succeed more often when replayed in the real environment. Pure prompting can look fine inside the simulator while failing on replay — fidelity *and* downstream usefulness diverge.

### Why it matters

Replicas of deprecated terminals, enterprise tools, or games unlock agent training/evaluation when the original system is gone. Trace2Env formalises the pipeline from traces to a frozen worldbook, raising the bar on simulator fidelity above raw prompting while staying training-free — a cheap on-ramp to agent sandbox construction.

## 5. MiMo-V2.6: Scaling RL Towards Self-Improvement

**Authors:** MiMo Core Team + ~149 contributors (Xiaomi/MiMo team) — *MiMo / Xiaomi*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.11959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11959) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.11959) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.11959)
**Topics:** `RL`, `omni`, `post-training`, `MoE`

### TL;DR

Tech-report-scale description of scaling RL compute for an omni-modal MoE model: **larger asynchronous batches at up to 1M-token context**, more diverse environments (code / general reasoning / visual / cyber), and more accurate graders for long-horizon tasks. Training stability is improved by **freezing the MoE router** during RL and by explicit reward-hacking defences. Framework and environments are open-sourced.

### Key idea

Treat RL compute as a resource to scale along three orthogonal axes: *batch × context*, *environment diversity*, *grader accuracy*. Then plug the usual stability holes: a drifting MoE router during RL (fixed by freezing) and reward hacking (fixed by graders that spot it). The result is a reproducible, open-sourced recipe for frontier-scale RL post-training.

### Main finding

Pushes an omni-modal model forward with RL post-training that scales to 1M-token asynchronous rollouts. Reports improvements on code / general / visual / cyber benchmarks and makes the full RL framework, environments, and training dynamics open-source.

### Why it matters

The missing playbook from most "RL scales" claims is *stability* at frontier scale. MiMo-V2.6 documents concrete recipes (router freeze, grader design, async-batch rollouts) rather than the usual gestures, and open-sources the environment stack — a step closer to reproducible RL post-training outside the biggest labs. Likely to become a reference for omni-modal RL pipelines.

## 6. U-Space: Uncovering When and Why Uncertainty Arises

**Authors:** Nils Loose*, Alexander Herzog*, Virginia Ceccatelli, Marcus Rohrbach, Thomas Eisenbarth, Lorenzo Cavallaro — *TU Darmstadt, UCL, U. Lübeck, Mila, MBZUAI*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.09087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09087) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.09087) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.09087)
**Topics:** `interp`, `uncertainty`, `probing`

### TL;DR

A low-dimensional *U-Space* in a language model's residual stream that captures when the model is uncertain mid-generation. Built from a few semantic anchors for "doubt" and "certainty" — no labels, no repeated sampling, no training. The projection gives both a per-token uncertainty map (the U-Lens) and a scalar score; the score beats sampling- and probe-based baselines on reasoning benchmarks.

### Key idea

Collect activations on short "I'm sure…" / "I'm not sure…" style prompts and define a subspace from the difference. The space is small (a handful of dimensions) but surprisingly discriminative. Project new activations onto it to localise uncertainty in time, and threshold the scalar score for calibration.

### Main finding

Beats sampling-free uncertainty baselines on reasoning benchmarks under length-controlled evaluation, and generalises across models better than supervised probes trained on labelled data. The scalar score transfers; the full U-Lens visualises *where* in the generation uncertainty actually spikes.

### Why it matters

Confidence estimation at test time without sampling or probes is the holy grail for high-stakes LLM use. U-Space is the probing-era analogue of activation-steering: a few anchor prompts give you a cheap, portable signal, and the per-token lens is a diagnostic tool for debugging reasoning traces. Builds a bridge between interpretability (activation subspaces) and calibration.

## 7. MC-Sparse

**Authors:** Jiarui Chen, Zeqiang Lai, Jiangshan Wang, Ziheng Ouyang, Ye Huang, Xiangyu Yue, Cewu Lu, Chunchao Guo — *Fudan, Tencent HY, CUHK MMLab, PKU, SJTU, Shanghai Innovation Institute*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.06801](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06801) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.06801) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.06801)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `efficient-inference`, `attention`

### TL;DR

Deconstructs why sparse attention loses quality at high sparsity in diffusion transformers (DiTs) — token *grouping*, inaccurate interaction selection, and lost residual contributions — then closes the gap with **MC-Sparse**, a training-free method that selects individual key-value tokens, groups similar queries into tile-aligned sets, and caches metadata + residuals across denoising steps.

### Key idea

Three fixes layered together: (1) per-KV-token selection instead of coarse block pruning; (2) tile-aligned similar-query grouping so GPU kernels still run full tiles; (3) carry sparse-selection metadata and residual attention contributions across diffusion steps, since neighbouring denoising steps share most of their attention structure.

### Main finding

**1.80× denoising speedup** on Minimax-H3-Base and **2.32× speedup** on 3D asset generation, both at negligible quality loss — the dense-sparse gap at high sparsity disappears.

### Why it matters

Diffusion transformers eat quadratic attention cost like LLMs did two years ago, but sparse-attention tricks that work for autoregressive models underperform on DiTs because of their multi-step denoising structure. MC-Sparse is one of the first methods designed around that structure, and its cross-step metadata reuse is a general pattern that generalises to video diffusion and flow-matching models.

## 8. Post-Training Frontier T2I by Composing Preference + Rubric Rewards

**Authors:** I-Hung Hsu, Anastasios Angelopoulos, Wei-Lin Chiang, Ion Stoica, Cho-Jui Hsieh — *Arena Intelligence (and UCLA)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.02967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02967) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02967) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.02967)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `post-training`, `RL`, `rewards`

### TL;DR

Post-training recipe for frontier text-to-image models that composes two reward signals: a **Bradley–Terry preference reward** trained on arena human votes, and **rubric-based rewards** for prompt faithfulness, constraints, and reward-hacking checks. A reward composition strategy weights them against each other; the result is Elo gains on the Arena leaderboard.

### Key idea

No single reward captures image quality: preference models learn aesthetics/style but reward-hack on prompt adherence, while rubrics catch constraint violations but miss subtle preferences. Compose them explicitly — preference RM for ranking, rubric reward for correctness and hack detection — with a weighting scheme chosen on held-out arena data.

### Main finding

Reports Elo gains on the Chatbot Arena-equivalent for text-to-image, with ablations attributing improvement to the composition rather than to either reward alone.

### Why it matters

Image RLHF has drifted from "one preference RM" to "many weak signals" exactly like text RLHF a year earlier. This paper gives a concrete composition recipe grounded in arena data and names the failure modes (reward hacking in preference RMs, blind spots in rubrics). The pattern will transfer directly to video diffusion and multimodal generation.

## 9. SparseDecoding

**Authors:** Author list not fully disclosed on HF page (correspondence: wanghuan@westlake.edu.cn) — *Westlake University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.12327](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12327) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12327) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.12327)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`, `pruning`, `kernels`

### TL;DR

Observes that layer-wise Hessian pruning for LLMs calibrates on *fixed text*, which doesn't match the self-generated token stream seen at decoding. SparseDecoding builds calibration matrices from dense-model activations recorded *during* autoregressive generation (excluding prefill), and ships an N:M sparse matvec kernel with bitmask indexing and fixed-step traversal.

### Key idea

Decoding-aware calibration + decoding-aware kernels. Two fixes line up: (i) calibrate pruning against the distribution the pruned model will actually see, not against training text; (ii) write a sparse kernel optimised for the memory-bound regime of single-token decoding on A100 — bitmask indexing avoids per-row metadata loads, fixed-step traversal keeps DRAM access regular.

### Main finding

**Up to 1.48× end-to-end decoding speedup on A100** at similar quality to dense models, outperforming prior Hessian-pruning methods calibrated on fixed text.

### Why it matters

LLM decoding is memory-bandwidth-bound: every weight parameter has to be streamed in per token. Weight pruning only pays off if the sparse pattern lines up with real decode-time activations. This paper nails the calibration-distribution shift and ships kernels that convert the sparsity into wall-clock throughput — the two things most "weight pruning for LLMs" papers hand-wave over.

## 10. Memento 3: Recursive Self-Improvement through Reflective Rulebooks

**Authors:** Haoyu Zhao, Zhengxu Yu, Zhiyuan He, Meng Fang, Rasul Tutunov, Haitham Bou-Ammar, Weilin Luo, Jun Wang — *UCL, Huawei Noah's Ark Lab (UK), U. Liverpool*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.11794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11794) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.11794) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.11794)
**Topics:** `agents`, `world-model`, `in-context`

### TL;DR

Memento 3 lets a *frozen* LLM agent learn explicit world models at inference time. It maintains a natural-language **rulebook** of hypotheses about environment dynamics, **compiles** the rulebook into executable code, and iteratively revises both via observation → reflection → compilation → verification. A population-of-rulebooks extension keeps several candidate world models at once.

### Key idea

Externalise the world model to a text rulebook plus its compiled program. Reflection reads traces; compilation renders the rulebook into deterministic code; verification stress-tests the code against new rollouts. Weights never change — only the rulebook does. The population extension turns rulebook search into something closer to evolutionary program synthesis.

### Main finding

A single-world-model agent **solves all 25 public ARC-AGI-3 games**; a learned controller wins **21:0 across three Atari Pong episodes** without weight updates.

### Why it matters

Frozen-weight self-improvement is a crowded space, but most work in it is memory tricks. Memento 3 points at *programmatic* self-improvement — the agent is literally writing and refining code that models its environment — which gives it something CoT or memory alone can't: executable predictions it can test. The ARC-AGI-3 clearance is a nontrivial reasoning result from a frozen model.

## 11. On-Policy Distillation Teaches New Skills but Not New Knowledge

**Authors:** Yixuan Tang, Yi Yang — *(affiliation not listed on HF page)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.09639](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09639) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.09639) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.09639)
**Topics:** `post-training`, `distillation`

### TL;DR

Reverse-KL on-policy distillation reliably transfers **compositional skills** across unseen reasoning structures, but transfers almost **no factual knowledge**. The paper draws the dividing line empirically across skill vs. knowledge probes and argues that on-policy distillation should be positioned as a *behavioural* training tool, not a knowledge-transfer one.

### Key idea

Separate two capabilities often conflated in distillation claims: the ability to execute a reasoning pattern (skill) vs. recall of a specific fact (knowledge). On-policy distillation samples from the student, then nudges student logits toward teacher logits via reverse KL — a dynamic that preserves student-space coverage and transfers *procedure* while barely touching lexical/factual memory.

### Main finding

Across controlled benchmarks, on-policy reverse-KL distillation closes the gap with the teacher on reasoning-structure transfer while leaving factual-recall gaps essentially unchanged — a clean dissociation of the two kinds of transfer.

### Why it matters

A lot of "small model matches big model via distillation" claims silently mix skill and knowledge, which inflates expectations. The paper gives practitioners a concrete decision: use on-policy distillation when you want to teach new procedures (CoT structure, tool-use pattern); use SFT on labelled data (or raw continued pretraining) when you want to inject new facts. This should recalibrate the distillation literature.

## 12. ReSPO: Reshaped Sequence Policy Optimization

**Authors:** Yuanhao Ban, Cho-Jui Hsieh (and co-author "yhangchen") — *UCLA*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.35433](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35433) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35433) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.35433)
**Topics:** `RL`, `post-training`, `off-policy`

### TL;DR

Identifies a **sign-dependent gradient starvation** problem in clipped off-policy RL (RLVR with rollout reuse): PPO's clip ratio suppresses under-generated positive responses at the *low*-importance-weight tail, while allowing severely over-generated negative responses to dominate the *high*-weight tail. ReSPO replaces the clip with a smooth, two-branch, sequence-level kernel derived from an α-divergence variational objective plus an exponential variance-control tilt.

### Key idea

Two branches: the *positive* branch preserves nonzero gradient weight for under-generated positive responses, so good long reasoning trajectories aren't ignored once they fall into the low-weight tail; the *negative* branch suppresses heavily over-generated negatives so they don't dominate the gradient. Derived cleanly from an α-divergence RL objective rather than hand-crafted.

### Main finding

On dense and MoE Qwen3 models, ReSPO **accelerates early optimisation**, raises **final training scores**, and achieves **higher held-out benchmark performance under rollout reuse** — i.e., keeps RLVR tractable when rollouts stay off-policy longer.

### Why it matters

Rollout reuse is the single biggest cost knob in RLVR — reusing rollouts across more policy updates means you can afford to generate more or longer trajectories. But reuse amplifies the exact gradient-starvation failure PPO's clip was never designed for. ReSPO's α-divergence re-derivation is the kind of principled fix that could become a drop-in replacement for GRPO's clipped surrogate in long-rollout settings.

## 13. SpecFold

**Authors:** (not listed on HF page) — *(affiliation unspecified)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.04875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04875) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.04875) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.04875)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`, `speculative-decoding`, `diffusion`

### TL;DR

Diffusion language models verify many candidate branches at once during speculative decoding; a lot of per-branch compute is redundant with the parent. SpecFold adds **token-level residual gating** that reuses parent computation whenever a child branch barely changes it, and backs it with a **Triton kernel** that converts the saved work into real throughput.

### Key idea

The multi-branch verification tree has lots of near-duplicate paths. Fold them: a learned gate decides, per token, whether to recompute or reuse the parent's hidden state. Residual gating + a hand-written Triton kernel that handles the irregular reuse pattern is what makes the saving show up as wall-clock speedup rather than FLOP accounting.

### Main finding

**Up to 1.64× throughput over Spiffy** and **1.99× over vanilla decoding** across two model families, five diffusion LMs, and five benchmarks, with comparable task performance.

### Why it matters

Speculative decoding for diffusion LMs is still rough — unlike autoregressive, the "guess, verify in parallel" step has to resolve several candidate futures. SpecFold is the first speedup clearly targeted at the diffusion-LM structure rather than ported from AR speculative decoding, and the gating idea generalises to any multi-branch rollout (search, best-of-N verification, tree-of-thought).

## 14. AgentMonBench + EBG

**Authors:** Zhongxiang Sun, Jiahao Yan, Hongkang Zhao, Haojie Ding, Boheng Zhang, Fan Yang, Xiao Zhang, Jun Xu — *Renmin University of China (RUC-GSAI-IIRLab)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.06406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06406) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.06406) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.06406)
**Topics:** `agents`, `safety`, `oversight`, `eval`

### TL;DR

A software-engineering benchmark + training-free method for *monitoring* long-horizon agents. **AgentMonBench** scores whether a monitor can (a) spot the consequential autonomous decisions in a long task, and (b) find the evidence a user would need to verify them. **EBG** (Evidence-Grounded Behavior Graph) groups source-linked evidence into behaviours and organises their relationships into a graph; task-oriented views of the graph are what monitors see.

### Key idea

Decouple *what happened* (the behaviour graph grounded in source) from *what you look at* (task-oriented graph views). The graph is training-free: it's built from already-attributable trace evidence. Views let monitors ignore irrelevant branches without losing the ability to drill into them.

### Main finding

Across eight models, EBG improves consequential-decision identification and evidence localisation versus feeding the raw context. Gains survive input-size and hyperparameter changes; case studies show practical oversight value.

### Why it matters

Long-horizon agents produce more decisions than any human reviewer can read. AgentMonBench is one of the first serious benchmarks for *the oversight problem itself* (not just task success), and EBG proposes a concrete data structure for an oversight UI. Expect the EBG template to leak into agent-safety tooling.

## 15. REMORY: Residual Memory for Context Compaction

**Authors:** Hanchen Xia, Baoyou Chen, Yutang Ge, Naihao Deng, Senqiao Yang, Zilong Dong, Weihao Yuan, Siyu Zhu — *(affiliation not listed on HF page)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.11287](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11287) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.11287) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.11287)
**Topics:** `agents`, `efficient-inference`, `memory`

### TL;DR

Textual summaries lose information agents need. REMORY trains a **small neural memory network** to append a bounded sequence of **soft memory tokens** after the summary; conditioned on the summary and history, these tokens help a *frozen* LLM approximate the continuation it would have produced with the full context.

### Key idea

Treat memory as a residual connection along the sequence dimension: summary + soft tokens ≈ full history. The soft tokens never touch the base model's weights — the memory network is small and trainable. The whole bundle plugs into any frozen LLM as a drop-in replacement for naive summarisation.

### Main finding

On SummHay, REMORY improves source attribution with near-unchanged insight coverage and approaches the full-context joint score using only **5.2% of the input positions**. Across long-horizon agent benchmarks (Qwen3.8-27B, GLM-5.3-Flash), it lifts task scores and reduces repeated tool outputs and tool errors on BrowseComp and Terminal-Bench 2.1.

### Why it matters

Agent context windows are capped; summarisation is lossy. REMORY is one of the first long-horizon-agent papers to treat memory compaction as a *trainable soft-token problem* rather than a prompt-engineering problem. The residual framing is elegant and should transfer to KV compression and prefix-tuning literature.

## 16. Learning to Steer, Steering to See: Geometry of RLVR

**Authors:** Yuchen Cai, Ding Cao, Qixiang Yin, Xin Xu, Kai Yang, Siye Wu, Pengyuan Wang, Jiaxuan Wang, Weijie Liu, Saiyong Yang, Guangzhong Sun, Guiquan Liu, Junfeng Fang — *Tencent Hunyuan*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.34344](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34344) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.34344) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.34344)
**Topics:** `interp`, `RL`, `steering`

### TL;DR

Uses **trainable steering vectors** as a diagnostic lens on RLVR. Two geometric claims: (a) the capacity needed to recover RLVR gains lives on a *very low-dimensional* manifold in activation space — but not infinitely compressible; (b) the effective control directions lie in the **low-variance complement** of the activation principal subspace, not along the top PCs. Introduces **Alpha-Stabler**, a plug-and-play monitor that stabilises RL training by watching this subspace.

### Key idea

Instead of asking "what weights change under RLVR," ask "what minimal activation perturbation reproduces the RLVR gain?" Fit trainable steering vectors and inspect their geometry. The low-dim-but-not-trivial result predicts both the ceiling (why ultra-low-rank adapters eventually plateau) and the surprising principal-subspace locus (why big PC-aligned moves don't help).

### Main finding

The RL gain lives in a small, specific subspace orthogonal to high-variance activation directions; Alpha-Stabler monitors this subspace online and improves training stability as a lightweight add-on to standard RLVR runs.

### Why it matters

A geometric explanation for why RLVR can look "easy" to replicate with LoRA/steering yet still fail without the right directions — and a concrete monitor that converts the diagnostic into training infrastructure. This blurs the interpretability/post-training boundary in a productive way.
