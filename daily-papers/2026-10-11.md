## 1. AgentGarten

**Authors:** Jiawei Chi, Shangchen Miao, Zhiyuan Shi, Kailu Wu, Hanyang Wang, Weiliang Chen, Qiyu Dai, Jinshan Ren, Jun Gao, Mingsheng Long, Yueqi Duan, Jiangran Lyu, Jialong Wu, Fangfu Liu — *MirroS, Tsinghua University, Peking University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.12374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12374) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12374) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.12374)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `world-models`, `multimodal`

### TL;DR

A platform for growing agent populations inside procedurally-generated, visually photorealistic "code worlds." Game engines handle state, physics and rules while a distilled real-time video model renders observations from structured conditions, so agents can act from pixels in novel environments without rebuilding a visual pipeline per world. Experience is passed to successors through natural-language playbooks.

### Key idea

Pair a symbolic simulator (engine of record for state and rules) with a shared **neural renderer** — a video diffusion model distilled with *Adversarial Forcing* that runs in real time — so one perception stack generalises across arbitrary code worlds. Lessons from one lineage of agents are compiled into playbooks that seed the next generation, turning experience into transmissible curriculum.

### Main finding

Agents reach competitive task performance after only **4 rounds** of experience, versus millions of environment episodes needed by conventional RL baselines. The authors cover a wide set of code worlds (platformers, puzzle environments, 3D navigation) with the same shared renderer.

### Why it matters

Attacks the central bottleneck of agent RL — environment coverage — by decoupling physics-truthful simulation from photorealistic observation generation. If the "one renderer, many engines" formula scales, it reframes agent training as *writing more code worlds*, not *rendering more pixels*.

## 2. MiMo-V2.6

**Authors:** Xiaomi MiMo Core Team (149 contributors) — *Xiaomi MiMo*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.11959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11959) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.11959) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.11959)
**Topics:** `RL`, `agents`, `multimodal`, `MoE`, `reward-hacking`

### TL;DR

An omni-modal frontier model trained by scaling agentic RL compute along three axes at once: asynchronous training batches up to 1M-token contexts, mixed environment classes (code, general, visual, cyber) with multiple agent harnesses, and groupwise agentic grading for better long-horizon rewards. The report also documents a stability recipe — frozen MoE router, multi-layer reward-hacking defense — and open-sources the training dynamics, environments, and framework.

### Key idea

Treat RL post-training as a three-axis scaling problem: (1) **context length**, batching rollouts of up to 1M tokens asynchronously; (2) **environment diversity**, mixing code / general-task / visual / cyber environments under different agent scaffolds; (3) **reward precision**, using groupwise agentic grading (one grader scoring a group of rollouts jointly) to tighten reward signal on long-horizon trajectories. For stability: freeze the MoE router during RL and stack defenses against reward hacking.

### Main finding

A full tech report of an omni-modal RL system. Headline claim is that these axes compound — scaling along any single one plateaus — and that frozen-router + layered hack defense is what makes the long runs survive. Full training dynamics, RL environments and framework are released.

### Why it matters

A rare published recipe for *agentic* RL at frontier scale, with concrete answers to the two things that break big RL runs: router drift in MoE policies and reward hacking in mixed environments. The 1M-token async rollout infrastructure is itself a contribution worth copying.

## 3. On-Policy Distillation Teaches New Skills but Not New Knowledge

**Authors:** Yixuan Tang, Yi Yang — *HKUST (inferred)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.09639](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09639) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.09639) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.09639)
**Topics:** `post-training`, `distillation`, `reasoning`, `interp`

### TL;DR

A controlled study that separates **compositional skill** from **factual knowledge** during distillation. Reverse-KL on-policy distillation (OPD) reliably transfers reasoning structure across unseen compositions, but almost no new factual knowledge enters the student. Switching to forward-KL restores factual transfer. The widely-reported math gains from OPD happen without parametric knowledge expansion.

### Key idea

Build a synthetic benchmark where facts and compositional reasoning structure can be independently controlled, then run on-policy distillation with reverse-KL vs forward-KL. Reverse-KL concentrates mass on high-probability student trajectories, which copies *behavior patterns* (skills) but misses the long tail of factual edits.

### Main finding

Reverse-KL OPD transfers compositional reasoning to new structures while leaving factual recall ≈ untouched. Forward-KL OPD recovers factual transfer at the cost of some skill-generalisation. Math-competition gains show up without any factual-memory expansion, implying the gains are skill-only.

### Why it matters

Clarifies when on-policy distillation suffices and when it does not: use OPD to teach reasoning *procedures*; use another mechanism (continued pre-training, retrieval, forward-KL) to install new facts. The KL-direction choice becomes a design knob with a concrete semantic.

## 4. TokenRouter

**Authors:** Tianyu Fu, Tengxuan Liu, Ruoxi Wang, Yixin Dong, Yi Ge, Yichen You, Yu Wang — *Tsinghua NICS-EFC*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.12242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12242) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12242) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.12242)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`, `systems`, `routing`

### TL;DR

A serving system that pushes LLM routing from the request level down to **individual tokens**. Developers write routing policy per request; the runtime holds one subserver per model and uses delayed batching to maintain high throughput despite the fine-grained traffic pattern. Reports 2.01×–64.15× decoding-throughput wins over existing serving systems.

### Key idea

Separate *request-centric programming* from *model-centric execution*. The programmer writes "which model does this token" callbacks; the scheduler delays batching just long enough to form homogeneous model micro-batches, so token-level routing stops thrashing the attention and KV-cache paths that model-level routing usually breaks.

### Main finding

2.01× to **64.15×** higher decoding throughput than baselines across models and workloads, with task quality preserved.

### Why it matters

Token-level routing (strong model for the hard tokens, cheap model for everything else) has been attractive on paper and painful in practice because current servers can't batch it. If this holds up, cascaded inference and expert-choice routing become actually deployable.

## 5. SparseDecoding

**Authors:** Not listed on HF page — *Westlake University (inferred from corresponding email)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.12327](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12327) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12327) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.12327)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`, `sparsity`, `quantization`

### TL;DR

Training-free weight pruning for LLM decoding, calibrated on **decode-time activations** instead of fixed text. Paired with an N:M sparse matrix-vector kernel using bitmask indexing. Delivers up to 1.48× end-to-end decoding speedup on A100s across Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.3-70B and Qwen3-14B/32B, with better long-form generation than fixed-text-calibrated pruning.

### Key idea

Decoding is memory-bound and self-generated tokens have a different activation distribution than fixed calibration text. Build the pruning mask from *actual decode-time activations* collected during warmup, then store the N:M sparsity pattern as a bitmask that a custom GEMV kernel can skip over cheaply.

### Main finding

Up to **1.48×** end-to-end decoding speedup, with higher quality than naive pre-trained-text calibration on WritingBench and ClassEval — holds across 8B and 70B models.

### Why it matters

Shows that *what you calibrate on* matters as much as the pruning algorithm for autoregressive LLMs. Low-hanging improvement for anyone running N:M sparse inference.

## 6. SpecFold

**Authors:** Not listed on HF page
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.04875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04875) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.04875) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.04875)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`, `speculative-decoding`, `diffusion-LM`

### TL;DR

Speculative decoding for diffusion LLMs: verify a main branch and several draft branches in a single forward pass, then **reuse** the parent branch's attention / FFN computation at positions where the draft hardly changed. A token-level residual gate decides what to reuse; a Triton kernel makes the sharing a real speedup. Up to 1.64× over Spiffy and 1.99× over vanilla decoding.

### Key idea

Draft branches share most of their tokens with the parent, so most positions are redundant work. Add a per-position gate that reuses the parent's attention+FFN activations when the residual is below threshold, while each branch still keeps its own residual stream. Orthogonal to temporal caching, composes with existing speculation strategies.

### Main finding

Up to **1.64×** throughput vs Spiffy and **1.99×** vs vanilla across 2 model families, 5 models, 5 benchmarks, with comparable task performance.

### Why it matters

Diffusion LMs were the odd ones out in the speculative-decoding literature — multi-branch verification seemed to double compute. SpecFold shows the redundancy is huge and reclaimable.

## 7. MC-Sparse

**Authors:** Jiarui Chen, Zeqiang Lai, Jiangshan Wang, Ziheng Ouyang, Ye Huang, Xiangyu Yue, Cewu Lu, Chunchao Guo — *Tencent HY / Fudan / CUHK / SJTU / PKU / Nankai / Shanghai Innovation Institute*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.06801](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06801) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.06801) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.06801)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `efficient-inference`, `attention`

### TL;DR

A diagnosis-and-fix for sparse attention in diffusion transformers (video, 3D). Oracle experiments pin the quality loss on three sources — token grouping constraints, inaccurate interaction selection, and missing attention mass — and MC-Sparse addresses each: individual key-value token selection, tile-aligned query grouping, and cached metadata/residuals reused across denoising steps.

### Key idea

Previous DiT sparse attention lost quality because it (1) grouped tokens into blocks before selecting, (2) picked wrong interactions, (3) threw away the attention mass of discarded tokens. MC-Sparse: pick individual KV tokens, group queries into tile-aligned sets for GPU efficiency, and reuse metadata + residuals across diffusion steps to recover the discarded mass.

### Main finding

Higher fidelity to dense attention than sparse baselines, with **1.80×** speedup on Minimax-H3-Base and **2.32×** on 3D asset generation.

### Why it matters

DiT inference latency is the main obstacle for interactive video/3D generation; closing the dense–sparse gap at high sparsity directly makes those pipelines deployable.

## 8. Rubric-Rewards for Text-to-Image

**Authors:** I-Hung Hsu, Anastasios Angelopoulos, Wei-Lin Chiang, Ion Stoica, Cho-Jui Hsieh — *Arena Intelligence Inc. / UCLA*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.02967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02967) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02967) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.02967)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `RLHF`, `rewards`, `multimodal`

### TL;DR

RLHF post-training for frontier text-to-image models that **composes** a learned human-preference reward with multiple rubric-based rewards (prompt faithfulness, no-extraneous-additions, anti-reward-hacking checks). A naive weighted sum fails; a non-linear composition keeps all signals active without collapsing to any one. Produces a FLUX.2-dev variant +69 Elo over base and an Ideogram-4 variant that tops the open-source Arena leaderboard.

### Key idea

No single reward captures all human preference, so compose preference models with explicit rubric graders where each rubric checks a specific failure mode (unrequested additions, instruction skipping, hacking patterns). The composition rule is explicitly non-additive — naive weighted sums are shown to under-perform.

### Main finding

**+69 Elo** over the base FLUX.2-dev; post-trained Ideogram-4 reaches **1223.5 Elo**, highest among open-source models on the Arena T2I leaderboard. Release includes the 1K-prompt **Arena-T2I-Training** subset.

### Why it matters

Transfers the composable-reward playbook from text LLM post-training into frontier T2I, and gives a cheap diagnostic (rubrics) for the reward-hacking problem that has haunted preference-only pipelines.

## 9. REMORY

**Authors:** Hanchen Xia, Baoyou Chen, Yutang Ge, Naihao Deng, Senqiao Yang, Zilong Dong, Weihao Yuan, Siyu Zhu — *Fudan University / SII (Fusion Lab)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.11287](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11287) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.11287) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.11287)
**Topics:** `agents`, `efficient-inference`, `memory`

### TL;DR

A neural memory module that augments textual history summaries with a bounded sequence of **soft memory tokens**, letting a frozen LLM behave as if it saw the full un-summarised history. Improves source attribution without hurting insight coverage and reduces repeated tool calls / errors on long-horizon agent benchmarks.

### Key idea

Textual summaries drop detail the model needed; raw history exceeds the context. REMORY learns a residual: a short sequence of continuous memory tokens trained to make a frozen LLM's output distribution match what it would have produced with the full history. Attach to the summary at inference time.

### Main finding

Better source attribution on **SummHay** at ≈ unchanged insight coverage; fewer repeated tool outputs and errors on **BrowseComp** and **Terminal-Bench 2.1**. Backbone stays frozen.

### Why it matters

Context compaction has lived between "lossy summary" and "no compaction" with no middle ground. A bounded, learned residual over a summary is a practical, cheap compromise for long-horizon agents.

## 10. Memento 3

**Authors:** Haoyu Zhao, Zhengxu Yu, Zhiyuan He, Meng Fang, Rasul Tutunov, Haitham Bou-Ammar, Weilin Luo, Jun Wang — *UCL / Huawei Noah's Ark Lab, UK / U. Liverpool*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.11794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11794) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.11794) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.11794)
**Topics:** `agents`, `world-models`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

Frozen LLM agents learn an **explicit world model** by maintaining a natural-language rulebook of hypotheses that is also compiled into executable prediction/planning code. Prediction errors trigger refinements, accepted only when the LLM certifies the code faithful and replay reproduces observed transitions. A population extension carries several world models in parallel.

### Key idea

Represent the world model twice — once as human-readable rulebook, once as compiled code — and only accept an update when both the LLM-as-judge and a replay test agree the code matches the rulebook and reproduces observations. The dual representation lets symbolic planning run against the compiled model while the natural-language form stays debuggable.

### Main finding

Clears **all 25 public ARC-AGI-3 games** with mean RHAE **100.0** using **44%** of the human action count; an Atari-Pong case study trains a controller that wins **21:0** across three evaluated episodes with no further LLM calls.

### Why it matters

A concrete recipe for recursive self-improvement on frozen base models — no fine-tuning, just a disciplined write-run-verify loop over an external, inspectable world model.

## 11. Trace2Env

**Authors:** Xiao Chen, Jianda Chen, Haozhen Zhang, Qisheng Hu, Jianzhu Bao, Wenya Wang — *HK PolyU / Nanyang Technological University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.06100](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06100) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.06100) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.06100)
**Topics:** `agents`, `world-models`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Build a reusable "environment worldbook" from historical interaction traces, so a world-model agent can stand in for an unavailable environment. Training-free: at runtime, the world-model agent consults the worldbook plus persistent episodic state to predict observations and lasting state effects. Beats prompt-based language world models on 9 environments.

### Key idea

Ingest raw agent-environment traces, distill them into a structured "worldbook" indexed by state and action patterns, and consult it at inference time. The persistent episodic state carries durable side effects across the simulation. All without training the base LLM.

### Main finding

Improves next-observation fidelity and long-horizon consistency over prompt-based language world models across 9 environments.

### Why it matters

Lets teams build training replicas of production systems from logs alone, without reconstructing the backend — potentially a huge unlock for RL-training agents against proprietary APIs or defunct services.

## 12. Learn2Play Bench

**Authors:** Yibo Li, Jinhang Qiu, Zhi Zheng, Qianyun Guo, Jiaying Wu, Shuo Ji, Bryan Hooi — *National University of Singapore*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.08215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08215) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.08215) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.08215)
**Topics:** `agents`, `eval`, `in-context-learning`

### TL;DR

A benchmark of text-based games with **novel or counterintuitive** rules, forcing LLM agents to learn by interaction. Three findings: (1) keeping complete action/feedback histories beats compressing them into rules, (2) top humans still beat all tested agents at peak score and strategy diversity, (3) swapping the agent *harness* at fixed backbone can simultaneously raise score and cut inference cost.

### Key idea

Novelty-first design: games whose rules don't match any plausible prior make retrieved knowledge useless, isolating the agent's ability to *learn from experience in context*. Then run matched ablations on history representation and on the surrounding harness.

### Main finding

(a) Raw history beats summarised rules; (b) agents trail humans in peak score and strategy diversity (humans repeat actions less); (c) harness changes alone recover large gains at lower cost than a model swap.

### Why it matters

Clean separation between "the model learns" and "the harness lets it." A reusable benchmark design for the broader "learning-from-experience" research direction, with concrete methodological lessons already baked in.

## 13. AgentMonBench

**Authors:** Zhongxiang Sun, Jiahao Yan, Hongkang Zhao, Haojie Ding, Boheng Zhang, Fan Yang, Xiao Zhang, Jun Xu — *Renmin University of China GSAI*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.06406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06406) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.06406) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.06406)
**Topics:** `safety`, `agents`, `eval`, `oversight`

### TL;DR

A software-engineering benchmark for monitoring long-horizon agent runs along two axes (requirements-vs-behavior alignment, and awareness of consequential autonomous decisions). Pairs the benchmark with the **Evidence-Grounded Behavior Graph (EBG)**: a training-free way to group source-linked evidence into behaviors and present task-oriented graph views that help human monitors find decisions worth verifying.

### Key idea

Oversight shifts from per-decision approval to post-hoc audit as agents take on longer tasks. EBG organises raw execution traces into a graph of *behaviors* with evidence links, letting monitors ask "what did the agent actually do here and why" without reading megabytes of transcript.

### Main finding

Across 8 models, EBG improves both decision identification and evidence localisation vs direct raw-context access; gains persist across input scales and hyperparameters.

### Why it matters

The scalable-oversight problem has concrete tooling now: a reusable benchmark and a drop-in method for human monitors reviewing autonomous SWE agents.

## 14. U-Space

**Authors:** Nils Loose, Alexander Herzog, Virginia Ceccatelli, Marcus Rohrbach, Thomas Eisenbarth, Lorenzo Cavallaro — *TU Darmstadt / UCL / U. Lübeck / Mila / MBZUAI*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.09087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09087) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.09087) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.09087)
**Topics:** `interp`, `uncertainty`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

A low-dimensional **subspace of activations** that tracks how an LLM's uncertainty evolves during generation. A readout (**U-Lens**) produces token-level uncertainty maps and a scalar score. No correctness labels, no repeated sampling, no training — and the scalar beats established uncertainty baselines on reasoning benchmarks, including under length-controlled evaluation.

### Key idea

Instead of estimating uncertainty from output distributions or sampling consistency, locate it in **activation space** as a bounded subspace whose coordinates predict calibrated uncertainty. Project activations into U-Space to read uncertainty off directly, token by token.

### Main finding

Beats established uncertainty baselines on reasoning benchmarks under standard **and** length-controlled evaluation; transfers across models more reliably than supervised uncertainty estimators.

### Why it matters

A cheap, label-free uncertainty signal that drops into existing decoding stacks — useful for selective prediction, abstention, and reasoning-step gating. Interpretability tooling with a direct product use.
