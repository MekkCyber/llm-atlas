## 1. Jeva: System One Models from First Principles

**Authors:** Li Qi, Xinchao Wang, et al. — *National University of Singapore*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.18773](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18773) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18773) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.18773)
**Topics:** `LLM`, `reasoning`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

Jeva reframes "system one" (fast, low-latency) LLM decisions as a *typed decision* problem: given a request and a candidate set, pick the applicable option or declare none applies. Instead of serializing options into a prompt, Jeva encodes the request and each option *separately*, then refines the request state against the option set, giving option-order invariance, scalability in candidate count, abstention, and reusable per-option computation.

### Key idea

Separate the request encoder from the option encoder (a cross-attention refinement loop), with an explicit "no option applies" head. Because option embeddings are request-independent, they can be cached across requests — a form of **cross-request KV reuse** specialized to retrieval-flavored decisions.

### Main finding

Strong accuracy on typed-decision benchmarks, exact permutation-invariance under tested reorderings, reliable null detection, and large latency reductions attributed to option-side caching.

### Why it matters

Most "fast path" LLM pipelines today abuse serialized prompts for what is really a structured decision — paying O(options) per request. Jeva argues for an architectural reset: build the fast path around the decision shape, not around chatty generation.

## 2. Agent-Reflex: Teaching Language Agents to Act Reflectively

**Authors:** Xinyu Yang, Jiajie Jin, Yuyang Hu, et al. — *Renmin University of China / NUS*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.17953](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17953) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.17953) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.17953)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `SFT`

### TL;DR

A three-stage recipe that trains a language agent to *detect* that a prior tool call was noisy or wrong, *reflect* on the discrepancy, and recover without restarting the trajectory. Stages: (i) reflection-skill discovery from noisy tool-call traces, (ii) reflective SFT on the discovered skills, (iii) on-policy *reflective* policy optimization.

### Key idea

Treat "noisy tool feedback" as a first-class training signal rather than noise to be filtered. The reflective SFT dataset contains explicit *correction turns* that supervise the model to notice a bad response and amend its plan; RL then rewards trajectories whose reflection step improves downstream success.

### Main finding

On Qwen3-8B: 58.6 overall across five long-horizon tool-use benchmarks and 29.1 on **AgentNoiseBench**, outperforming standard agentic SFT and vanilla RL.

### Why it matters

Agents fail in the real world not because they can't call tools, but because they can't tell when the tool returned garbage. Agent-Reflex is one of the first training-side (not prompting-side) treatments of this failure mode.

## 3. VideoJev: System One Model for Video Understanding and Interaction

**Authors:** Xiangyu Zeng, Qingyi Si, et al. — *NJU / JD.com / UCAS / THU / XMU / SJTU / USTC / FDU*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.18615](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18615) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18615) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.18615)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `video`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

Applies the Jeva "typed-decision" idea to video: a shared scoring head handles both temporal grounding (score an interval) and multiple-choice video QA (score an option). Visual computation is reused across requests via *Cached inference* and *Joint inference* modes.

### Key idea

Reparametrize two video tasks that today use different heads as the same *option-scoring* problem over a shared embedding, so the heavy vision tower is amortized across both.

### Main finding

Beats single-task MLLM baselines on grounding and multi-choice QA while cutting per-request compute via cross-request visual caching.

### Why it matters

Video MLLMs are brutally expensive at inference. Treating decision and grounding as the same scoring op opens a path to serving them from one cached vision pass.

## 4. Tev: Scaling Long-Horizon Agentic Environments with Dense Progress Rewards

**Authors:** Wenbo Hu, Krishna Kumar Singh, Nanyun Peng, Kai-Wei Chang, Yong Jae Lee — *Adobe Research / UCLA*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.18868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18868) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18868) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.18868)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `rewards`

### TL;DR

Open-sources a 1,170-environment long-horizon agent dataset (**Tev-Gym**) built from a hidden "latent world" generator, plus a **PPRS** (per-step progress reward) dense signal for RL training. A 27B agent trained with the recipe matches GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5 at Pass@5 on ALE-CM and generalizes to Terminal-Bench subsets.

### Key idea

The missing piece for long-horizon RL isn't the algorithm — it's a *verifiable, dense* reward. Tev generates each environment with a hidden ground-truth state the reward model can read, letting it score progress at every step instead of only at termination. Sparse-reward RL becomes dense-reward RL.

### Main finding

Tev-27B at Pass@5: on par with frontier closed models on ALE-CM; generalizes to held-out Terminal-Bench tasks. Scale: 1,170 verified environments, each with a progress oracle.

### Why it matters

Dense progress rewards fix the credit-assignment problem that has stalled long-horizon agent RL. The environment generator is as important as the policy algorithm.

## 5. Harness²: Recursive Agent Harnessing for an Open World

**Authors:** Yanfei Chen, Zhongying CuiZhu, Bhavana Dalvi Mishra, et al. — *Google Cloud AI Research / Northwestern*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.14224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14224) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.14224) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.14224)
**Topics:** `agents`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

A plug-and-play framework in which an agent *refines its own harness* at two levels: (i) task-level — a contrastive Propose–Probe–Compose loop that improves the current harness without verifiers, and (ii) harnessing-level — the refinement experience is itself distilled into a continual-improvement meta-harness.

### Key idea

Treat "the scaffold around the model" (prompts, tools, routers, retries) as a learnable artifact. Propose candidate harness changes, probe them on local traces, compose the winners — no ground-truth verifier required.

### Main finding

Gains across three professional benchmarks and four model–harness configurations, including bringing weaker base models within range of stronger ones under the same budget.

### Why it matters

Most agent papers improve the model and freeze the scaffold; this one freezes the model and improves the scaffold. It opens an orthogonal axis of improvement for deployed agents.

## 6. GreenWAM: A World Foundation Model for Robotics

**Authors:** Gennady Sidorov, Valerii Kornilov, Denis Grankin, Polina Fedotova, Aleksey Postnikov — *Sber Robotics Center*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.18774](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18774) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18774) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.18774)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `VLA`, `agents`

*Borderline: included because GreenWAM is a shared-action-space VLA foundation model trained jointly across 20 robot families — within scope for multimodal/foundation-model work even though downstream users are roboticists.*

### TL;DR

A world+action foundation model with a shared action space covering 20 robot families. Trained jointly on world-modeling and policy modes, with an *action expert* decoupled from the generator so the same model serves rollout prediction and policy execution.

### Key idea

Factor the model into a generator (predicts the next observation given state+action) and an action expert (predicts actions given state). Shared action space across embodiments means transfer across robot families is finetuning-free.

### Main finding

Top on WorldArena 1.0 for world modeling; competitive on RoboTwin 2.0, CALVIN, LIBERO; higher real-humanoid success than policies adapted from π0.5 and Cosmos 3 Edge.

### Why it matters

A credible open-source counterweight to closed VLA stacks, with the action-expert factorization pointing at a cleaner way to serve one foundation model as both planner and controller.

## 7. AesCode: Aesthetic Code Generation with Decoupled Cross-Modal Rewards

**Authors:** Xin Gao, Yifan Yang, Xin Li, et al. — *Microsoft / Yale / NTU / Pitt / SJTU / Fudan*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.18860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18860) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18860) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.18860)
**Topics:** `RL`, `rewards`, `multimodal`

### TL;DR

Trains an LLM to generate code (SVG, Manim, HTML/CSS, Three.js, etc.) that renders into visually *aesthetic* outputs, by splitting the reward into (i) a correctness signal on the code and (ii) a cross-modal aesthetic signal on the rendered image. The two rewards are optimized without entangling them.

### Key idea

Instead of one monolithic reward that averages correctness and beauty (and gets gamed), *decouple* them: optimize correctness as a verifier-style reward on code, and aesthetic preference as a separate reward on the rendered artifact. A policy update step then combines gradients without letting one reward override the other.

### Main finding

AesCode wins pairwise aesthetic preference against GPT-4-class code generators on multiple visual-code benchmarks, without regressing on correctness.

### Why it matters

Decoupled cross-modal rewards generalize beyond "aesthetic code": any task where you want to reward correctness *and* a human-preference signal computed in a different modality fits this template.

## 8. TPPO: Tracking-Value PPO For Language Model Reasoning

**Authors:** Chenxi Li, Jianhao Yan, Yun Luo, et al. — *Zhejiang U. / Westlake / Shanghai AI Lab / HKUST / NJU / SJTU / NTU*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.16794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16794) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.16794) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.16794)
**Topics:** `RL`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

Replaces PPO's single value head with a *tracking value* — a Bayesian decomposition of value change across the reasoning trajectory, in log-odds space. Position-wise outputs are summed into value logits, trained on terminal outcomes, giving a dense but outcome-anchored critic.

### Key idea

Rather than regressing V(s) at each step from Monte-Carlo returns, model the *delta* in log-odds-of-success at each token. The critic becomes an increment accumulator; terminal supervision alone is enough.

### Main finding

On Qwen3-4B-Base and 8B-Base, TPPO beats vanilla PPO on math reasoning benchmarks, with better OOD generalization and gains on non-math tasks.

### Why it matters

A clean fix for PPO's long-trajectory credit-assignment pain, without needing process rewards or step-level labels — a middle path between RLVR and PRM-style training.

## 9. DoSA: Dots Sparse Attention

**Authors:** (not disclosed in content) — *Likely associated with the DeepSeek-V3.2 / GLM-5.2 serving stack*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.13990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13990) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.13990) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.13990)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`, `sparse-attention`

### TL;DR

A **lossless** sparse-attention indexer that still returns the *exact* Top-K keys but scores far fewer candidates. Builds a certificate (seed-set lower bound + nonnegative-head upper bounds + activation-informed aggregation) that proves a candidate cannot be Top-K, letting it be rejected without full scoring.

### Key idea

Top-K selection doesn't need every candidate's exact score — only enough evidence to reject the losers. DoSA formalizes "enough evidence" as a certificate and only fully scores candidates whose certificate doesn't rule them out.

### Main finding

Up to **2.74× speedup over SGLang** and **1.69× over LiteTopK** for the indexer step. End-to-end: **1.94× TTFT on GLM-5.2** and **1.50× on DeepSeek-V3.2** over the better of the two baselines. English, Chinese, and code workloads.

### Why it matters

A rare "pure win" — same selected tokens, same loss, less compute. Likely to be adopted into the next generation of sparse-attention serving stacks.

## 10. Scoring Higher, Answering Worse — ProRubric

**Authors:** Maoqi Liu, Junwei He, Bowen Zhang, Feiran Li, Wentao Ma, Rongyi Lin, Shuhan Zhong, Quan Fang — *BUPT / ByteDance*
**Links:** [arXiv:2609.38847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38847) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38847) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.38847)
**Topics:** `RL`, `rewards`, `safety`

### TL;DR

Shows that **additive aggregation** in rubric-based RL creates a reward-hacking exploit: the policy can lose the one criterion that matters and buy the points back on padding criteria. In a clinical-consultation benchmark, this makes **coverage go up while appropriateness goes down**, below even the untrained model. **ProRubric** fixes it by grouping criteria into *protocol-level dimensions* that must *all* hold together, and only aggregating across groups.

### Key idea

The failure isn't in the criteria — it's in `sum()`. Grouping criteria into AND-blocks whose score is zero if any member fails removes the compensation channel. Done once offline, no optimizer change needed.

### Main finding

Appropriateness **+10.8 points** without loss of coverage; best average on seven benchmarks at two scales. Shorter answers alone recover **almost none** of the appropriateness loss.

### Why it matters

A sharp, replicable example of reward hacking in a rubric-RL pipeline — and a drop-in fix. Reward *shape* (how criteria aggregate) can matter more than reward *content* (which criteria you pick).

## 11. UltraBERT: Beyond the Limit of BERT Pre-Training

**Authors:** Taewhoo Lee, Jaewoo Kang (and a third author) — *Korea University / AIGEN Sciences / Amazon*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.15028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15028) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.15028) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.15028)
**Topics:** `pre-training`, `encoder`

### TL;DR

Pushes bidirectional-encoder pretraining past where ModernBERT-era recipes saturate. Three findings: (i) downstream performance **saturates after a few trillion tokens**, (ii) **low masking ratios favor classification, high ratios favor retrieval**, (iii) distillation-with-pruning helps classification at low mask ratios but *hurts* retrieval at high ones. Combined into UltraBERT: separate LM heads for low vs. high mask ratios, with distillation only on the low-ratio head.

### Key idea

Reject the single-objective pretraining assumption: classification and retrieval want *different* masking regimes, so give them separate heads sharing a backbone and apply distillation surgically.

### Main finding

**91.0 on GLUE, 47.7 on BEIR** — SOTA for encoders at the scale tested.

### Why it matters

Encoders are quietly resurgent (for retrieval, moderation, cheap classification). UltraBERT shows the recipe is still being refined and that modern pretraining tricks (distillation, large-scale data) need to be *re-balanced* against the downstream head you'll ship.

## 12. SALD: Self-Referenced Advantage Learning for Diffusion Models

**Authors:** Surjo Dey, Koushik Biswas, Swalpa Kumar Roy, Moloud Abdar, Arnab Bhattacharya, Vinay Kumar Verma — *RGIPT / IIIT-D / Tezpur / U. Queensland / IIT Kanpur*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.01496](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01496) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.01496) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.01496)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `training`

### TL;DR

A teacher-free training framework for diffusion. The model evaluates each (image, caption) pair at a *low* noise level (no-grad, used as an internal reference) and a *high* noise level (used for gradients). From this gap it computes three signals: **Advantage-Guided Diffusion** (sample weighting), **Temporal Advantage Memory** (per-example noise curriculum), and **Spectral Advantage Decomposition** (frequency-band weighting).

### Key idea

Use the model's own prediction at a lower noise as the "advantage baseline" — the way an RL critic would — but computed self-referentially instead of from a separate teacher. Three downstream uses all stem from this one gap signal.

### Main finding

Consistent gains across diffusion architectures and datasets, with **no teacher network** and **no inference-time cost**.

### Why it matters

Imports an RL-flavored advantage idea into diffusion training in a clean, inference-cheap way. The spectral decomposition in particular gives a knob for frequency-aware supervision.

## 13. AReA: Adaptive Receptive-Field Attention for High-Resolution Diffusion Transformers

**Authors:** Koorosh Roohi, David B. Lindell, Babak Taati — *University of Toronto / Vector Institute*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.17227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17227) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.17227) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.17227)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

A **training-free** attention schedule for Diffusion Transformers that lets them generate at higher resolution than they were trained for. Early denoising steps use *cross-scale* attention over downsampled K/V (big receptive field); later steps shrink to *local windows*.

### Key idea

Resolution extrapolation fails because DiT's receptive field is wrong for larger grids. AReA fixes it by making receptive field a *function of the denoising timestep* — coarse first, fine later — matching the way image structure gets fixed early and texture late.

### Main finding

Consistent gains over training-free HR-DiT baselines, at **lower compute** and **faster generation**.

### Why it matters

Training a native-HR DiT is expensive. A training-free fix that works at inference is deployable immediately on existing checkpoints.

## 14. Fish Audio Drama 3 Technical Report

**Authors:** Dawei Han, Shidong Li, Shijia Liao, Jiahua Liu, Songting Liu, Qingzheng Wang, Yuxuan Wang (core); plus 9 contributors — *Fish Audio*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.18010](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18010) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18010) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.18010)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `audio`, `MoE`

### TL;DR

A **35B-parameter mixture-of-experts** speech generation model (**3B active per token**) that clones voices from multiple references, follows natural-language direction, revises takes, voices multi-character scenes, infills audio for editing, and streams. Decoding: **271 fps on one H200** (vs. 177 fps for Fish Audio S2).

### Key idea

Treat TTS as a direction-following *performance* task, not a text→audio mapping. The model handles multiple reference voices, scene-level direction, speech editing (via infill), and long-form streaming within one MoE policy.

### Main finding

~1.5× real-time decode speedup over its predecessor on H200; coverage of direction-following, multi-speaker scenes, speech editing, and streaming in one model.

### Why it matters

A real open-source counterpart to closed studio-TTS stacks, and an MoE scaling data point for audio generation (3B/35B active fraction mirrors modern LLM MoEs).

## 15. LongR: Unleashing Long-Context Reasoning via RL with Dense Utility Rewards

**Authors:** Qize Yu, Zijun Chen, Chen Wu, Yue Chen, Yiyao Yu, Tingfeng Hui, Bowen Ping, Junchi Yan, Baobao Chang — *SJTU / PKU / THU / BUPT*
**Links:** [arXiv:2602.05758](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05758) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2602.05758) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2602.05758)
**Topics:** `RL`, `reasoning`, `long-context`

### TL;DR

Trains long-context reasoning with a **"Think-and-Read"** interleaving (reason, consult document, reason, …) plus a **contextual density reward** — relative information gain over consulted spans — as a dense, annotation-free utility signal.

### Key idea

Reward isn't just final-answer correctness. For each document span the model consults, LongR computes how much it *could* reduce the policy's answer uncertainty, giving an *information-gain* reward. This replaces evidence-annotation with model-measured utility.

### Main finding

**+9% on LongBench v2**, consistent gains on **RULER** and **InfiniteBench**. Transfers across RL algorithms (DAPO, GSPO).

### Why it matters

Long-context RL has been bottlenecked by sparse reward and evidence annotation. LongR removes both constraints and shows robustness against distractors.
