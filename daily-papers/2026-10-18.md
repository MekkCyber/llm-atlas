## 1. Waypoint-OPD

**Authors:** Guochao Jiang, Guofeng Quan, Weiqing Li, Wenfeng Feng, Guohua Liu, Yuewei Zhang — *Alibaba Cloud Computing*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.21601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21601) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.21601) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.21601)
**Topics:** `RL`, `distillation`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

Classical RL→SFT distillation hands the student only the teacher's final policy, discarding *how* the policy shifted during RL. Waypoint-OPD treats the pre-RL vs post-RL logit delta as the distillation signal, uses an intermediate checkpoint as a "waypoint" to detect direction reversals, and gates (zeros out) the signal on tokens where early and late shifts disagree.

### Key idea

Compute Δlog p = log p(RL student at step T) − log p(SFT teacher before RL), take a mid-training waypoint Δlog p at step T/2, and keep only tokens where the two deltas agree in sign — the rest are considered RL noise or oscillation. Train the student with on-policy distillation on the gated, agreeing-direction tokens.

### Main finding

Across Qwen3-4B → Qwen3-8B/14B/30B-A3B student transfers on math and code reasoning benchmarks, Waypoint-OPD beats endpoint-only distillation and vanilla on-policy distillation. The authors report that gating removes a large fraction of the noisy teacher signal while preserving the useful policy shift.

### Why it matters

RL post-training is expensive at scale; transferring the *shift* rather than redoing RL on each size variant is a cheaper route for model families. The waypoint-gating trick is a general diagnostic for "did my teacher actually improve on this token, or is it drifting?"

## 2. MJev-Spatial

**Authors:** Not listed on HF page — *affiliation unspecified*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.22276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22276) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22276) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.22276)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `efficient-inference`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Spatial VQA models normally generate free-form text, which is slow and under-constrained. MJev-Spatial reframes every spatial decision as a *classification* over candidate answers scored by the backbone's own Q/K projections — no new parameters, one forward pass, zero answer tokens generated.

### Key idea

"Native attention matching": the model embeds the question and each candidate answer, projects both through the pretrained query/key heads, and takes the max-similarity candidate as the answer. Since the Q and K weights are inherited from a pretrained VLM, no extra classifier head is trained — the matching head *is* the model's attention.

### Main finding

Built on Qwen3.5-4B over 12.3M converted spatial Q&A pairs across three spatial task types; scores 65.64% overall (top among 8 tested models). Reports 2.5× speedup vs the fastest autoregressive baseline and 5.6× vs the base model.

### Why it matters

Shows that for closed-vocabulary multimodal tasks you can skip autoregression and reuse the backbone's attention weights as a free classifier — a path to the "System 1 fast perception" layer of multimodal agents without extra training infrastructure.

## 3. OpenGCUA

**Authors:** Hanzhang Zhou, Long Li, Quyu Kong, Lei Wang, Panrong Tong, Long Chen, Tianyu Xia, Gongjie Zhang, Xu Zhang, Chen Liu, Jianan Zhang, Chenglin Cai, Zhengwen Zeng, Steven Hoi, Yue Wang — *Alibaba Tongyi-MAI*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.22200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22200) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22200) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.22200)
**Topics:** `agents`, `computer-use`, `eval`

### TL;DR

A full open stack (model, harness, SDK) for computer-use agents. Combines visual GUI control with programmatic (code) interfaces inside one loop and introduces "harness-to-model decision transfer": decisions historically made by handcrafted harness rules (when to screenshot, when to retry, when to switch tool) are moved into the model via SFT+RL on harness trajectories.

### Key idea

Rather than keep bolting more heuristics onto a scaffolded agent, train the model to *subsume* the harness's branching logic. The harness becomes a thin IO adapter; the model owns scheduling, tool selection, and recovery. Combined with a code-execution channel alongside visual control, the agent collapses many turn-level loops into one.

### Main finding

Improves scores for all 8 evaluated base models on OSWorld-v2 and uses ~53% fewer steps per task. Ships a cross-device SDK and a preliminary real-device harness.

### Why it matters

Pushes computer-use agents from "scaffolds around a frozen model" to "models that absorb the scaffold." Fewer steps per task is a direct cost win and reduces failure surface. The open stack lowers the barrier to reproducing frontier computer-use results.

## 4. Self-Evolving Agent Harness

**Authors:** Chuyang Wei, Haoxiang Guan, Minghang Zhu, Kexin Zhou, Jiyan He, Xiren Zhou, Huanhuan Chen, Yifei Shen — *Zhongguancun Academy / USTC / Zhongguancun AI Institute*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.18813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18813) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18813) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.18813)
**Topics:** `agents`, `theory`

### TL;DR

Position + theory paper: *weights-frozen* agent improvement by evolving the surrounding harness — prompts, memory, workflows, callable tools, control logic, plugin composition. Formalizes which pieces may change, how proposals are generated, and how admission uses fresh validation data under continual PAC-learning guarantees.

### Key idea

Separate three axes: (a) *what* evolves (prompts vs tools vs repo-level control vs plugin graphs), (b) *who proposes* changes (an outer-loop LLM, evolutionary search, user feedback), and (c) *how* a candidate is admitted (continual PAC with bounded cumulative regressions and high-probability acceptance on held-out validation). Keep model weights fixed throughout; accepted revisions persist across runs.

### Main finding

Not an empirical SOTA paper — gives generalization bounds relating validation-set size to admission thresholds and discusses risks (safety, reliability, accountability) for deployed self-evolving systems. The authors argue harness-only evolution is the right near-term layer because it is reversible and auditable.

### Why it matters

As weights-frozen deployment becomes the norm (closed-API agents, local inference), the "update unit" shifts from gradient steps to harness edits. Having PAC-flavored admission rules gives operators a principled stopping/accepting criterion for prompt/tool updates in production.

## 5. MAI-Gym

**Authors:** Panrong Tong, Quyu Kong, Pengxiang Li, Tianyu Xia, Hanzhang Zhou, Lei Wang, Chenglin Cai, Long Li, Long Chen, Jianan Zhang, Zhengwen Zeng, Xu Zhang, Gongjie Zhang, Yue Wang, Steven Hoi — *Alibaba Tongyi-MAI*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.20939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20939) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20939) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.20939)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Open platform unifying GUI agent evaluation, data collection, and online RL across mobile, computer, and web. Supports 10 benchmarks, scales to 20,000 parallel environments, and ships synthesized resettable "mocked apps" (e.g. a mocked train-ticketing app) plus execution-validated cross-app tasks.

### Key idea

The bottleneck for agent RL is cheap, resettable, high-fidelity environments. MAI-Gym synthesizes these by cloning real app UIs into deterministic mocks with execution verifiers (did the booking actually get placed?). The same gym drives SFT data collection, benchmark evaluation, and PPO/GRPO-style online RL.

### Main finding

MAI-Gym-9B scores 78.4% MobileWorld and 76.2% OSWorld-Verified. RL in a mocked train-ticketing env lifts real-phone task success from 28.8% → 48.1% — direct sim-to-real transfer for GUI agents at scale.

### Why it matters

Makes computer-use RL reproducible outside frontier labs. Mocked-app sim-to-real for GUI is the analogue of robotics sim-to-real: both show that execution-grounded synthetic envs are enough to teach real-world tasks.

## 6. SAE Compositionality

**Authors:** Ziye Mei, Frederic Sala — *UW–Madison (CS)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.21261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21261) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.21261) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.21261)
**Topics:** `interp`, `SAE`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Introduces SAE Additivity Residuals (SAR), a metric for how much of a model's behavior under feature steering is explained by linear (additive) combinations of SAE features vs higher-order interactions. Tests across 18 model/behavior settings and shows learned SAE coordinates beat sum-preserving random rotations of the same subspace.

### Key idea

Define residual behavioral variance left after summing contributions of features at interaction order k. Compare *learned* SAE directions to random orthogonal rotations of the same subspace — if SAEs are genuinely compositional, their first-order residual is smaller than the rotational baseline.

### Main finding

Learned SAEs have smaller first-order residuals than random rotations in all 18 settings. Adding pairwise terms improves held-out coalition ordering; in a combined steering experiment, rank fidelity rises from 0.5874 to 0.7790.

### Why it matters

The field has been debating whether SAEs give "clean" compositional features or just a basis change. SAR gives a quantitative answer per setting: SAEs *are* more compositional than random, but second-order terms are still needed for reliable combined steering.

## 7. Speculative Forcing

**Authors:** Not listed on HF page (one contributor was intern at NAVER AI Lab)
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.22347](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22347) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22347) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.22347)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `video`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

Few-step autoregressive video generators (e.g. LongLive, Lingbot-World) still spend most compute on tokens that have already converged. Speculative Forcing treats intermediate denoising outputs as drafts, freezes tokens once they stop changing, and uses two verifiers — a training-free "similarity + stability" check and a learned per-token residual predictor — to decide when to stop.

### Key idea

Port speculative decoding's draft-verify pattern into the denoising dimension of an AR video model. The "draft" is an under-denoised token; the "verifier" accepts a token once its predicted remaining change is below threshold. Different tokens exit at different denoising depths.

### Main finding

On LongLive, matches 4-step quality at ~1.7 denoising steps per token (>1.5× speedup). On Lingbot-World 2.0, ~2.5 steps per token for a 1.35× speedup. Quality metrics preserved vs the full few-step baseline.

### Why it matters

AR video generation's compute is quadratic in tokens × steps; selective exit per token attacks the step axis. Reuses the speculative-decoding mental model, which is now the dominant inference-side acceleration recipe for text, in a diffusion-flavored setting.

### Knowledge graph integration

**Suggested new pages:**

- `inference/speculative-forcing.md` *(depth)* — the per-token denoising-depth variant for AR video.

## 8. MRTPO

**Authors:** Haonan Wang (NUS), Zhining Liu (UIUC), Linxin Song (USC), Xuwei Ding (UPenn), Jingcheng Liang (U. Minnesota), Jian Kang (MBZUAI)
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.21439](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21439) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.21439) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.21439)
**Topics:** `RL`, `rewards`, `credit-assignment`

### TL;DR

In multi-reward RLVR (format reward + correctness + tool-use validity …), GRPO/GDPO assign every reward to every token identically. MRTPO replays each response with and without each reward verifier's information, measures the per-token Jensen–Shannon distance between next-token distributions, and uses that mask to route each reward's advantage only to tokens relevant to it.

### Key idea

Treat "which tokens does reward r actually depend on?" as a counterfactual: ablate reward r's verifier context and see which tokens' next-token distribution moves. The JS-divergence over tokens becomes a soft mask over the per-token advantage for reward r.

### Main finding

Faster convergence and higher final performance than GRPO and GDPO on three multi-reward tasks: text-to-SQL, multi-constraint instruction following, and tool calling.

### Why it matters

As RL recipes stack verifiers (RLVR + format + safety + tool-use), uniform credit assignment underfits each reward and causes interference. Reward-specific token credit is a principled way to let different rewards shape different parts of the response.

## 9. Mosaic

**Authors:** Minrui Xu, Xinyu Zhou, Xiang Long, Haochen Tan, Zhijiang Guo — *HKUST(GZ) / Huawei FML / CityU HK*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.22174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22174) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22174) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.22174)
**Topics:** `RL`, `agents`, `long-horizon`

### TL;DR

Most agentic RL assumes one trajectory = one LLM session. In practice, long-horizon agents compact context or fork subagents, producing *multi-session* trajectories. Mosaic is a critic-free RL method that estimates advantages at the trajectory *and* session level, so credit can flow across session boundaries without naive broadcasting.

### Key idea

Two-level advantage: (1) session-aware trajectory-level advantages (the outer return) and (2) session-level advantage *shaping* that discounts or amplifies within-session signals based on how much that session contributed to the final outcome. Critic-free: both levels are group-relative estimates.

### Main finding

On BrowseComp with Qwen3.5-4B, Mosaic beats broadcasting the final reward to every session, both in context-compaction and subagent-forking configurations. Uses fewer sessions on average per trajectory while winning on task success.

### Why it matters

As agents grow longer and start forking workers / compacting memory, "one session = one trajectory" breaks. Mosaic is one of the first clean RL formulations for the setting the frontier agents are already in.

## 10. Terminal-RSI

**Authors:** Not listed on HF page
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.22192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22192) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22192) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.22192)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `self-improvement`

### TL;DR

Terminal/shell agents that improve themselves across runs by (a) *recursive self-rewrite* of their own tool definitions, prompts, and skill library and (b) *policy-refine*, an RL loop over the resulting self-edited harness. Combines Phase 2-style weights-frozen harness evolution with on-policy weight updates.

### Key idea

Alternate two outer loops: the agent proposes rewrites to its own harness (new macros, new skill files, improved prompts), those are admitted via held-out validation (echoes paper #4's PAC-style admission), then on-policy RL fine-tunes the model on the new harness. Both the harness *and* the weights move; neither alone would suffice.

### Main finding

(Full numbers not available from the abstract-level fetch.) The authors claim recursive self-rewrite plus policy-refine beats either axis alone on terminal-task benchmarks, with monotonically improving cumulative performance across generations.

### Why it matters

Pairs with paper #4: once a system can rewrite its harness, the natural follow-up is to also tune the policy against the new harness. Terminal-RSI sketches the loop; its safety properties (does the self-rewrite stay in-distribution?) are the obvious next concern.

## 11. MLExpert

**Authors:** Jinxin Shi, Shuaiyu Zhang, Yiming Wu, Junpeng Zhao, Shiyang Feng, Xin Li, Liang He, Xiangchao Yan, Bo Zhang — *Shanghai AI Lab / East China Normal U.*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.22344](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22344) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22344) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.22344)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `SFT`, `MoE`

### TL;DR

A 35B MoE agentic model for long-horizon ML engineering. Combines three ideas: a task factory built from historical Kaggle competitions, hierarchical agentic SFT, and Windowed Interaction RL (WIRL) — a reward-sharing scheme that assigns a shared outcome reward to all decisions between two consecutive experiment results.

### Key idea

WIRL defines a "window" as the span of agent decisions between successive experiment-result observations (e.g. between two training-script runs). All decisions in the window share the outcome reward of that experiment. This matches the natural credit boundary of ML engineering: you don't know whether your plan was right until the run finishes.

### Main finding

MLE-Bench Lite medal rate jumps 37.9% → 72.7% over the base model — comparable to frontier closed models. Also reports gains on NatureBench Lite and transfer to agent-scaffold and algorithm-optimization tasks.

### Why it matters

ML engineering is the canonical long-horizon-with-sparse-feedback agent task. Windowed credit assignment on experiment boundaries is a general recipe for any domain where "did this trial work?" is the natural reward granularity (not per-token, not per-episode).

## 12. FlashMask-3

**Authors:** Qianyue He, Siming Wu, Haoyang Xie, Qiwen Bao, Jinle Zeng, Jiabin Yang, Tian Wu, Dianhai Yu (contact: Guoxia Wang) — *Baidu*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.17901](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17901) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.17901) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.17901)
**Topics:** `systems`, `efficient-inference`, `long-context`

### TL;DR

Long-context training with structured attention masks (document packing, bidirectional + causal mixes, custom masking for multimodal) is both compute- and communication-bound. FlashMask-3 adds a Hopper-tuned kernel and a *distributed* extension with zero-communication mask localization, a load balancer, and compute/comm overlap.

### Key idea

Three systems moves: (1) local mask tiling that doesn't need to exchange mask layout across ranks (zero-communication localization), (2) load balancing across ranks because structured masks make the per-rank workload unequal, (3) overlapping the mask metadata exchange with attention computation.

### Main finding

Outperforms FlashMask, FlexAttention, MagiAttention, and Megatron-Core CP at both single-GPU and multi-GPU scales. End-to-end: 7.3–11.0% training speedup for a 118B-A14B MoE model.

### Why it matters

Mask-aware attention has been a per-model systems scramble; FlashMask-3 generalizes the kernel + distribution pattern. The single-digit end-to-end speedup on a 118B MoE is real money at pretraining scale.

## 13. LeanPRM

**Authors:** Salman Rahman, Jingyan Shen, James Shiffer, Saadia Gabriel, Pavel Izmailov — *UCLA / NYU*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.17697](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17697) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.17697) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.17697)
**Topics:** `reasoning`, `PRM`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Process reward models usually need expensive per-step human labels (PRM800K-style). LeanPRM uses the Lean proof assistant as an automatic step-labeler for natural-language math proofs: each NL step is translated to Lean and checked for soundness, producing a corpus of 10,634 proofs with 152K labeled steps — no human annotators.

### Key idea

Translate each NL step into a Lean goal, let Lean's kernel decide correctness, and use that as the step label. The resulting corpus becomes the training set for a Qwen3-4B PRM. The step-grounding comes from a formal system rather than human raters.

### Main finding

Beats a PRM trained on PRM800K human labels on 4 of 5 benchmarks. In best-of-N over IMO-level problems, selects stronger proofs. Releases both the training corpus and a 200-proof LeanPRM-Bench.

### Why it matters

PRM scaling has been bottlenecked by human labels. Formal-system-grounded step labels are cheap, consistent, and open the door to vastly larger PRM training sets — paralleling what RLVR did for outcome rewards.

## 14. Copy-Paste Language Models

**Authors:** Not listed on HF page
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.19247](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19247) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.19247) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.19247)
**Topics:** `LLM`, `architecture`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

Adds a lightweight "copy head" to a standard LM that predicts the next token by pointing to an earlier position in the context, rather than generating a vocabulary distribution. The copy head is interpolated with the standard LM head via a learned gate. Faster training, better perplexity, stronger long-context retrieval.

### Key idea

The output softmax over a 100k+ vocabulary is a bottleneck for both training speed and long-context recall. A pointer head over context positions is cheap (linear in context length) and matches the exact-match copy pattern that transformers currently learn implicitly inside attention. The authors argue the pointer path provides a second gradient route that eases the vocabulary-head bottleneck.

### Main finding

With a sliding-window attention backbone, CPLMs match the perplexity and downstream performance of full-attention baselines while using much less memory. Long-context retrieval metrics improve substantially; added parameters are minimal.

### Why it matters

Pointer networks have been around since 2015 (seq2seq); seeing them land cleanly as a drop-in LM head suggests the long-context retrieval problem is partly a *loss function* problem, not just an attention problem. Pairs well with sliding-window attention and KV-cache compression.

## 15. Virtual Head Attention

**Authors:** Xibo Ding, Guoxia Wang (equal), Jinle Zeng, Jiabin Yang, Sirui Tao, Chao Yang, Dianhai Yu — *Baidu / Peking University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.17959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17959) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.17959) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.17959)
**Topics:** `architecture`, `attention`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

Virtual Head Attention (VHA) halves the number of physical query heads and uses only two KV heads, but adds a *Q Premix* (linear expansion of queries into virtual heads) and a *Linear Postmix* (linear mix across heads). Both linear operations fold into the standard projection weights at inference, so you get a plain GQA-2 model that still beats regular GQA.

### Key idea

Make the attention expressiveness/compute tradeoff *linear-only* at training time, so inference can collapse the extra ops into the normal projection weights. At inference it is architecturally GQA-2; at training it is as expressive as more heads.

### Main finding

VHA beats GQA at every scale from 0.6B through 30B-A3B MoE, while reducing KV cache size and training/prefill FLOPs. The authors frame it as a strict Pareto improvement over GQA at matched inference compute.

### Why it matters

GQA is the de facto attention variant in modern LLMs; a drop-in Pareto improvement at matched inference cost is a straight win for inference economics. The "fold linear premix/postmix into projections at inference" trick is reusable for other heads-level architectural knobs.
