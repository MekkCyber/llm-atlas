## 1. The Trinity: Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents {#1-trinity}

**Authors:** Weidong Tang, Ruiheng Wang, Jingxuan Zhang, Hexin Wang, et al. — *InfRec, Xidian U., Tongji U., NUS, UC Berkeley, CUHK, HKUST, HKU, Shanghai Innovation Institute*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.36077](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36077) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.36077) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.36077)
**Topics:** `agents`, `multimodal`, `RL`, `self-improvement`

### TL;DR

A three-role framework (Creator / Explorer / Evolver) that bootstraps multimodal-agent capability by generating simulated worlds, routing tasks by shared underlying capability, and evolving per-capability "Harness" modules. Instead of lumping tasks by surface category (math, coding, embodied), it clusters them by what skill they actually need, builds specialized Harnesses for each cluster, and composes them at inference via a learned router.

### Key idea

Tasks with wildly different modalities and surface forms (e.g. an abstract reasoning puzzle and a navigation task) can share the same underlying capability requirement. The Creator synthesizes focused worlds, the Explorer runs the agent and collects experience, and the Evolver updates Harness configurations tied to a capability tree. The router picks a compatible Harness at test time.

### Main finding

+34 pt mean improvement across 28 training benchmarks; +26 pt across 18 unseen benchmarks (including two real-world embodied tasks). The capability-tree abstraction transfers out of distribution — a far stronger generalization signal than category-based evolution.

### Why it matters

Recursive self-improvement discussions have been mostly conceptual; this is a concrete recipe where a *harness* (not just the model) evolves against capability-sliced curricula and transfers to real embodied tasks. If replicable, it reframes agent training as curriculum construction over capabilities rather than fine-tuning over tasks.

## 2. Multiscale Policy Optimization for Long-Horizon LLM Agents {#2-mpo}

**Authors:** Tao Ren, Hui Yang, Jia Deng, Tingyang Chen, Qiyao Wang, et al. (19 authors) — *IQuestLab*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.35013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35013) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.35013) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.35013)
**Topics:** `RL`, `agents`, `credit-assignment`

### TL;DR

Policy optimization for long-horizon agentic RL that aligns credit assignment and policy updates at the *turn* scale, not the token scale. Observes that critic values vary primarily across turns (not tokens inside a turn), and introduces Multiscale Advantage Estimation (MAE) with turn-level importance sampling to match.

### Key idea

Each assistant action is one turn, but tokens inside a turn are tightly correlated — treating each token as a separate decision inflates variance and misaligns updates with the actual decision points. MPO computes advantages at the turn scale (MAE) and applies turn-level importance sampling, while still training over tokens.

### Main finding

Consistent gains over strong RL baselines on search, tool-use, and automation tasks, tested on 4B and 30B-A3B models. The gains survive scale and show up across multiple task families.

### Why it matters

Long-horizon agent RL has been bottlenecked by credit-assignment misalignment between token-level loss and turn-level decision structure. MPO is a tidy fix to a widespread problem — the kind of thing most frontier labs may quietly adopt.

## 3. VibeVideoBench: Evaluating Multimodal Agents on Code-Driven Video Generation {#3-vibevideobench}

**Authors:** Muxi Diao, Wenbin Xu, Lele Yang, et al. (24 authors) — *BUPT / Kuaishou / PKU*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.35864](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35864) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.35864) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.35864)
**Topics:** `eval`, `agents`, `multimodal`, `code`

### TL;DR

A 239-task bilingual benchmark asking multimodal agents to *write code* that renders a complete MP4 (with sound) within one hour — no image or video gen models allowed. Evaluation combines program checks, ~13k rubric checks, and three VLM judge panels.

### Key idea

Instead of asking a model to generate a video directly, ask it to author the full code pipeline (rendering, animation, audio mix) that produces one. This stresses planning, long-horizon tool use, and code-driven creative work in a verifiable way.

### Main finding

GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5 lead across the field of nine agent systems; "delivery" failures (late or unfinished renders inside the one-hour budget) are a major source of error. Judge rankings closely match human preference.

### Why it matters

A rare agent benchmark where the output is a creative artifact *and* mechanically verifiable: a program that compiles and produces a watchable video. Also exposes agent cost/latency failure modes.

## 4. Memory Tax: Dissecting the Net Value of Agent Memory {#4-memory-tax}

**Authors:** Xuan He, Sining Zhoubian, Ruozhen Yang, et al. — *Tsinghua / UIUC / RUC / Verdent.ai / U. Waterloo*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.34073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34073) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.34073) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.34073)
**Topics:** `agents`, `memory`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Agent memory systems often **lose** to plain RAG over the raw conversation history. The authors formalize *memory benefit* (gains over RAG) vs *memory tax* (losses) and attribute the losses to reorganization/distillation steps that silently remove or obscure useful evidence.

### Key idea

Treat "RAG over raw history" as the baseline. Any memory layer that summarizes, structures, or distills on top is adding operations that can only hurt recall of the specific detail a task needs. Measure net value in both directions, not just wins.

### Main finding

Across several memory systems and tasks, many agent-memory configurations have net *negative* value relative to the RAG baseline. The paper locates specific design choices (compression, forgetting, graph consolidation) that systematically introduce the tax.

### Why it matters

Agent-memory research has mostly reported wins over no-memory baselines, which is a trivial comparison. Setting RAG-over-history as the floor and demanding positive net value is the right bar. This paper reframes the whole subfield.

## 5. HABIT-Bench: Evaluating Habit Induction from Longitudinal Weak Evidence in Agent Memory {#5-habit-bench}

**Authors:** Junming Zhang, Rubin Wei, Xu Cheng, Hao Sun, Jiarui Wang, et al. — *LUMIA Lab, SJTU / Shanghai AI Lab*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.24520](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24520) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.24520) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.24520)
**Topics:** `eval`, `agents`, `memory`

### TL;DR

Benchmark testing whether agent-memory systems can *induce habits* from scattered, incomplete evidence across many sessions — not just retrieve past facts. Four-choice tasks over four domains (food, finance, software, travel), with distractors for temporary exceptions, changed habits, and rejected suggestions.

### Key idea

Habit induction is noisier than fact retrieval: the signal is in repetition and context, often across hundreds of turns. The benchmark forces memory systems to synthesize weak evidence rather than return literal matches.

### Main finding

Best of 8 memory methods tops out at **33.6% macro accuracy**. Performance stays limited even when the ground-truth evidence is handed to the model directly — meaning the bottleneck is reasoning over the evidence, not retrieval of it.

### Why it matters

Pair this with Memory Tax (#4): one shows current memory systems can be *worse than nothing*, the other shows that even perfect evidence doesn't unlock habit-level reasoning. Together they are a stress test for how we talk about "agent memory."

## 6. ThinkWorld: World Imagination Reinforces Chain-of-Dynamics Reasoning {#6-thinkworld}

**Authors:** Chaehyun Kim, Chi-Pin Huang, Jisoo Kim, Min-Hung Chen, Yu-Chiang Frank Wang, Seungryong Kim, Fu-En Yang — *KAIST / NVIDIA*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.37666](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37666) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.37666) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.37666)
**Topics:** `reasoning`, `RL`, `VLA`, `world-model`

*Borderline: VLA/robotics, but included because the paper proposes a new RL training recipe that uses world-imagination rewards and could transfer back to multimodal LLM reasoning.*

### TL;DR

VLA models are bottlenecked by action-labeled robot data. ThinkWorld teaches a VLA to emit a *Chain-of-Dynamics* — a sequence of discrete motion tokens linking the current state to a future state — and refines it with RL rewards from a world-imagination model, allowing unlabeled human videos to supervise action learning.

### Key idea

Replace supervised action-label demonstrations with a two-stage recipe: (1) SFT on labeled data to learn to emit motion-token chains; (2) RL on unlabeled videos where the reward is "does my imagined next-state match what actually happens next in the video?" Decouples action reasoning from the specific robot embodiment.

### Main finding

The authors report effective human-to-robot transfer and generalization to unseen tasks and environments in sim and real-robot experiments.

### Why it matters

The pattern — RL on process-level self-consistency rewards derived from a world model — is a template for any domain where ground-truth action labels are expensive but process traces are cheap. Related to process-reward RL for LLM reasoning.

## 7. EmbeddingGemma 2: Unified and Efficient Multimodal Representations {#7-embeddinggemma-2}

**Authors:** Henrique Schechter Vera, Sahil Dua, Jovan Andonov, Sara Smoot, et al. (~75 authors) — *Google DeepMind*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.37364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37364) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.37364) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.37364)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `embeddings`, `tech-report`

### TL;DR

Open multimodal embedding model from DeepMind: text + image + audio + video (and their combinations) into one shared space. Multi-stage training curriculum, simplified per-layer embeddings, truncatable dimensions, quantization support, selective encoder loading, and adjustable vision-token budgets for deployment.

### Key idea

A single tower with modality-specific encoders feeding a shared contrastive space, trained in stages. Deployment knobs (truncation, quantization, selective loading) are first-class — the model is engineered to shed capacity when the application doesn't need it, not just at inference time but at module load time.

### Main finding

Reportedly beats specialist models and 2–12× larger generalist models on standard retrieval and classification benchmarks across all four modalities.

### Why it matters

Unified multimodal embeddings are the retrieval backbone for RAG, agents, and search. Shipping one with clean deployment knobs matters more than any single eval win: it's the shape of production stacks.

## 8. h1: A Taste of the Bitter Lesson in Automated Theorem Proving {#8-h1}

**Authors:** Skander Moalla, Ahmad Rammal, Niket Patel, Rémi Munos, Julia Kempe — *EPFL / NYU / Meta FAIR*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.35962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35962) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.35962) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.35962)
**Topics:** `reasoning`, `agents`, `theorem-proving`

### TL;DR

A plain agentic loop over Lean 4 — model, verifier feedback, self-reflection, test-time compute scaling — with *no* decomposition, tactic search, or hand-crafted structure, matches or beats hand-designed workflows at state-of-the-art levels. The bottleneck is efficient Lean interfaces, not search structure.

### Key idea

The "bitter lesson" bet: on a tightly verified domain, scaled test-time compute + a tight verifier loop beats bespoke scaffolding. But *simplicity alone is not enough* — the loop's efficiency and the Lean interface design are what make scaled compute actually useful.

### Main finding

State-of-the-art on multiple Lean 4 ATP benchmarks. The gap vs. hand-designed workflows (tree search, decomposition) is closed by compute scaling. Interface design (how fast and how richly the verifier talks to the model) dominates cost and solve rate.

### Why it matters

Pushes the "do simpler things with more compute" case into formal reasoning, which was long thought to require explicit search. The implication: investment should move to verifier interfaces and compute scaling, not clever search harnesses.

## 9. Channel Selection for Mixture-of-Experts Decoding Needs No Calibration Data {#9-moe-channel-selection}

**Authors:** Geunhyoung Kim, Sung Ju Hwang — *KAIST / DeepAuto.ai*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.37386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37386) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.37386) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.37386)
**Topics:** `inference`, `MoE`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

For MoE decoding at low batch sizes, expert weight loading is the bottleneck. Prior channel-selection methods calibrate a threshold on offline data; the authors show calibration is unnecessary — a simple per-token top-k over channels matches or beats it, and avoids a nasty repetition-loop failure of threshold selection on Gemma 4. Plus a calibration-free reader using a ternary sketch + low-rank reconstruction.

### Key idea

Threshold-based selection doesn't actually control how many channels get loaded; distributional drift breaks it (visible as a repetition loop on Gemma 4). A per-token top-k is deterministic, data-free, and works. The channel reader (ternary screen + low-rank map) also needs no calibration data.

### Main finding

**98.1% of full-model accuracy** while reading **17.8%** of routed-expert weights. Triton kernels run MoE blocks **1.60–1.90× faster than optimized vLLM at batch 1**, across three post-trained MoE models.

### Why it matters

Clean elimination of a calibration step for a hot inference optimization — makes the technique drop-in and robust. Memory-bandwidth-bound MoE decoding is the dominant single-user case for frontier MoE serving.

## 10. What Effort Level Should I Choose? On the Economics of Reasoning Effort in LLM Agents {#10-reasoning-effort-economics}

**Authors:** Kaiser Sun, Yongchan Kwon, Zain Hasan, Shang Zhu, Kaitlyn Zhou, Federico Bianchi — *Johns Hopkins / Columbia / Stanford*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.34491](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34491) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.34491) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.34491)
**Topics:** `reasoning`, `agents`, `cost`

### TL;DR

Reasoning effort is a *credence good* — buyers (users and agent frameworks) can't tell if extra compute was warranted. Higher effort raises aggregate accuracy but hurts on tasks lower effort already solved; on easy tasks the top level can cost **up to 42×** the lowest. Proposed fix: start low, escalate on verified failure.

### Key idea

Treat the model-side effort knob as a market good with information asymmetry. Measure not just gains but regressions and dollar cost per solved problem. The escalation policy makes compute scale with actual difficulty, not the user's uncertainty.

### Main finding

Escalation-based routing matches or beats "always-top-effort" at lower cost on most proprietary models, across four benchmarks. Higher effort changes behavior (more self-verification, longer trajectories) in ways that cost without buying.

### Why it matters

Reasoning APIs now expose effort/thinking budgets; this paper is the first careful cost/benefit analysis across models and tasks. Framework-level routing of effort is likely to become standard.

## 11. The Lexical Imprint of Pre-training Data {#11-lexical-imprint}

**Authors:** Jeonghoon Kim, Woojin Chung, Woomin Song, Cheonbok Park, Nako Sung, Jinwoo Shin — *KAIST / NAVER*
**Links:** [arXiv:2602.10657](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10657) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2602.10657) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2602.10657)
**Topics:** `data`, `pre-training`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Word-level Unigram Cross-Entropy (UCE) — a *model-free* measure of lexical alignment between a pretraining corpus and a target benchmark — predicts zero-shot accuracy strikingly well. Lower UCE → higher zero-shot score, consistently across 10 benchmarks, 4 corpora, and 3 model scales.

### Key idea

Alignment can be measured without running the model: just compare word distributions between corpus and benchmark. Document-level and prompt-level variants let you score individual training documents or prompts for benchmark relevance.

### Main finding

UCE is a strong zero-shot predictor across scales, with no training required. Suggests surface-form lexical overlap drives more of zero-shot benchmark accuracy than people usually admit.

### Why it matters

Model-free data-vs-benchmark alignment is cheap enough to run before training. Also uncomfortable for benchmark design: evals that reward lexical alignment are measuring data overlap more than reasoning.

## 12. On the SPOT: Enhancing Speculative Decoding for Diffusion Language Models via Commit-Order Prediction {#12-spot}

**Authors:** Eliezer Shlomi, Eliron Rahimi, Avi Mendelson, Chaim Baskin — *Technion / Ben-Gurion U.*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.22543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22543) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22543) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.22543)
**Topics:** `inference`, `speculative-decoding`, `diffusion`

### TL;DR

Diffusion language models (DLMs) can't use standard speculative decoding — the decoder must also pick the *order* in which positions get committed. SPOT is a 0.62M-param drafter that predicts the commit order from the target model's own output distributions, enabling speculative decoding for DLMs. SPOT-Sets extends it to dynamic-threshold decoding.

### Key idea

Speculative decoding for AR LMs predicts the next *token*. For DLMs it must predict both *what* and *where*. The drafter learns commit-order from the target's probability patterns, so no separate order model is needed. Empirically, the model's own prediction from the previous diffusion pass already contains the right token at >90% of positions.

### Main finding

Up to **6.8× fewer target passes** and **up to 3.4× higher throughput** on SDAR-8B — a serious speedup for DLM inference.

### Why it matters

DLMs have been a serving-cost liability relative to AR models. SPOT closes a big part of the gap and shows that DLM-specific inference optimizations are their own research area, not AR techniques retrofitted.

## 13. Cross-modal Steering Vectors Exist Across Both Image and Text Generation {#13-cross-modal-steering}

**Authors:** Donghao Zeng, Nishant Subramani, Tamar Rott Shaham, Benno Krojer, et al. — *MIT / CMU / Mila*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.36269](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36269) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.36269) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.36269)
**Topics:** `interp`, `steering`, `multimodal`

### TL;DR

In unified multimodal models (shared params for language + image generation), a steering vector derived from one modality (e.g. text) can steer generation in the other (e.g. image) zero-shot. Transfer is strongest when the vectors point in *similar directions* despite the modality-gap offset between text and image representations.

### Key idea

Concepts like "emotion" or "object size" have shared internal directions across modalities inside unified models. The modality gap is largely a translation; the concept-direction is cross-modal. This is a strong existence result for shared concept circuits in multimodal LLMs.

### Main finding

Bidirectional transfer across several concepts. Strongly-transferable vectors align in direction across modalities — the gap is primarily a bias, not a rotation.

### Why it matters

A clean mech-interp result for multimodal models and a practical lever: steering-vector tooling built for text LLMs may plug into image generation inside the same backbone. Also constrains theories of multimodal representation.

## 14. Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion-Uni: Elastic Self-Refining Unified Diffusion LLM with Cross-Paradigm On-Policy Distillation {#14-nld-uni}

**Authors:** Shufan Li, Hanrong Ye, Greg Heinrich, Yonggan Fu, Haotian Zhang, Sifei Liu, Aditya Grover, Pavlo Molchanov — *NVIDIA / UCLA*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.36466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36466) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.36466) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.36466)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `multimodal`, `distillation`

### TL;DR

A unified discrete diffusion LLM for visual understanding + image generation. Two contributions: (1) a cross-paradigm on-policy distillation (OPD) framework that transfers understanding from AR VLMs and generation from continuous flow models into one discrete diffusion model; (2) a progressive self-refining diffusion process that gradually corrupts tokens over training (instead of hard-masking), enabling commit-then-refine at inference.

### Key idea

Combine the strengths of two paradigms (AR for understanding, flow for generation) by distilling both into a single discrete diffusion model, trained with on-policy samples. The elastic self-refining process lets the model commit to intermediate tokens and iteratively correct them — a middle ground between hard mask-filling and continuous flow.

### Main finding

State-of-the-art among unified diffusion LLMs on VQA, multimodal reasoning, object grounding, text-to-image, and image editing.

### Why it matters

Unified diffusion LLMs are a plausible successor to AR-text + flow-image stacks. Shows the paradigm can be competitive across understanding and generation without sacrificing one for the other, and that cross-paradigm distillation is a tractable training path.

## 15. What Outcome RL Buys: Finishing, Reliability, Rarely Reach {#15-outcome-rl-analysis}

**Author:** Shan Yang
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.34894](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34894) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.34894) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.34894)
**Topics:** `RL`, `reasoning`, `analysis`

### TL;DR

Outcome-reward RL raises pass@1 but — according to this analysis of 20 released RL models (1.5B–32B) — the gain decomposes into **finishing** (answering within the token budget), **reliability** (being right once finished), and **reach** (solving problems the base couldn't). Reach is almost always small (≤2.1 pp); most pass@1 gain is finishing or reliability.

### Key idea

Decomposing pass@1 improvement into finishing/reliability/reach is a sharper lens than "did pass@1 go up." It separates "RL taught the model something new" from "RL made the model finish inside the budget" and "RL made the model more consistent on things it already knew."

### Main finding

At 4k–8k budgets, 82% of pass@1 gain comes from finishing. At larger budgets the gain shifts to reliability. Reach adds at most **2.1 pp**, and only 3.5% of 144 physics problems "only RL solved" held up on resampling. In a controlled 8B RL run, new-problem gains were pure finishing.

### Why it matters

Reframes the empirical story of outcome RL for reasoning: it looks like "RL unlocks new capability," but under this decomposition it's mostly budget management and reliability. If true, pass@k at large k is the right metric for "did the model learn something new," and outcome RL is doing less of that than headlines suggest.
