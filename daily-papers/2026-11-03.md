## 1. FLAMIA

**Authors:** Yandex Research team — *Yandex Research*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.03344](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03344) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03344) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.03344)
**Topics:** `architectures`, `quantization`, `efficient-training`

### TL;DR

FLAMIA ships a family of attention kernels for NVIDIA Blackwell (B200/B300) that run causal pretraining in MXFP8 and mixed NVFP4/FP8 precision. The core engineering challenge is that block-level shared microscales can leak information across the causal boundary; FLAMIA formalizes and fixes this, and demonstrates the kernels are drop-in stable for Llama-style pretraining at long context.

### Key idea

Attention tiles share one microscale per token-block. In a causal mask, naively scaling a block that straddles the causal frontier lets future tokens influence the scale of past ones (causal leakage). FLAMIA uses asymmetric scale layouts and consistent forward/backward scale handling to guarantee that no scale depends on tokens past the current position, while still packing documents densely in the same batch.

### Main finding

End-to-end speedups on packed causal workloads on B200 and B300 versus FP16/BF16 flash attention, with no measurable regression in pretraining loss on Llama-architecture models. MXFP8 is competitive for long-context pretraining; NVFP4/FP8 mixed path adds further throughput at matched stability.

### Why it matters

Microscaled formats (MXFP8, NVFP4) are Blackwell's selling point but historically caused silent accuracy issues in attention. A causally-safe attention kernel removes one of the last blockers to running full pretraining in sub-FP8 precision on Blackwell, lowering the per-token cost of frontier training.

## 2. Order-Only On-Policy Distillation

**Authors:** Guangzeng Han, Yu Wu, Xiaolei Huang — *University of Memphis*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.02112](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02112) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.02112) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.02112)
**Topics:** `post-training`, `distillation`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

Standard on-policy distillation matches the teacher's full probability distribution on the student's own rollouts. This paper shows that matching only the teacher's *ranking* of the top-K tokens — ignoring the actual probability values — beats probability matching on math reasoning across six teacher-student pairs. Being probability-free makes the loss invariant to teacher temperature and softens the reward signal where the teacher is overconfident.

### Key idea

Order-only loss: for each rollout step, take the teacher's top-K logits, convert to a ranking, and train the student with a pairwise rank loss over those K tokens. No KL, no cross-entropy on soft labels. The student's distribution is free to find its own shape as long as token *order* agrees with the teacher.

### Main finding

Beats standard on-policy KL distillation on HMMT25, AIME24, AIME25 across six teacher-student pairs. Gains are most pronounced when the student is much smaller than the teacher — exactly the regime where exact probability imitation is poorly conditioned.

### Why it matters

Reasoning distillation is becoming a dominant recipe (DeepSeek-R1-Distill, Kimi, Qwen). This paper argues that the *target* should be coarser than a probability distribution — the useful signal is which tokens are plausible, not how plausible each one is. Cheap methodological change; large practical lift.

## 3. BYOD — Build Your Own DLM

**Authors:** Ruurd J. A. Kuiper, Robert A. Bagheri — *UMC Utrecht*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.41574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41574) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.41574) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.41574)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `LLM`, `LoRA`

### TL;DR

A LoRA-only recipe that converts an off-the-shelf autoregressive LLM into a diffusion language model (DLM) while leaving the base weights frozen. Applied to Gemma 2 9B, Llama 3.1 8B, Qwen 2.5 7B, and Ministral 8B. The converted models generate from fully masked sequences through iterative denoising and come within range of LLaDA 8B Instruct on nine tasks.

### Key idea

Diffusion LMs normally require pretraining from scratch with masked-denoising objectives (LLaDA, DiffuLLaMA). BYOD instead injects the diffusion behavior through LoRA adapters only: the frozen AR backbone keeps its knowledge; the LoRA learns the bidirectional-attention / denoising-head corrections needed for parallel mask-fill decoding.

### Main finding

BYOD-Gemma 2 9B reaches competitive quality against LLaDA 8B Instruct on nine downstream tasks with only LoRA-sized added parameters. Main remaining weakness: output quality degrades at low denoising-step budgets, so the usual DLM "sample in few steps" wins over AR don't yet materialize.

### Why it matters

Opens a cheap migration path from the vast pretrained AR ecosystem into diffusion-style generation. If step-budget issues are solved, converted AR checkpoints could take DLM's parallel-decoding wins without a full pretraining run.

## 4. Library of Layers (LoL)

**Authors:** Sean McLeish, Ang Li, Sanae Lotfi, Pascal Frossard, Micah Goldblum — *EPFL / NYU / Columbia / U. Maryland*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.03526](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03526) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03526) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.03526)
**Topics:** `architectures`, `efficient-inference`, `routing`

### TL;DR

LoL is a transformer that stores a *shared library* of blocks and picks the ordering of layers per-input via a small router, at inference time. One scalar — the library size — moves the model smoothly between parameter reuse (small library, like Universal Transformer / looped) and sparse compute (large library, MoE-adjacent). The emergent routing paths correspond to legible behaviors (language, puzzle class, safety label).

### Key idea

Instead of a fixed stack `L_1 ∘ L_2 ∘ … ∘ L_N`, the model keeps `K` candidate blocks and a per-token router that picks which one to apply at each depth. Weights are shared across positions in the stack. Adjusting `K` interpolates between weight-sharing extremes.

### Main finding

Competitive with dense, looped, and MoE baselines of matched compute on text. Routing paths are interpretable: different paths activate for different input languages, different classes of reasoning puzzles (sudoku vs. maze), and even for safety-relevant vs. innocuous inputs — and *forcing* a path can steer the model's output.

### Why it matters

A unified architecture that subsumes looped-transformers and MoE under a single routing scalar, and whose routes are legible enough to serve as a cheap control/interp surface. Suggests "which block is used when" is a useful alternative axis to "how many experts fire."

## 5. From Fragmentation to Fusion — Omnimodal Embeddings Survey

**Authors:** Ziyin Zhang, Yuyu Guo, Ying Zhang, Siqiao Xue, Hao Wu, Zhaoyang Zhu, Ruipeng Li, Zhaokun Jiang, Lei Lei, Rui Wang, Hang Yu — *Ant Group / SJTU / ANU / Tongji / NUS*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.00172](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00172) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.00172) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.00172)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `survey`, `retrieval`

### TL;DR

A unification-oriented survey of multimodal embedding research. Argues the field is fragmented by modality but converging on three shared capabilities — coverage, comparability, compositionality — reachable via three method families: transformation, alignment, and joint embedding.

### Key idea

Rather than treat text / image / audio / video embedding literatures separately, the authors factor every known method into *which of the three capabilities* it targets and *which of the three families* it uses. Yields a 3×3 taxonomy that makes gaps and overlaps explicit.

### Main finding

Survey — not a benchmark result. The main claim is that compositionality is the most under-addressed capability, and that joint-embedding approaches (contrastive multi-tower setups) lead on it, while transformation-based approaches dominate coverage.

### Why it matters

Grounds a taxonomy page in the KG. Omnimodal retrieval is becoming a shared backbone for RAG and agents; a clean carving of the space is useful before more depth files land in `multimodal/`.

## 6. On-Policy Self-Distillation with Teacher Evolution (TESD)

**Authors:** Shufan Shen, Zhongni Hou, Chao Bi, Yufei Zhang, Guojun Yin, Shuhui Wang — *ICT CAS / UCAS / Meituan*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.38637](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38637) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.38637) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.38637)
**Topics:** `post-training`, `RL`, `distillation`

### TL;DR

In on-policy self-distillation (OPSD), a student learns from a "self-teacher" — the same model given privileged context. Training plateaus because the self-teacher isn't uniformly reliable. TESD lets the self-teacher *evolve* via its own RL rollouts, with rewards for correctness and context independence, and down-weights student samples whose teacher guidance looks unreliable.

### Key idea

Two coupled optimizations: (i) teacher-oriented — the self-teacher rolls out, gets a reward that mixes task correctness with a "context-invariance" bonus, and takes RL updates; (ii) student-oriented — standard OPSD but with per-sample confidence weights derived from the evolved teacher's agreement with itself across context variants.

### Main finding

Outperforms representative token-level (plain OPSD) and sequence-level (STaR-style self-improvement) baselines across several datasets and model scales. The confidence down-weighting is what unblocks the training plateau observed in vanilla OPSD.

### Why it matters

OPSD is attractive because it needs no stronger external teacher, but has been empirically noisy. A principled "fix the teacher first" mechanism matters for any pipeline that uses a model to supervise itself — distillation, self-play RL, iterated self-refinement.

## 7. DecisionRank

**Authors:** Dian Yu, Chongyang Bai, Jieyi Zhang, Rui Liu, Tianyi Chen, Shike Mei, Hang Cui, Frank Shyu, Sandeep Pandey, Luke Simon, Chunwei Liu, Xi Liu — *Meta / Purdue*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.42128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42128) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.42128) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.42128)
**Topics:** `RL`, `distillation`, `retrieval`

### TL;DR

A reranker trained with *item-space* on-policy distillation (OPD): the student produces a ranking, a generative LLM teacher gives feedback on that ranking, and the student is updated against that feedback combined with offline supervision. A 1.7B student surpasses its 27B generative teacher on TREC DL19–23; an 8B student is faster than a listwise generative reranker.

### Key idea

Rather than train the student to imitate the teacher's full output (listwise text), score *the ranking the student produces* with the teacher and use that as the on-policy reward. The teacher never ranks — it only judges. Combined with standard offline ranking supervision, this gives a decision-model (small, fast) that beats the teacher it was distilled from.

### Main finding

SOTA on TREC DL, BEIR, BRIGHT. The 1.7B student passes the 27B teacher on TREC DL19–23. 8B version faster than listwise generative rerankers at comparable quality.

### Why it matters

Reranking is a bottleneck in RAG stacks. Shows that item-space OPD — scoring the student's actions, not imitating the teacher's text — is a cleaner distillation target for decision tasks than listwise text imitation.

## 8. LeanGuard

**Authors:** Sreehari Sankar, Debargha Ganguly, Vikash Singh, Mohsen Hariri, Weicong Chen, et al., Joe Stubbs, Vipin Chaudhary — *Case Western Reserve / UT Austin*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.02332](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02332) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.02332) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.02332)
**Topics:** `safety`, `agents`, `formal-verification`

### TL;DR

A runtime guardrail for tool-using agents: every proposed tool call is checked against a policy written in **Lean 4** before it fires. Denied calls can either go back to the model with the violated rule as feedback, or be auto-repaired by Lean searching for a nearby admissible call and proving it. Catches real policy violations missed by unguarded agents on τ²-bench and DTap.

### Key idea

Policy as proof obligation. The admissible-action predicate is a Lean 4 definition over the tool-call's arguments and the current world-state. Each proposed call is rejected unless Lean can discharge the proof. The novelty over text-based agent safety is that the guardrail cannot be social-engineered — it's a mechanical check.

### Main finding

Unguarded agents on τ²-bench and DTap executed tool calls that violate the spec. LeanGuard blocked them and, for many, produced an auto-repaired alternative. Failure mode is false rejects when the Lean policy under-specifies the admissible set.

### Why it matters

A route to agent safety that doesn't lean on another LLM as judge. For high-stakes domains (finance, healthcare, infra), a formal check is the one guarantee the LLM safety stack currently lacks.

## 9. SWE-Trail

**Authors:** Ziming Li (?), Lu Yu, Jiatan Huang, Qing Cui, Jun Zhou, Chuxu Zhang — *Ant Group / U. Connecticut*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.02074](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02074) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.02074) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.02074)
**Topics:** `agents`, `post-training`, `data`

### TL;DR

Trajectory-collection recipe for SWE-bench-style coding agents. Earlier trajectories on the *same issue* are used to decide where new collection should branch out, while the agent itself is never shown that history. Collection becomes targeted exploration rather than independent resampling.

### Key idea

Per issue, keep a history of previous agent runs' decisions. Use the history to identify high-value branching states (unexplored subtrees, places where prior runs diverged). At each such state, let the agent propose several candidate next actions, briefly execute each from the same repo checkpoint, and keep the branch that the repository evidence (tests, type checks, grep) most supports.

### Main finding

Training a 124B student on SWE-Trail trajectories beats independent-sampling trajectory training by **+2.33 pts** on SWE-bench Multilingual and **+1.60 pts** on SWE-bench Pro.

### Why it matters

Coding-agent RL is bottlenecked by trajectory quality; naive sampling wastes compute on near-duplicate solutions. History-guided exploration of the *state graph* (not the decoding distribution) is a data-side alternative to on-policy RL.

## 10. CUA-Master

**Authors:** Gen Li, Ji Zhao, Zhangquan Chen, Congcong Wang, Shuai Dong, et al. — *MMLab CUHK / THU / HKUST(GZ) / JD Explore Academy*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.03126](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03126) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03126) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.03126)
**Topics:** `agents`, `multimodal`, `RL`

### TL;DR

A 27B computer-use agent trained in a nested double-loop recursive-improvement harness. Outer loop ties open-world corpus collection, task construction, and model optimization, using observed weaknesses to drive the next pass. Inner loop seeds tasks from instructional videos, composes synthetic variants, and uses snapshot-rollback retries to collect reliable trajectories.

### Key idea

Recursive self-improvement without the usual failure mode of "model's own outputs become training data." The outer loop re-opens the data source (open-world corpora, new tasks mined from video) on every iteration so the model keeps encountering *novel* weakness-targeted data; the inner loop only uses model rollouts for the trajectory choice, grounded in snapshot-rollback execution.

### Main finding

CUA-Master-27B reaches strong results on OSWorld Verified, OSWorld 2.0, WeaveBench, and Agents' Last Exam. Nested-loop design is credited with avoiding the stagnation seen in single-loop recursive self-improvement.

### Why it matters

Computer-use is the current proving ground for agent scaling. CUA-Master's double-loop template may be transferable to other long-horizon RL-trained agents where data staleness kills single-loop iteration.

## 12. Wave Forcing

**Authors:** Yijie Jin, Yuxuan Liu, Jiajun Xu, Ye Huang, Chuzhi Wang, Jiayang Li, Ruiyang Chen, Chenling Hong, Xu Wang, Zhijie Deng — *SJTU DENG Lab / PKU / XJTU / ECNU / NTHU*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.00859](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00859) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.00859) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.00859)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `video`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

Streaming video generation at near-interactive speed. Combines a causal "wavefront" generation schedule (chunks of future frames denoised in parallel along a causal diagonal), a three-stage training recipe, and a multi-GPU runtime that pipelines denoising steps and KV-cache refreshes. A 4-step model hits **134.8 FPS end-to-end on 8× H200s**.

### Key idea

Standard rolling diffusion generates frame-chunks sequentially. Wave Forcing instead advances a *wavefront* across (time × denoising-step): at any instant, early frames are near-clean while later frames are still noisy, and compute on each GPU is organized around that diagonal. The training recipe teaches the model to tolerate the causal shape of attention within the wavefront.

### Main finding

5-step Wave Forcing matches Rolling Forcing on overall quality with higher motion scores; 4-step runtime reaches 134.8 FPS end-to-end on 8× H200. Target regime is long-form (minute-plus) streaming video.

### Why it matters

Pushes video diffusion toward real-time generation — important for games, live content, and video-based world models used by agents. Also a demonstration that *scheduling* diffusion compute across steps and GPUs is as important as the model architecture.

## 13. LEMS + KFAC-SVD

**Authors:** Moritz Thoma, Maximilian Forstenhäusler, Emad Aghajanzadeh, Manoj Rohit Vemparala, Christos Anagnostopoulos, Pierpaolo Mori, Nael Fasfous, Alexander Frickenstein, Daniel Mueller-Gritschneder, Ulf Schlichtmann — *TUM / BMW Group / Glasgow / TU Wien*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.32878](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32878) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.32878) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.32878)
**Topics:** `quantization`, `compression`

### TL;DR

Two improvements to SVD-based LLM compression. **LEMS** (Layer-wise Error Modeling Search) replaces uncalibrated proxies for layer-rank allocation with a layer-wise error surrogate plus an ILP, giving a principled global rank budget. **KFAC-SVD** fixes the rank-collapse problem of Fisher-based decomposition by using token-wise KFAC statistics.

### Key idea

Rank allocation in SVD compression has historically used heuristic error proxies. LEMS models per-layer error as a composition of local approximation error + a propagation bias to downstream layers, then solves an integer linear program for the global rank budget. KFAC-SVD addresses the orthogonal decomposition-quality side by using KFAC (Kronecker-factored curvature) over token-wise activations.

### Main finding

Up to **+4.8 pp** zero-shot accuracy over prior rank-allocation strategies across Mistral / Qwen3 / Llama 3 families, holding up to 70B. KFAC-SVD improves average perplexity by **~15%** over Fisher-based SVD.

### Why it matters

SVD-based compression is a weight-only, calibration-light alternative to GPTQ/AWQ, attractive when quantization hardware isn't available. LEMS+KFAC closes a lot of the gap to quantization, and the layer-wise-error framework is reusable for any budget-allocation problem (pruning, mixed-precision quantization).

## 14. ProVe

**Authors:** Ziqi Jin, Weiwen Xu, Jiaxi Li, Yujie Zhong, Qi She, Aixin Sun — *NTU (presumed from author overlap with prior work)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.27815](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27815) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.27815) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.27815)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`, `speculative-decoding`

### TL;DR

Reverses the roles in speculative decoding: fine-tune the **large** model to propose entire token blocks in one forward pass, then **verify** them with a smaller (or identical) model. The lossless variant hits 2.5× throughput with a 32B proposer; a 3B verifier at ≤2.6-point accuracy loss gives 2.7× throughput.

### Key idea

Classical speculative decoding: small draft model proposes; large target verifies. ProVe: large target is *fine-tuned* to emit a candidate block in one forward pass (parallel positions share the forward); a verifier then validates. This folds the "draft" and "target" into roles of the *same* fine-tuned model, trading a small specialized head for the end-to-end latency.

### Main finding

Lossless: **2.5× throughput** with a 32B proposer, matching autoregressive quality. Lossy: **2.7× throughput** using a 3B verifier at 2.6 points average accuracy drop. Tradeoff is tunable by choice of verifier.

### Why it matters

Speculative decoding has historically been small-drafts-first; ProVe shows the complement is viable. Removes the need for a separately trained draft model — an obstacle for new or fast-moving model families.

## 15. History Forcing

**Authors:** Hangyi Kuang, Chengzhi Wang, Yefei He, Weijie Wang, Yuke Zhao, Zhuokun Chen, Zisu Huang, Yifan Yang, Xiaoxuan He, Feng Chen, Bohan Zhuang — *Zhejiang U. / Microsoft Research / Monash / Adelaide*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.02782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02782) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.02782) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.02782)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `video`, `distillation`

### TL;DR

Distillation framework for few-step autoregressive video world models. Takes teacher predictions *with* and *without* historical context, subtracts them, and adds the weighted difference to the student's distillation target. Decouples long-range consistency from the teacher's per-frame visual quality.

### Key idea

A clean "history delta" term: `target = teacher(no_history) + λ · (teacher(with_history) − teacher(no_history))`. The history signal is isolated and can be amplified, so the student learns history-dependent continuation without inheriting the teacher's visual artifacts. For long-video gen, previously-generated chunks serve as history; for world-model consistency, a fixed-view teacher rollout serves as history.

### Main finding

With 1.3B models: **VBench 85.07** for 15-second videos and **VBench-Long 79.82** for 60-second videos. On out-of-sight benchmarks, improved evolution quality and object consistency upon revisit — the standard failure mode of long-horizon video gen.

### Why it matters

Long-horizon consistency is the main open problem in video world models used by agents. A distillation-stage fix (rather than an architecture or data fix) is a cheap route into existing pipelines.
