## 1. Turns Matter

**Authors:** Shuai Dong, Kanghui Tian, Linghan Chen, Wenqi Shao, Hongrui Wu, Haowen Hou, Yongfu Zhu, Xuehui Wang, Ziyue Wang, et al. — *Fudan University / Shanghai AI Laboratory (+ JD.com, Stanford, CUHK, Peking, HKUST, HKU)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.06728](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06728) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06728) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.06728)
**Topics:** `RL`, `multimodal`, `reasoning`

### TL;DR

Standard GRPO applied to streaming video agents (where the model must repeatedly decide "respond now" vs. "keep watching") assigns one trajectory-level advantage to every turn. TurnWise instead scores each decision by its *remaining* return — a "silence-completed prefix" — and averages token losses within each turn before averaging across turns. The combo gives per-decision credit without a learned critic or extra rollouts.

### Key idea

Two decoupled fixes on top of GRPO: **silence-completed prefix scoring** assigns each turn a credit equal to the remaining-trajectory return (treating later uninformative silences as if they were silent), and **turn-mean policy optimization** divides token losses first inside a turn (token-mean) then across turns (turn-mean), so a one-token "wait" and a 500-token "respond" carry equal weight.

### Main finding

Outperforms vanilla GRPO and prior streaming-video RL baselines on the authors' streaming video QA / commentary benchmarks without changing the base policy or using a value function. The method is cheap: no critic, no extra rollouts, same compute per step.

### Why it matters

Streaming agents (live commentary, phone assistants, driving copilots) make a stream of short decisions where "say nothing" is as valuable as "say the right thing." GRPO's single advantage-per-trajectory setup silently discourages the long tail of correct silences; this paper is a drop-in fix for that pathology and a template for turn-aware credit in any multi-turn agent RL setup.

## 2. NEO-VLA

**Authors:** Weichen Fan, Haiwen Diao, Penghao Wu, Siyuan Hu, Ziqi Huang, Tianqi Liu, Mingze Sun, Ziyi Wang, Zhongang Cai, Hanming Deng, Quan Wang, Lei Yang, Lewei Lu, Fangzhou Hong, Zhaoxi Chen, Dahua Lin, Ziwei Liu — *NTU S-Lab / SenseTime / CUHK*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.06918](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06918) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06918) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.06918)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `VLA`, `diffusion`

### TL;DR

Most Vision-Language-Action models bolt a frozen CLIP/SigLIP encoder onto an LLM, which compresses pixels before action reasoning even starts. NEO-VLA removes the separate encoder: a thin patch-conv tokenizer feeds the shared Transformer, and a flow-matching action expert reads visual features from *multiple* depths instead of just the top layer.

### Key idea

**Encoder-free, multi-depth feature access.** Tokenize images with lightweight patch convolutions; let the action head attend to intermediate visual layers; probe layer-wise to show that action-relevant signal peaks *before* the final visual layer — so encoder-based VLAs are throwing it away.

### Main finding

In controlled comparisons, native (encoder-free) VLAs outperform encoder-based ones; distribution-shift robustness improves across manipulation suites. Layer-wise probing confirms the "peak before final" claim.

### Why it matters

VLAs have inherited the Flamingo/LLaVA "freeze an encoder, project, done" template. This paper is one of the clearest arguments that the frozen-encoder recipe leaves control-relevant signal on the floor — and that flow-matching action heads can exploit mid-stack features when given the chance.

## 3. FrontierPhysics

**Authors:** Bingran You, Wenjun Ke, Ria Rosenauer, Bo Liu, Wenbo Chen, Min Chen, Shuqi Xu, Yue Ma, et al. (72 authors) — *BenchFlow / UC Berkeley / Caltech / Harvard / UW / Stanford / …*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.06910](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06910) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06910) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.06910)
**Topics:** `eval`, `agents`

### TL;DR

An agent benchmark for *end-to-end* physics research: 56 tasks across 9 domains (theory, computation, experiment), each authored by one of 42 physicists from their own published work. Agents must review literature, plan, run experiments, and ship structured outputs + a manuscript — graded by deterministic verifiers plus expert rubrics.

### Key idea

Move from "solve this exam question" to "produce the deliverable of a research project." Pairing programmatic verifiers (did the code produce the number?) with expert rubrics (is the reasoning rigorous enough for a referee?) lets the benchmark score *research quality* alongside correctness.

### Main finding

Across **22 models in 26 configurations**, no configuration passes more than **46 of its 168 rollouts** (~27%). Manual trajectory review attributes failures to *insufficiently rigorous reasoning* and *limited expert intuition*, not missing physics knowledge or execution errors.

### Why it matters

Pass rates on math/code benchmarks have saturated for frontier models; "research-grade" benchmarks are the natural next test. FrontierPhysics is one of the first large, expert-authored ones in physics and sets a template (verifier + rubric, author-from-own-paper) that other disciplines can copy.

## 4. Multi-Turn RL

**Authors:** Kejian Zhu, Zhuoran Jin, Shangqing Tu, Tianyi Men, Yupu Hao, Jiachun Li, Kang Liu, Jun Zhao — *CAS / Tsinghua*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.06483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06483) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06483) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.06483)
**Topics:** `RL`, `agents`, `eval`

### TL;DR

An analysis paper asking: what does multi-turn RL for LLM agents actually buy you? Using Pass@(K,T) — a two-axis metric varying sampling breadth K and interaction depth T — the authors find that agentic RL gains shrink when the interaction budget is tight, that RL doesn't uniformly sharpen single-step decision quality, and that its biggest contribution is **error recovery and long-horizon resilience**.

### Key idea

Decompose "agentic RL improves agents" into *where* it improves them. **Pass@(K,T)** separates breadth of sampling from depth of interaction; the gap between single-turn Pass@K and multi-turn Pass@(K,T) isolates what interaction depth is really contributing. Introduces **BudgetMix RL**, which varies T during training so the policy is robust across deployment budgets.

### Main finding

Trajectory-level RL advantages collapse under short turn budgets. Per-step decision quality is often *not* improved by RL — recovery from mistakes is. BudgetMix RL keeps performance stable across T budgets at train time.

### Why it matters

Papers claim multi-turn RL "works" without saying on which axis. This paper gives the field a clean decomposition and a concrete practice (randomize T during training) that follows from it. Expect Pass@(K,T) to become a standard reporting axis for agent RL.

## 5. EmoReward

**Authors:** Mingzhe Zheng, Yue Wu, Jing Tan, Chang Zou, Dengyang Jiang, Zeyue Tian, Xuanhua He, Ryan Xu, Yunyang Ge, Sihui Ji, et al. — *HKUST / Tencent Hunyuan / Fudan / Waterloo*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.06518](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06518) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06518) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.06518)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `RL`, `reward-modeling`

### TL;DR

RLHF on video generators usually fixes a weighted mix of visual-quality / motion / text-alignment rewards. For emotional video, those miss the point and can be gamed. EmoReward plans grounding cues from the emotional prompt, scores with multiple signal types, instance-gates each signal (e.g. only trust face-emotion when face detection is confident), and after a training run **re-weights the mixture** based on measured per-signal reliability — without retraining any reward model.

### Key idea

**Self-evolving reward calibration.** A memory tracks how reliable each reward component was during the last training iteration (e.g., disagreement with other signals, ablation deltas). The next iteration's weighted reward mixture is adjusted accordingly. The reward *models* are frozen; only their *weights* evolve.

### Main finding

Improvements across several RL algorithms on HunyuanVideo 1.5 for emotional T2V; ablation shows the instance-level gating and the memory-based re-weighting each matter; benefits transfer to SD 3.5 and FLUX image generation.

### Why it matters

Reward-mix tuning is one of RLHF's most under-documented pain points — practitioners currently grid-search weights manually. A principled, data-driven loop for reward weighting is a reusable primitive for any multi-reward RL system (not just emotional video).

## 6. OPD Limits

**Authors:** Yichen Wang, Yufa Zhou, Siqi Zhu, Haozheng Luo, Jingzhi Bao, Zhichao Liu, Zijun Wei — *(mixed academic)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.05609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05609) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.05609) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.05609)
**Topics:** `RL`, `distillation`, `eval`

### TL;DR

On-policy distillation (OPD) is the current favorite recipe for consolidating an RL-trained model back into a smaller base. The authors extend it to **multi-teacher OPD** and find a sharp pathology: students hit 96–103% of teacher in-distribution scores but drop **2.45–16.25% below the base model** on OOD tasks. They patch it with geometry-based parameter merging and base-preserving replay.

### Key idea

OPD overfits to teacher trajectories' distribution. Two remedies: **geometry-based patching** (project student weights toward the base along low-loss directions) and **base-preserving replay** (mix base-model outputs into the distillation targets) — both preserving OOD behavior while keeping in-distribution wins.

### Main finding

Across several setups, OPD and MOPD recover near-teacher in-distribution performance but regress on OOD; the two proposed patches close most of the OOD gap with only small ID cost.

### Why it matters

OPD has become the de facto "fold RL gains back in" step, popularized by DeepSeek-R1 and successors. Showing that this step causes measurable OOD regression — and giving two mitigations — is directly actionable for anyone running the standard RL → distill → ship pipeline.

## 7. TAC-CoT

**Authors:** Juseon Do, Sangmin Bae, Jeesu Jung, Hwanjun Song — *KAIST*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.01926](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01926) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.01926) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.01926)
**Topics:** `reasoning`, `agents`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

Explicit chain-of-thought is slow, especially when it's wrapped around tool calls that already produce external evidence. TAC-CoT replaces the natural-language reasoning inside a tool-using loop with *latent* reasoning (hidden-state passes), keeping plain text only for tool calls, tool observations, and final answers.

### Key idea

**Interleave explicit and implicit CoT.** Reasoning between tool-invocation turns is run in latent space (no visible tokens), but the surface tokens needed for routing to the tool and parsing its output stay explicit. Training uses a curriculum that gradually shifts text tokens into latent segments.

### Main finding

Up to **+8.8 points** accuracy and **up to 6.8× fewer forward passes** across agentic math/QA tasks, with improved tool-use quality in both single- and multi-tool settings.

### Why it matters

Implicit CoT (COCONUT, Quiet-STaR etc.) has been limited to tool-less settings. Showing it composes cleanly with tool use — and *speeds up* agent loops without hurting tool quality — opens an inference-cost lever most agent stacks aren't pulling yet.

## 8. MUON-RT

**Authors:** Aryan Das, Vinay Kumar Verma, Aakansha Mishra — *Amazon*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.41104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41104) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.41104) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.41104)
**Topics:** `optimizer`, `distillation`, `fine-tuning`

### TL;DR

Muon's momentum buffer is a big chunk of optimizer state; in a self-distillation loop you already have access to a persistent "teacher" copy of the weights, and the teacher→student displacement is *itself* an exponentially weighted average of recent updates. MUON-RT replaces Muon's momentum buffer with that displacement for hidden weight matrices — no momentum, less state.

### Key idea

**Reuse the teacher-student displacement as momentum.** In Self-Distillation Fine-Tuning you maintain a slow-moving teacher anyway. The vector `θ_student − θ_teacher` captures the same signal Muon's EMA would, so you can drop the buffer.

### Main finding

On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct with Self-Distillation Fine-Tuning: **+2.6 avg-accuracy points over AdamW with 42% less persistent memory**, and **+5.4 points over momentum Muon with 27% less persistent memory**.

### Why it matters

Optimizer state is now a first-order concern for 7B+ fine-tuning on consumer-ish setups. Muon is replacing AdamW in several open stacks; making it momentum-free *when you're already doing self-distillation* is a clean memory win without touching training throughput.

## 9. SpecOPD

**Authors:** Tianyu Liu, Jun Zhang, Wenjie Huang, Yuhao Shen, Tianqi Xu, Xinyue Hao, Hongyaoxing Gu, Xuyang Liu, Baolin Zhang, et al. — *Alibaba Qwen*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.02846](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02846) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.02846) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.02846)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`, `distillation`

### TL;DR

Training a draft model for speculative decoding with on-policy distillation is bottlenecked by sequential rollouts. SpecOPD runs several draft branches in parallel, packs them into shared proposal trees with specialized attention, and trains the drafter with **E2E-TV** — a surrogate that directly optimizes expected acceptance length instead of per-token cross-entropy.

### Key idea

**Branch-parallel on-policy distillation.** Independent draft branches sample simultaneously, tree-pack their proposals so the target model verifies them in one pass, and the training loss targets the quantity you actually care about at serving time: accepted tokens per step.

### Main finding

**Up to +41.45% mean acceptance length**, **16.09× supervised-token throughput** versus sequential-rollout OPD, and **up to 3.56× end-to-end generation speedup** over autoregressive decoding.

### Why it matters

Drafter training has been the main cost gate on EAGLE/Medusa-style speculative decoding in production. A 16× supervised-throughput win makes shipping a bespoke drafter per model/release realistic — and directly optimizing acceptance length instead of cross-entropy is the kind of correction that reliably compounds.

## 10. VA-TDM

**Authors:** Yihong Luo, Tianyu Pang, Tianyang Hu — *HKUST / Sea AI Lab*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.06796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06796) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06796) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.06796)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `distillation`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

Few-step diffusion distillation usually matches student/teacher outputs at a few discrete timesteps. VA-TDM instead trains a critic to compare student and teacher **velocity fields** along the trajectory and uses that disagreement as the student's training signal — a unified view of adversarial and trajectory-matching distillation.

### Key idea

**Velocity Adversarial Distillation (VAD).** A learned discriminator scores agreement between student and teacher velocities; the generator (student) minimizes it. Applied along intermediate sampling steps, it becomes VA-TDM, extending Trajectory Distribution Matching with an adversarial critic.

### Main finding

State-of-the-art 4-step text-to-image and text-to-video performance; VA-TDM-H3 beats the few-step Fast-H3 baseline and closes most of the gap to the many-step H3 model in human preference studies.

### Why it matters

Diffusion inference cost is still dominated by step count. Distillation to 1–4 steps is the frontier, with a bestiary of competing losses (DMD, CMD, LCM, PGD, …). Unifying several of them through a velocity-field adversarial view both clarifies the design space and measurably improves it.

## 11. Hybrid Computer Use

**Authors:** Haoqing Wang, Xiaoyi Zhang, Sicheng Fan, Zhexin Zhang, Xinjie Zhang, Jiahao Li, Bin Li, Yan Lu — *Microsoft Research Asia*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.41432](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41432) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.41432) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.41432)
**Topics:** `agents`, `multimodal`

### TL;DR

Pure vision ("screenshot in, click out") is slow and brittle; pure accessibility tree is complete but verbose and fragile across apps; pure code is only possible where a stable API exists. HCU gives the agent **all three** as a unified interface — programmatic (shell/code), semantic (AX tree), visual (screenshot) — and batches AX observations/actions to cut round trips.

### Key idea

**Three-modality unified interface** with AX **diff reporting**: tools return compact AX observations, actions are batched, and effects come back as AX diffs rather than full snapshots. The agent picks the right modality per step instead of being locked into one.

### Main finding

Highest Partial and Binary scores on **OSWorld 2.0** among evaluated systems; every model tested improves when switched from a single-modality interface to HCU.

### Why it matters

OSWorld, AndroidWorld, and WebArena have pushed agent-stack research toward a single modality (often vision). This paper argues — with numbers — that the right abstraction is modality-pluralist, and that cutting round trips via diffs is as important as model quality.

## 12. LoopVQ

**Authors:** Minsoo Cheong, Sungjoo Yoo — *Seoul National University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.05304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05304) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.05304) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.05304)
**Topics:** `quantization`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

Looped transformers (Ouro, Huginn) run the same block repeatedly, which multiplies KV-cache cost by loop depth. LoopVQ vector-quantizes the KV cache *across* loop steps — jointly coding each channel's values over all iterations — and fine-tunes the codebooks to reproduce full-precision outputs. Pushes the cache to **sub-1-bit / element**.

### Key idea

**Joint cross-step VQ for looped KV.** Instead of quantizing each step's K/V tensor independently, treat a channel's values across all loop iterations as one codebook entry; the per-element bit budget then shrinks with loop depth.

### Main finding

At **0.5 bits/element** beats prior KV VQ methods on Ouro and Huginn; combined with TaSQ, retains most of BF16 accuracy at **0.25 bits/element**. In SGLang serving: ~50% decode-latency reduction at 32K context and **up to 4.3× / 6.1× peak throughput** over BF16 on the 1.4B / 2.6B Ouro models.

### Why it matters

KV cache is already the dominant memory cost at long context, and *looped* models amplify that by the loop count. LoopVQ shows the amplification can be turned into a compression bonus — the shared structure across loop steps is exactly what VQ wants.

### Knowledge graph integration

## 13. SIMPLE Law

**Authors:** Hiari Pizzini Cavagna, Andrea Proia, Daniele Cesarini, Zeynep Kiziltan, Andrea Bartolini — *University of Bologna / CINECA*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.03330](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03330) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03330) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.03330)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`, `systems`

### TL;DR

A four-parameter analytical model for LLM inference energy that separates **prefill** from **decode**, and inside decode separates **KV-cache movement** from **next-token compute**. No per-model recalibration; one fit covers a model family.

### Key idea

**Phase-and-component decomposition.** Prefill cost scales with prompt length squared (attention) and model size; decode cost per step has a KV-read term that grows with context and a compute term that doesn't. Four physically motivated parameters estimated from a handful of profiling runs.

### Main finding

Across 13 dense LLMs from 6 families on H100 GPUs, achieves **R² = 0.969** and **11.97% MAPE** — **3.1–3.5× lower error** than prior models — *without* per-model calibration.

### Why it matters

Energy / cost modeling for LLM serving has mostly been black-box regressions calibrated per model. A compact analytical law with physical semantics lets capacity planners and research on "what architecture is cheap to serve" reason without constant re-profiling.

## 14. MSafeTemp

**Authors:** Max Neuwinger, Hanna Yukhymenko, Imanol Schlag — *ETH Zürich*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.40516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40516) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.40516) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.40516)
**Topics:** `safety`, `data`

### TL;DR

Safety alignment concentrates on English, which leaves big holes in low-resource languages. The authors build **MSafeTemp**: extract safe templates from harmful prompts, translate those templates into 60 languages, fill slots with *culturally localized* harmful/benign entities — then measure that some attacks fail in English but succeed after translation, and that template-based augmentation improves cross-lingual safety.

### Key idea

**Template extraction + cultural slot-filling.** A harmful prompt is decomposed into a reusable template and entity slots; translation preserves structure while localization swaps in locale-appropriate entities, producing a *scalable* multilingual safety corpus without manual per-language annotation.

### Main finding

English-only safety evaluation misjudges multilingual safety: multiple frontier models refuse an attack in English and comply with its translation. Training on MSafeTemp improves refusal consistency across languages.

### Why it matters

Low-resource-language jailbreaks are an established hole in safety training; this paper offers the first template-augmentation pipeline at the scale of 60 languages, with evidence that English-only red-teaming is systematically overestimating multilingual safety.

## 15. Multi-layer Indexer

**Authors:** Serdarcan Dilbaz, Arjun Krishna, Kiran Kamble, Daniel M. Bikel — *Writer*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.03768](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03768) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03768) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.03768)
**Topics:** `architecture`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

DeepSeek Sparse Attention's "indexer" picks which tokens attention actually reads, but the indexer runs serially with its layer's attention. **MLI (Multi-layer Indexer)** lets a layer's attention reuse the *previous* indexer layer's selection, so the current layer's indexer can run **in parallel** with attention — no retraining, no new weights.

### Key idea

**Overlap selection with attention by one layer.** Because token-selection patterns evolve slowly across depths in DSA, reusing layer `l−1`'s selection in layer `l`'s attention is a mild approximation that unlocks a full layer of parallelism. Composes with IndexCache.

### Main finding

On DeepSeek-V3.2 at retention 1/2: **up to +14.5% decode speed** over IndexCache. At 128K context, retention 1/4: **1.32× unmodified decode throughput**, versus IndexCache's 1.28× at retention 1/8.

### Why it matters

DSA is the current sparse-attention default inside DeepSeek's stack and is spreading; making its indexer free at decode time removes the main runtime cost that sparse attention was supposed to *save*. Small, concrete, retrofit-friendly.
