## 1. SAE Interprets MoE

**Authors:** Anyuan Zhuo, Shaohua Zhang, Yuan Lin, Pinyan Lu — *ByteDance Seed*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.08089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08089) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.08089) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.08089)
**Topics:** `interp`, `MoE`, `SAE`

### TL;DR

A sparse autoencoder (SAE) is trained on the pre-routing hidden states feeding an MoE layer, and each learned feature is attributed to the expert(s) it most strongly activates. The experts turn out to carve up concepts at a much finer and more human-interpretable granularity than router statistics or domain tags alone would suggest.

### Key idea

Fit an SAE on the residual-stream vectors entering an MoE block, then compute, per SAE feature, the distribution of expert routing probabilities over examples that fire that feature. A concentrated distribution means the expert specialises on *that* feature; aggregating these gives an interpretable description of what each expert does.

### Main finding

Individual experts in common MoE LLMs show fine-grained semantic specialization (e.g. formatting, specific languages, syntactic constructs) that is invisible to coarse routing metrics. The specialization is stable across checkpoints and does not reduce to domain or token-type labels.

### Why it matters

Gives the first scalable interpretability probe for MoE LLMs: instead of hand-labelling expert inputs, SAE features act as concept classifiers whose expert-attribution profile explains what each expert contributes. Useful for debugging routing collapse and for safety audits on frontier MoE models.

## 2. Muir: Linear Attention with Sensitivity-Allocated Gating and Matched-Filter Readout

**Authors:** Gil Tabak, Clemens J. S. Schaefer, et al. — *Google*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.08376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08376) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.08376) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.08376)
**Topics:** `architectures`, `linear-attention`, `SSM`

### TL;DR

Muir is a linear-attention block that extends Gated DeltaNet-2 with a low-rank, sensitivity-allocated gating projection and a matched-filter readout gate that re-weights state rows at query time. Parameter savings from the low-rank gate fund a wider FFN, giving a strict-upgrade over GDN-2 and Transformer at matched compute.

### Key idea

Decompose the gate into a low-rank projection whose columns are allocated by estimated sensitivity (the "sensitivity-allocated gating"), and add a matched-filter readout that scales each state row by its correlation with the query — a cheap approximation of attention-style retrieval on top of the recurrent state. A boundary-pivoted chunkwise training kernel keeps the pass efficient on wide matrix units (TPU/H100).

### Main finding

At 166M → 1.34B parameters, Muir reports lower perplexity than GDN-2 and than a Transformer of the same size, plus better commonsense, recall, and long-context (32K) retrieval. Training is faster than a Transformer at 32K context thanks to linear-time attention + chunkwise kernels.

### Why it matters

Keeps pushing the linear-attention / SSM family toward Transformer parity: rather than inventing a new recurrence, the paper shows that *how* you gate and *how* you read out can close the remaining gap, and that the budget can be recycled into width. Argues for linear attention as the long-context default.

## 3. VFlash: Fast, Accurate Visual Planning via Flash Primitive Decoding

**Authors:** Shihao Wang, Jin Huang, Chi-Pin Huang, Chiao-An Yang, et al. — *NVIDIA + Johns Hopkins*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.08449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08449) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.08449) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.08449)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `agents`, `efficient-inference`, `VLA`

### TL;DR

Instead of autoregressively emitting one coordinate token at a time, VFlash predicts entire *visual primitives* (points, bounding boxes, trajectories) in a single forward pass via a small drafter head, then validates the draft against structural constraints before accepting it.

### Key idea

A "flash primitive" is a tuple of coordinates with a fixed cardinality (e.g. 4 for a bbox, N for a trajectory). The drafter outputs all coordinates jointly conditioned on the VLM features; a structural verifier (ordering, bounds, type) rejects malformed drafts. In spirit this is speculative decoding specialized to structured geometric outputs.

### Main finding

Lower coordinate error than autoregressive decoding on 2D/3D robot-trajectory benchmarks, ≈25× faster generation on 3D point-motion forecasting in preliminary single-rollout tests, and 70.2% average success rate on four MolmoSpaces task suites vs 62.6% for the AR baseline.

### Why it matters

Makes real-time visual planning plausible for VLA controllers: autoregressive coordinate decoding is a known bottleneck in VLMs that emit boxes or paths, and a structure-aware draft+verify loop is a clean way to break it. Likely to be adopted by downstream VLA pipelines.

## 4. SLA: Scaling Spatial-Language-Action Model via Continual Pre-training

**Authors:** Wenxuan Song, Qizhou Huang, Ziyang Zhou, Jingbo Wang, Junwu E, Pengwei Wang, Yan Wang, Shunbo Zhou, Haoang Li — *HKUST (Guangzhou), BAAI, Tsinghua, OLA Dimensions*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.08373](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08373) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.08373) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.08373)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `VLA`, `pre-training`

### TL;DR

SLA takes a pretrained π0.5 VLA policy and performs a continual-pretraining stage that *aligns the VLM's intermediate visual tokens with 3D spatial embeddings* from a frozen perception backbone, while jointly predicting actions. This installs explicit spatial reasoning into a robot-action model without re-training it from scratch.

### Key idea

A two-objective loss on the base VLA: (a) the usual action-prediction loss, (b) a cosine-alignment loss between selected VLM visual tokens and spatial features from a frozen 3D-scene encoder. Continual pre-training first, then post-training on action data; no change to the base π0.5 architecture.

### Main finding

State-of-the-art on RoboDojo-Real, RoboTwin 2.0, RoboCasa-Panda, and LIBERO-Plus; +24.0-point average success-rate improvement over the π0.5 baseline across 8 real-world robot-arm conditions.

### Why it matters

Shows that VLA models can be upgraded with 3D-awareness post-hoc, without discarding the expensive language-action pretraining. Points toward continual-pretraining as a lightweight way to install new perceptual modalities into foundation policies.

## 5. Harness-S: Learning from Sibling Harnesses for Generalizable Agent Training

**Authors:** Wenxiang Jiao, Bowen Fang, Shuai Shao, Jiachen Zhu, et al. — *Xiaohongshu / SJTU / CMU*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.04277](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04277) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.04277) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.04277)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `post-training`

### TL;DR

Agent harnesses (prompting + scaffolding around the policy) are usually brittle — small edits to the base harness change behaviour non-monotonically. Harness-S trains one policy to behave well across a *pool of sibling harnesses* and uses them to rescue the tasks the base harness fails on, with an adaptive router at inference time.

### Key idea

Three parts. (1) A fixed pool of sibling harnesses, each a small structural edit of the base. (2) Rescue training: on tasks where all base-harness rollouts fail, generate trajectories under sibling harnesses and distil only the successful ones back into a single policy that runs under the base harness. (3) An adaptive router that, at test time, picks the harness via behaviour-profile matching + LLM voting.

### Main finding

Beats policy-only training on AppWorld, SWE-Gym and DBBench; further gains from Rank-1 routing. Confirms that harness edits give *locality, complementarity and nonstationarity*: different siblings fix different failure modes.

### Why it matters

Reframes "agent prompt engineering" as a training-time signal. Instead of picking one harness and overfitting the policy to it, treat the harness as an augmentation dimension. Natural fit for long-horizon agent RL where credit assignment is thin.

## 6. Random Is Almost Enough? Stable and Low-Cost Benchmark Compression via BestRand

**Authors:** George Ling, Shanshan Zhong, Lijin Yang, Hao Yang, Nanxiang Li, Zhongzhan Huang — *Carnegie Mellon University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.00883](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00883) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.00883) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.00883)
**Topics:** `eval`

### TL;DR

Random sub-sampling already gives a near-unbiased estimator of a benchmark's full-set score, but has a fat failure tail. BestRand draws a small number of random subsets and picks the one whose estimated score minimises an auxiliary stability proxy — keeping random sampling's cost while killing the long tail.

### Key idea

Rather than cluster or learn an item-difficulty model (like Anchor-IT / IRT-based compression), BestRand reduces compression to a *multi-draw selection*: draw K random subsets, score each one by a cheap stability proxy (variance across model families at the sampled items), and return the best. Simple, no training, and strictly dominates plain random on tail error.

### Main finding

Validated across 70+ text, multimodal, and agent benchmarks: lower high-error risk, lower mean estimation error, and lower construction cost than competing compression methods, including IRT-based ones.

### Why it matters

Benchmark compression keeps getting adopted for cheap-eval loops (RL rewards, model cards). The paper argues the sophisticated methods often don't beat a well-tuned random baseline — a strong "simplicity" result that should influence how teams build their eval harnesses.

## 7. WeLM-Image: A Unified Image Generation Model towards Visual Intelligence

**Authors:** WeChat Vision team — *Tencent Inc.*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.07392](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07392) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.07392) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.07392)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `multimodal`

### TL;DR

WeLM-Image is a single image-generation model that unifies text-to-image, image editing, controllable generation (depth / pose / segmentation conditions), and multimodal understanding behind one tokenizer+diffusion backbone. Aimed at being a one-model drop-in for the whole image-generation stack of a product.

### Key idea

A shared visual tokenizer feeds both an autoregressive text/image planner and a diffusion image decoder; conditioning (edit mask, control signal, reference image) is injected as additional tokens into the same context. Training stacks text-to-image, instruction editing, and controllable generation objectives on one backbone.

### Main finding

Reports competitive results against specialist models on benchmarks spanning generation, editing, and controllable generation with a single checkpoint. Architecture and recipe described in detail, intended as a Tencent-internal unification effort.

### Why it matters

Follows the same consolidation trend as WeLM for text: one model, many conditioning modes. Contrasts with the "best-of-breed per task" approach of the current OSS stack. Interesting datapoint for teams deciding whether to maintain separate T2I / edit / control models.

## 8. TailSteer: Trajectory-Tail Steering for Drift-Resistant Multi-turn Image Editing

**Authors:** Ruikang Li, Zixuan Chen, Jiarui Wu, Mingde Yao, Yiyuan Zhang, et al. (38 authors) — *MMLab-CUHK*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.06741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06741) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06741) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.06741)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `multimodal`

### TL;DR

Multi-turn image editing (edit → edit → edit) accumulates drift: subjects warp, identity shifts, colours slide. TailSteer fine-tunes only the *trajectory tail* (the last few denoising steps) on the editor's own rollouts, keeping the early denoising path close to the base model and concentrating corrections where drift shows up.

### Key idea

Split the denoising trajectory into "early" (near-noise) and "tail" (near-image) steps. Freeze early-step behaviour; do RL/SFT on the tail steps against a multi-turn-drift reward that compares turn-N output to turn-N-1 anchors. Final-stage training only — no change to the base editor.

### Main finding

Reduces cumulative drift on multi-turn editing across FLUX, SenseNova, and Qwen model families. Keeps single-turn edit quality at baseline; improves identity preservation and semantic stability over 3+ turns.

### Why it matters

Multi-turn editing is becoming the main UX surface for image editors (chat-style iteration). Drift is the dominant failure mode; this is a surgical, trajectory-aware fix instead of yet another full-pipeline RLHF.

## 9. EvoMemState: Evolving Memory State for Personal Agents

**Authors:** Zhishang Xiang, Yunbo Tang, Zhongqiao Liu, Junkai Chen, et al. — *Xiamen University, NTU, UBC, Waterloo, Jilin*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.03743](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03743) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03743) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.03743)
**Topics:** `agents`, `RL`, `memory`

### TL;DR

Personal LLM agents today store "facts" in a flat memory and retrieve by similarity, which forgets validity windows and relationships. EvoMemState stores each fact with a *Relational State* (validity, scope, other-fact links) and a *Reasoning State* that decides how a fact applies to the current query, both updated by RL.

### Key idea

Three components: (1) ontology-guided state induction decides which state fields a new fact gets; (2) an RL stage evolves Relational-State fields as new facts arrive (invalidating, scoping, linking); (3) state-aware reasoning uses the states at retrieval time to filter candidates before the LLM sees them.

### Main finding

Beats flat-memory and graph-memory baselines on long-term dialogue and memory-use benchmarks. Larger gains on retrieval completeness than on raw accuracy — the states let the retriever return the right *subset* of facts, not just a semantically close one.

### Why it matters

Personal-agent memory is becoming a product surface (ChatGPT memory, Claude projects). The explicit-state framing is a cleaner alternative to the "throw everything into a vector DB" default, and is amenable to debugging.

## 10. On the Opportunities and Challenges of Prefill-to-Autoregressive Cross-Model Transfer

**Authors:** Junru Lu, Wei Liu, Jiangnan Ye, Jiazheng Li, Di Yin, Xing Sun, Tameem Adel, Yali Du, Lin Gui, Yulan He — *KCL / Tencent Youtu Lab / NPL*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.06976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06976) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06976) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.06976)
**Topics:** `architectures`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

PLatour pairs a large "sender" model that only does prefill with a small "receiver" model that generates the response, bridged by a thin learned linear projection. Decoding cost drops to the small model's; prefill quality stays close to the large model's. The paper also introduces PACT-Check, a probe that catches "fake" gains where the sender's signal was never actually used.

### Key idea

Freeze both models; learn a zero-initialized linear bridge between the sender's final hidden states and the receiver's input embeddings, trained with task rewards. The sender is called once per prompt (prefill-only), the receiver auto-regresses. PACT-Check runs ablations swapping in uninformative sender signals or receiver-only training, and flags any setting where those still match PLatour.

### Main finding

Outperforms OSS baselines on generation tasks and beats Mostik's reported math/coding scores with a strictly smaller sender. Controlled experiments show that for some settings the gain can be reproduced by uninformative signals — a cautionary result that most prior cross-model-transfer work doesn't report.

### Why it matters

Serves the "big-prefill, small-decode" pattern that is becoming a standard serving topology (prefill/decode disaggregation). PLatour gives a trainable cross-model bridge, and PACT-Check gives the community a cheap way to tell "real" cross-model transfer from measurement artifact.

## 11. StepX-OPD: Rethinking Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation in Diffusion Models

**Authors:** Chenru Wang, Xuanyang Zhang, Yucheng Han, Wenbo Nie, Wei Cheng, Xianfang Zeng, Gang Yu, Chi Zhang — *StepFun*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.04386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04386) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.04386) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.04386)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `distillation`

### TL;DR

Diffusion training usually separates "capability acquisition" (instruction following, text rendering, preference) from "step distillation" (few-step sampling). StepX-OPD merges them: one student rolls out trajectories and is supervised on the states it actually visits by multiple frozen specialist teachers, each contributing one capability.

### Key idea

On-policy: the student samples a trajectory; frozen specialist teachers predict the target velocity at the student's visited states; a weighted loss regresses the student onto a blend. Low-noise segments get an extra velocity-regression term. A variant penalizes unwanted text rendering from prompt words. Works with SD3.5-Medium and FLUX.1-dev.

### Main finding

Highest normalized average among four-step methods, beats the 40-step Flow-OPD teacher on SD3.5-Medium using ~37% fewer GPU-hours than sequential DiffusionOPD+step-distillation.

### Why it matters

Unifies two expensive stages of the modern diffusion recipe, saving a non-trivial chunk of compute. The multi-teacher, on-policy framing is also likely to transfer to video diffusion where specialists already dominate.

## 12. Decode Before You Hear: Speculative Listening for Chunk-Interleaved Full-Duplex Speech LMs

**Authors:** Yutong Bian, Yanglin Zhang, Yuxiang Wang, Luoyuan Zhang, Rongshen He, Zihao Wang, Junwen Qiu, Jiaqi Li, Zhizheng Wu — *CUHK-Shenzhen*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.06982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06982) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06982) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.06982)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`, `speech-LLM`

### TL;DR

Full-duplex speech LLMs alternate "listen" and "speak" chunks; during "listen" the model is idle. Speculative Listening uses that idle compute to pre-draft the *next* speak chunk under a silence assumption (plus a runner-up branch), then validates against the audio that actually arrives. Training-free, lossless.

### Key idea

Standard speculative decoding uses a small draft model. Here the "draft" is the big model itself running speculatively on the hypothesis that the next audio is silence; when audio arrives the KV cache is either accepted or rewound to the branch point. Adds a runner-up-first-token branch to cover the common "quick reply" case.

### Main finding

2.6–2.8× speedups on speak chunks, up to 23.4× on tool-call chunks (where silence is the default assumption), 73% reduction in pre-response decoding delay, and 94.4% reduction in maximum lag behind real time on the authors' TickTock-Bench.

### Why it matters

Full-duplex is becoming the default UX for voice assistants (Moshi, OpenAI Realtime). Latency is still the dominant bottleneck; this exploits a free compute budget that every full-duplex model has.

## 13. Tools Need Not Speak in Text: Latent Context Compression for Agents (Coca-Cola)

**Authors:** Tsinghua AI Agent Group — *Tsinghua University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.06500](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06500) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06500) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.06500)
**Topics:** `agents`, `efficient-inference`

### TL;DR

Agents today dump raw tool outputs back into the prompt. Coca-Cola replaces those outputs with a small number of *latent* tokens embedded directly into the agent's input embedding space, trained so that the agent's next-action distribution is preserved under compressed vs raw observation.

### Key idea

Train a compression model that maps a tool observation to K continuous latent tokens, inserted into the agent's context in the same slot the text would go. The training objective ("Action-Preserving Training") aligns the agent's next-action distribution under compressed observations with the full-observation distribution — i.e. KL-matching on action logits, with the agent frozen or updated jointly.

### Main finding

On four web-search QA benchmarks across two model families: 70–89% reduction in agent token consumption, 24–56% end-to-end runtime reduction, with task performance *better* than the raw-text baseline on most settings. Code released at THU-Agent/Coca-cola.

### Why it matters

Agent context budgets are the main cost driver in production agent stacks, and tool outputs dominate those budgets. Latent compression breaks the "pay for every token the tool emits" assumption without needing a bigger context window.

## 14. Identically Trained Language Models Break on Different Jailbreaks

**Authors:** Chi-Wei Chang, Mu-Tien Kuo, Kuan-Wei Lee, Meng-Hsun Tsai — *Cerulean Labs*
**Links:** [arXiv:2610.34074](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34074) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.34074) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.34074)
**Topics:** `safety`, `jailbreaks`, `eval`

### TL;DR

Train 150 copies of a 1B LLM with the same data and the same safety recipe but different random seeds, and they are vulnerable to *different* jailbreaks. Per-prompt non-refusal correlations across pretraining seeds (0.81) are lower than across identical pretraining runs (0.98). Jailbreak susceptibility is a property of the *specific* training trajectory, not the recipe.

### Key idea

A controlled factorial: 25 data recipes × 3 pretraining seeds × 2 post-training seeds = 150 models. Evaluate a battery of jailbreak templates on each. Compute per-prompt non-refusal pattern correlations across pairs of models grouped by what changed between them.

### Main finding

Per-prompt non-refusal correlates 0.984 between identical pretraining runs, 0.811 across pretraining seeds, 0.779 across data recipes. The "best" attack template stays best after a new pretraining seed in only 27% of pairs, vs 67% after a new post-training seed. A model's per-prompt jailbreak pattern is a fingerprint of its pretraining run.

### Why it matters

Red-team evaluation of a single frontier model probably underestimates fleet risk: a next seed will fail differently. The result argues for seed-ensemble jailbreak eval and against treating "one model passes" as evidence the recipe is safe.
