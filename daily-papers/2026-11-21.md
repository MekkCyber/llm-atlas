## 1. WeMM-Video

**Authors:** Changli Tang, Jie Yang, Tianyi Wang, Lei Li, Daoze Zhang, Zijie Xin, Zhan Liu, Li Li, Jinchang Xu, Fengyun Rao, Chao Zhang, Jing Lyu — *WeChat Vision, Tencent Inc. (Tang, Z. Liu, C. Zhang also Tsinghua)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.26409](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26409) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26409) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.26409)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `MoE`, `video`, `post-training`

### TL;DR

A bilingual omni-modal foundation-model family (2B, 4B, 9B, 35B-A3B) jointly trained on text, image, audio, and video. Backs a hybrid-thinking recipe where the same weights serve both fast non-thinking and step-by-step reasoning modes, trained on ~1.2T tokens through progressive pre-training and multi-stage post-training.

### Key idea

A single transformer backbone covers all four modalities and a mode switch toggles explicit chain-of-thought on or off. The flagship uses MoE (35B total / 3B active) to buy capacity without paying activation cost. Post-training stages chain SFT, preference optimization, and reasoning RL on top of the omni-modal base.

### Main finding

New open-source SOTA on 120+ benchmarks for omni-modal video understanding. The 9B dense variant beats the larger open Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct, and the 35B-A3B leads the open-source field. Models and pipeline are released.

### Why it matters

The frontier of open omni-modal models has been Qwen's Omni line. WeMM-Video is a credible competitor and gives the community a reference recipe for hybrid thinking at MoE scale — the first serious omni "think / don't think" release outside closed-API frontier labs.

## 2. AutoMaster

**Authors:** Shuang Sun, Fanzhe Meng, Xinyu Tang, Ruihua Song, Wayne Xin Zhao (+ one unnamed first author) — *Gaoling School of AI, Renmin University of China*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.26510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26510) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26510) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.26510)
**Topics:** `post-training`, `SFT`, `agents`, `self-improvement`

### TL;DR

Task success is a weak target for distillation — a teacher can succeed via lucky guesses, repetition, or shortcut verification. AutoMaster is an LLM agent that *designs* natural-language "skills" for the teacher to follow, then fine-tunes a student on those skill-guided rollouts. The agent iteratively refines the skill set from evaluation feedback.

### Key idea

**Skill-FT**: keep the teacher weights frozen and instead steer its rollouts via a library of natural-language skill prompts the agent writes, selects, and refines. The student is fine-tuned on these higher-quality teacher trajectories, *including* the skill instructions in the training input even though they're absent at eval time.

### Main finding

On Terminal, Search, and software-engineering tasks, AutoMaster-distilled 4B and 9B students beat standard SFT on successful teacher trajectories. Keeping skill prompts in the training input (not just in rollout) yields further gains despite being dropped at inference.

### Why it matters

Reframes autonomous post-training as an **optimizer over natural-language skill libraries** rather than over trajectory selection. A middle layer between vanilla distillation and RLHF that costs much less than RL but captures behaviors success-only distillation misses.

## 3. Coda-DaVinci

**Authors:** Qixun Wang, Yang Shi, Zhuoran Zhang, Yifan Xu, Tengfei Liu, Yuqi Tang, Xiaohan Zhang, Shujie Li, Jiafu Tang, Xuanyu Zhu, Xinyu Liu, Yan He, Yushuo Guan, Zihao Wang, Xianghua Ying, Shixuan Liu, Haotian Wang, Wenjing Yang, Zhaoxiang Zhang, Yuanxing Zhang, Pengfei Wan, Jiaheng Liu — *KlingAI Research · Peking University*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.26595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26595) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26595) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.26595)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `video`, `agents`, `multimodal`

### TL;DR

A systematic study of **Code2Video**: having an agent build 3D proxy scenes (with physics) and condition the video generator on them. Proxy granularity trades off against motion realism and temporal consistency. Coda-DaVinci then dispatches between proxy-guided and direct generation per prompt.

### Key idea

Decouple scene planning from pixel synthesis: an LLM-coded 3D proxy specifies geometry, motion, and physics; a video diffusion model renders it. An agentic router picks whether a prompt benefits from proxy guidance. Coarse proxies win on natural motion; medium-grained proxies win on object/scene consistency.

### Main finding

Agentic dispatch yields stronger instruction following, scene consistency, and physical plausibility than direct generation across the evaluated video benchmarks, while remaining limited on long-video transitions and scene-setting fidelity.

### Why it matters

Pushes video generation toward the same **LLM-planned-+-generator-executes** split that has worked for image generation in Code2Image / DALL·E tool-use. Shows that proxy fidelity isn't monotonically good — more detail hurts motion naturalness.

## 4. HiUni

**Authors:** Zhenyu Zhang (lead), Jiahao Hu, Jingdong Du, Yichen Zhang, Yi Song, et al. — *Affiliation not disclosed on HF page*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.26107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26107) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26107) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.26107)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `diffusion`, `unified`

### TL;DR

A unified understanding-and-generation model that reuses a pretrained VLM's language-aligned ViT and dense transformer backbone for both. All task-specific computation is pushed to the output side: a Pixel Diffusion Head for generation, plus a lightweight **HiGate** pathway to recover fine spatial detail.

### Key idea

Rather than maintaining separate visual encoders / backbones per task (as most unified models do), HiUni keeps *one* dense transformer and *one* ViT. The dense-backbone invariance between understanding and generation is preserved; the HiGate side-path routes high-frequency signal around the shared trunk when the task is generation.

### Main finding

Understanding quality stays near the original VLM's; generation and editing are competitive with task-specialized unified baselines. The authors argue this shows a single dense transformer trunk can serve both heads without the usual understanding/generation trade.

### Why it matters

Challenges the standard "two visual pathways or two backbones" blueprint for unified multimodal models (e.g. Chameleon, Emu, Show-o). Points toward sharing a single VLM backbone across generation, giving smaller, cheaper unified stacks.

## 5. System 1 agents

**Authors:** Eleftherios Tzanis, Michail E. Klontzas — *ATI Lab, Dept. of Radiology, University of Crete*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.21053](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21053) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.21053) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.21053)
**Topics:** `agents`, `tool-use`, `safety`

### TL;DR

Tool-calling agents for clinical use can't send patient data to cloud APIs, and local LLMs invent tool arguments. System 1 agents sidestep generation entirely: a deterministic compiler turns each tool schema into a multiple-choice question answered in a single forward pass. No generated text means no hallucinated arguments.

### Key idea

Precompile tool selection into a closed-set **classification problem** over a dynamically generated MCQ: the schema is the choice-set, the user request is the stem, and a single-pass decision model picks one. Zero free-form generation ⇒ zero argument hallucination.

### Main finding

Across 1,952 requests over 157 imaging and biomedical tools, the best System 1 agent reaches 97.0% correct tool selection / correct refusal, beating 18 local LLM-agent configurations. Rate of invented tool arguments: 0% (vs. 5.5–19.7% for the LLM agents). Open source.

### Why it matters

Shows that for latency- and privacy-constrained agent deployments, you can often get away without generative tool-use at all. A useful baseline to measure LLM agent "freedom tax" against, and a template for hardened agents in regulated settings.

## 6. Posterior Matching Distillation

**Authors:** Not disclosed on HF page.
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.26629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26629) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26629) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.26629)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `distillation`

### TL;DR

Diffusion distillation usually collapses sample diversity because reverse-KL favors dominant modes. PMD matches *exponentially reweighted averages* of student samples to the teacher's posterior means, which under Gaussian corruption reduces to a single shifted teacher evaluation per step.

### Key idea

At zero population loss, PMD recovers the teacher's *full* posterior over clean images at each trained noise level — not just its mean or its mode. The objective requires only one teacher forward per training step despite targeting a distributional match, because the posterior mean under Gaussian noise is a cheap shift of the teacher's output.

### Main finding

Best 4-step FID among compared methods on SDXL and FLUX.2 [klein] 4B, with higher intra-prompt diversity than reverse-KL distillation baselines.

### Why it matters

Four-step samplers are the practical frontier for diffusion serving. PMD narrows the quality/diversity gap vs. the teacher without extra teacher calls, making aggressive step compression more attractive for production image and (eventually) video models.

## 7. WM^MLLM

**Authors:** Enze Xie, Jincheng Yu, Jin Wang, Haozhe Liu, Yitong Li, Juanxi Tian, Felix Heide — *Affiliations not fully listed on HF (Heide: Princeton / NVIDIA)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.24193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24193) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.24193) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.24193)
**Topics:** `multimodal`, `world-model`, `agents`

### TL;DR

An interactive world model built on top of MLLMs: the world state lives *outside* the image/video generator, as a panorama with a revision history. The MLLM parses each request into operations on that state, decides where local edits are written, and gatekeeps candidates before committing.

### Key idea

Separate **state** (a persistent panorama + history) from **view** (what any pixel generator renders). The MLLM acts as the state manager: it names operations, picks local edit regions, verifies candidate edits for return-and-locality consistency, and only then commits. This gives world models the properties MLLMs are good at — long-horizon memory, symbolic querying, local edit reasoning — without changing the pixel backbone.

### Main finding

Beats 6 world-model baselines on MBench-A and 15 on CompBench. All revisits and passing local edits satisfy the authors' return and locality checks (a property most prior models fail even when they look visually plausible).

### Why it matters

MLLMs have been shown to drive agents; this paper is a cleaner story about using MLLMs to *structure the environment itself*, pushing world modeling from "predict next frame" toward "edit a maintained scene representation under natural-language operations."

## 8. VSpark

**Authors:** Suho Yoo, Youngjoon Jang, Hyebin Cho, Taeyeop Lee, Jaewook Jeong, Joon Son Chung — *KAIST AI*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.22666](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22666) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.22666) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.22666)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`, `speculative-decoding`, `multimodal`, `VLA`

### TL;DR

Speculative decoding tailored to autoregressive vision-language-action (VLA) models that emit robot actions one token at a time. Three changes: action-noise-calibrated rollouts for drafting, feature alignment between draft and target hidden states, and reuse of predictions from rejected drafts.

### Key idea

VLA token distributions are action-shaped, not text-shaped, so drafting policies trained on text underperform. VSpark pre-conditions draft training with action-space noise, aligns draft and target hidden features so acceptance becomes more predictable, and does not waste the compute from rejected drafts — predictions from them are cached and reused.

### Main finding

Lossless 1.28×–2.10× speedups across four VLA backbones and four manipulation benchmarks. Zero regression in task success relative to the base VLA.

### Why it matters

As VLA models are deployed in real robots, latency per action tops the serving budget. VSpark brings speculative decoding — until now a text-LLM trick — to the autoregressive VLA setting with VLA-specific techniques, not just a reused text drafter.

## 9. LightningMoBA

**Authors:** Not listed on HF page (code at github.com/Tencent/LightningMoBA; Tencent-authored).
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.26268](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26268) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26268) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.26268)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`, `sparse-attention`, `MoE`, `kernels`

### TL;DR

Keeps Mixture of Block Attention's block-scoring rule and softmax attention, but redesigns how blocks are selected and executed. Three ingredients: threshold-based dynamic routing (instead of fixed Top-k), KV-centric work ownership with fanout-aware splitting, and Hopper pipelining.

### Key idea

**Dynamic routing** uses a per-query threshold on block scores, so the number of selected blocks varies per token instead of being capped at a fixed Top-k. **KV-centric execution** owns work by KV block rather than by query, which fanout-splits long sequences without redundant KV reloads. **Hopper pipelining** overlaps the selection pass with attention compute, exposing TMA/async-copy on H100.

### Main finding

1.20×–1.60× end-to-end generation speedup over FlashMoBA at matched Top-16 retention, across five checkpoints. Mean RULER score rises from 74.42 → 75.28 — speedup *and* a small quality gain at long context.

### Why it matters

Sparse-attention variants live or die by their kernel implementation. LightningMoBA shows that most of MoBA's untapped headroom was in execution strategy, not in the scoring rule itself — the usual story for sparse attention at generation time.

## 10. RL Post-Training in Diffusion Transformers Is Activation Steering

**Authors:** Kaan Oktay (fal), Alec Helbling (Georgia Tech / IBM), Atakan Kara (fal), Duen Horng Chau (Georgia Tech), Pinar Yanardag (Virginia Tech)
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.26627](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26627) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26627) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.26627)
**Topics:** `diffusion`, `RL`, `interp`, `steering`

### TL;DR

Argues that most of the gain from RL post-training of diffusion transformers can be reproduced by a small, fixed set of steering vectors added to a *frozen* base model's block outputs. The vectors are fitted to match the RL-tuned model's velocity predictions — no reward in the training objective.

### Key idea

Fit per-block additive activation offsets so the frozen base reproduces the RL model's outputs. A constant shift, applied at every step, already captures 90–96% of the reward gains on six image models and ~90–112% on two video models. When trained with online RL directly against the reward, the steering vectors *exceed* weight-based RL on SD3.5-M text rendering and human preference.

### Main finding

Reward gains that look like "learning" from RLHF/RLAIF for diffusion are, on these models, mostly a constant-shift effect the frozen generator turns into prompt-specific behavior. Steering-only RL recovers them with a tiny parameter footprint.

### Why it matters

A striking reframing: RL in diffusion isn't moving the policy far; it's nudging activations. If this generalizes, diffusion RLHF collapses into something closer to activation-steering fine-tuning — far cheaper to train, store, and merge. Also sharpens the interp claim that steering vectors can replace parameter updates.

## 11. A CPU Inside an LLM

**Authors:** Not listed on HF page.
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.26450](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26450) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26450) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.26450)
**Topics:** `interp`, `weight-editing`, `arithmetic`

### TL;DR

An automated compiler writes deterministic arithmetic and Boolean circuits *directly into* a pretrained model's weights — no gradient fine-tuning, no training data, no runtime hooks — and claims to preserve natural-language ability. Evaluated as a sparse weight-space sidecar on Granite 4.2 3B and Qwen 3.5 4B.

### Key idea

Treat the model as a programmable substrate: closed-form constructions implement signed multi-digit arithmetic and parity/majority/Boolean circuits as sparse weight edits. No gradients mean no catastrophic forgetting, no held-out data, no training compute.

### Main finding

High arithmetic accuracy on the compiled operations, reports said to exceed LoRA fine-tuning on the same tasks, and small memory footprint for the sparse sidecar. Base NLP behavior claimed intact.

### Why it matters

Zero-gradient compilation into pretrained weights is a distinct lane from LoRA/SFT/RL for giving a model a fixed deterministic capability. Interesting both as a practical tool (reliable arithmetic without tool use) and as an interp artifact — exact circuits now co-exist with learned ones inside the same weights.

## 12. NarrowSpec

**Authors:** Not listed on HF page (corresponding author at @nvidia.com; NVIDIA inferred).
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.23835](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23835) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.23835) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.23835)
**Topics:** `efficient-inference`, `speculative-decoding`

### TL;DR

A speculative-decoding draft model's residual width need not match the target's. NarrowSpec halves or quarters the draft width and reports largely preserved acceptance plus a non-trivial end-to-end speedup, across two GPUs, two drafter families, and two targets.

### Key idea

The conventional wisdom is that draft and target should share a width to keep acceptance rates up. NarrowSpec shows this is largely free overhead — the drafter can be substantially narrower, with only a small acceptance hit that is easily paid back by the cheaper draft forward.

### Main finding

1.06×–1.44× end-to-end speedups over width-matched drafters across the eight evaluated configurations, with matched or near-matched quality.

### Why it matters

Serving fleets that already run EAGLE- or Medusa-style drafters can shrink them without retraining the target. One of the cleanest "almost free" wins for speculative decoding since the original acceptance-rate papers.

## 13. Probing vs. Steering Directions

**Authors:** Afshin Sadeghi, Daniel Becker — *AI Assurance and Assessments (AAA), Fraunhofer IAIS*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.19499](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19499) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.19499) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.19499)
**Topics:** `interp`, `probing`, `steering`, `eval`

### TL;DR

A direction in a hidden state can be read (probing) or written (steering). This paper parameterizes a family of candidate directions from the covariance-adjusted discriminant to the raw mean difference and shows — across three instruction-tuned models, on truthfulness and sentiment — that the best probing direction is *not* the best steering direction. Steering favors mean-difference-like directions.

### Key idea

Probing and steering optimize different criteria. Probe quality rewards directions along which a classifier separates classes; steering quality rewards directions whose *addition* changes behavior reliably. The paper shows these objectives can disagree, and that mean-difference directions transfer across models better for probing than for steering.

### Main finding

- Best probe ≠ best steerer for truthfulness and sentiment.
- Probe directions transfer across models more reliably than steering directions.
- On PopQA, steering toward "unknown" mainly converts uncertain errors into abstentions rather than correcting them — a safety-leaning but not accuracy-improving effect.

### Why it matters

Interp papers routinely conflate "we found a direction" with "we can steer with it" and vice versa. This paper separates the two objectives and quantifies the gap, which should refactor how activation-steering interventions get reported.

## 14. Language Models Can Rewrite Their Own Weights

**Authors:** Neil He, Changyi Yang, Andy Mao, Xi Zhu, Chaoran Cheng, Kaiqu Liang, Shuaike Shen, Zetian Li, Ruihan Guo, Ge Liu — *UIUC · CMU · Rutgers · Princeton · Stanford (not mapped per-author)*
**Links:** [arXiv:2611.21885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21885) · [HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.21885) · [AlphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.21885)
**Topics:** `interp`, `weight-editing`, `emergence`

### TL;DR

A 27B open LM can propose edits to its *own* weights that improve itself with no gradient steps: +6.6 accuracy points on 490 unseen problems in one test. The same mechanism repairs a deliberately corrupted copy of the model, and the repair carries over to image benchmarks. The capability is scale-emergent: 2B makes no edit, 4B and 9B break themselves, only 27B helps itself.

### Key idea

Ask the model to emit a weight patch for itself. At small scales the model doesn't know how to edit without destroying its own circuits; at 27B it both self-improves on unseen problems and *repairs* an adversarially corrupted copy of its own weights. No gradients, no training loop.

### Main finding

- +6.6 pts on 490 held-out problems via a single self-edit.
- Successful repair of a corrupted weight copy, with transfer to image benchmarks that were never part of the editing target.
- Capability threshold between 9B and 27B on the four model sizes tested.

### Why it matters

An emergence result for self-modification: at scale, LMs reason about their own parameters well enough to improve themselves without any outer training loop. If this holds up under replication it reshapes both the self-improvement and interpretability agendas — and the safety story, since a model that can repair itself can also mis-edit itself.
