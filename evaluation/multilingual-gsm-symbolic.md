# Multilingual GSM-Symbolic
*Depth — one specific technique, grounded in its source paper(s).*

**TL;DR:** A 30,000-item extension of GSM-Symbolic to 15 languages. Symbolic templates generate millions of variants per seed to resist contamination, and a joint regression over model size, language resource level, reasoning use, and typological distance quantifies cross-lingual transfer in common units. Predicts a model's accuracy on an unseen language within 6.0 pp (r = 0.96). Introduced in *Multilingual GSM-Symbolic* (2026, arXiv:2610.03367).

## What it is

A multilingual mathematical reasoning benchmark built from symbolic templates. Each seed problem has an abstract-syntax representation (variables, operators, constants); concrete instances are generated by sampling values. Each seed is translated to 15 languages, keeping the math identical. This gives item-matched evaluation across languages — the same reasoning task in every language, differing only in surface form.

## How it works

Three design choices:

1. **Symbolic templates for decontamination.** Each seed can produce millions of surface variants. Even if a model memorized GSM8K, memorization is unhelpful on freshly instantiated variants.
2. **Item-matched translation.** For every seed, there is one problem in each of 15 languages carrying the same mathematical content. Within-model, within-seed differences are therefore pure language effects.
3. **Joint linear regression.** Fit $\mathrm{accuracy} \sim \beta_1 \cdot \mathrm{size} + \beta_2 \cdot \mathrm{resource} + \beta_3 \cdot \mathrm{reasoning} + \beta_4 \cdot \mathrm{typ\_dist} + \ldots$ across all (model, language) pairs. Coefficients are directly comparable.

Reported coefficients: size β = 1.77, resource level β = 0.77, reasoning β = 0.67, typological distance β = -0.25. Explains 92% of between-language variance but only 23% of model×language variance.

## Why it matters

Lets developers *predict* performance in low-resource languages from a few template evaluations instead of building a full dataset per language. A 32B model in Marathi is estimated to perform like a 10B model in English — directly usable guidance. The large between-language explained variance combined with small model×language explained variance says most of the "which languages are hard" question is about *the languages*, not model-specific quirks.

## Gotchas & tricks

- **"Reasoning" is a binary flag for whether the model uses CoT.** Treating it continuously (CoT token budget) would likely change the coefficient.
- **Typological distance uses a specific family metric** (lang2vec-style). Alternate metrics shift β₄ slightly.
- **Symbolic variants are only as decontaminated as the templating.** If a template leaks a GSM8K phrase verbatim, variants inherit the leak.
- **The 23% model×language explained variance leaves room for model-specific tuning.** The regression tells you where a *typical* model lands; a given deployed model may be systematically better/worse on a given language.
- **Not a replacement for human-authored math evals.** Templates are rigid; a template-only benchmark misses the "weird reasoning paths humans try" surface area.
