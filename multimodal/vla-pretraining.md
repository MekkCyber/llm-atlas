# VLA Pretraining on State-Transition-Labeled Trajectories
*Depth — one specific technique, grounded in its source paper(s).*

**TL;DR:** A Vision-Language-Action **pretraining recipe** that annotates raw manipulation trajectories with natural-language descriptions of the *scene state transitions* they produce ("the mug is now on the shelf"), then trains the VLA to predict actions conditioned on those descriptive captions. The captions are auto-generated by a scalable labeling pipeline, decoupling embodiment-agnostic *what changed in the world* from embodiment-specific *how this robot did it*. Enables an LLM-style pretrain / post-train split for robotics — used at scale by Xiaomi-Robotics-1 (100K+ hours, 2026).

## What it is

VLA pretraining has historically been either (a) small-scale supervised learning on hand-labeled task demonstrations, (b) large-scale pretraining on internet video (semantic-rich but action-free), or (c) trajectory-only prediction with weak language conditioning. None of these scale cleanly the way LLM pretraining does.

State-transition-labeled trajectory pretraining sits in a fourth position: trajectories are cheap (UMI-style hand-held collection scales past 100K hours), and *language captions of what changed* are also cheap when generated by an auto-labeling pipeline. Together they give a dense, language-conditioned pretraining signal that generalizes across embodiments — because "the mug is now on the shelf" is true regardless of which robot moved the mug.

## How it works

The recipe has three moving parts:

1. **Trajectory collection at scale.** Use embodiment-decoupled devices (Xiaomi uses UMI, a hand-held gripper) to collect real-world manipulation trajectories cheaply — no dedicated robot fleet needed. Xiaomi-Robotics-1 uses 100K+ hours.
2. **Auto-labeling pipeline.** For each trajectory clip, generate a natural-language caption describing the *scene state transition* — the difference between the start and end states of the scene. Typical implementation: a VLM captions the before/after frames and describes the change. The output is a caption per clip.
3. **Pretraining objective.** Train the VLA to predict actions conditioned on the current observation *plus the state-transition caption*. The model learns to produce actions that induce the described transition — general-purpose action-generation, tied to language.

Post-training (Stage 2 of the recipe) then re-aligns the model with imperative task prompts and embodiment-specific control conventions, converting the descriptive-transition head into an imperative-task-prompt head.

The auto-labeling step is the enabling primitive: without it, pretraining requires either expensive human annotation or a language-free trajectory-only objective (which under-uses the model's language capacity).

## Why it matters

- **Unlocks LLM-style scaling for VLA.** Trajectory data + language captions are both cheap; combining them gives a dense, scalable pretraining signal.
- **Embodiment-decoupled.** State transitions describe the world, not the robot. A VLA pretrained on human-collected UMI trajectories can be post-trained onto arbitrary robot embodiments.
- **Scaling behavior transfers to post-training.** Xiaomi-Robotics-1 reports that a stronger pretraining checkpoint yields better real-robot post-training performance — the "pretraining ceiling determines post-training ceiling" pattern from LLMs.
- **Data-efficient downstream fine-tuning.** With the pretrained foundation in place, novel dexterous tasks fine-tune from small demonstration budgets.

## Gotchas & tricks

- **Caption quality bounds the model.** If the auto-labeler mislabels transitions (missing objects, wrong verbs, low temporal resolution), the pretraining signal is noisy. Ablate the captioner on a held-out set.
- **UMI ≠ robot.** UMI-collected data has no robot kinematics; the post-training stage does the embodiment adaptation. Skipping post-training on UMI-only pretraining gives a model that "understands" tasks but can't control the robot.
- **State transitions are coarse.** Descriptions of end-state changes miss mid-trajectory events (obstacle avoidance, in-hand manipulation micro-motions). For dexterous tasks, augment captions with sub-trajectory descriptions.
- **Balance descriptive vs. imperative during post-training.** Over-training on imperative prompts collapses the descriptive-transition head; the paper's schedule preserves it.
- **Scaling laws are less clean than LLMs.** Trajectory data is more heterogeneous than text tokens; expect noisier scaling curves that still trend in the right direction.
