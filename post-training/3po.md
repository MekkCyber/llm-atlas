# 3PO — Perturbed Parameter Policy Optimization
*Depth — variational, parameter-space exploration for RLVR — a drop-in over GRPO.*

**TL;DR:** RLVR loops usually explore in *action space* (higher sampling temperature, entropy bonuses), which distorts the answer distribution the reward function grades. 3PO explores in **parameter space** instead: cast the policy as a *distribution* over parameters, and each rollout is generated by a slightly perturbed policy sampled from that distribution. On OLMo and Qwen2.5-Math it outperforms standard GRPO at comparable compute and produces fewer stuck-mode rollouts late in training.

**Prereqs:** [grpo](grpo.md), [rlvr](rlvr.md), [_rl](_rl.md)
**Related:** [_post-training](_post-training.md), [ppo](ppo.md)

---

## What it is

A variational RL post-training method for LLMs that treats the policy $\pi_\theta$ as a random variable — the training loop learns a *distribution* $q_\phi(\theta)$ over policy parameters. Rollouts are generated by first sampling $\theta \sim q_\phi$ and then generating from $\pi_\theta$. Advantages are computed the RLVR way (verifiable ground-truth rewards) with the group-relative baseline.

## How it works

The per-step loop:

```
for step in range(n_steps):
    for prompt q in batch:
        θ_1, ..., θ_G ~ q_φ(θ)              # sample G perturbed policies
        r_g = generate(π_{θ_g}, q)          # one rollout per perturbed policy
        reward_g = verifier(q, r_g)         # binary / scalar verifier reward
    advantages = group_relative(rewards)    # GRPO-style
    update q_φ                              # variational objective
```

The perturbation distribution is typically Gaussian around a central set of weights and cheap to sample (small, structured, or low-rank). Because exploration is *external* to the token-generation process, the model's own token distribution is not distorted by entropy hacks that make it worse at the actual task.

The parameter-space perturbation replaces (or complements) action-space entropy control. Standard PPO/GRPO clipping and KL-to-reference terms carry over unchanged.

## Why it matters

- **Action-space exploration is coupled to reward shape.** Higher temperature both explores *and* moves the answer distribution off the mode the reward function grades. Parameter-space exploration decouples the two.
- **Fewer problematic rollouts.** GRPO late in training tends to collapse into a small set of degenerate modes; sampling perturbed policies breaks that symmetry without changing the reward signal.
- **Drop-in replacement.** Runs on the same infra as GRPO; the only added cost is sampling and applying the perturbation, which is negligible for structured/low-rank perturbations.
- **Sits in a growing family** of Bayesian / variational RL-for-LLMs approaches trying to inject principled uncertainty into a very brittle loop.

## Gotchas & tricks

- **Perturbation scale is the key hyperparameter.** Too small and it's indistinguishable from GRPO; too large and you're sampling incoherent policies. Anneal it with training.
- **Structured perturbations beat unstructured ones.** Perturbing only the last few layers, or restricting to a low-rank subspace, gives most of the benefit at a fraction of the cost.
- **Value networks are still not needed.** 3PO inherits GRPO's group-relative baseline — no critic.
- **Doesn't help when the base model has zero success rate.** As with any RLVR method, if every rollout scores 0 the gradient is 0. SFT up to a nonzero success rate first.
- **Diagnose via rollout-diversity metrics, not just reward curves.** The point of 3PO is diverse, useful rollouts; monitor rollout entropy or verifier-pass-rate variance across the group.
