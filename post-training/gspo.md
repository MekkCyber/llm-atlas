# GSPO — Group Sequence Policy Optimisation
*Depth — one specific technique, grounded in its source paper(s).*

**TL;DR:** A policy-gradient variant that generalises [GRPO](grpo.md) by treating the entire generated **sequence** (rather than one sample per prompt) as the unit for group-relative baselines. In harnessed agentic RL, a single trajectory is composed of many LLM calls and tool responses; GSPO computes the policy-gradient ratio *at the sequence level* and normalises the advantage across a group of full sequences that share the same starting condition, keeping rollout–training log-prob correlation high under compaction and re-serialisation.

## What it is

GRPO removed PPO's value model by scoring $G$ i.i.d. completions from the same prompt and using their reward mean/std as the baseline. GSPO keeps the group-baseline idea but scales to **long, structured sequences** — coding-agent trajectories that span many turns, tool calls, and compaction events — by defining the group over full sequences and computing the importance ratio at the sequence level.

## How it works

### The sequence-level ratio

For a group of $G$ sequences $\tau_1, \ldots, \tau_G$ generated by an old policy $\pi_{\text{old}}$ for the same starting condition, GSPO estimates

$$
r_i = \frac{\log \pi_\theta(\tau_i)}{\log \pi_{\text{old}}(\tau_i)}
$$

with the two log-probabilities summed over all *policy-generated* tokens in $\tau_i$ (excluding tool outputs and injected system content).

### The group-relative advantage

The advantage of sequence $i$ is its reward minus the group mean, standardised by the group std:

$$
A_i = \frac{R(\tau_i) - \text{mean}(R)}{\text{std}(R) + \epsilon} .
$$

No value model; the group is its own baseline. The PPO clip is applied to $r_i$ at the sequence level rather than token-by-token.

### Why sequence-level

In a harnessed agent trajectory the model's tokens are interleaved with tool outputs, environment observations, and (crucially) *compacted* history injected by the harness. Token-level importance sampling breaks under this — a small mismatch in tokenisation between rollout and trainer accumulates and drives $r$ far from 1. Sequence-level $r$ is much more robust: only the model-generated tokens count, and the same set of tokens must be recomputable at train time given the exact bytes the harness saw.

## Why it matters

- **Fits harnessed agentic RL.** In frameworks like LEGO-RL, GSPO is the algorithm of choice because it keeps rollout↔training log-prob correlation ≥0.99 even when the harness compacts context or re-serialises tool outputs.
- **Handles variable-length turns cleanly.** PPO's token-level advantage assumes stable turn structure; GSPO's sequence-level baseline is invariant to how many tokens ended up in a trajectory.
- **Reduces reward-hacking flexibility.** Because advantage is computed over full trajectories, small mid-trajectory exploits that don't change the terminal reward don't get the amplified positive signal they would under token-level advantage.

### Reported use

LEGO-RL (Du et al., 2026) trains Qwen3.5-35B-A3B with GSPO across three coding harnesses on SWE-bench Verified:

- OpenHands SDK: **64.0 → 70.4**
- Claude Code: **62.4 → 68.2**
- OpenCode: **57.2 → 66.6**

Rollout–training log-prob correlation stays **>0.99** throughout.

## Gotchas & tricks

- **Only count model-generated tokens in $r_i$.** Tool outputs and injected system content must be masked out — including them makes the ratio meaningless under compaction.
- **Log-prob correlation is the health check.** If rollout↔training log-prob correlation drops, tokenisation drift or compaction handling is broken — fix the plumbing before touching the RL hyperparameters.
- **Group size matters.** $G$ too small → noisy baseline; $G$ too large → wasted rollouts. Practical sweet spot in coding-agent RL is $G \in [4, 16]$.
- **Clip range on sequence ratios differs from token ratios.** Sequence-level $r$ has smaller variance; a clip range that is fine for PPO token-level ratios will be too permissive at sequence level.
- **Not a replacement for GRPO in short-turn settings.** For single-turn RLHF/RLVR without harness compaction, GRPO's token-level advantage is fine and cheaper to reason about. GSPO's win is *harness in the loop*.
