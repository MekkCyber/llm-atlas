# Test-Time Training (TTT) and the Self-Feedback Failure
*Depth — one specific technique, grounded in its source paper(s).*

**TL;DR:** Test-time training (TTT) lets a model update its own weights during inference — e.g., Adam steps on predicted tokens as they stream in. On long streams, retaining updates from the model's own outputs makes prediction on independent human text *worse*. The 2026 KAUST analysis isolates the cause: **self-generated feedback**, not generation quality. The fix is **Settlement**: evaluate each candidate state on independent evidence before committing the update.

## What it is

A family of methods that keep adapting the model after deployment. The simplest version: as a stream of tokens arrives, do a few optimizer steps on them before predicting the next chunk. Variants include storing updates in **fast weights** (e.g., a LoRA head) rather than in the base model.

The appeal is online adaptation to shift (new user, new domain, new topic). The failure is that if updates are kept across chunks and the chunks are *generated by the same model*, the model drifts: each update changes the generator for the next training example, closing a feedback loop.

## How it works

- **Baseline TTT.** Stream in; after every $K$ tokens, run $m$ Adam steps on them to update $\theta$; proceed. Updates persist.
- **The failure mode.** Across 128K-token streams, three TTT-E2E configurations (125M, 760M, 3B) and Adam-updated Qwen3-4B all show that **retaining self-generated updates degrades prediction on independent human text**.
- **Three matched ablations.**
  - *Fixed Generation* — freeze the generator, update as before. Removes >98% of the damage at 125M and 760M. Writing isn't the failure.
  - *Recorded Replay* — separate the loss from *reading* already-corrupted text vs. the loss from *storing* a new update on it.
  - *Paired single update* — one update improves prediction on its source but worsens new real text. Shows the local conflict.
- **Settlement.** Before committing a candidate state, evaluate it on **independent real text**. Keep the update only if the independent evidence stays good. Leaves mean endpoint gaps of 0.07 and -0.02 nats at 125M/760M while retaining real-text adaptation.

## Why it matters

- Rules out "writing is intrinsically bad for TTT" and names the actual culprit (self-feedback). The remedy is a **general design principle** for any online learner: commit updates only after validating on data not generated by the current loop.
- Applies to any system where the policy generates its own training data: TTT, self-improvement loops, online RL with on-policy rollouts without external verifiers, agent self-distillation.

## Gotchas & tricks

- **Settlement needs an evidence stream separate from the generation stream.** In practice, a held-out slice of the input or a small periodic probe suffices; the paper's probes are inexpensive.
- **Fast weights don't automatically solve it.** Even LoRA-stored updates participate in the feedback loop if they influence the generator.
- **Short streams hide the failure.** A few thousand tokens isn't enough for drift to compound; evaluate on 100K+ tokens or many sessions in series.
- **Signals that look like adaptation can be collapse.** A monotonically dropping loss on self-generated text + rising loss on real text is the classic signature. Monitor both.
